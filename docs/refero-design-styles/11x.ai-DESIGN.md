# 11x — earthbound futures

## Overview

Cinematic desert landscapes and personable portraits make advanced software feel tangible and human. The light editorial canvas shifts between warm stone, pale blue-grey and deep petrol, with tightly set grotesk headlines and compact pill actions. Product interfaces appear as clean floating objects against rugged terrain, while spacious proof mosaics and translucent footer panels keep the experience substantial rather than futuristic for its own sake.

### Essentials

- A landscape is the opening stage, not a decorative thumbnail: full-bleed warm rock imagery fills the 900px first screen, with a left-aligned white 120px/1 headline at weight 500 and -0.03em tracking.
- Human scale meets monumental terrain: stage a portrait or a floating interface against muted ochre landscapes; keep the interface predominantly white and its surroundings atmospheric. Without portrait media, use a real product workflow floating over a photographic rock scene.
- Conversion controls stay small against the scenery: 45px black pill buttons with 12px 24px padding inside the page; the opening uses a 46px cream pill beside a translucent portrait-led pill.
- Color arrives in architectural zones, not rainbow accents: white and stone #d7cecc dominate the reading areas, with a substantial #0b252a chapter and restrained pastel blue, teal, lavender and sand proof tiles. Keep saturated color inside interface details and logos.
- Proof is a mosaic rather than a row of identical testimonials: a 1024px four-column grid with 16px gaps mixes 244px metric/logo tiles and 504px quote tiles, all with 16px corners; metrics use 46.1px/1 and quotes 28px/1.2.
- Carry the landscape through the ending: a white 24px-radius conversion card overlaps a terrain-backed footer; separate 16px-radius translucent stone panels hold navigation and utility content, leaving the central landscape visible.
- Use compact moving marks beneath expansive statements: monochrome customer logos line the hero base, and three rows of 38px-high outcome chips fade into a deep-petrol chapter at both edges. Preserve this ticker device with relevant proof or outcomes rather than unrelated decoration.

- Headlines are medium rather than bold; optical density comes from tight tracking and near-solid line spacing.

- The monumental result numerals are regular weight, with a much more open 1.3 line height than headlines.

## Imagery and graphics

### Cinematic terrain

**Use:** Opening and closing signature scenes, with smaller echoes behind product demonstrations.

**Observed:** Rust, ochre and brown rocky valleys under hazy olive-tan skies. Strong foreground rock texture and soft distant mountain silhouettes give depth; the opening is an expansive close view of rugged terrain rather than a centered object. White typography sits directly on it. The footer scene includes two small silver-suited figures walking into the landscape.

**Transfer:** Use original rocky landscape photography or a carefully art-directed render with the same warm mineral palette and depth. Retain a journey-like sense of scale with tiny human figures or a path; without suitable figures use a textured foreground and distant horizon, not a generic gradient.

**Build:** Use a full-bleed cover image, layered with a black veil of 0.2–0.5 alpha. Choose a broad foreground ridge across the lower third and a hazy horizon behind it; preserve dark negative space behind white text. Reuse a related landscape at the footer, with small figures or a path centered in the open space between panels.

### Personable portrait stage

**Use:** Product introductions, live demonstration panels and small action avatars.

**Observed:** Naturalistic head-and-shoulders portraits, softly lit against warm beige backgrounds; business clothing, close framing, direct gaze and a friendly expression. Glass status pills and a dark translucent message tray sit over the portrait. These are large human focal points, not ornamental avatar grids.

**Transfer:** Use an original presenter or expert portrait appropriate to the new product. Keep the lighting warm and the background restrained; a detailed product artifact is the fallback when a human persona is inappropriate.

### Floating product artifacts

**Use:** Capability explanations and the large product stage.

**Observed:** White interface windows, small profile cards, calendars, charts and notification objects float over light grey, cream or rocky backings. Screens are readable enough to signal function but not dense enough to compete with marketing headings. The broad stage frames one main app view above four descriptive tabs.

**Transfer:** Show one decisive task using real product UI. For a minimum version, create a clean white window with a sidebar, task list and one highlighted result over an original warm terrain photograph.

**Build:** Place an original interface image inside a 24px-radius cream stage with 40px padding. Behind the interface, use a wide terrain image with soft warm exposure; stack only a few rounded white task cards, offset slightly in depth. Keep shadows local and soft rather than glowing.

### Feature light studies

**Only for:** comparison-page feature discovery

**Use:** Compact square feature links in a horizontally clipped rail.

**Observed:** Black tile interiors carry blurred bronze light bars, soft rings or diagonal pools, framed by a thick pale sand rim. A tiny index sits top left, a northeast arrow top right and white feature text bottom left.

**Transfer:** Assign a simple light form to each feature rather than making each tile a miniature screenshot.

**Build:** Inside a 24px-radius sand backing with 8px inset, use a black square. Add one blurred tan ellipse, ring or 45-degree strip with low-opacity radial or linear gradients; clip it within the rounded interior. Reserve the lower quarter for the short white label. Match the soft glow; exact stops are not essential.

### Company photography

**Only for:** company storytelling pages

**Use:** Team and office chapters.

**Observed:** Large warm candid team photographs with rounded corners; city skyline photographs at dusk sit in two equal columns over the recurring landscape. Location information sits below in translucent cream panels.

**Transfer:** Use original team photographs with genuine interaction and city views with atmospheric evening light; retain clear separation between human culture and product demonstration.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small monochrome filled arrows and utility symbols at 16px, 24px and 32px. Use-case benefit icons are white 24px outlines with 2px round strokes inside black 40px squares with 8px corners.

**Transfer:** Use a consistent simple icon set, keeping filled arrows for actions and outlined pictograms within black benefit badges. Interface logos may retain their own colors.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Landscape promise opening

**Observed:** The first 900px is one edge-to-edge terrain scene. A 55px black announcement band sits above a 72px transparent navigation row. Behind everything is detailed warm rock imagery with a dark veil; navigation, headline, support and the customer-logo rail are white. The display headline begins below the navigation at roughly the upper fifth of the screen, spans about two thirds of the width and wraps into two tightly stacked lines. It is followed by a 24px trust line with 24px spacing, then the Opening button and Portrait action with a 16px gap. The lower third stays mostly scenic, with monochrome logos and small case-study captions along the base. The entire promise, support, action pair and terrain are visible in the first screen; the live demonstration begins below it.

**Transfer:** Use a compact two-part outcome promise over an original textured landscape, preserving the large type and scenic lower breathing room even without source-quality video. A still photograph is sufficient. Company storytelling uses a single 152px manifesto line; personified products split the opening between a 103.7px greeting and a large portrait; use-case pages use a 100px title plus 56px benefit; comparisons center an 80px contrast headline and short glass form over a dark bronze atmosphere.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the full-bleed terrain with a small right-hand app mockup on white, center the default headline, or place a giant opaque card behind the opening copy.

**Text style:** `display` — ES Allianz 500 · 120px/1 · tracking -0.03em

**Observed:** A 56px or 64px headline sits within 896px, usually centered. Supporting copy is 16px or 18px and constrained to 640px, separated by 24px. A compact action follows, then 48px or more before media. Major sections use 128px vertical padding.

**Transfer:** Pair one crisp outcome with one explanatory paragraph; choose one headline size by hierarchy rather than mixing sizes within the same rank.

**Observed:** Centered heading and support sit above the Live demonstration in a 1024px container on white. The first demonstration has a tall portrait and hands-on controls; a second introduction uses a shallower portrait-and-copy split card.

Warm cream cells, 1px low-alpha rules, colored header bands and small status icons distinguish the offerings.

**Transfer:** Write parallel, factual comparisons with short statements and consistent row heights.

### Objection handling

**Only for:** use-case and comparison pages

**Observed:** Use-case pages use Compact FAQ centered on white. Comparisons instead put a two-line 48px heading left and a broad rounded question list right, with tiny question indexes and plus controls.

**Transfer:** Choose the compact panel for practical setup questions and the split layout for a more editorial comparison.

## Writing

Confident, outcome-led and humanizing. Concrete work verbs and operational detail make ambitious automation claims feel actionable, while short contrasts supply the emotional hook.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, named worker personas, customer logos, photographs, quotes, funding claims, comparative assertions or performance figures. Replace all claims with verified facts for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your team. The company uses we for partnership; a personified product can introduce itself in first person, while ordinary capability copy uses the product name or an implied subject.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are typically 3–9 words and often split into two compact clauses. Support is one or two plain sentences; longer operational explanation belongs in cards or FAQs. Actions are brief verb phrases without exclamation marks.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Pair an advanced capability with a human or business outcome. | 4–7 words | “Digital Workers, Human Results”<br>“Be more human.” |
| Section headline | State the changed experience as a concrete contrast. | 5–10 words | “Pipeline from leads you'd written off.”<br>“Every customer gets a conversation, not a sequence” |
| Support | Explain what the product does and what the team gains. | One or two sentences | “Power your growth with digital workers that run your full pipeline and free your team for higher impact work.” |
| Feature title | Compact verb-led capability or balanced human-centered phrase. | 2–5 words | “Always learning”<br>“Human when it counts” |
| Action | Verb plus tangible next experience. | 2–5 words | “Get a Live Demo”<br>“Read customer story”<br>“Join us” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** on autopilot; around the clock; your team; from first step to outcome; scale; adapt; research; engage; live; human; every

**Avoid:** synergy; revolutionary ecosystem; magical; next-gen solutioning; unverifiable superlatives; cute robot slang

**Rules:**

- Use task verbs to explain automation rather than a string of abstract AI adjectives.
- Follow an aspirational headline with a concrete workflow or operational consequence.
- Use contrasts such as conversation versus sequence to explain a change in experience.
- Keep technical acronyms within audience-appropriate explanatory copy, not the broad opening promise.
- Use quantified proof only with supporting context and a clear attribution.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Set the default opening at 120px/1, weight 500 and -0.03em tracking.
- Keep 72px desktop gutters so the broad stage and closing card share a 1296px span.
- Keep standard actions at 45px with 12px 24px padding and a fully rounded silhouette.
- Use 16px gutters and 16px corners to unify the mixed-width proof mosaic.
- Give major sections 128px vertical breathing room while keeping paragraph and action gaps near 24px.
- Use white interfaces and warm rocky surroundings together, not as unrelated image styles.
- Preserve the three-row faded outcome ticker with compact 8px-radius chips.
- End with separate translucent footer panels and an open scenic center.

### Don't

- Do not make display type 700-weight; tight medium-weight letterforms create the intended mass.
- Do not use neon greens, electric blue page fills or rainbow gradients for the marketing canvas.
- Do not put drop shadows under every card; broad flat color fields and fine edges define most surfaces.
- Do not make all proof cards identical three-column testimonials; use narrow metrics beside wider quotations.
- Do not stretch action pills to the whole container width on desktop.
- Do not uppercase or letter-space ordinary section tags; the 12px uppercase index belongs to the comparison variant.
- Do not replace the scenic footer with a uniform opaque multi-column slab.
- Do not let a dense application screen become the default first-screen focal point; the opening promise needs scenic scale.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared: opening action transitions use 0.15s; the translucent action increases its white fill and border alpha on hover.
- Declared: footer links transition opacity over 0.2s, from 0.5 to 1 on hover.
- Declared: a horizontal marquee animation runs 30s linear infinitely.
- Declared: the live demonstration panel transitions opacity and transform over 0.7s cubic-bezier(0.2,1,0.3,1).

**Transfer:**

- Keep ticker movement continuous and subordinate to the headline; maintain the petrol edge fades.
- Preserve gentle opacity feedback on utility links rather than introducing bright hover fills.

**Unknown:**

- Scroll-trigger sequencing, video playback behavior and mobile motion are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 479px, 767px, 768px and 991px; some templates also declare 480px, 992px, 1440px and 1920px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the full-bleed scenic opening but wrap the headline before it collides with the focal media.
- Stack portrait demonstrations and split feature cards while retaining the warm stage around the media.
- Collapse proof mosaics to a readable narrow grid and keep quotes wider than metric tiles when space permits.
- Reflow footer panels into grouped rows without turning the landscape into an opaque background color.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, gutter values, menu construction and breakpoint-specific stacking are unknown; these are adaptation rules rather than measured mobile layouts.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
