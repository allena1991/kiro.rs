# a16z — documentary warmth, glass hardware

## Overview

A bright, spare marketing style with tightly packed grotesk headlines and a surprisingly tactile layer of glass-like controls. White and pale gray architecture leaves the color to warm documentary footage, candid working scenes and informal portraits. It suits ambitious products that want to feel both institutionally credible and personally invested; spacious chapters alternate with dense, practical proof.

### Essentials

- Big promises feel spoken, not typeset as posters: 104px/1 hero text at weight 600 with -0.07em tracking, followed by 56px/60px chapter headings with -0.06em tracking.
- The opening is a human documentary stage: a full-width 450px media band beneath a 64px white header, centered white headline, support and one glass action; use real working footage or a candid still when video is unavailable.
- Controls look like tiny transparent hardware: 4px corners, 48px action height, diagonal white sheen, 12px backdrop blur and inset highlights; the header action is a compact 26px version, never a pill.
- Color belongs to life inside the imagery: the marketing architecture is entirely white, #f3f3f3, black and translucent gray. Natural sunset warmth and clothing colors occupy media, not branded background fills.
- Proof stays small inside large structures: three 408px-wide bordered cards use 19.5px semibold metrics rather than oversized numerals, with 16px muted explanations and aligned lower actions.
- A small transparent emblem and off-axis photo strip soften the grid: place a 172px glass-frame symbol near a chapter heading and stage candid rectangular photos at alternating angles. For products without people photography, use original process or workshop images and a simple geometric symbol in the same transparent frame.

## Imagery and graphics

### Documentary opening

**Use:** Default landing-page media stage.

**Observed:** Edge-to-edge footage of people and a camera crew on a terrace at sunset. Warm gold sky, hazy hills and reflective architecture surround a foreground person; the crop feels like a working moment rather than staged stock. White type floats directly over the scene.

**Transfer:** Use candid footage of the new product's real environment or a warm, well-composed still. Keep the center legible and human context visible at the edges.

### Transparent emblem

**Use:** Small sculptural punctuation around a major heading.

**Observed:** A compact clear square frame with thin nested edges and corner glints encloses a dark symbol; the object occupies roughly 172px rather than becoming a full hero render.

**Transfer:** Create an original simple glyph inside a transparent frame; even a crisp SVG frame with directional highlights is sufficient.

**Build:** Make a 172px square SVG viewBox. Center two inset rounded-square outlines with 1px black strokes at 0.25 opacity, add offset white strokes at 0.8 opacity and small diagonal corner facets. Place a black geometric glyph at the center; keep the center mostly transparent and the glints localized to corners.

### Working scene and portrait rail

**Use:** Documentary chapters explaining the product's real-world context.

**Observed:** A 1248px-wide natural-color interior photograph has no mockup bezel or rounded frame: foliage in the foreground, communal tables and exposed services give depth. A horizontal rail uses tall 303px by 455px candid seated portraits, 12px gutters, black names and muted affiliation captions.

**Transfer:** Photograph real use in a coherent location, then show collaborators or users in candid portrait format. Maintain natural light and ordinary clothing colors, not a uniform duotone.

### Tilted photo strip

**Use:** Human proof beneath a centered promise and action.

**Observed:** Four candid rectangular photos span beyond the content edges and are clipped by the section bottom. Alternating slight clockwise and counterclockwise rotations and uneven upper edges make a loose collage, without heavy borders or Polaroid captions.

**Transfer:** Use original customer, process or workshop stills with varied crops. For a software-only product, show people using the software or tangible work outputs, not four floating UI windows.

**Build:** Create a clipped full-width flex strip with four images roughly 320–380px wide, heights around 240–320px and narrow or overlapping gaps. Alternate rotations around -8deg, 0deg, 5deg and 12deg, vary translateY by 20–50px and align the lower portions beyond the clipping edge. These angles are a construction guide, not fixed transforms.

### Monochrome logo ticker

**Use:** Credibility between benefit cards and the next broad chapter.

**Observed:** Large organization marks rendered in muted gray move across the full viewport in two separate strips with small left-aligned context labels. A compact pause control sits at the right.

**Transfer:** Normalize permissioned partner or compatibility marks to a common gray visual weight; if no partner marks exist, use a restrained text strip of verifiable capabilities.

**Build:** Lay out repeated SVG or text marks in a no-wrap flex track; use rgba(0,0,0,0.58) for monochrome masks, generous breathing room and an overflow-hidden viewport. Duplicate the track for seamless movement and provide the Compact action pause control.

**Observed:** Small filled monochrome SVGs, commonly 20px for secondary actions, 24px for theme control and 16px for compact pause or utility marks. Arrows and social symbols are simple and dark.

**Transfer:** Choose compact solid glyphs with matching optical weight; avoid colorful outlined icon badges as default decoration.

## Writing

Confident, practical and personally invested. Short promises are backed by specific quantities and descriptions of what actually happens; warmth arrives through direct address and occasional informal punctuation rather than grandiose slogans.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the speedrun or a16z marks, names, proprietary program labels, portfolio identities, funding terms, partner counts, claims or attributed quotations. Replace all facts, testimonials and logos with the new product's own verified material. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address readers as you and your; use we for concrete help and commitments. The product name appears naturally in explanations, not in every heading.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are brief and conversational, often 3–7 words; larger chapter titles can run to two balanced lines. Support usually fits one or two sentences. Dense explainer passages mix plain prose, short lists and occasional emoji.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A short relational phrase that says how the product stands with the reader. | 3–5 words | “Your first believers” |
| Support | A direct verb followed by a concrete three-part benefit. | 8–14 words | “Providing you with capital, customers, and talent” |
| Chapter heading | Name the experience in plain language, with a concrete time or place when relevant. | 3–7 words | “Twelve weeks in San Francisco”<br>“What you get” |
| Action | Verb plus destination; use a short immediate verb for conversion. | 2–3 words | “Apply now”<br>“Explore companies” |
| Question heading | A natural spoken question, not an institutional support label. | 3–5 words | “Got any questions?” |
| Explainer closing | A personal supportive sentence with light informal punctuation. | 10–14 words | “We’re excited to provide the support you need to scale :)” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** you; we; get; build; help; explore; first; access; real; from day one

**Avoid:** synergize; revolutionary ecosystem; unleash limitless possibilities; best-in-class solutions; frictionless paradigm

**Rules:**

- Pair claims with tangible mechanisms: what the team or product does, who is involved and what the reader gets.
- Keep metrics paired with their unit and explanatory line, not isolated as decorative numbers.
- Reserve emoji and emoticons for conversational explainer prose; keep large marketing headlines typographic.
- Use sentence case for chapter labels and actions; condensed uppercase belongs to directory utilities.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening at 450px beneath the 64px white header so the next chapter is visible immediately.
- Preserve -0.07em tracking on the 104px opening headline and -0.06em on the main chapter headings.
- Use 96px major section padding and align wide content to the 1248px desktop measure.
- Keep benefit metrics at 19.5px semibold with 16px explanatory lines.
- Make glass faces diagonal and translucent with 4px corners and 12px blur.
- Use natural-color documentary photography without a device mockup frame.
- Separate the 56px lightweight quotation role from 56px semibold section headings.
- Finish with small four-column resource navigation rather than a giant footer wordmark.

### Don't

- Do not apply a saturated brand color to cards or full-width marketing bands; keep the architecture neutral.
- Do not round the 48px action into a capsule; its 4px corners are part of the hardware character.
- Do not add floating shadows to benefit cards; use their 1px translucent black border.
- Do not inflate proof values to headline size; the card title and evidence have deliberately different jobs.
- Do not use the condensed utility font for ordinary marketing body copy.
- Do not desaturate every photo to match the neutral page; natural color is the counterweight.
- Do not make the header action as tall as chapter actions; retain the 26px compact variant.
- Do not replace the sparse footer with a dark full-width conversion wall.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Photo float is declared as 6s ease-in-out infinite on four collage photos.
- Opposing logo marquee directions are declared with linear infinite animation; durations are not specified.
- Header color transitions are declared at 0.3s; several action and surface transitions use 0.4s cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1).
- Reduced-motion classes remove transforms and restore full opacity on reveal headings.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the collage's drift slow and local rather than animating the entire section.
- Give moving logo tracks a compact pause control and preserve static readable proof when movement is disabled.

**Unknown:**

- Video playback behavior, marquee durations and the exact timing of scroll reveals are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- CSS width breakpoints are declared at 376px, 768px, 1200px and 1440px.
- Benefit markup provides a single-column base and three-column wider variant; directory markup provides one-, two- and four-column variants.
- Header markup provides a 51px base height and 64px wider height; centered navigation is hidden at narrower widths.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, stack outcome cards while retaining their title-proof-explanation-action order.
- Reduce large headline sizes to preserve balanced readable lines; keep documentary media full bleed and the transparent emblem compact.
- Let portrait and question suggestion rails scroll horizontally rather than shrinking their contents into tiny tiles.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, media crops, gutter changes and navigation replacement behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
