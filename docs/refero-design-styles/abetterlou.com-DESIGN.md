# A Better Lou — sunlit strength, amber medicine

## Overview

A cinematic, reassuring style that pairs aspirational human photography with the directness of a service offer. Warm cream, almost-black brown and golden amber frame a single contemporary grotesk, with expansive image-led chapters alternating with denser practical information. It suits premium personal services and products that need both emotional appeal and clear explanations of process, price and support.

### Essentials

- The promise reads like a two-part personal transformation, not an advertising slogan: left-aligned PP Neue Montreal at 69.1px/1, weight 500, -0.1px tracking, followed by an equally prominent offer line.
- The first screen is an inhabited landscape: a 900px full-bleed photographic stage, the person dominant on the right, copy on the left with 32px gutters, and three ruled benefit cells occupying the bottom 197px. Use an equivalent real-world product or user scene when a portrait is unavailable.
- Conversion controls have a bubble-and-satellite silhouette: amber pills paired with a separate circular northeast arrow, with 4px between the shapes; secondary contact actions sit underneath rather than competing as oversized blocks.
- Color comes in deliberate chapters: brown #140b00 and cream #fff1e0 carry most of the page, amber #ffb442 marks actions and key words, then expands into a compact 373px conversion band and one half of a paired offer comparison.
- Information is small beneath a cinematic promise: 59px medium section headings sit over 13px/1.5 explanatory text, while offer prices become 41.8px/1.1 bold. Preserve this unusually strong scale contrast.
- The page needs a human-scale statement and a practical closing anchor: a centered 54.7px/1 statement on brown, plus a near-black footer with a two-by-two link grid, outlined pill fields, amber social discs and a restrained lower-left wordmark. Use a genuine product philosophy for the statement and the new identity for the wordmark.

### Surfaces

#### Brown canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default narrative background.

**Use:** Offer selection, FAQ, editorial and human-proof chapters.

**CSS:** `background: #fff1e0; color: #140b00`

**Observed:** Flat, edge-to-edge cream, not pure white; content shares the same outer gutter as the dark chapters.

**Transfer:** Group practical decisions into a continuous light plane rather than a sequence of floating white cards.

**Use:** Legibility layer over full-screen media.

**CSS:** `background: linear-gradient(rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.27), rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.3) 47%, rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.71))`

**Transfer:** Put this between a cover image and live text; do not bake text into the picture.

#### Amber conversion plane · level 0

**Use:** Compact high-emphasis offer recap.

**CSS:** `background: #ffb442; color: #140b00; padding: 46.1px 32px`

**Observed:** A full-width saturated interruption amid long dark and cream chapters.

**Transfer:** Concentrate broad brand-color coverage into the point where the reader is ready to act.

## Imagery and graphics

### Aspirational human scene

**Use:** Default opening and selected emotional proof chapters.

**Observed:** An older bearded man in sunglasses fills the right side against a soft-focus blue sky, mountain horizon and muted vegetation. Natural skin color, dark clothing and the photographic shade balance warmth and authority. Another scene presents the same mature figure in a blue jacket at dusk, centrally framed with room for left copy and lower-right information.

**Transfer:** Photograph a credible user or maker in a relevant lived environment. Keep the subject large, the landscape softly defocused, and the text side low-detail. Minimum acceptable version is a carefully art-directed still photograph with the same asymmetric composition and darkened lower edge.

### Portrait and editorial media

**Use:** Human proof and practical learning chapters.

**Observed:** Tall interview videos appear four across at about 336×439px with a name and role beneath, plus small circular play controls. Journal cards use landscape images around 451×346px, followed directly by a medium title and compact summary. Media is rectangular, not surrounded by elaborate card chrome.

**Transfer:** Keep interviews close-framed and credible; use contextual editorial photography instead of unrelated stock decoration.

### Amber product levitation

**Only for:** physical-product program pages

**Use:** Product-specific opening and related-offer thumbnail.

**Observed:** A reflective amber glass vial floats diagonally, cap toward the upper right, against a blue-to-brown atmospheric field. A golden elliptical pool of light and a soft dark shadow below separate it from the ground. The object dominates the center while upper-left promise and lower-right details stay live.

**Transfer:** Use a cutout or render of the new product with warm directional reflections. If no physical object exists, make a single substantial symbolic form or material study rather than a collage of interface tiles.

**Build:** Use a full-bleed muted blue upper field fading into espresso at the bottom. Position a transparent object image centrally, tilted about 18deg clockwise, occupying roughly 40% of the scene height. Behind its base, layer an amber radial ellipse about 55% of the scene width and 18% of its height with a soft blur around 40px. Place a smaller rgba(0,0,0,0.45) ellipse beneath the floating object, blurred around 12px. Apply Photographic shade above the completed scene; keep highlights warm rather than neon.

### Scientific material sculpture

**Only for:** company and philosophy pages

**Use:** Immersive positioning and conceptual chapters.

**Observed:** Large dark rounded molecular nodes and short connecting rods occupy the foreground and recede into an amber-lit background. The scene feels like a shallow-focus macro photograph: silhouettes are nearly black, highlights are subtle, and some forms run beyond the viewport edges. Cream type with amber key phrases sits over the material rather than in a separate panel.

**Transfer:** Replace literal molecules with connected forms appropriate to the product's underlying system; retain macro scale, depth and warm backlight.

**Build:** Construct an SVG or 3D scene with irregular clusters of circles or spheres connected by short thick rods at varied angles. Scale foreground nodes to about 15–30% of the viewport width, crop several at the edges, and reduce/blur background clusters. Use espresso silhouettes with narrow amber edge highlights, an amber radial backlight near the upper right, and a brown vignette. A static layered SVG with two depth planes is the minimum acceptable version.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Inhabited landscape opening

**Observed:** A 900px full-bleed Aspirational human scene forms the back layer, Photographic shade sits above it, and live cream text and Fixed navigation sit in front. The right half is occupied by a large waist-up person whose head approaches the header; the left half remains open sky and soft landscape. The promise begins about 115px down with a 32px left gutter: two lines of 69.1px medium display, then a matching-size price line. **Avoid:** Do not turn this into a centered headline above an isolated thumbnail, or put an opaque rectangle behind all the copy. Do not let the bottom benefit strip slip entirely below the first screen.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Personal promise → mechanism and human proof → practical process and offers → deeper reassurance → objections and conversion recap → learning or related options → utility close.

### Broad marketing entry point balancing aspiration with an immediately understandable offer

## Writing

Direct, reassuring and personally addressed. Emotional promises are balanced by concrete steps, practical terms and a calm professional explanation of the mechanism.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse ABL or A Better Lou names, logo, proprietary program names, identifiable portraits, medical claims, quoted testimonials, clinical numbers, prices, certification marks or contact details. Supply the new brand's verified claims and assets. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to the reader as you and describe the service as we; use an impersonal professional voice only for terms, eligibility and safety information.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are often two short clauses, sometimes contrasting an unwanted condition with a desired outcome. Support uses short sentences and plain verbs; three-beat fragments provide the strongest refrains. Longer mechanism paragraphs follow only after a clear short claim.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the desired change, then protect a valued benefit. | 6–10 words | “Lose the weight Keep the muscle” |
| Problem reframe | Remove personal blame and identify the mechanism. | 7–12 words | “Willpower isn't the problem. Your biology is.” |
| Practical support | A concrete action or delivery detail stated without flourish. | 4–12 words | “Prescription within hours”<br>“Medication ships overnight from a U.S.-based pharmacy” |
| Benefit refrain | Three clipped advantages with full stops. | 3–6 words | “Fast. Easy. Affordable.” |
| Primary action | Verb-led invitation with immediate timing. | 2–4 words | “Start Today”<br>“Call Us Now” |
| Values comparison | Prefer a humane quality over a restrictive one. | 3–5 words | “Progress over perfection” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** simple; clear; personalized; measurable; guided; real human support; what happens next; built around you

**Avoid:** biohacking bravado; miracle; guaranteed transformation; revolutionary ecosystem; punitive self-discipline language; excessive exclamation marks

**Rules:**

- Follow an emotional claim with a concrete process, term or explanation rather than another slogan.
- Use time, cadence and inclusions to remove uncertainty, only where those details are verified.
- Phrase objections in the reader's own words and organize answers by the decision being made.
- Keep limitations explicit and separate from the large promise; professional confidence is not certainty of outcome.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the opening promise at weight 500 and 69.1px/1 on the desktop stage.
- Align major chapter content to the same 32px desktop gutters.
- Use 6px corners on offer and newsletter panels; let pills carry the strongly rounded geometry.
- Pair the principal amber capsule with a detached circular arrow across a 4px gap.
- Keep small narrative body text at 13px/1.5 and offer explanations at 17px/1.45.
- Use equal offer-card structures with amber/brown inversion and 41.8px bold prices.
- Retain a compact amber conversion band and a large centered human-purpose statement.
- End with the two-by-two footer link grid alongside the understated subscription panel.

### Don't

- Do not substitute pure white and neutral black for #fff1e0 and the two warm dark browns.
- Do not make every heading bold; reserve 700 for prices, process numerals and conversion emphasis.
- Do not turn the 6px offer panels into 24px-radius floating dashboard cards.
- Do not add drop shadows to practical panels; separation comes from fill, spacing and 1px rules.
- Do not shrink the monthly cadence into a tiny superscript beside the offer price.
- Do not spread amber across every chapter; broad fills belong to offer selection and the compact conversion interruption.
- Do not replace the footer's modest identity and small social discs with a giant wordmark wall.
- Do not enlarge every support paragraph to headline-adjacent scale; the contrast between cinematic titles and compact explanations is intentional.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions commonly declare 0.4s transitions; pill fields declare 0.45s. Navigation background-color declares 0.4s.
- Several media surfaces declare transform 0.3s cubic-bezier(0.165, 0.84, 0.44, 1).
- Long narrative chapters use pinned spacers and split text lines; still imagery and overlays remain the visual anchor.

**Transfer:**

- Use the declared opacity and link-color changes rather than introducing bouncy button movement.
- Keep a complete still composition for each cinematic scene; text and offer information must remain legible without animated media.

**Unknown:**

- Precise scroll choreography, scene progression, reveal durations and reduced-motion behavior are not established.
- Focus-visible states are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 479px, 480px, 767px, 768px, 991px, 992px, 1024px, 1280px and 1440px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the subject's face or product silhouette, move the lower benefit strip into a stacked continuation, and keep the promise above it.
- Stack paired offers and split FAQ content in reading order; retain amber/brown inversion and fine rules.
- Turn the footer's side-by-side link and newsletter zones into a vertical stack without losing the link-group hierarchy.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile typography, gutter values, navigation collapse and image positioning are not established; narrow-screen behavior above is adaptation guidance.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
