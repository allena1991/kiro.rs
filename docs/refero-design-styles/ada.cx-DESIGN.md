# Ada — intelligence through smoked glass

## Overview

An atmospheric, human-facing technology style with oversized regular grotesk typography and dark teal rooms lit by blurred olive and cyan photography. Mint actions and fine translucent edges make complex products feel approachable rather than mechanical. Spacious marketing compositions alternate with dense proof panels; lighter advisory and colorful culture variants retain the same rounded controls and photographic warmth.

### Essentials

- A soft-focus room, not a flat dark theme: a #162429 canvas carries full-bleed blurred photography in the opening and luminous imagery inside 22px rounded proof and conversion panels. Recreate these light fields with blurred photographic fragments or layered translucent ellipses when footage is unavailable.
- Headlines speak softly at monumental scale: regular 400 grotesk at 72px/1 with -0.03em tracking, section leads at 60px/1 with -0.03em tracking, and selected outcome words in the same face’s italic cut.
- The opening pairs a narrow four-line promise with a tall human scene: two 568px columns separated by 48px, a 448px support line and integrated email/action capsule, with an arched video on the right and a floating smoked-glass demonstration panel.
- Actions are small mint capsules inside an expansive world: #8fbe8e fill, dark text, 14px/1 type, 12px 24px padding and 38px height. Navigation is itself a 66px-high glass pill, inset 24px from the top.
- Color lives in light, not everywhere: dark teal and near-black dominate ordinary landing pages; mint is concentrated in actions, small uppercase proof captions and status dots, with copper for minor labels. Large saturated tiles belong to the culture variant, not the default.
- Proof feels like an instrument readout laid over a photograph: 60px regular numerals, smaller 30px suffixes, 12px uppercase tracked captions, hairline column dividers and restrained grain. Include a large quantified proof treatment, a luminous closing call and an oversized identity mark in the footer; a typographic wordmark is the minimum identity treatment.

## Imagery and graphics

### Atmospheric photographic light

**Use:** The opening background and luminous proof and closing panels.

**Observed:** Directional blur stretches trees, windows or outdoor color into deep olive, gold, teal and cyan veils. Black fades protect text, and tiny grain keeps the material photographic rather than glossy. Wide panels remain mostly dark, with light concentrated near the upper edge and a few soft streaks.

**Transfer:** Use a photograph related to the new product’s human setting, blur beyond recognition and let the original colors survive only as light. Without photography, layered elongated light fields are the minimum acceptable equivalent.

**Build:** Use an oversized image layer behind content, blurred 24px and saturated about 1.12. For a procedural substitute, layer 600px by 180px cyan and olive ellipses at the top and middle over charcoal, rotate slightly, blur 80px, and reduce each to 0.18–0.3 alpha. Add a transparent-to-near-black vertical veil and 0.03-opacity SVG fractal-noise overlay. Match the soft glow; exact stops are not essential.

### Arched human scene

**Use:** Default hero’s right-hand object.

**Observed:** A 568px by 670px portrait-oriented live-action scene has a broad semicircular top and straight lower sides, heavily darkened and softened. It occupies approximately half the content width, with Glass demonstration card crossing its lower-right area.

**Transfer:** Use a person or setting experiencing the product’s result rather than a laptop mockup. A still photograph with the same arch mask and glass overlay is sufficient.

**Build:** Mask portrait media into a container with top-left and top-right radii of 50% and square lower corners. Use object-fit cover and a dark translucent veil; keep the overlay card outside the clipping container.

### Blurred proof-image logos

**Use:** Customer rows and compact proof cards.

**Observed:** Crisp white customer identities sit centered over strongly horizontal-blurred natural or urban imagery; the image reads as colored light, not a documentary illustration.

**Transfer:** Use approved customer identities over a common image-treatment system. If no customer assets exist, place a concise proof-category label over the same material.

**Build:** Fill a square or wide tile with a photo, apply a horizontal SVG Gaussian blur using stdDeviation 18 2, then a near-black veil. Add the sharp logo on a separate layer; preserve enough contrast to read it instantly.

### Product scenes

**Use:** Technical chapters and worked examples.

**Observed:** Readable compact interface fragments float on dark cyan or olive atmospheric backdrops, with 16px corners, 1px alpha-white edges and translucent panes. The large worked example is a vertical request → actions → outcome sequence with numbered circles, a thin colored connecting rail and a mint-lit result.

**Transfer:** Show one understandable task with a clear beginning and outcome. Rebuild only the controls necessary to tell that story, not an entire application.

**Build:** Use a broad dark panel, then stack translucent subpanels with generous 24px–32px internal spacing. Draw a 1px vertical rail between small numbered circles; use restrained mint and blue dots with soft 12px glows to distinguish state. Keep UI detail subordinate to chapter text.

### Research editorial art

**Only for:** research editorial pages

**Use:** Article covers and large research-thrust rows.

**Observed:** Article covers mix diagrams with blue electric-cloud imagery. Large rows pair a 300px blue-gray square containing a flat black-and-ice diagram with a near-black-to-slate text area and thick 16px top edge.

**Transfer:** Create simple symbols for the research concept: connected circles, waveform bars or a process diagram, not cute robot mascots.

**Build:** On a blue-gray square, draw two black disks with four small holes and a thin double-headed connector, or three rounded black vertical bars with pale circular nodes. Use basic SVG shapes, centered with abundant space.

### Light advisory media

**Only for:** advisory pages

**Use:** Hero and broad explanatory graphics.

**Observed:** The opening is a pale sky-blue haze with blurred orange streaks behind centered dark type. Later graphics are 1024px-wide interface compositions on motion-blurred grass or warm, grainy sunset fields; windows overlap rather than sitting in hardware frames.

## Writing

Confident and outcome-oriented, with a conversational human warmth. Technical detail is allowed, but it is framed as practical behavior, operational control and measurable results rather than abstract futurism.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Ada’s name, logo, product names, trademarks, customer identities, proprietary frameworks, numerical performance claims or testimonial quotations. Write new substantiated claims and use approved assets for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you or your, and use we for a team’s commitments. Describe the product directly by what it does; culture writing is more personal and frequently lowercase.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines often run 5–12 words, with a short italic payoff. Supporting copy uses one useful paragraph or two to three clipped declarative sentences. Actions are short verb phrases; periods and contrast pairs create confidence without exclamation marks.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Name the category and the audience or transformation plainly. | 5–12 words | “The agentic customer experience platform”<br>“Applied AI research at the frontier of customer experience” |
| Support | Concrete verbs, then a short assurance about scale or trust. | 20–35 words | “AI customer service agents that resolve, act, and continuously improve. Designed for scale. Trusted by global brands.” |
| Proof heading | Conversational repetition makes operational evidence feel human. | 4–10 words | “Real results. For real businesses” |
| Technical explanation | Explain a capability through an action and boundary, not jargon alone. | 20–45 words | “Each step above used one of these. You author them, turn them on, and govern what the agent can touch.” |
| Primary action | Start with a verb and name the next human or useful step. | 2–6 words | “Speak to an expert”<br>“View open roles” |
| Closing invitation | Two compact imperatives or a direct acceleration promise. | 5–9 words | “Solve hard problems. Ship real things.”<br>“Accelerate your ACX strategy” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** proven; real results; built for; designed for scale; your team; continuously improve; control; extraordinary; not just

**Avoid:** magic; effortless everything; revolutionary disruption; crush the competition; limitless; exclamation-heavy hype

**Rules:**

- Connect every capability to an action or outcome the reader can picture.
- Pair aspirational headlines with concrete evidence nearby.
- Use contrast phrases such as ‘not just’ only when the next sentence explains the distinction.
- Keep eyebrow labels classificatory and brief; reserve detail for body copy.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep default display and major section type at weight 400, with -0.03em tracking.
- Use 16px card corners and 22px large-panel corners as separate construction levels.
- Preserve 120px major-section separation even when panels inside are dense.
- Constrain hero support and email conversion to 448px rather than stretching them across the whole text column.
- Use 12px uppercase captions with 0.04em tracking to make numerical proof distinctive.
- Pair crisp text and logos with heavily blurred photographic backing.
- Make mint conversion capsules compact at 38px height; let the surrounding atmosphere provide scale.
- Use the 596px-wide footer identity as a graphic anchor, with useful small links beside it.

### Don't

- Do not use semibold or bold display type to imitate impact; 72px regular is the intended mechanism.
- Do not turn every card into smoked glass; ordinary proof cards use a solid near-black surface.
- Do not use 30px–40px rounded corners for ordinary cards; reserve the softer 28px treatment for light footer containers.
- Do not spread culture pink, vermilion and ochre through a default dark landing page.
- Do not make all proof values and suffixes the same size; the 60px numeral and 30px suffix contrast matters.
- Do not replace compact mint text-only actions with oversized rectangular gradient buttons.
- Do not make every section a boxed card; assurance chapters are flat ruled columns.
- Do not render photographic atmosphere as sharp, high-contrast background imagery behind paragraph text.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions declare 0.2s transitions with cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1); active controls scale to 0.96.
- Editorial controls and footer links declare .15s transitions with cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1).
- Logo rails declare 24s and 48s linear loops; image marquees declare 36s linear loops. Product diagrams declare directional particle loops around 1.5s–1.8s and waveform bars at 1.05s.
- Some reveal layers declare opacity, translate and filter transitions at 0.7s with cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1).

**Transfer:**

- Use subtle motion in atmospheric media and data rails while keeping text layers steady.
- Confine continuously looping particles to system diagrams and counters rather than sprinkling them over every section.

**Unknown:**

- Exact sequencing, scroll-trigger thresholds and media playback timing are not defined.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 400px, 640px, 768px, 1024px, 1280px and 1536px.
- Hero markup changes from one column to two at the large breakpoint; the stat band changes from one to two to four columns. The footer changes from two to three to six columns.
- The email form becomes a horizontal glass capsule from the small breakpoint; below that it uses stacked controls.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, place the arched media after the promise and conversion, preserve the arch and let the glass interaction sit in normal flow if overlapping would hide the content.
- Stack customer-proof media, headline/outcome and attribution rather than shrinking the three desktop columns.
- Retain the atmospheric material and a large final identity, but allow headline wraps to follow the new copy.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen dimensions and the mobile navigation interaction are not specified.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
