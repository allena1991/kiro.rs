# Airbnb.org — human stories, sweeping hearts

## Overview

An open, people-first marketing style that pairs bold rounded sans-serif statements with intimate documentary photography. White and pale gray carry most of the experience, while deep berry, pink and hand-painted loops add warmth at moments of action. The density is low, the imagery expansive, and the tone practical rather than promotional; it suits services whose value is best explained through real people and tangible outcomes.

### Essentials

- A public promise reads like a neighbor speaking plainly: centered 72px/82px, weight 700, -0.02em tracking, with a narrower 20px/28px gray explanation beneath it.
- The opening gives words their own white stage before the film: an 88px sticky header, 96px of top breathing room, a two-line headline about 922px wide, then edge-to-edge 16:9 documentary media entering the first screen.
- Actions are berry rounded rectangles, not pills: #b30057, 12px corners, 16px/20px medium labels, 14px 24px padding and a 48px body height; the header action is 56px high.
- Color arrives in concentrated emotional moments: white and #f7f7f7 dominate the long page; berry stays in small actions and one inset 1280×270px action banner, with pale pink supporting illustrations rather than coloring every section.
- Real people carry the proof: four 308×411px portrait cards with 12px corners, lower-edge dark gradients, 14px context above 18px names, and restrained chevrons; use authentic customer or maker portraits when there is no documentary film.
- A sweeping hand-drawn loop makes the experience feel cared for: include a white brush gesture cropped beyond a berry action panel and echo its softer pink language in benefit artwork. A custom open loop or embracing arc is the fallback, rather than requiring a literal heart.

### Containers and grids

- **Page container**: max width 1440px · side padding 80px. Centered desktop wrapper with 1280px of usable content at the reference width; media can deliberately escape it to full bleed.
- **Centered introduction**: max width 806px. Major centered section title; support text narrows further to approximately 634px.

- **Four media columns**: 4 columns · gap 16px. 308px square benefit cards or taller portrait cards.
- **Narrative pair**: 2 columns · gap 0px. Equal-width chapter columns; a 544px image is inset inside its 640px half. Alternate image side between adjacent chapters.

- **FAQ pair** (only for objection-handling sections): 2 columns · gap 48px. Section title on the left; approximately 616px of accordion rows on the right.
- **Compact story list** (only for editorial archive endings): 2 columns · gap 16px. Two visible columns of 632px horizontal cards, three rows per carousel view.

## Imagery and graphics

### Documentary photography and film

**Use:** Opening film, category grids, case studies and portrait proof.

**Observed:** Real families, hosts, pets and working partners in lived-in rooms, doorways, hospitals and affected landscapes. Natural skin tones and mixed indoor light are retained; candid embraces, small gestures and ordinary activity matter more than polished posing. The opening film is full-width 1440×810px without rounded outer corners; inset 1280×720px feature photographs and smaller cards use 12px corners.

**Transfer:** Photograph people actually using, making or benefiting from the new offering, with environmental context. If film is unavailable, use one honest wide photograph with a strong human moment; do not simulate a video player over a still.

### Painted embracing loop

**Use:** Signature conversion artwork, with smaller echoes over selected photographs or between chapters.

**Observed:** White brushwork sweeps across the right side of a luminous berry banner, crossing into a loose heart-like loop and disappearing beyond its lower and right edges. The line varies in width and has dry-brush broken edges. A smaller berry loop animates in a centered 384×216px white media area between chapters.

**Transfer:** Use an original open loop or embracing arc that suggests care without needing the same symbol. It must remain visibly handmade and oversized relative to the panel.

**Build:** Create an SVG with a transparent background and two long intersecting cubic paths. Use white strokes around 22–34px at a 1280px banner width, round joins, no fill, and slight width variation from outlined paths. Add several thin interrupted parallel strokes along the outer edge for dry-brush texture. Place the SVG over the right 45% of Expressive berry field, extend its viewBox beyond the panel and clip it at the 12px rounded boundary. For the small interlude, use one thin berry path on white; a static fully drawn gesture is the minimum version.

### Soft pink benefit tableaux

**Use:** A coordinated row of visual explanations within a flagship benefit chapter.

For a ripple panel, center a white circular icon disc and add three 1px translucent-white circles about 120px, 200px and 280px across, with a berry radial wash at low opacity. For a collage, overlap three small 12px-rounded photos at rotations between -6 and 5 degrees, with 2px white borders and local soft shadows. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Plain promise → real-world experience → explanation → human proof → trust mechanism or capability depth → wider impact → relevant action or further reading → practical footer.

### Flagship marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Centered bold statement and narrower explanation, followed by immersive edge-to-edge documentary film.
2. **Capability overview** — Small gestural interlude, centered heading and three photographic category cards.
3. **Human proof** — Invitation and four portrait stories.
4. **Trust mechanism** — Pale-gray assurance chapter with a warm illustrated scene.
5. **Differentiation** — Four coordinated soft-pink visual benefit cards.
6. **Quantified proof** — Split heading and paragraph with outlined action, followed by a people-centered panoramic reach graphic.
7. **Closing action** — Inset berry brushwork banner, then three-column utility footer.

**Transfer:** Balance emotional media with concrete explanations, using one expressive action moment after trust and impact are established.

### Editorial discovery and story index

1. **Orientation** — Pale-gray split opening with a square documentary film and left-aligned title.
2. **Featured proof** — Centered story invitation and one large landscape photograph.
3. **Breadth** — Four-column square-photo story index with place or event captions.
4. **Action** — Inset expressive banner interrupts the index before a related-story chapter.
5. **Themed proof** — Deep-berry collection of four portrait stories.
6. **Brand context** — Pale-pink mission-film band.
7. **Further reading** — Compact two-column story carousel, then utility footer.

**Transfer:** Let readers move from a single compelling account to an index, a themed cluster and a denser archive.

## Writing

Warm, direct and grounded. Confidence comes from explaining what happens and whom it helps, with specific evidence woven into ordinary sentences rather than slogans or superlatives.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Airbnb.org or Airbnb names, logos, brush-heart asset, program names, beneficiary identities, partner marks, quotations, impact figures, percentages, dates or donation amounts. Replace every factual claim and story with truthful material for the new organization. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** The organization usually explains itself by name or with 'we' and 'our'; 'you' and 'your' appear when describing participation or an immediate benefit. People are named as people, not treated as segments.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headings, compact one- or two-sentence support blocks, and explicit verb-led actions. Occasional longer narrative paragraphs are reserved for case studies; exclamation marks and promotional urgency are absent.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | State the useful outcome and who it is for, in plain language. | 6–10 words | “Free emergency housing for neighbors in need.” |
| Section heading | Introduce a concrete mechanism, difference or group of people. | 3–7 words | “Reinventing emergency stays.”<br>“Meet people we’ve helped” |
| Support line | Explain the mechanism with familiar nouns and practical examples. | 18–35 words | “[Brand] works with partner organizations to provide free short-term housing with space for families, kids, and pets.” |
| Primary action | Short verb phrase that tells readers what happens next. | 2–4 words | “See all stories”<br>“Become a partner”<br>“View active responses” |
| Story title | A person or family plus an understandable change in their life. | 4–10 words | “Tawnessa finds pet-friendly housing after flooding” |
| Process title | Number, then a short description of a real step. | 3–6 words | “1. An emergency happens”<br>“4. Recovery happens” |
| Assurance | Explain why an important promise can be trusted; emphasize the key clause inside the sentence. | One compact paragraph | “Airbnb covers [Brand]’s day-to-day operating costs as a nonprofit,”<br>“so 100% of donations fund emergency stays.” |
| Question | Use the reader's practical question without marketing language. | 4–12 words | “How do donations work?”<br>“What does an organization need to do to become an [Brand] partner?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** help; people; neighbors; together; space; support; works with; short-term; free; families; community; how it works

**Avoid:** revolutionary; disruptive; best-in-class; unprecedented; unlock your potential; act before it’s too late; victims as a generic audience label

**Rules:**

- Favor a visible human benefit over abstract institutional language.
- Use examples of everyday needs to make capabilities tangible.
- Place verified numbers in explanatory sentences and connect them to a concrete outcome.
- Make button labels specific enough to predict the destination or task.
- Describe difficult situations with dignity; avoid turning hardship into spectacle.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 72px bold display type only for the flagship promise; step down to 48px for the major narrative headings.
- Keep 80px desktop side gutters and the 16px rhythm between repeated media cards.
- Pair a 26px medium card heading with 18px/28px gray description, not another bold heading.
- Keep portrait caption context above the person's name, using 14px then 18px and a 4px gap.
- Use 96px vertical breathing room to separate narrative chapters.
- Preserve the concentrated berry action banner with its 40px white message and handmade overscale loop.
- Use a 1px #dddddd rule for FAQ rows and compact story-card edges.
- Let photos and illustrations supply emotional color while the surrounding page remains white or pale gray.

### Don't

- Do not replace the 12px filled-button corners with full pills or use the outlined button's 8px corners for every action.
- Do not turn ordinary benefit cards into shadowed boxes; the only raised marketing panel is the scoped transaction form.
- Do not place white captions over bright portraits without the lower-edge 60% black gradient.
- Do not convert conversational impact paragraphs into giant standalone KPI counters.
- Do not treat pale pink as a universal replacement for the #f7f7f7 structural bands.
- Do not use grayscale filters or a uniform berry duotone on the documentary photographs.
- Do not oversize partner logos to fill their white square tiles; optical whitespace is part of the directory.
- Do not add gradients, decorative underlines or all-caps styling to the charcoal display headings.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The opening documentary film plays with pause, subtitles and sound controls. A separate small looping brush-heart animation sits between narrative chapters.
- Declared button transitions include box-shadow 0.2s and transform 0.25s with cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1); background and border transitions are 0.3s with the same curve.
- Carousel arrow hover and focus-visible scale to 1.04; active returns to 1. Portrait links use underline and focus-ring changes rather than a large image zoom.

**Transfer:**

- Keep interaction motion concentrated in button compression and simple carousel feedback, rather than animating every paragraph.
- Use the gestural interlude as a brief visual breath; a static fully drawn loop should retain the visual identity when motion is reduced.

**Unknown:**

- The complete timing and drawing sequence of the brush animation is not specified.
- Scroll-entry sequencing and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

**Transfer:**

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
