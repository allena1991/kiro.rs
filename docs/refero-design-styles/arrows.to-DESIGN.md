# Arrows — ember on ruled paper

## Overview

Warm, operational software marketing with the energy of a furnace behind a composed editorial grid. Cream paper, wine-dark ink and grainy yellow-to-red ribbons surround tightly set geometric sans headlines, practical body text and luminous interface models. Orange pencil portraits add human warmth; spacious chapters alternate with compact, finely ruled capability grids. It suits business products that turn complex work into a clear, tangible result.

### Essentials

- Heavy promises sit beside glowing work, not above a dashboard: a 64px/1, weight 700, -2px tracked headline occupies the left half of a 1224px stage; the right half holds a small luminous interface against fiery grain.
- Paper is structural, fire is theatrical: #fbf3ed and white carry most reading, #370615 carries text and dark chapters; saturated yellow, orange and red occupy bounded media panels and 119px interstitial bands rather than every card.
- The grid remains visible through the story: 1px #e3d7d4 rules align outer rails, equal 612px feature halves and gapless 408px capability cells; copy sits 32px inside its cell.
- Actions are cut rectangles, labels are little lenses: primary actions use 2px corners and 50px height, while 12px sentence-case eyebrows sit in 25px translucent pills beside a tiny orange orb.
- Make work feel alive inside glass: 20px frosted frames, 6.4px inset padding and 30px backdrop blur surround pale interface cards with wine-colored active rows. If there is no software interface, stage a legible process or output card in the same frame.
- Carry the warm ribbon language to the ending: a grainy curved-light device must appear in the page, and the closing wine-dark field repeats it as enormous low-contrast arcs behind a 48px/56px two-line statement, pale action and newsletter/navigation columns.

## Typography

- **Plus Jakarta Sans** — Headlines, feature titles and substantial quotes. Geometric sans with tight joins, rounded counters and a firm bold silhouette. OpenType features: "dlig", "lnum", "tnum". Font stack: "Plus Jakarta Sans", "Segoe UI", sans-serif.
- **Saans** — Body copy, navigation, actions and captions. Compact contemporary grotesk with friendly, readable proportions. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use 400–500 weights; retain compact widths and measured line heights. Font stack: "Saans", "DM Sans", "Helvetica Neue", Arial, sans-serif.

- The 64px promise is twice the ordinary section-heading size. Keep the headline compact through -2px tracking; section headings tighten by -1px.

### Containers and grids

- **ruled stage**: max width 1224px · side padding 0px. Centered continuous content rails, leaving 108px outer gutters at a 1440px window; internal rules persist between chapters.
- **feature split**: 2 columns · gap 0px. Equal 612px halves at desktop: copy left, clipped saturated media right; copy inset 32px and usually limited to 400px.
- **capability grid**: 3 columns · gap 0px. Equal 408px cells separated by hairlines, each with text above a compact illustration.
- **rollout grid**: 4 columns · gap 0px. Equal 306px timeline cells beneath a centered heading.
- **question column**: max width 586px. Centered narrow accordion below a section heading.
- **proof collection** (only for customer proof collections): max width 1144px · 2 columns · gap 20px. Two-column proof-card layout within 40px insets.
- **team grid** (only for company pages): 3 columns · gap 20px · side padding 40px. Portrait rows with bordered cells inside the main rails.
- **supporter matrix** (only for company pages): 4 columns · gap 0px. 100px-tall cells with centered names and affiliations.

## Imagery and graphics

### Grainy thermal ribbons

**Use:** Signature graphic in media stages and shallow transitional bands.

**Observed:** Broad folded or concentric curved ribbons flow from golden yellow through orange and red into deep wine. Fine, highly visible grain makes them feel printed or airbrushed rather than glossy 3D. Some scenes add thin luminous diagonal paths; the 119px bands show only a close slice of the larger form.

**Transfer:** Every new page needs this warm curved-light language. If custom artwork is unavailable, build two or three oversized curved gradient ribbons with fine grain; a plain linear gradient alone is insufficient.

**Build:** In an overflow-hidden panel, place three oversized SVG curved bands sweeping from upper right toward lower left. Fill each with gold-to-orange-to-wine gradients, offset the bands to create alternating bright rims and dark folds, and soften the inner shadows with 20px–40px blur. Overlay monochrome SVG feTurbulence grain through soft-light at low opacity. For the hero add sparse 2px gold paths tilted about 25 degrees. Match the soft glow; exact gradient stops are not essential.

### Luminous working model

**Use:** Primary product media in the opening and feature chapters.

**Observed:** A 326px-wide portrait-format interface floats in a 612px-wide warm panel. Opaque pale content is surrounded by the Frosted model frame; wine active rows and small orange status lights establish hierarchy. Small dark status chips overlap opposite edges. A later chapter expands the interface to about 1143px wide on a richly colored field.

**Transfer:** Show a real output or focused interaction at readable scale. Without an app, create a three-step process card with one completed row, one active row and a prepared result; keep the floating status chips and warm backing.

**Build:** Center a pale 16px-radius card within the Frosted model frame. Divide its header from three compact rows with faint rules. Put one wine row and a 13px orange orb inside it; overlap two small wine status labels at opposite corners. Keep labels concrete and avoid filling the model with decorative gibberish.

### Orange pencil people

**Use:** Human proof alongside a quote; repeated as small selectable thumbnails.

**Observed:** Chest-up cutout portraits are rendered in orange-red pencil hatching on cream, with white highlights and feathered lower edges. A large portrait fills roughly the right half of a testimonial while the quote occupies the left.

**Transfer:** Use portraits of relevant people with one-color warm engraving or sketch treatment. A minimum version is a high-contrast cream/orange duotone with a fine hatch mask and a softly faded lower edge; do not use circular full-color headshots for the main portrait.

### Quiet closing arcs

**Use:** Signature background of the closing field.

**Observed:** Huge low-contrast wine-on-wine curved bands run behind all footer content, extending beyond the viewport. Fine grain remains visible, but the arcs never compete with the pale statement.

**Transfer:** Repeat the page's ribbon shape as a dark tonal watermark at the end; use abstract curves rather than requiring a logo-shaped asset.

**Build:** Draw three oversized nested SVG arc bands with centers above the left edge, each wider than the content stage. Fill with white at roughly 0.03–0.06 alpha over wine, add a fading mask at the top and fine monochrome grain. Clip to the footer; keep the strongest tonal change below the heading.

### Trust medallions

**Use:** Visual reassurance inside a dark technical chapter.

**Observed:** Four equal 306px-wide, 344px-high tiles contain a large centered circular seal or simple lock/shield drawing above a short caption. Tiles alternate red/wine and orange/gold ribbon slices, producing a bright strip below the calm dark introduction.

**Transfer:** Use real assurance categories or relevant guarantees; preserve the bright circular emblem and short label arrangement.

**Build:** Place a 124px circle near the upper third of each tile, use a simple white or warm contrasting line symbol inside, and back the tile with a distinct slice of Grainy thermal ribbons. Avoid imitating official certification seals for unsupported claims.

### Team portrait wall

**Only for:** company pages

**Use:** Introduce the people behind the product.

**Observed:** The Orange pencil people treatment expands into three-column portrait cells. Names and roles sit below the art, with a small social icon aligned right. Informal offsite photography appears separately as a full-bleed horizontal triptych with thin white mounts and natural color.

**Transfer:** Keep staff art consistent in scale and tone; reserve candid full-color photos for the shared-life strip, not the primary card wall.

### Icons

**Observed:** Sparse functional icons: 24px plus/minus marks at FAQ row ends, 13px orb marks in eyebrows, 32px social icons on team cards, and 64px integration logos in ruled dark cells. Directional text links use a plain right arrow.

**Transfer:** Use simple single-purpose outline symbols for controls and recognizable partner marks only in integration grids; do not turn each heading into an icon badge.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Split promise opening

**Observed:** A 56px Navigation rail tops a 720px-tall, 1224px-wide split stage. The left half is cream, with 32px inner inset: a 64px/1 bold wine promise wraps into three short lines, followed by a 476px-wide support paragraph and one Primary button at 24px stack spacing. The right half is a full-height fiery panel: grainy warm ribbons sit behind a centered 326px-wide Luminous working model with overlapping wine status chips. A 64px status ticker hugs the bottom of the copy half. In the first 900px the full hero, ticker and a 120px warm ribbon band are visible; the editorial letter begins below that band. Layers run paper → thermal artwork and thin paths → frosted frame → pale model → active rows and floating chips.

**Transfer:** Keep the equal split and concentrated promise for a new product landing page. A focused process/output model is the fallback when no interface exists. Utility pages instead open with a centered headline, support and optional action on paper; company introductions use the more conversational display variant.

**Avoid:** Do not center the main landing-page copy above a giant browser window, replace the thermal scene with generic floating blobs, or add a second competing hero CTA.

**Text style:** `display` — Plus Jakarta Sans 700 · 64px/1 · tracking -0.0313em

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible work → explanation and human proof → capability or adoption detail → objections → shared closing invitation.

### Product landing page that makes an abstract service tangible

1. **Promise** — Split promise opening with an active working model and status ticker.
2. **Point of view** — Raised editorial letter explaining the problem and alternative.

## Writing

Confident, direct and grounded in work that gets done. Warmth comes through conversational turns and human detail rather than jokes or exuberance.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Arrows branding, logos, personal names, customer marks, testimonial wording, proprietary feature names, numerical outcomes, founding/funding facts, subscriber counts or certification claims. Source examples illustrate syntax only; replace them with truthful content for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the buyer as you or your team; use we for the people building and helping deploy the product. Product statements name the product as the actor.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are usually short outcomes or two clipped clauses. Support paragraphs explain the mechanism in plain sentences; longer viewpoint prose alternates concrete frustration with a clear alternative.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Lead with a benefit in direct verb form. | 5–10 words | “Double the capacity of your AEs” |
| Feature heading | Contrast what the product does with the old burden. | 3–10 words | “Proactive, not prompted.”<br>“One focused space to move deals forward” |
| Process heading | Two compact clauses make the buying process feel concrete. | 6–10 words | “Scope the work. Then price it clearly.” |
| Support | Explain inputs, work and outcome rather than stacking adjectives. | 20–45 words | “We’ll identify the first work [Brand] should take on, the deals it should cover, and what success looks like. You’ll know the scope before you buy.” |
| Action | Name an actual next step, using a verb and object. | 2–5 words | “Book an agent kickoff”<br>“Read the story” |
| Company heading | Plain human introduction or two contrasting clauses. | 3–7 words | “Small team. Large ambition.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** work done; ready; focused; context; next step; live; clear; your team; in days, not quarters

**Avoid:** revolutionary; unlock limitless potential; seamless synergy; magic; world-class solution; future-proof everything

**Rules:**

- Explain what changes in a real workflow, not just what technology powers it.
- Make contrasts concrete: preparation versus creation, done versus delayed, one focused place versus scattered work.
- Keep section labels literal and sentence case.
- Give each metric a qualifier and a clear attribution or context note.
- Answer objections with a direct yes or no when possible, then explain the mechanism.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main promise at 64px/1 and -2px tracking, with a distinct 32px/1.2 section tier.
- Align section boundaries to the 1224px central rails and use 1px separators.
- Inset ordinary grid copy by 32px rather than floating it arbitrarily in a cell.
- Reserve the 2px radius for main actions and 20px glass frames for staged models.
- Use a 119px warm ribbon slice to create a visual breath between major arguments.
- Keep capability text at 14px/21px below 18px bold titles.
- Give the closing promise its own 48px/56px scale and dark tonal artwork.
- Pair orange portrait art with a substantial 32px quote instead of an oversized star-rating badge.

### Don't

- Do not use pill-shaped primary CTAs; pills belong to compact orb labels and small model statuses.
- Do not erase the grid by giving every capability an independently rounded card with wide gutters.
- Do not turn the palette into cool blue SaaS gradients; its saturated range is gold, orange, red and wine.
- Do not make the reading canvas pure white throughout; cream and white have different chapter roles.
- Do not put heavy drop shadows under every cell; paper-grid cells are flat and proof cards have only a fine edge.
- Do not use thin 300-weight display lettering; the promise depends on a firm 700-weight silhouette.
- Do not render people as unrelated stock-photo circles; the large human proof uses warm line-textured portraits.
- Do not make every support line 20px; ordinary feature prose is 16px and compact grid prose is 14px.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Header links declare background 0.15s transitions. Story cards declare box-shadow 0.2s and transform 0.2s with a -2px hover lift.
- The opening model declares a 0.8s entrance with cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). A status ticker declares 28s linear infinite scrolling; task shimmer loops use 2.6s ease-in-out.
- Progress bars declare background-color 0.5s ease with a per-index 22ms delay. FAQ icons declare opacity and transform 0.2s cubic-bezier(0.215, 0.61, 0.355, 1).

**Transfer:**

- Keep motion concentrated in work-status models, the ticker and progressive tick fills; the editorial reading fields remain stable.
- Use the ticker as a clipped horizontal sequence of concise work-state changes rather than a promotional announcement carousel.

**Unknown:**

- Exact trigger positions, complete intermediate states and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- The defined desktop stage uses 1224px central content, 612px feature halves and a 56px sticky header. No width breakpoints are established.

**Transfer:**

- For a narrow layout, stack copy before the warm model stage, retain one bounded ribbon scene and keep the model readable instead of shrinking a full dashboard.
- Collapse ruled grids by reducing column count while keeping shared edges; move the closing action beneath its statement and stack newsletter and navigation groups.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile type sizes, navigation replacement and exact breakpoint values are unknown; narrow-layout rules here are transfer recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
