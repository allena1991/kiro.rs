# Awesomic — moss meets machined gloss

## Overview

A bright, people-forward technology style with a surprisingly tactile organic world beneath its clean interface. Rounded geometric sans typography, white and cool gray canvases, and glossy black controls keep the structure businesslike, while mossy landscapes, vivid work samples and friendly portraits add life. It suits service platforms and product businesses that need substantial proof without feeling institutional; the overall pace is airy, with dense information contained inside soft cards.

### Essentials

- Businesslike headlines contain a softer changing word: Cosmica 64px/1.12 at weight 600, normal tracking, black phrases interrupted by a gray #a1a1aa noun; section headings use 40px/1.28.
- The opening is an asymmetric promise-and-action spread, not a centered poster: a 648px headline column faces a 475px support-and-email column in a 1280px container, with proof below and a cropped horizontal media rail entering the first 900px.
- Controls feel like polished physical keys: black 49px-high primary buttons have 16px corners, a 0.5px translucent white border and multiple inset highlights; compact controls use 14px corners rather than full pills.
- The page stays overwhelmingly white and cool gray: #ffffff and #f4f4f5 alternate in broad bands with 48px upper corners; saturated color belongs mainly to imagery, with a localized #fe45e2 overlay in an organic scene, not to everyday text or buttons.
- A tactile landscape is a signature material: moss-green ridges back a major scene, peek into card corners and fill a monumental closing wordmark. Recreate it with a textured terrain image or layered SVG ridges when bespoke 3D art is unavailable; do not replace it with generic gradient blobs.
- Human proof interrupts the geometry: circular headshots appear beside quotes and even inside support copy or a closing headline; media cards use 28px or 36px corners and 12px rail gaps.
- The closing identity becomes landscape-sized artwork: after practical contact and link columns, a texture-filled wordmark spans a 1344px-wide, 204px-high stage. Use the new identity or a short identity phrase as the mask, preserving the textured overscale finish.

**Notes:**

- Normal tracking is deliberate even at display sizes; achieve the soft, spacious character through the family and line height rather than negative letter spacing.
- Headlines are semibold, not ultra-bold. Reserve weight 700 for compact numbers and proof names.
- The 64px promise is tighter vertically than the 56px closing heading: both use 71.7px line boxes. Do not make the final line look like a compressed hero.
- Use sentence case throughout. Small labels remain readable words rather than tracked uppercase technical markings.

### Surfaces

#### Canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default page background.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff`

#### Rounded gray band · level 0

**Use:** Broad proof, comparison, closing and footer chapters.

**CSS:** `background: #f4f4f5; border-radius: 48px 48px 0px 0px`

**Observed:** Bands read as large overlapping sheets; the following chapter can rise by 48px with the same upper corner curve.

**Transfer:** Switch canvas tone at meaningful narrative turns rather than boxing every section.

#### White card · level 1

**Use:** Proof cards on gray and standard pricing cards.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border-radius: 36px; box-shadow: none`

**Observed:** Soft silhouette with little elevation; the background contrast supplies separation.

**Transfer:** Group one proof idea per card.

#### Inset shell · level 1

**Use:** Process rows, FAQs and selectors.

**CSS:** `background: #f4f4f5; border: 1px solid #ececee; border-radius: 36px; padding: 8px; box-shadow: inset 0px 1px 0px 0px #e4e4e7`

**Observed:** A thick pale surround frames a smaller white or transparent inner panel with 28px corners.

**Transfer:** Use nesting to distinguish interaction from static white cards.

#### Contrast panel · level 1

**Use:** Problem lists, selected comparisons and inverse plan treatment.

**CSS:** `background: #27272a; color: #ffffff; border-radius: 36px`

**Observed:** An isolated charcoal island within a light chapter, not a page-wide dark theme.

**Transfer:** Use one such island to emphasize a decisive comparison or alternative.

#### Scene glass · level 2

**Use:** A floating demonstration over textured terrain.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.2); border-radius: 16px; backdrop-filter: blur(8px)`

**Observed:** Large translucent demonstration panel floats inside a 36px-rounded landscape frame; white UI cards remain legible inside it.

**Transfer:** Confine glass to imagery-backed demonstrations rather than navigation or ordinary content.

## Imagery and graphics

### Living terrain

**Use:** Signature art for a major conversion scene, demonstration backdrops, small card-corner intrusions and closing identity.

**Observed:** Undulating hills are covered in short dense moss-like fibers, ranging from pale yellow-green sunlit crests to deep forest valleys. Diffuse daylight gives tangible depth; a pink patch or edge occasionally warms the green. It appears both as full-bleed landscape and a low horizon beneath broad white space.

**Transfer:** Create an original organic material world for the new product, preserving the finely textured ridges, diffuse light and green-to-dark depth. Minimum acceptable execution is a carefully lit macro texture mapped onto layered ridge shapes, reused consistently at several scales.

**Build:** Use an original moss macro image over an SVG field of 5–7 overlapping asymmetric hill paths. Fill rear ridges pale olive, mid ridges moss green and foreground ridges forest green; add feTurbulence at fine frequency, feDisplacementMap on ridge edges and a directional light/shadow layer. Keep a pale upper sky for low-horizon variants. For the saturated patch, place a 479px by 442px #fe45e2 ellipse near the lower middle, blur 25px, rotate -50deg and mix-blend-mode overlay; clip it with the terrain frame. Avoid a smooth mesh-gradient substitute.

### Texture-filled identity

**Use:** Monumental final visual anchor.

**Observed:** The final identity is nearly edge-to-edge lowercase artwork, filled with the same moss terrain rather than a flat color. It sits on cool gray in a 1344px by 204px stage and reaches the bottom edge.

**Transfer:** Use the new brand's identity or a short product phrase; preserve readable letter silhouettes and organic photographic detail.

**Build:** Create an SVG text mask or an outline mask for the new identity; place a cover-scaled terrain image behind it. Set the stage width to the wide container and fit the glyphs to its height without distorting the letterforms. Keep all background outside the glyphs #f4f4f5.

### Work media rail

**Use:** Visual breadth near the opening and within capability chapters.

**Observed:** Rounded portrait tiles combine laptop/interface scenes, brand stationery on green natural backing, bold editorial graphics and quiet text-led lists. Titles and fine tags sit over the artwork or inside a neutral card; thin segmented progress bars sit along the top. The rail extends beyond both viewport edges.

**Transfer:** Use credible product outcomes, real objects or designed artifacts, mixing image-rich and text-led tiles. For a product without a portfolio, show three meaningful workflows with distinctive art direction rather than repeated identical UI screens.

### Human proof

**Use:** Testimonials, tiny inline face clusters, profile rails and video.

**Observed:** Friendly headshots use circular or soft-square masks; profile tiles use light neutral backgrounds and tight head-and-shoulder framing. Testimonial video is tall and close-up. Tiny overlapping faces can replace a small span within headline or support text.

**Transfer:** Use relevant real users or team members with consistent crop scale and natural expression; keep inline clusters small enough to sit on the text baseline.

### Terrain-backed demonstration

**Use:** Explain a workflow alongside process steps.

**Observed:** A large 789px by 608px landscape card with 36px corners sits beside a narrower accordion. A translucent panel holds a coherent interface example and floats within the terrain, leaving a visible border of landscape on all sides.

**Transfer:** Stage one real product workflow on Scene glass. Keep the interface legible as an object without adopting its internal colors or typography as marketing tokens.

### Documentary community imagery

**Only for:** organization and culture pages

**Use:** Establish real people, origins and shared activity.

**Observed:** Three wide event photographs open the visual story after the centered statement; a large 1280px by 720px founder video follows. Later square photo cards show candid small groups and conversations with natural and venue lighting, not stylized monochrome treatment.

**Transfer:** Use documentary photographs of your own people, work and community, with generous rounded framing and brief grounded captions.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small monochrome utility marks commonly measure 16px or 20px; category or feature marks also use 28px. Chevrons, external-link arrows, check-in-circle marks, a phone and calendar appear around controls. Tiny tactile rocks, cubes and crystal-like objects identify plans and categories.

**Transfer:** Use a consistent compact icon family with clear solid silhouettes for checks and utility actions; reserve miniature 3D objects for a handful of category identifiers. Preserve 16–20px control icons and avoid colorful icon badges across every card.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible outcomes → buying or capability choices → human and quantified proof → explanation → objection handling → a low-friction call and practical, artful ending.

### Broad product or service landing page

1. **Promise** — Split headline and email action with miniature verification, proof counts and entering media rail.
2. **Choice** — Compact selector beside stacked offer summaries.
3. **Problem resolution** — Contrast panel, testimonial and sideways statistics.
4. **Capability and proof** — Profile rail, mixed proof mosaic, project outcomes and category-led gallery.
5. **Conversion and validation** — Terrain-backed invitation followed by varied customer proof.
6. **Explanation** — Numbered steps with landscape demonstration and quantitative progression cards.
7. **Closing and objection handling** — Large face-interrupted call, FAQ/helper split, then editorial resources, contact columns and texture-filled identity.

**Transfer:** Adapt the length to the buying decision, retaining the split opening, organic material world, mixed-format proof and monumental ending without reproducing every chapter.

### Organization and trust narrative

1. **Mission** — Centered multiline statement and short support.
2. **Human context** — Three-photo event rail and large founder video.
3. **Credibility** — Monochrome backing logos and a four-column portrait/name grid.
4. **History** — Gray story band with left narrative and photo-led timeline cards.
5. **Belonging** — Candid photo cards and a nested community-action card.
6. **Closing call** — Plain gray centered statement with inline faces and email form, followed by the shared editorial/contact/identity ending.

**Transfer:** Use origin, credible people, milestones and documentary activity rather than extending a product feature grid.

### Buying and comparative evaluation

1. **Buying promise** — Centered statement above a rounded gray four-option plan stage.
2. **Detailed comparison** — Long grouped feature matrix followed by a compact repeated pricing strip.
3. **Social proof** — Ratings, muted logo rail and three-column varied testimonials.
4. **Alternatives** — Dark product column versus three lightly ruled alternative columns.
5. **Closing and objections** — Plain gray call with faces, FAQ/helper rail and shared monumental footer.

**Transfer:** Keep commercial details explicit and layer detail after the initial choice; use shared human proof and material identity to keep a long comparison page warm.

## Writing

Direct, optimistic and conversational, with a confident bias toward getting work finished. It names operational annoyances in everyday language, then answers them with speed, simplicity and a concrete next step.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Awesomic names, wordmarks, proprietary terrain artwork, customer identities, quotes, ratings, funding figures, prices, approval rates or delivery claims. Replace all proof and promises with substantiated material for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to 'you' and 'your'; use 'we' for ownership of delivery. Prefer an active product promise to impersonal corporate narration.
- **Rhythm:** Short clause-driven headlines alternate with one or two compact explanatory sentences. Questions expose friction; contractions and occasional em dashes keep the language spoken. Actions are usually two or three words.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | A broad desired outcome made tangible through a concrete job or category. | 8–14 words | “All your product tasks done — from launch to scale” |
| Section heading | A blunt friction statement or a buyer-oriented question. | 4–10 words | “We solve the bottlenecks that kill your speed”<br>“Why hire when you can subscribe?” |
| Supporting line | Name the familiar pain, then move quickly to the easier result. | 12–35 words | “Skip the hiring pain and freelancers headache. Move right to awesome results”<br>“We solve the problem of endless searching, messy interviews, and hit-or-miss freelancers. Just great matches and real results.” |
| Primary action | A concrete verb plus the immediate next step, not an abstract aspiration. | 2–3 words | “Book demo”<br>“Get matched” |
| Offer description | Open with a practical question and follow with specifics and a plain-language benefit. | 20–40 words | “Hiring takes forever? Get a dedicated talent and a PM to handle setup, comms, and quality. No unreliable freelancers. Fast and personal.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** get; done; build; launch; scale; skip; without headache; real results; one simple; whatever works best for you

**Avoid:** synergistic; best-in-class ecosystem; revolutionize everything; unprecedented paradigm; formal institutional language

**Rules:**

- Lead with the customer's finished outcome rather than the mechanism.
- Use specific examples to support a broad promise; avoid adding several unproven superlatives.
- Keep each CTA attached to a predictable action: a call, a match, a plan or an account.
- Use figures as proof with short explanations, not as decorative giant numerals.
- Let the human quote sound more personal than the surrounding concise marketing copy.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main promise at 64px/1.12 and weight 600; use a muted gray word to soften the black statement.
- Pair 1280px narrative containers with 1344px media stages rather than forcing every chapter onto one width.
- Use 12px gaps for repeated cards and reserve 24px or 28px for their internal breathing room.
- Give primary controls the inset-highlight black-key finish and 16px corners.
- Reuse the same finely textured organic world in a large scene, a detail and the closing identity.
- Make quantitative evidence readable with 32px bold counts or 40px semibold percentages plus adjacent explanation.
- Use 48px top corners to make white and gray chapters feel like overlapping sheets.
- Keep people close to claims through small author faces, portrait video or restrained inline clusters.

### Don't

- Do not turn the black primary controls into full-pill flat buttons; their 14–16px corners and layered highlights are distinctive.
- Do not tint every card green: white and #f4f4f5 are the structural palette; green belongs to the material imagery.
- Do not add uppercase mono eyebrows or tight negative headline tracking; the tone relies on sentence-case geometric sans at normal tracking.
- Do not apply drop shadows to all white cards; most rely on background contrast and 36px corners.
- Do not substitute identical dashboard thumbnails for the mixed portrait, artifact, text and material media language.
- Do not center percentage labels vertically above all captions; preserve the compact sideways statistic treatment where space permits.
- Do not make dark mode the default because a few charcoal comparison panels exist.
- Do not shrink the closing textured identity to a conventional tiny footer logo.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions and several surfaces declare 0.25s transitions; some category controls specifically transition padding-left over 0.25s.
- The headline word loop declares a 10s infinite animation using cubic-bezier(0.175, 0.885, 0.32, 1.275); a conversion word loop declares 8s ease-in-out.
- Text and reverse text loops declare 50s linear infinite movement; decorative button elements declare 1.5s ease infinite animations.

**Transfer:**

- Keep changing words clipped within a stable headline line box so motion does not shift the promise.
- Use slow looping movement for tag or proof streams and small inward chevrons to enliven conversion keys, leaving reading surfaces stable.

**Unknown:**

- Exact entrance choreography, playback synchronization and the full animated button construction are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop compositions use 1280px and 1344px maximum content widths. Declared width breakpoints include 479px, 767px, 991px, 1279px and 1440px.
- Names of layout variants indicate tablet hiding for some header utilities and centered or narrower text groups on smaller screens.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, stack the split promise and form, keep one meaningful media tile visibly entering after proof, and preserve horizontal rail overflow rather than squeezing all cards.
- Stack the process text before its landscape demonstration; keep texture detail and a readable interface instead of scaling the entire desktop scene to illegibility.
- Fit the closing identity to the available width while preserving its mask proportions and material fill.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, gutters, card stacking behavior and menu interaction are not established. These narrow-screen rules are design recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
