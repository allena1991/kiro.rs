# Bajgart Office — editorial wit on display

## Overview

A light, gallery-like identity that pairs fine editorial serifs with matter-of-fact grotesk text and a mischievous streak. White and pale grey give saturated project imagery and colorful printed-card compositions room to perform. It suits personality-led products, creative tools and expert services where tangible work carries more weight than feature clutter; the overall density is sparse, with concentrated pockets of detail.

### Essentials

- Headlines make a typographic conversation: light serif at 72px/72px with -0.06em tracking, a 70px grotesk word, and a 72px italic serif ending tracked at -0.07em.
- The opening is a left-aligned editorial statement, not a centered app billboard: 24px side margins, a 120px fixed masthead, three headline lines and a staggered image strip entering the bottom of the first 900px.
- Actions are small black lozenges amid white space: 40px height, 50px radius, 16px/24px regular sans text and 8px 16px padding; pale outlined pills carry secondary navigation.
- Color belongs to things on the page rather than the page itself: white canvas, #fcfcfc panels and #f6f6f6 labels dominate the surrounding interface; 8px colored dots, vivid artwork and the closing card fan supply concentrated color.
- Work is staged as an overflowing gallery: 402px square image fields, 24px gutters and alternating square and shorter landscape images with captions above, not boxed feature cards. Use original product details and typographic application boards when photography is unavailable.
- A monumental serif word sculpture with an inset human photograph and a playful fan of overlapping printed-message cards give every new page a memorable signature. Rebuild these with the new identity and original short lines; simple flat colored cards and a candid detail photo are the minimum equivalent.

## Typography

- **Rhymes Text** — Editorial headlines, italic emphasis and identity lettering. Delicate high-contrast serif with narrow proportions, sharp details and expressive italic forms. If unavailable, use **Bodoni Moda**. Use light regular and italic cuts; tune line breaks to preserve the compact setting. Font stack: "Rhymes Text", "Bodoni Moda", Georgia, serif.
- **Saans** — Body, navigation, captions, labels, pricing and deliberate headline contrast. Neutral contemporary grotesk; mostly regular with occasional medium or bold emphasis. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use 400, 500 and 700; avoid adding weight to ordinary labels. Font stack: "Saans", "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- The serif carries persuasion while the sans carries explanation. Keep the contrast within a headline intentional rather than alternating fonts word by word.
- Tracking is much tighter in serif display type than body text. Body and small labels retain normal tracking and sentence case.
- Medium and bold sans are reserved for short lead-ins and selected testimonial clauses; they do not replace the light serif hierarchy.

## Imagery and graphics

### Staggered application gallery

**Use:** Opening preview and repeated visual proof chapters.

**Observed:** Edge-to-edge strips mix identity graphics, website screens, phone mockups, social layouts and photographed applications. 402px square fields alternate with shorter landscape panels, separated by 24px. Some images align at the top and some lower down; the strip continues past the viewport edge. Color is vivid and specific to each demonstration, with no global tint or dim overlay.

**Transfer:** Show the new product at multiple scales: a tight interface detail, a whole workflow, a real-world use and a typographic outcome. If no assets exist, create original flat application boards and a believable device composition rather than generic stock imagery.

### Printed-message card fan

**Use:** Signature playful graphic accompanying the closing conversion area.

**Observed:** Six colorful rounded printed cards overlap in two loose groups across almost the full width, with slight individual rotations. Electric blue, lemon yellow, lavender on dark brown, bright pink on plum, pale pink and green-on-white vary both typography and message. They remain flat, crisp and largely shadowless; the foreground cards obscure parts of the back cards.

**Transfer:** Write original witty mini-promises for the new product. Preserve the physical, collectible-card feeling even when the rest of the product is technical.

**Build:** Use six SVG groups or absolutely positioned rectangles around 400px by 250px at desktop, with 20px rounded corners. Rotate back cards by about -10deg and 8deg and foreground cards by -4deg and 0deg. Overlap each neighboring pair by roughly one third. Use electric blue with white text, a lemon-yellow sheet with dark ink, pale pink with orchid lettering and a dark purple sheet with vivid pink lettering; choose these artwork hues by eye. Center three short lines in each card, alternate serif italic and sans lettering, and add a small identity line near the bottom. No glow or cast shadow.

### Monumental identity sculpture

**Use:** Signature transition into the closing call.

**Observed:** A black, two-line high-contrast serif identity spans 966px by 238px in its outlined graphic form. A small upright candid photograph interrupts the first line between words; the second line is centered below. It is far larger than ordinary section titles.

**Transfer:** Create a new two-line identity or very short closing phrase as original outlined lettering. Insert a candid portrait or meaningful product-detail photograph between its first-line parts; preserve the huge-to-small contrast.

**Build:** Compose an SVG with a 966 by 238 viewBox. Set the upper words across the width and the lower word centered, with thin hairlines and tight letter fit. Reserve a roughly 156px square photographic interruption near the first-line center. A live-text equivalent can use Bodoni Moda with the font sized to fill the same graphic footprint.

### Cutting-mat study board

**Use:** A physical-looking graphic for exploratory work and its teaser.

**Observed:** An electric-blue rounded cutting mat carries fine cyan square grid lines, edge ruler ticks, small numbers and a row of white circular holes along its top. White title markings occupy an upper corner; several colorful paper studies fan out in the center. In the collection opening the mat is tilted, enlarged and clipped by the right viewport edge; the surrounding white field has a barely visible square grid.

**Transfer:** Use a blue workbench to present alternative concepts, prototypes or experiments. It is a contained artwork, not a blue background theme for the entire website.

**Build:** Draw a rounded blue SVG rectangle about 970px wide with 1px cyan lines at 22px intervals and a stronger line every fifth interval. Add inward ruler ticks and tiny numerals around the perimeter, plus evenly spaced 10px white circles near the top. Layer five rectangular concept sheets with rotations between -5deg and 5deg; bring one saturated original design to the front. Add subtle monochrome SVG noise for printed-material texture, not a luminous gradient.

### Process pinboard

**Use:** Tangible explanation beside an editorial benefit statement.

**Observed:** A pale grey backing with oversized white binder holes carries tilted schedule sheets, a clipped visual-direction card and a large warm studio video still. A central dark play button is backed by the person rather than a synthetic product render.

**Transfer:** Stage real process artifacts, milestones and a short human demonstration. Keep the supporting documents smaller and the useful demonstration dominant.

**Build:** Use a pale neutral rectangular backing, a top row of white circles and two white paper rectangles rotated in opposite directions. Draw a thin black paperclip with an SVG path at the upper sheet corner. Overlap a wide video or original still in the foreground; allow colored milestone marks on the smaller document.

### Candid studio photography

**Only for:** company-story pages

**Use:** Profile opening, values grid and small identity-photo interruptions.

**Observed:** Warm natural-light studio photographs show an informal seated person, books, plants, a leather sofa, tiled surfaces and everyday details. The opening portrait is a sharp-cornered vertical rectangle about 439px by 545px. Later images mix with bold typographic posters in a three-column editorial grid.

**Transfer:** Photograph real people in a lived-in working environment with natural colors and approachable gestures. Avoid corporate headshot cutouts and artificial blue lighting.

### Icons

**Observed:** Sparse black utility marks: plus signs in faint circles, up-right arrows on editorial links, a double right-pointing triangle on the secondary conversion action, and a white triangle on a dark video play control. Category information uses 8px solid circles rather than pictograms.

**Transfer:** Use simple monochrome SVG geometry and typographic arrows; do not add a multicolor feature-icon library.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Editorial gallery opening

**Observed:** White canvas behind Fixed masthead. Below the masthead, a left-aligned three-line statement starts after a generous white interval and spans roughly half the desktop width. The 72px light serif supplies the main sentence; a 70px sans concept and a 72px italic ending share the third line. A 16px support sentence follows, then Category pills and a single Primary button. The first 900px include all these elements and the upper part of Staggered application gallery: a tall vivid phone photograph rises near the left-middle while neighboring lower panels enter from both edges. The rest of the gallery and the explanatory chapter continue below the fold. There is no central floating app window or large colored hero background.

**Transfer:** Use a three-line promise with an intentional rational-versus-emotional font contrast and a staggered preview of actual outputs. Without product media, create two or three original application boards and one contextual detail photograph. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** promise → tangible outputs → explanation and trust → deeper exploration or buying detail → objection handling → personable closing invitation

### A broad landing page that wins trust through tangible outcomes

1. **Promise** — Mixed-serif editorial statement, category pills and a single action over an entering staggered gallery.
2. **Delivery explanation** — Benefit statement beside a process pinboard, followed by unboxed quotes.
3. **Product proof** — Long sequence of short caption headers and colorful overflowing application strips.
4. **Objection handling** — Split FAQ with a repeated conversion action.
5. **Further exploration** — Question-led teaser with the cutting-mat study board.
6. **Closing call** — Monumental identity, centered invitation, card fan and minimal footer row.

**Transfer:** Let a new product's best demonstrations carry the middle of the page; the number of chapters should follow the useful material.

### A visual collection that makes experimental thinking discoverable

1. **Curiosity hook** — Question-led serif introduction beside a tilted blue workbench graphic.
2. **Exploration** — Two-column collage cards with centered speculative questions.
3. **Closing call** — Large identity with inset photograph, invitation, card fan and footer row.

**Transfer:** Use for prototypes, resources or alternative approaches rather than reproducing a long sales page.

### A detailed buying page that makes delivery and scope concrete

1. **Promise** — Split pale opening panel pairs serif timing promise with a vivid product example.
2. **Method** — Approach copy and a three-tab visual explanation.
3. **Buying options** — Vertically stacked offer narratives with scope, duration-price line, action, example board and testimonial.
4. **Objection handling** — Compact two-column FAQ panel.
5. **Closing call** — Shared identity-and-card-fan invitation.

**Transfer:** Distinguish offers by buyer need and output, not by a generic escalating feature checklist.

### A company or maker story that combines expertise with personality

1. **Human introduction** — Editorial biography and black route pill beside a candid studio portrait.
2. **Point of view** — Blue-framed white statement sheet and a centered recognition line.
3. **Personality** — Three-column mix of graphic statements and informal photographs.
4. **Social proof** — Testimonial grid and further social endorsement panel.
5. **Objection handling** — Compact FAQ panel.
6. **Further exploration** — Question-led study teaser and cutting-mat artwork.
7. **Closing call** — Shared identity-and-card-fan invitation.

**Transfer:** Balance a clear point of view with observable work habits and real human detail; keep identity graphics confined to meaningful moments.

## Writing

Direct, warm and personally accountable. The persuasion connects practical progress to an emotional result, with dry, playful exaggeration confined to artwork and small asides.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Bajgart names, logos, project artwork, client identities, quotations, awards, availability, prices, timelines or experience claims. Source examples illustrate tone only; all promises and proof must belong to the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to the reader as you. Use I for an individual expert and we only where a real team or shared decision is involved.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often a compact promise followed by a contrasting phrase. Support copy is plain spoken and compact; fragments and paired negatives add emphasis. Questions invite curiosity or remove practical uncertainty.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A practical shortcut joined to an emotional result. | 10–16 words | “Your shortcut to a brand that connects meaning with feeling.” |
| Section headline | Concrete delivery promise followed by lasting value; one phrase earns italic emphasis. | 5–14 words | “Brand ready in two weeks. Built to be remembered for years.” |
| Supporting line | Explain the useful outcome in first-person, everyday language. | 12–25 words | “Your difference is already there. My job is to find it and make people feel it.” |
| Primary action | A specific conversational next step, not an abstract sales command. | 3–5 words | “Book your intro call”<br>“Send details” |
| Reassurance | State the effort and remove one friction point with a middle dot. | 4–7 words | “25 min · No prep needed” |
| Exploration title | What if one experience felt like a surprising, concrete analogy? | 8–14 words | “What if research felt more like excavation?” |
| Objection question | Ask the practical question the reader is already considering. | 5–11 words | “What if I don’t like either direction?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** what if; what makes you different; hands on; built to; feel; launch-ready; no layers; you leave with

**Avoid:** disruptive solutions; synergize; world-leading ecosystem; unlock limitless potential; enterprise-grade transformation

**Rules:**

- Pair an emotional benefit with a concrete mechanism or output.
- State who an offer fits and what the buyer leaves with before the price.
- Use numbers only when they help a real decision, not as decorative giant statistics.
- Keep wit concentrated in short printed-card messages; explanations remain straightforward.
- Name the next action precisely and reassure without pressure.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 72px light serif with tight tracking for the default opening, not a bold sans display.
- Keep body and action labels at 16px/24px regular sans.
- Preserve the 24px desktop outer inset and let image strips reach beyond it.
- Keep the primary action at 40px high with 50px rounded corners.
- Use 8px colored circles inside neutral category pills.
- Give media more area than its caption; use 402px square fields as the gallery anchor.
- Use 40px internal padding and 24px corners for centered secondary-page panels.
- Retain both monumental identity artwork and overlapping message-card artwork as signature devices.

### Don't

- Do not turn all headings into italic serif; italics emphasize selected phrases.
- Do not apply the serif's -0.06em tracking to body copy.
- Do not color the navigation text by category; taxonomy color belongs to the dot.
- Do not add drop shadows, glass blur or bevels to neutral panels.
- Do not wrap every gallery chapter in a rounded card; the default gallery is open and edge-clipped.
- Do not inflate ordinary prices into enormous gradient numerals; use the plain 26px duration-price line.
- Do not convert the bottom footer into a dense multi-column sitemap.
- Do not make all visual examples monochrome for consistency; the neutral interface exists to support different vivid outputs.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The masthead stays fixed while content scrolls.
- No action or surface transition timings are established.

**Transfer:**

- Preserve the fixed white masthead and its independent centered identity while the staggered artwork moves with the document.

**Unknown:**

- Gallery movement, rotating-card behavior, tab transitions, accordion expansion timing and animation easing are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Width breakpoints include 810px, 1200px and 1600px; narrow-width families also use 390px or 400px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the serif/sans/italic contrast and readable line breaks rather than shrinking the whole desktop composition.
- Stack split panels and collection cards while retaining the distinction between crisp foreground content and lateral artwork.
- Keep the signature card fan as an overlapping composition scaled to the available width; simplify its arrangement rather than replacing it with ordinary cards.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile typography sizes, exact navigation collapse, panel insets and which media are hidden are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
