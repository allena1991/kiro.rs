# Bee — everyday life, amplified

## Overview

A buoyant, people-first technology style pairing oversized bold grotesk lettering with candid, richly colored everyday photography. The predominantly light canvas opens into broad sky-blue and coral fields, sunny yellow objects and a dramatic black ending. Softly rounded product renders and small floating controls make sophisticated products feel companionable rather than technical; the pace is expansive, with compact explanatory copy.

### Essentials

- A human moment is the product stage: a full-bleed 900px-tall lifestyle opening carries a centered two-line 124px, weight-700 headline with 0.85 line height, a small support label and two compact actions; the product remains visible in the scene.
- Headlines behave like solid shapes: Antarctica bold, 92px/1 for major choices and 124px/1 for introductions, with approximately -0.03em tracking; ordinary feature headings drop sharply to 32px/1 rather than forming a smooth editorial scale.
- Controls feel pocket-sized against huge scenes: a centered 250×48px glass navigation capsule and 42px actions with 48px corners, 12px 24px padding and 16px regular text.
- Color arrives as entire chapters, not scattered decoration: use uninterrupted #94bdfb and #ef4e44 fields around big introductions, #fac609 for conversion and a bright demonstration card, with white explanation sections and #060606 capability and ending fields. Photography and these broad fields occupy most of the visual area.
- Give the explanation a spoken, progressive presence: a left-aligned 72px/1 bold statement reveals words from faint to black over a blue field, with 140px vertical breathing room. Carry this device to a concise product thesis, not an ordinary paragraph.
- Stage both people and objects: pair natural-light real-world scenes with a large isolated, softly lit product render on flat blue; use 40px outlined choice cards and a 400px yellow demonstration card with 72px corners. If no physical object exists, use a carefully composed interface artifact alongside a real user scene.
- End with a symbol, not just a sitemap: a black, approximately 900px closing field centers a yellow emblem in a 350×256px stage with 180px vertical padding, then places a small product link tile opposite yellow navigation columns and a subdued legal row. Use a new brand's own simple emblem or an original abstract token.

- Use sentence case, bold structural headings and regular explanatory copy; do not turn category lines into uppercase technical labels.

## Imagery and graphics

### Everyday human scenes

**Use:** Opening, large feature storytelling and closing media.

**Observed:** People wear or use the product in lived-in creative rooms, on sofas, at a café table and beside work materials. Window light, textured brick, plants and vivid blue or red clothing create warmth without a color wash. Wide scenes fill the viewport; inset versions have very large rounded corners. The product is discovered in the person’s activity rather than held up to camera.

**Transfer:** Photograph real use in a textured, naturally lit setting, with enough midtone background for bold white copy. For software, show a person doing the actual work with the relevant device present. A well-lit still photograph is the minimum version.

### Soft isolated product

**Use:** Monumental blue introduction and white choice cards.

**Observed:** A lavender wearable floats diagonally against flat blue, softly shaded with broad highlights, satin surfaces and small tactile details. Choice-card objects are more upright, dark or pale yellow, centered with substantial empty space and no display plinth.

**Transfer:** Use a clean cutout or softly lit render of the new object; for digital products, isolate a rounded interface artifact on the blue field rather than a dense browser mockup.

### Human-scale interface excerpt

**Use:** Inside the yellow demonstration card.

**Observed:** A white rounded panel shows one task with a black title, tiny controls and a lightly colored content area. The excerpt is secondary to the vivid card and the surrounding human scene.

**Transfer:** Show only enough interface to make the benefit concrete; retain a white inner panel and generous round corners.

### Progressive word field

**Use:** A required graphic thesis device in a long landing narrative.

**Observed:** Large black words gradually give way to gray and faint words along a continuous left-aligned statement. The blue background stays flat, without a gradient behind the whole section.

**Transfer:** Write a short new thesis in a bold, wrapping paragraph and reveal its words along the reading order.

**Build:** Split the paragraph into inline spans. Set Antarctica 72px/1, weight 700, tracking -0.03em. Use #060606 for revealed words and rgba(0,0,0,0.18) for unrevealed words; transition the active boundary through several gray words. Place on #94bdfb with 40px side inset and 140px vertical padding. For reduced motion, show the full statement in ink.

### Monumental closing emblem

**Use:** Required black ending shared by landing and editorial page types.

**Observed:** A large flat yellow organic mark sits alone above the navigation and product tile, surrounded by far more black space than the smaller text receives. There are no glows, gradients or texture in the emblem.

**Transfer:** Use the new brand's own emblem, or create an original abstract symbol with comparably soft geometry; a simple yellow typographic monogram is the minimum equivalent.

**Build:** Create a clean SVG silhouette in #fac609, centered in a 350×256px box. Use two or three broad rounded masses or a custom monogram, no outline, no shadow. Give its stage 180px top and bottom padding on #060606; keep all navigation below the stage rather than overlaying it.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Human promise → tangible offering → explanatory thesis → capability and trust proof → invitation or new direction → emblem-led ending.

### Long-form product landing narrative

1. **Promise** — Full-bleed human scene with giant centered benefit and two small actions.
2. **Product introduction** — Flat blue field, monumental heading and isolated satin product.
3. **Explanation and choice** — Progressive thesis followed by two outlined offering cards.
4. **Capability chapters** — Rounded immersive scene and bright demonstration overlay.
5. **Objection handling** — Product-detail trust split and dark ecosystem library.
6. **Social proof** — Focused review carousel with softened neighboring quotes.
7. **Future direction** — Coral announcement with broad rounded lifestyle media.
8. **Closing identity** — Black emblem-led ending, product reminder and navigation.

**Transfer:** Borrow the changes of scale, light/dark chapters and alternation between human use and tangible object; shorten or rearrange chapters to fit the new decision.

### Company or mission narrative

1. **Mission** — White left-aligned bold assertion and two offset explanatory columns.
2. **Human context** — Wide rounded lifestyle photograph.
3. **Present-tense proof** — Inset yellow band with a large assertion and concise support.
4. **Belief** — Tall human portrait beside a progressive statement.
5. **Invitation** — Friendly centered heading, support and email action.
6. **Closing identity** — Shared black emblem-led ending and utility navigation.

**Transfer:** Replace shopping logic with a clear argument about people, backed by real scenes and a simple contact invitation.

## Writing

Warm, plainspoken and confidently useful. Technology is described through what it lets a person notice, remember or do, not through speculative grandeur.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Bee or Amazon names, marks, product names, proprietary imagery, prices, ratings, customer quotes, usage figures, privacy promises or availability claims. Source examples illustrate tone only; supply independently supported content for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to 'you' and 'your'; use 'we' when describing beliefs or inviting contact. Refer to the product by name when explaining its role.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often three to six words; one-sentence support lines and compact action labels. Longer editorial arguments use ordinary full sentences and contractions. Exclamation marks belong to genuine quoted reviews, not the main marketing voice.

| Hero promise | A simple imperative tied to a human outcome. | 3–6 words | “Remember what matters” |
| Support | Explain the value without making the technology the center of attention. | One short sentence | “The best AI stays out of the way so you can stay in the moment.” |
| Feature heading | Name the useful task or reassuring property in everyday language. | 3–5 words | “Search your conversations”<br>“Confidential by design” |
| Action | Direct verb and object, or a familiar low-pressure continuation. | 2–4 words | “Learn more”<br>“Watch video” |
| Editorial invitation | Friendly social language rather than a formal sales demand. | One short phrase | “Let’s bee friends” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** what matters; in the moment; your; helps; remember; understand; everyday; connect

**Avoid:** revolutionary platform; unlock limitless potential; enterprise-grade synergy; disrupt; supercharge everything

**Rules:**

- Lead with the human benefit and explain the mechanism afterward.
- Keep one feature claim per heading and its support paragraph.
- Use specific, verifiable reassurance rather than generic badges of trust.
- Let a playful turn appear in a friendly invitation, not in every headline.
- Explain limitations and subscription conditions in plain, compact language.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep opening type at weight 700 and 0.85 line height, with a deliberate two-line break.
- Use the 32px/1 heading recipe for compact feature explanations, even beside very large media.
- Keep main desktop gutters at 40px and grid gaps at 20px.
- Put 42px pill actions against scenes and fields that are much larger than the controls.
- Use faint 1px #ededf1 outlines for white choice cards instead of pronounced elevation.
- Include both a progressive word-field thesis and the monumental yellow-on-black closing symbol.
- Reserve the 72px radius for the bright demonstration card and 64px for broad inset media.
- Keep explanatory trust copy narrow, around 325px, with 32px spacing between heading, paragraph and action.

### Don't

- Do not turn the 250px floating navigation into a viewport-wide menu bar.
- Do not use thin or regular display headings; the large lettering relies on weight 700.
- Do not apply the 124px display size to every feature title; most functional headings are 32px.
- Do not tint all white chapters blue or coral; those colors belong to broad intentional stages.
- Do not add heavy drop shadows to the outlined choice cards; their separation comes from the pale edge.
- Do not wrap every icon-led capability in a filled card; the dark library uses open space.
- Do not place customer identity inside the review bubble; keep it below with the avatar.
- Do not replace the footer's yellow navigation with a mass of bright white links competing with the emblem.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Buttons declare background-color 0.2s transitions; black actions hover to rgba(6, 6, 6, 0.75).
- Navigation declares background-color 0.3s and width, height and border-radius 0.42s cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1).
- Review cards declare opacity 500ms cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.68, 1), and transform/filter 1000ms cubic-bezier(0.32, 0, 0.6, 1). Peripheral review cards are blurred 6px at 0.4 opacity.
- The narrative uses progressive word emphasis and a full-size human scene that becomes inset; the footer is a sticky reveal.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the word-reveal boundary aligned to natural reading order rather than random letters.
- Retain the distinction between a sharp active review and softened peripheral reviews; preserve the pause affordance.
- For reduced-motion presentations, render the thesis fully readable and use a static rounded human scene.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll thresholds and timing of word emphasis, scene transformation and footer reveal are unspecified.
- Focus-visible styling is not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 479px, 767px, 768px, 991px, 992px, 1280px and 1440px; the editorial styling also includes 950px.
- The navigation constrains its width to calc(100% - 40px); product choices support wrapping.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, retain the centered small navigation and full-bleed human opening; reframe the photograph so the product detail remains visible.
- Stack choice cards, trust media/text and portrait/belief pairs rather than shrinking all columns. Keep the emblem as the main footer event and let link groups wrap beneath.
- Reduce monumental type to preserve a strong short-line silhouette; keep body text readable and the action pair usable.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen typography, media crops, column changes and spacing are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
