# Billow — cloudborne business clarity

## Overview

A bright, buoyant software identity with dark navy editorial serifs, rounded sans-serif detail and electric blue actions. White and icy blue space is animated by soft cyan light, cloudlike atmospheres and carefully staged interface imagery. It suits connected tools and service products that need to make complex work feel approachable, with sparse marketing copy around detailed product demonstrations.

### Essentials

- Editorial promises sit above practical software: Lastik headings at 92px/1.05, weight 400 and -0.05em tracking, paired with Open Runde support at 17px/1.5.
- The opening is a centered promise over a luminous product stage: a 700px headline block, 500px support block, one pill action, and a 1152px-wide interface emerging near the bottom of the first 900px.
- Actions feel like small polished blue lozenges: blue-to-violet gradients, 46px height, 16px semibold lettering, rounded ends and a trailing white chevron; navigation uses a compact 31px navy variant.
- Color lives in light rather than heavy bands: white and #fcfdff occupy most of the page, #001c2e and #446278 carry text, and cyan-blue atmospheres gather behind media and closing content rather than flooding reading areas.
- Software floats between clouds and paper: 12px inner media corners, 20px padded outer frames, faint blue-gray edges, overlapping detail panels and soft white fades. Include a luminous product scene; when no interface exists, stage a believable process diagram in the same light.
- The ending is a useful inventory, not just a slogan: a left-aligned 64px serif promise inside a centered 672px block, three 208px feature columns with 20px check discs, tiny roadmap pills and a blue action, flowing into a pale sky footer.

## Typography

- **Lastik Variable Variable** — All marketing headings, including small card and footer headings. Compact, high-contrast editorial serif with soft, expressive terminals and tightly fitted capitals. If unavailable, use **DM Serif Display**. Use weight 400; tune wrapping to retain compact two-line statements. Font stack: "Lastik Variable Variable", "DM Serif Display", Georgia, serif.
- **Open Runde** — Body text, navigation, actions, feature lists and labels. Friendly rounded sans with an efficient text width and open counters. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use weights 400–600; preserve the negative tracking. Font stack: "Open Runde", "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Inter** — Tiny status badges and embedded interface details. Compact neutral sans for utility-sized labels. Font stack: "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Gveret Levin** (only for interactive product demonstrations) — A single playful handwritten invitation beside the main demo. Loose, informal handwriting that contrasts with the careful serif and sans system. Font stack: "Gveret Levin", cursive.

**Notes:**

- Keep large serif type regular, not bold. Its tight -0.05em fit supplies the visual density while the surrounding layout stays open.
- The sans runs at 15px for working copy and 17px for introductory copy; reserve semibold for actions and specific inline leads.
- Use intentional short headline wraps. Two-color capability pairs are two continuous serif lines, not separate boxed labels.
- Do not apply the serif recipe to dense embedded application tables; product UI stays compact and neutral.

## Imagery and graphics

### Luminous product stage

**Use:** Signature main demonstration and small product-art echoes.

**Observed:** A large white dashboard floats over overlapping cyan and electric-blue rounded lobes. Light is strongest immediately around the top and side edges, blurs into white farther out and never tints the interface itself. A handwritten note and curved doodle point toward interaction.

**Transfer:** Stage a real interface or a simple readable workflow model in Demo glass. The minimum equivalent is one useful white panel backed by three soft cyan/blue light masses.

**Build:** Behind a centered 1152px panel, overlap three broad ellipses: cyan left and right and electric blue in the middle. Make each roughly 400–600px wide and 500–700px tall, vary vertical alignment, blur by 24–48px and soften the outside with a white radial mask. Use #00d8ed and #007bff as luminous centers, not outlines. Keep the art behind the panel and below the hero copy.

### Cloud atmosphere

**Use:** Signature media backdrops, sky conversion card and the continuous closing-to-footer background.

**Observed:** Pale cyan sky contains softly billowing white clouds with no hard horizon. It is richer at the upper edge of conversion artwork and behind the closing inventory, then dissipates toward the lower white field. Footer navigation rests on a very pale blue continuation.

**Transfer:** Use soft sky-like light even for non-software products. It must remain a recognizable atmospheric device rather than a flat blue rectangle.

**Build:** Layer a #dbf3ff field with irregular overlapping white radial gradients, 160–400px across, concentrated along the lower and left edges. Add a broad low-opacity cyan region at the top right, blur cloud edges by 20–40px and mask the whole layer into #fcfdff with a vertical white fade. For the footer, stretch the cloud layer across the full width and reduce saturation and contrast; exact cloud contours are not essential.

### Workflow demonstrations

**Use:** Repeated capability chapters.

**Observed:** Wide, almost monochrome interface views show lists, boards or documents. A larger detail window overlaps at the right, while the lower portion dissolves into white. Marketing text occupies a shallow header row above a much larger image.

**Transfer:** Show the full workspace plus one important task detail; preserve pale surfaces, tiny dark UI labels and ample visible structure rather than turning it into a collage.

**Build:** Place the overview in Product frame, then a roughly 530px-wide white detail frame at the right with 8px inset and 20px corners. Apply a white gradient mask across the lower quarter of the scene. Keep callouts within the same perspective and light.

### Miniature benefit scenes

**Use:** Top halves of onboarding cards.

**Observed:** Small invoice, import and chart fragments float over cloudy blue backdrops; blue objects or small rounded number markers supply focal points. Edges fade into the pale card rather than ending with a separate image border.

The near-white opening starts its centered content at 160px with a pale utility capsule. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → tangible product proof → connected capability chapters → purchase reassurance → useful inventory and final action → atmospheric utility footer.

### A product landing page that makes a complex tool feel immediately usable

1. **Promise** — Centered serif outcome over the top of a luminous product stage.
2. **Product demonstration** — A large dashboard invites interaction with a handwritten aside.
3. **Activation proof** — Image-led equal cards show first useful results.
4. **Capability chapters** — Large workflow views, overlapping details and compact two-color headings show connected work.
5. **Expansion** — A split upcoming-feature card and audience tabs broaden relevance.
6. **Conversion** — A cloud-backed promise card repeats one primary action.
7. **Objection handling** — Compact joined question rows.
8. **Closing call** — A narrow left-aligned promise and checked inventory lead to the final action and sky footer.

**Transfer:** Borrow the narrative contrast between concise promises and expansive demonstrations; rearrange proof and reassurance to suit the new buyer.

### An editorial release index that makes ongoing progress legible

1. **Orientation** — A single large serif title and short support over pale clouds.
2. **Progress proof** — Repeated sticky title/date rails beside large release artwork and explanatory prose.
3. **Invitation** — A small blue gradient text action ends each release.
4. **Closing call** — Shared checked inventory and blue action flow into the atmospheric footer.

**Transfer:** Use consistent covers, sparse metadata and specific shipped-behavior explanations; retain the common brand ending.

### A plan selection page with an exceptional offer and a regular comparison

1. **Promise** — Two-line serif title and brief trial reassurance.
2. **Exceptional offer** — Cloud-backed panel with translucent plan cards, compact prices and green savings.
3. **Pricing** — Regular plan cards with billing control and one softly glowing recommendation.
4. **Objection handling** — Small reassurance beneath actions and a centered contact link.
5. **Closing call** — Shared capability inventory, final action and sky footer.

**Transfer:** Explain the plan structure clearly while keeping monetary numbers subordinate to plan names and inclusions.

## Writing

Practical, lightly conversational and confident without sounding grand. Benefits are framed as relief from scattered work, supported by concrete tasks rather than abstract transformation.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Billow or Trueform names, logos, customer names, proprietary artwork, product-specific claims, price points, savings figures, release facts or trial promises unless independently true for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your work. The product is named naturally in explanations; we appears when discussing releases and listening to feedback.
- **Rhythm:** Short two-part headlines, often a promise followed by relief. Support usually runs one or two sentences; release notes can become longer and more explanatory.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the span of work, then promise a unified place or outcome. | 7–10 words | “From lead to paid. All in one place.” |
| Capability pair | Positive action followed by a familiar frustration removed. | Two short clauses | “Own Your Pipeline. Stop Losing Warm Leads.” |
| Support | Explain the outcome with specific tasks, then remove a practical concern. | One or two sentences | “[Brand] is built to get you up and running fast. No long onboarding, no complicated setup.” |
| Primary action | A direct verb and low-friction next step. | 3–4 words | “Start your trial” |
| Objection heading | Acknowledge that questions are reasonable in everyday language. | 3–5 words | “Totally fair to ask.” |
| Release explanation | Lead with a shipped behavior, then describe what the user can now do. | A practical paragraph | “[Brand] now talks to AI assistants.” |
| Plan reassurance | State billing expectations plainly rather than using promotional hype. | One short sentence | “You won't be charged before the trial ends.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** all in one place; connected; no more; right where you need it; get up and running; what's working; without the mess; included

**Avoid:** revolutionary; unparalleled; synergy; next-generation ecosystem; supercharge everything; effortless magic

**Rules:**

- Make headings about outcomes and frustrations, not feature taxonomy alone.
- Use concrete lists of tasks to explain broad connection claims.
- Pair assertive statements with specific proof or a visible workflow.
- Keep playfulness in small asides rather than making every paragraph jokey.
- Use availability and billing language literally and plainly.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main display at weight 400 and -0.05em tracking; its serif shape is the dominant voice.
- Set core support at 17px/1.5 and working copy at 15px/1.5.
- Keep the main wrapper at 1280px including 24px side padding.
- Preserve the 8px heading-to-support relationship against 72–80px demonstration gaps.
- Make the primary 46px blue pill visibly more luminous than the 31px navigation action.
- Include cyan-blue light around a main product stage and softer clouds near conversion or closing content.
- Use joined 60px question rows with 28px tinted plus squares.
- Keep the final checklist group narrow at 672px while the atmosphere extends across the page.

### Don't

- Do not replace the editorial serif with a bold geometric sans at display sizes.
- Do not use electric blue as the default paragraph color; keep copy slate and headings navy.
- Do not turn the 16px paper-card corners into exaggerated pill-shaped card bodies.
- Do not enlarge plan prices into hero-scale numerals; the plan names carry the hierarchy.
- Do not place dark gray drop shadows under every panel; use faint navy edges and restrained lower-edge lift.
- Do not make the footer a navy block; its atmosphere is pale and continuous with the closing light.
- Do not use angular black icon tiles for the closing feature list; keep the small blue check discs.
- Do not scatter independent badges and floating windows through reading copy; keep them attached to a product scene or feature row.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Text links declare 0.6s color and decoration transitions with cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1).
- Embedded application controls declare 0.15s background, opacity and border-color transitions.
- The sky conversion artwork is a video layer with saturate(1.25) and multiply blending.

**Transfer:**

- Keep text-link color changes soft and measured; preserve the distinction between luminous atmospheric media and quick utility feedback.
- If recreating sky media, let changes stay in the cloud layer rather than moving the reading copy.

**Unknown:**

- Exact cloud-video timing and loop behavior are not specified.
- No main button hover, focus-visible or entrance choreography is established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width boundaries occur around 810px, 1200px and 1440px.

**Transfer:**

- For narrower layouts, retain the centered serif promise and place its product stage underneath, not alongside it.
- Stack the three-card and checklist columns when they no longer fit; keep the cloud layer behind the whole group.
- For release indexes, move metadata above its image while retaining a clear title-to-prose hierarchy.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile font sizes, navigation replacement, spacing reductions and exact breakpoint behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
