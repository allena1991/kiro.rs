# Buy Me a Coffee — friendly yellow orbits

## Overview

An approachable, people-first marketing style with round geometric sans typography, sunny yellow actions and soft ivory surroundings. Spacious light pages stage little interface scenes as friendly objects, with portraits and small reactions bringing human warmth. It suits self-serve tools and community-oriented products; feature-focused variants add immersive photography and emphatic yellow demonstration bands.

### Essentials

- A big promise sits inside a ring of human proof: centered 96px/95px bold geometric type, a 730px text column, six small tilted portrait cards around the outer edges and a proof line above.
- Headlines feel round and emphatic rather than technical: Circular at visual weight 700, -0.025em tracking for the opening and 64px/1.25 with -0.03125em tracking for chapter headings.
- The action is a sunny lozenge, not a rectangular control: #ffdd00, 9999px radius, 74px tall and 24px bold text in the default opening.
- Most of the page is warm near-white and white; yellow is concentrated in conversion actions. Full-width yellow is reserved for feature demonstrations and their closing conversion scenes, while other saturated colors live mainly inside media.
- Product proof is a small floating theater: white 48px-radius chapter panels hold a central interface object, overlapping smaller cards and soft layered shadows. Recreate the theater with simplified real controls and outcome badges when polished artwork is unavailable.
- An assertion gets a softer echo: 64px headings pair #222222 first lines with #717171 second lines, then resolve into left-aligned icon-led benefits rather than ending every page with another oversized button.

## Typography

- **Circular** — All standard marketing headings, paragraphs, labels and controls. Round geometric sans with open counters, single-storey forms and friendly compact bold shapes. Book, Regular, Medium and Bold cuts belong to one family. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use 400 for Book and Regular, 500 for Medium and 700 for Bold; preserve tight display tracking. Font stack: "Circular", "DM Sans", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Borel** (only for review-collection titles) — Handwritten display title alongside a heart. Loose connected handwriting, used as a brief affectionate interruption rather than a body face. Font stack: "Borel", cursive.

#### Canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default light marketing environment.

**CSS:** `background: #fdfdfa; color: #222222`

**Observed:** Near-white supports white islands without a hard edge.

**Transfer:** Keep the surrounding field pale and warm.

#### Chapter island · level 1

**Use:** A major capability chapter.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border-radius: 48px; padding: 48px 96px`

**Observed:** Large flat white panel with no visible stroke or drop shadow; nested interface objects supply the depth.

**Transfer:** Give one customer outcome its own spacious island.

#### Floating object · level 2

**Use:** Human proof and simplified interface objects.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border-radius: 16px; box-shadow: 0px 0px 2px 0px rgba(0,0,0,0.15), 0px 8px 40px 0px rgba(0,0,0,0.04), 0px 2px 5px 0px rgba(0,0,0,0.05)`

**Observed:** Faint shadow edges make white objects legible on white without prominent outlines.

**Transfer:** Reserve elevation for the little objects, not every section.

#### Yellow stage · level 0

**Only for:** feature-led demonstrations and closing conversions

**Use:** Immersive walkthrough or final personalized action.

**CSS:** `background: #ffdd00; color: #222222`

**Observed:** Flat saturated yellow spans the entire width, with white and charcoal controls on top. The closing footer continues the same color.

**Transfer:** Use as a decisive tonal change, not a wash behind all content.

#### Photographic scrim · level 1

**Only for:** photographic feature openings

**Use:** Legibility layer between a full-bleed photograph and white navigation.

**CSS:** `background: linear-gradient(rgba(0,0,0,0.8), rgba(0,0,0,0)); height: 100px`

**Observed:** Darkness is strongest at the top; the photograph remains the dominant material.

**Transfer:** Keep the person's face clear while protecting the navigation.

## Imagery and graphics

### Floating interface theater

**Use:** Signature product explanation within chapter islands.

**Observed:** A central payment form, tier fan or product card is surrounded by detached messages, small reaction bubbles and outcome badges. Objects are front-facing white cards with rounded corners and diffuse shadows. Small flat gift, book and envelope illustrations add color without filling the section.

**Transfer:** Stage a recognizable task from the new product with one dominant control and two to four outcome objects. A minimum version is a CSS-built form, a success message, an avatar and a compact metric badge, not an entire dashboard.

**Build:** Use a relative white stage about 824px wide. Center a 288–386px-wide card; place smaller 110–240px cards at left and right, overlapping by 16–32px. Apply the Floating object surface. Use circles of 40–56px for one or two reactions. For a tier fan, place three white cards side by side and bring the middle one forward and slightly higher; keep the colored artwork confined to the card headers.

### Orbiting human proof

**Use:** Signature framing around the default opening.

**Observed:** Six portrait or logo tiles sit in two uneven outer columns. Small rotations and clipped outer edges suggest a surrounding community while leaving a broad clear center.

**Transfer:** Substitute real customer or project identities. If portraits are unavailable, use original monograms and concrete task descriptions with the same scale and soft edges.

**Build:** Position three Floating object cards on each side of the hero, roughly 200px wide. Alternate small rotations around 5deg and -5deg; stagger the vertical placements. Clip the outermost edges while maintaining a clear 730px central text lane. Use 48px circular avatars and concise centered copy.

### Media-format collage

**Use:** Show a product supporting several formats in one chapter.

**Observed:** A large landscape video still, stacked square album covers, a small note, a locked cover and a horizontal green audio player form an asymmetric collage on white. Rounded media corners and actual faces or outdoor scenes soften the interface.

**Transfer:** Use two or three authentic output formats from the new product; preserve mixed aspect ratios and overlap rather than substituting identical tiles.

### Immersive human photograph

**Only for:** photographic feature openings and demonstrations

**Use:** Give a feature a real-world setting.

**Observed:** A full-screen photograph places a person at a desk, face on the right and open space for text on the left. Purple ambient light supplies atmosphere; a later rounded scene overlays dark translucent editing controls on a smiling presenter and laptop.

**Transfer:** Photograph the new product in use with a person, preserving a natural expression, directional ambient color and deliberate text space. Use a single convincing scene instead of a generic abstract gradient.

### Personalized art stage

**Only for:** visual customization feature openings

**Use:** A visual-only opening that shows a personalized object before explaining it.

**Observed:** A 510×540px composite centers a tall portrait card with leafy backing, inset portrait, a scannable badge, reaction circles and white customization chips. Behind it is a deeply blurred green photographic field fading to dark at the bottom.

**Transfer:** Use an original personalized output and a real portrait or content preview; retain the hovering controls and organic blurred backdrop.

**Build:** Fill a 900px-high scene with a blurred nature photograph enlarged beyond its bounds, then overlay a transparent-to-50%-black diagonal scrim. Center a tall rounded output card; add one upper-left badge, two small white control panels across its lower corners and a vertical group of white reaction circles on the right. Keep the object crisp against the defocused background.

### Community portrait rail

**Only for:** feature-page social proof

**Use:** Wide evidence of different people and projects.

**Observed:** Large upright cards use full-bleed portraits or bold identity artwork, rounded corners, white captions over a dark lower fade and a tiny yellow heart next to a supporter count. The rail extends beyond both viewport edges and fades to white at the sides.

**Transfer:** Use varied real customer identities with a short description and a relevant truthful metric. Mix photographic and graphic covers without forcing one filter.

**Build:** Build a horizontally overflowing flex rail. Use tall cover cards with a bottom black-to-transparent gradient and bottom-aligned white text; overlay 24px-wide white-to-transparent gradients at both container edges.

### Social video proof

**Only for:** review collections

**Use:** Keep authentic customer testimony in native media shapes.

**Observed:** Portrait videos use 259×461px rounded frames, white play circles and handles below; landscape video thumbnails use 364×205px frames with their native bold titles and play marks.

**Transfer:** Keep portrait and landscape groups separate and retain author attribution; let the content supply its own palette.

### Icons

**Observed:** Functional icons are compact monochrome SVGs: search around 18px, navigation glyphs around 20px, social marks around 24px and small 12px chevrons. Principle checks sit inside thin circles; benefit icons are larger black line-and-fill pictograms around 40–48px.

**Transfer:** Use friendly rounded silhouettes and simple outlines. Keep the small controls understated and let larger benefit pictograms carry the visual punctuation.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Human proof opening

**Observed:** A fixed Navigation sits over a warm near-white field. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → tangible task or output → useful capabilities → human trust and practical reassurance → a page-appropriate resolution.

### Broad self-serve product introduction

1. **Promise** — Centered large outcome with peripheral human proof and one yellow action.
2. **Capability chapters** — Rounded white islands alternate simple benefit copy with floating UI theaters and a media collage.
3. **Trust** — Two-tone principle heading and circled-check statements.
4. **Resolution** — Wider comparative benefit island followed directly by the compact ivory footer.

**Transfer:** Use the soft chapter-island language as the default; organize chapters around outcomes that matter for the new product.

### Standalone social-proof collection

1. **Warm introduction** — Handwritten heading, yellow heart, conversational history and star proof.
2. **Social proof** — Independent quote, portrait-video and landscape-video rails, each with platform labels and circular arrow controls.
3. **Resolution** — Ivory utility footer without a new conversion panel.

**Transfer:** Let authentic voices supply density and color; keep the wrapper friendly and restrained.

### Feature landing page grounded in real usage

1. **Promise** — Full-bleed human photograph with white left-aligned headline and yellow action.
2. **Activation** — Three setup cards.
3. **Product demonstration** — Large rounded photograph with feature controls overlaid.
4. **Compatibility proof** — Pastel logo tile grid.
5. **Human proof** — Wide community cover-card rail.
6. **Objection handling** — Centered FAQ accordion.
7. **Closing call** — Yellow personalized field, dark button and continuous yellow footer.

**Transfer:** Pair a real usage scene with a simple setup path, then answer specific adoption concerns.

### Visually led feature with a short sequential workflow

1. **Product invitation** — Personalized output floats on a blurred organic background without a visible hero headline.
2. **Activation** — Three instruction cards.
3. **Product demonstration** — Yellow scroll-linked phone scene with active and inactive step labels.
4. **Human proof** — Community rail.
5. **Objection handling** — Narrow FAQ column.
6. **Closing call** — Yellow personalized conversion scene and matching footer.

**Transfer:** Use the visual-only opening only when the output is immediately legible, then explain it through a concrete sequence.

## Writing

Warm, plain-spoken and confidently useful. The product speaks like a friendly service for individuals, with practical explanations, occasional conversational asides and direct reassurance.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, logo, creator identities, testimonials, audience counts, earnings figures, comparative percentages or other proprietary claims. Replace all such assets and claims with original, substantiated material. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your; use we for the people behind the product. Actions often use my to help the reader imagine ownership.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines tend to be three to nine words, sometimes broken into a dark assertion and gray qualifier. Supporting copy is one or two conversational sentences; instructions are short imperatives. Exclamation marks appear mainly in reassurance or authentic quotes.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A short verb-led outcome, not a category definition. | 4–7 words | “Fund your creative work”<br>“Celebrate support, live.” |
| Support | Concrete actions followed by a reassuring simplification. | One or two sentences | “Accept support. Start a membership. Setup a shop. It’s easier than you think.” |
| Action | First-person ownership or a direct setup action; free is attached to a meaningful task. | 3–7 words | “Start my page”<br>“Set up stream alerts for free” |
| Chapter heading | State the benefit in the reader's language. | 4–10 words | “Give your audience an easy way to say thanks.”<br>“Publish your best work” |
| Reassurance | Answer an unspoken worry with concrete friction reduction. | One short sentence | “It’s free and takes less than a minute!” |
| Question answer | Direct conversational answer, then a practical explanation. | One or two sentences | “Nope! Alerts pop up cleanly on screen without disrupting your content or audio.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your work; easy; in seconds; free; start my; a couple of taps; talk to a human

**Avoid:** enterprise-grade transformation; unlock synergies; monetization infrastructure; revolutionary ecosystem; users as transactions

**Rules:**

- Lead with what someone can do, then explain how little effort it takes.
- Make trust tangible through ownership, control, privacy or access to a real person.
- Keep personality in short phrases and human examples, not a joke in every heading.
- Use numbers only when they prove a specific benefit; do not inflate the story with arbitrary statistics.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default display at 96px/95px with -0.025em tracking on desktop.
- Center the main promise in a 730px lane and narrow the support to 600px.
- Use 48px corners for major chapter islands and 16px for small proof tiles.
- Keep nested product scenes lightly elevated with the three-layer Floating object shadow.
- Use a single 74px yellow pill for the default opening action.
- Set concluding benefit descriptions at 20px/24px in muted gray.
- Use 32px gaps between repeated chapter panels or instruction cards.
- Keep the footer a compact 144px ending, with utility links centered.

### Don't

- Do not make all headings tightly stacked: the default chapter recipe is 64px/80px, not the feature variant's 64px/64px.
- Do not use bright blue or coral as marketing actions just because those hues appear inside product scenes.
- Do not add a hard gray outline to every white object; soft shadows carry the floating-card edges.
- Do not apply the handwritten title face to ordinary product headlines or body text.
- Do not replace 9999px action radii with small rounded rectangles.
- Do not turn the simple footer into a tall multi-column sitemap.
- Do not tint every chapter yellow; neutral fields dominate the default presentation.
- Do not stretch the portrait video rail into landscape cards or normalize the different media formats.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Chapter containers declare fade-up animation at 1s cubic-bezier(0.2,0.75,0.28,0.9) with 0.1s delay; inner chapter headings declare 0.8s with the same curve.
- Small proof cards declare 0.2s transitions. The opening yellow background declares an all-property 0.1s transition with cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1).
- Feature actions declare 0.2s transitions and yellow at 80% alpha on hover. Footer social links declare scale(1.1) on hover.
- The phone walkthrough changes active step and phone content across scrolling; review collections use horizontal rails.

**Transfer:**

- Reveal chapter containers and their content as related groups rather than animating every line independently.
- Keep hover effects on the small floating objects subtle, and preserve a static readable workflow when scroll-linked behavior is unavailable.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll trigger thresholds, phone pinning implementation and reduced-motion behavior are not established.
- No focus-visible or active treatment is established for the main actions.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 480px, 576px, 768px, 900px, 1024px and 1200px, among others.
- Opening classes specify 48px type with 55px leading on narrow screens; chapter panels specify 24px corners instead of 48px.
- Instruction grids declare a one-column variant; feature headings specify 36px/36px for narrow layouts. Peripheral proof cards and navigation search have hidden variants.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, remove the outer orbit before narrowing the central promise into an unreadable strip.
- Stack instruction cards while retaining their image-first hierarchy; simplify the phone demonstration into an ordered sequence.
- Reduce chapter side padding and switch chapter text toward left alignment as space tightens.

**Unknown:**

- Only desktop rendering is established; exact breakpoint-to-variant mappings and narrow-screen media arrangements are not verified.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
