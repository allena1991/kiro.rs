# Baba — red linen, human relief

## Overview

A reassuring, people-first marketing style with an unexpectedly vivid red opening and a warm cream reading environment. Tight grotesk headlines sit above highly legible humanist body text, while cutout photography, paper objects, handwritten annotations and woven textures make practical work feel tangible. It suits services and products that remove complexity for people, balancing spacious storytelling with detailed illustrated explanations.

### Essentials

- Relief is pictured at human scale: a full-width red opening, 747px tall at desktop, pairs a two-line white 64.8px/1.05 headline with a large unboxed photographic cutout anchored to the lower right.
- Headlines are assertive without shouting: Söhne 600, -0.03em tracking for the opening, then 48px/52px section headings at -0.01em; body copy switches to Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 16px/24px.
- Color behaves like cloth laid on a cream table: #faf9f1 dominates the long reading body; red occupies the opening, closing call and footer, with isolated #113048 and #1d0b10 textured proof panels rather than alternating saturated full-width bands.
- Actions are small rounded rectangles, not capsules: 44px high, 12px corners, 20px side padding, 15px/22.5px medium text; white on red stages, bright #ea1111 on cream, with a fine outlined companion.
- Practical outcomes become physical illustrations: 10px-radius warm-stone tiles contain oversized phones, calendars, notes or documents, with faint looping pencil lines and short red category captions. Every new page needs a tangible task vignette; use a simple document-and-checkmark collage when bespoke imagery is unavailable.
- Proof gets a tactile dark stage: white regular-weight 36px numbers, coral arithmetic symbols, red hand-drawn underlines and fine white-alpha column rules on wine-colored woven panels. Include this annotated proof device using genuinely supported facts or concrete process milestones.
- Human warmth interrupts the system diagrams: overlapping white-bordered portrait prints, irregular rotations and handwritten Caveat 24px/30px annotations temper the orderly grids. Use original customer, maker or team portraits, or purpose-made photographic object prints when people cannot be shown.

## Typography

- **Söhne** — Headlines, card titles, quotes and statistics. Compact contemporary grotesk with firm medium and semibold weights and tightly fitted display lines. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use 500–600 for headings, 400 for statistics; preserve the distinct tracking of each role. Font stack: "Söhne", "Inter", -apple-system, system-ui, "Segoe UI", sans-serif.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** — Paragraphs, navigation, buttons and supporting labels. Open, highly differentiated humanist sans that keeps practical explanations approachable. Font stack: "Atkinson Hyperlegible Next", -apple-system, system-ui, "Segoe UI", sans-serif.
- **Caveat** — Handwritten captions and annotations beside task illustrations. Loose handwritten strokes, used as a small human intervention rather than a display face. Font stack: "Caveat", cursive.

**Notes:**

- Keep the font split visible: firm grotesk headings, open humanist explanations, handwritten annotations only.

## Imagery and graphics

### Human cutout stage

**Use:** Primary opening image and audience-specific hero variants.

**Observed:** A warmly lit older adult in natural clothing occupies the right half and bleeds through the lower edge of the red stage. A thin cool-colored offset silhouette separates the figure from the red cloth. A small charcoal call-status card and friendly illustrated bird overlap the person; typography remains unobscured on the left. Professional variants put a clinician and client on cream, or one centered person on a call beneath the headline.

**Transfer:** Photograph a real person benefiting from the new product, remove the background, retain natural skin tones and posture. Without suitable photography, create a large isolated physical object in the same bottom-right composition and add a compact task-status card.

**Build:** Layer a full-bleed red radial field, woven texture, then a transparent subject PNG. Duplicate the subject mask behind it with a low-opacity cool tint and a small offset. Add a charcoal gradient status card with 14px backdrop blur and 9px corners; keep it well below the headline and smaller than the subject.

### Woven paper and cloth

**Use:** Red opening, blue trust banner and wine proof panels.

**Observed:** Fine irregular crosshatching resembles linen or dense woven paper, visible across saturated red and dark blue or wine areas. It is tactile and low contrast, not a smooth luminous gradient. Closing red scenes add faint sketch-like clouds or landscape strokes.

**Transfer:** Retain the woven material on every major colored stage; the exact thread pattern is less important than its low-contrast matte texture.

**Build:** Use an SVG feTurbulence texture with independent fine horizontal and vertical stitch-like lines, grayscale it and tile at roughly 560px by 750px. For the red opening, apply grayscale(1) brightness(0.64) contrast(2), opacity 0.65 and mix-blend-mode soft-light over a red radial field. For dark panels lower the texture contrast. Add broad sparse SVG scenic strokes with multiply blending at 0.16 opacity for the closing invitation.

### Task-object collage

**Use:** Capability mosaic, onboarding, feature rows and professional explanation cards.

**Observed:** Oversized phones, forms, pill bottles, calendars and mailers float on pale warm-stone or cream backing. Mostly white paper surfaces carry small colored confirmations and hand-drawn strike-throughs. The lower edge often fades into the tile background. Thin looping pencil trails sit behind the objects; imagery is larger than the text and focuses on one task.

**Transfer:** Create a simplified physical analogue of the new product's work: a document, object and completed-action marker. Minimum version is one well-drawn white paper object with a soft shadow, two readable status lines, a handwritten note and a faint background loop.

**Build:** Place a white rounded paper panel above the stone backing with a soft warm shadow. Draw a long cubic Bézier loop behind it in warm ink at about 5% opacity, using a 2px stroke and no fill. Add a small red mark, a handwritten annotation and a green completion check within the illustrated object; fade only decorative edges into the background. Do not style the whole marketing page like a messaging app.

### Photographic paper prints

**Use:** Human trust banner and educational overview opening.

**Observed:** Natural, informal headshots sit inside thick white print borders. Four photographs overlap with individually varied rotations; the smaller banner prints are roughly 171px by 203px while the overview prints are roughly 349px by 463px with name-and-role captions below.

**Transfer:** Use original team, customer or maker portraits with varied framing. If people are inappropriate, stage original product or craft photographs as the same overlapping print construction.

**Build:** Use white rectangular cards, 12px upper and side padding, 20px bottom padding, with a 0px 14px 36px rgba(42,29,24,0.16) shadow. Alternate small clockwise and counterclockwise rotations, overlap neighboring edges and keep captions inside the lower white strip.

### Annotated proof drawing

**Use:** Wine statistics and supporting research graphics.

**Observed:** Numerals sit above uneven red underlines with coral symbols; a faint globe or region outline occupies the upper-right backing without stealing contrast. No decorative chart axes surround the main proof row.

**Transfer:** Use a subdued line drawing relevant to the new product's reach or mechanism. Preserve the underline-and-symbol treatment even without a geographical concept.

**Build:** Draw a two-segment slightly uneven SVG underline beneath each value using action red and a 3px stroke. Place a large thin-line diagram behind the right side at low white opacity, clipped by the panel. Use 1px rgba(255,255,255,0.15) vertical separators between the statistics.

### Untangling line

**Only for:** educational process explanations

**Use:** A three-step explanation of complexity becoming manageable.

**Observed:** A thin red line begins as several loops on the left and gradually straightens across the width. Small white pill labels name problems around the knot. Three red dots align with explanatory columns below and are joined by fine dashed lines.

**Transfer:** Use this metaphor when the product organizes tangled work; label real difficulties and end with concrete resolutions.

**Build:** Use an SVG cubic path with several overlapping loops in its left third, one broad wave in the center and a nearly level right third. Stroke brand red at 2px with no fill; place small white bordered label pills around the loops, and three red nodes over a faint dashed horizontal guide.

**Observed:** Navigation uses 14px chevrons and small phone symbols; buttons use 16px arrows. FAQ plus/minus icons are 20px, 2px round stroke. Narrative illustrations use larger irregular hand-drawn warm-black line icons rather than filled colored badges. Social glyphs are white, 18px, centered in 40px translucent white circles.

**Transfer:** Keep utility icons crisp and restrained, with rounded 2px strokes. Use a separate lightly hand-drawn icon language for narrative concepts, not a large multicolor stock icon library.

## Writing

Warm, practical and on the reader's side. Confidence comes from describing completed work and explicit responsibilities, not futuristic superlatives.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Baba name, wordmark, bird character, customer identities, portraits, quotations, phone number, insurance coverage assertions, research claims or numerical outcomes as assets or claims for another product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your needs; use we for accountable work. Professional pages address the reader's team and its responsibilities without becoming impersonal.
- **Rhythm:** Short relief-oriented headlines, often 4–9 words; one or two compact support sentences. Operational paragraphs use lists of concrete tasks and finish with an action or result. Long questions are allowed where they match how readers actually ask.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening headline | Name the burden and offer relief rather than announce a technology. | 4–9 words | “Don't manage your healthcare alone”<br>“Follow-through after discharge” |
| Support | Begin with recognizable work, then say who handles it. | 1–3 short sentences | “We make the calls, work through the paperwork, and follow up with your doctors and insurance company to help you get the care you need.” |
| Feature title | A verb-led task with a concrete object or outcome. | 4–9 words | “Find a ride to your appointment”<br>“Explore ways to afford your medicine” |
| Professional proof | Concrete mechanism followed by a carefully bounded result and citation. | 1–2 sentences plus attribution | “Adherence is logistics before it is behavior.” |
| Action | Plain verb and direct next step. | 2–5 words | “Get started”<br>“Request a demo”<br>“Talk to our team” |
| Closing reassurance | Acknowledge the reader's burden, then explain the immediate next step. | Short headline and 1–2 sentences | “You shouldn’t have to figure this out alone.”<br>“Tell us what you need help with. We’ll check your coverage and explain what happens next.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** help; on your side; follow through; get it sorted; keeps track; next step; practical; calls; paperwork; clear plan

**Avoid:** revolutionary; disruption; frictionless ecosystem; unprecedented; magical automation; AI-first as a headline; guaranteed outcomes

**Rules:**

- Explain what actually happens after the reader clicks.
- Describe tools as enabling accountable people, not replacing judgment.
- State boundaries and qualifications beside the relevant promise, not only in legal copy.
- Use concrete verbs in task captions and questions phrased as a reader would ask them.
- Attribute external research and distinguish it from direct product performance.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use the red cloth opening with a bottom-anchored human or physical-object cutout and white 64.8px headline.
- Keep the long reading environment cream, with warm-black headings and soft-brown 16px/24px body text.
- Use 44px-high actions with 12px corners and 20px horizontal padding.
- Make the capability mosaic asymmetric on a 12-column grid with 20px gutters.
- Give proof regular-weight white numbers, coral symbols and irregular red underlines.
- Stage portraits as overlapping white prints rather than uniform circular avatars.
- Keep task artwork bigger than its 18px title and short body explanation.
- Separate chapters with about 96px vertical padding, not thick decorative rules.

### Don't

- Do not apply the scoped 56px editorial heading size to every ordinary marketing section; 48px is the default.
- Do not use pill-shaped primary CTAs; capsules belong to small tags and social controls.
- Do not turn the warm-brown text ramp into cool gray or blue-black.
- Do not make all capability tiles white elevated cards with heavy shadows; their default is flat warm stone.
- Do not spread blue or wine across the entire page; keep them as inset trust or proof stages within cream.
- Do not use Caveat for body paragraphs or primary headings; its 24px role is annotation.
- Do not make statistics semibold like headings or color the entire value coral.
- Do not replace the footer with an oversized word sculpture; its character is a modest identity block, grouped links and fine legal separation.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Buttons declare background, border, color, shadow and text-decoration transitions at 0.3s cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1).
- FAQ bodies declare grid-template-rows transitions at 0.3s ease-out.
- Document tabs declare transform transitions at 0.32s cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1); selected case content declares a 0.38s settling animation.
- Scroll narratives pair changing task illustrations with explanatory stages; navigation changes from inverted to cream-backed.

**Transfer:**

- Reserve longer scroll-led scenes for a real change in task state; always keep the explanation readable.
- Keep button motion to color and surface changes rather than making the whole page feel elastic.

**Unknown:**

- Full entrance choreography, scroll distances and all animation endpoints are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared breakpoints include 640px, 768px, 1024px and 1280px; detailed fluid heading formulas are retained with their recipes.
- Professional form uses a desktop split layout; footer desktop columns include desktop-only grouping. Narrow-screen rendered layouts are not established.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow widths, preserve readable headline-to-subject separation by moving the cutout beneath the copy; keep the red textile stage rather than discarding the signature scene.
- Collapse the capability mosaic into a single reading column while keeping one featured object scene visually larger.
- Stack proof cells and retain the red underline and coral arithmetic; replace vertical dividers with horizontal rules.
- Stack document selection before the active sheet, keeping tabs operable without horizontal overflow.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile spacings, menu behavior, portrait overlap and hero subject offsets. These transfer rules are recommendations, not measured mobile layouts.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
