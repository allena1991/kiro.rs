# Compound — people and folded light

## Overview

A people-first, technologically capable style with spacious white compositions and occasional near-black stages. Regular grotesk typography, restrained gray gradients, warm cream highlights and natural portraits make a complex service feel personal rather than institutional. Sculptural folded ribbons and polished interface presentations add depth without making the overall page busy. It suits considered purchases, expert services and products that combine human support with software.

### Essentials

- Headlines speak in two tonal layers: regular Monument Grotesk at 60px/1.1 for the opening, with 36–72px section statements and gray or gradient continuations rather than bold emphasis.
- The opening pairs a specific promise and a vertical audience list with an asymmetric portrait collage: two roughly equal columns within a 1280px container, a 600px headline limit, 26px/1.1 audience labels and compact paired actions.
- Conversion controls are small polished capsules, not oversized blocks: 41px height, 9999px radius, 9px 20px 8px padding, a dark charcoal face and a small right chevron.
- Color stays in narrow signals: a 48px cream announcement strip and one cream audience highlight against predominantly white and pale-gray sections; the dark product stage and closing card provide the major contrast.
- People and product occupy deliberate stages: staggered rectangular natural-light portraits in the opening, circular professional portraits in proof cards, and a large desktop interface on a near-black presentation stage.
- Folded light is a signature device: include a low-contrast ribbon sculpture and a 24px-rounded black conversion panel with a metallic-gray statement. Recreate the folds with curved SVG bands and restrained grayscale shading when bespoke 3D art is unavailable.

**Notes:**

- Regular weight carries the visual hierarchy; size and gray contrast do the work normally assigned to bold type. Medium 500 is reserved for questions, short editorial titles and outlined plan actions.
- Keep normal tracking throughout. Avoid mechanically tightening large text.
- A heading often begins in ink and resolves into gray, or uses a gray-to-ink text gradient. On black, use the panel-rule to caption-gray gradient for the closing statement.
- Do not introduce secondary serif or mono body typography. A logotype can retain its own identity without becoming a headline font.

- **main-container**: max width 1280px · side padding 32px. Centered default desktop wrapper; yields 1216px of usable width at 1440px.
- **editorial-inner**: max width 1280px · side padding 64px. Inset text and numeric proof within the main page rhythm.

- **resource-grid**: 3 columns · gap 24px. Three 368px-wide editorial cards within a 1152px content span.
- **faq-column**: max width 650px. Centered narrow reading column with left-aligned text.
- **people-grid** (only for people directories): 4 columns · gap 32px. 280px-wide directory cards across the main content area.
- **plans** (only for service pricing): max width 960px · 3 columns · gap 0px · side padding 32px. White pricing panel divided by fine vertical rules.

## Imagery and graphics

### Human opening collage

**Use:** Main marketing opening.

**Observed:** Three rectangular portraits form a stepped collage on the right: a tall candid woman above, a lighter smiling portrait offset right, and a smaller older person below. Natural skin tones, dark clothing and neutral interiors bring warmth without a saturated backdrop. A very faint hexagonal pattern fades into white behind them.

**Transfer:** Use three authentic users or product-in-context details at different scales. Keep uncrowded rectangular cuts and subdued lighting; if portraits are unavailable, use three documentary object or workflow photographs with the same stepped silhouette.

**Build:** Place three unrounded image planes in a roughly 500px by 661px collage. Use a tall upper-left plane, a medium right plane overlapping its lower edge and a smaller bottom-left plane. Behind them, repeat an SVG hexagon outline in rgba(0,0,0,0.03), fading to transparent toward the left and bottom.

### Folded ribbon sculpture

**Use:** Signature connective artwork and closing-stage material.

**Observed:** Broad satin-like bands twist around open space. The light version is ivory-gray and translucent against white; the dark version reads as black metal or silk with faint bright edges, crossing diagonally through the closing panel. It stays behind text rather than competing with it.

**Transfer:** Include a dimensional fold motif even without 3D resources. A restrained SVG with two shaded curved bands is the minimum acceptable version; prioritize the ribbon silhouette and soft light over texture.

**Build:** In a 1216px by 655px SVG, draw two broad curved bands sweeping from bottom-left toward top-right, with a large open lens-shaped center. Shade each band across its width using black and white stops at alpha 0.03–0.18 on black, or black at alpha 0.02–0.12 on white. Add thin white edge paths at alpha 0.08 and a softly blurred highlight near the upper fold. Clip to a 24px rounded panel and overlay a black radial vignette from transparent near center to rgba(0,0,0,0.85) at the perimeter.

### Desktop product theater

**Use:** Dedicated software chapter.

**Observed:** A large black monitor occupies most of the central width beneath a centered two-line headline. Its bright interface contains subtle pastel charts; a dark folded backdrop ties the object to the stage. Tabs and a short explanation sit below.

**Transfer:** Use the actual product in a clean dark monitor frame. Keep application colors inside the interface; for a non-software product, stage one useful artifact or output on a similarly dark material backing.

### Pale product presentation

**Only for:** focused product pages

**Use:** Centered product hero.

**Observed:** An oversized ivory twisting ribbon runs vertically behind the headline, support line and dark pill. A wide pale application panel begins in the lower third of the first screen and extends below it, softly framed in layered light borders.

**Transfer:** Show one meaningful product state, not multiple tiny feature tiles. Use pale sculptural bands to connect the headline with the interface plane.

### People portraits

**Only for:** people and expertise pages

**Use:** Expert carousels, directory cards and the team introduction.

**Observed:** Professional headshots are centered in circular gray backings. The people-page introduction instead uses a tilted wall of rectangular headshots at right, fading into a black text field on the left.

**Transfer:** Keep portrait lighting and head scale consistent. For the introductory wall, use a real team grid with a strong left-to-right black overlay rather than bright individual portrait frames.

**Build:** Arrange a repeated portrait grid, tilt it slightly, clip it inside a 16px-rounded black panel, then cover the left side with linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(0,0,0,1) 0%, rgba(0,0,0,0.94) 35%, rgba(0,0,0,0) 100%).

### Icons

**Observed:** Tiny right chevrons are 10–12px with 2px strokes and butt caps. Comparison checks use filled 24px circles. Editorial heading icons are simple monochrome 24px symbols; portrait metadata uses small location pins.

**Transfer:** Keep utility symbols black, white or gray, with no colored icon tiles. Use small chevrons as action punctuation, not large arrows.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: People-first opening

**Observed:** The first 900px shows a fixed two-tier navigation, a left promise broken over two lines, a vertical audience list, two compact actions, and the complete stepped portrait collage at right. The white canvas and faint hexagonal backing sit behind the images; text and actions stay in front with clear open space between columns. A 1280px wrapper with 32px inner sides frames two broad halves. The 60px/1.1 regular headline is limited to 600px; the list uses 26px/1.1 text in 52px rows with one cream rectangular selection. Actions sit under the list, using Primary button and Secondary button with a 12px gap. The collage is about 500px by 661px and rises above the headline. Press logos sit below the first screen, not compressed beside the actions.

**Transfer:** Open a new landing page with a specific promise, a vertical list of relevant users or outcomes and the Human opening collage. Substitute documentary product-context photos when people are unavailable. Focused product pages use a centered promise over Pale product presentation; comparison pages use a centered two-tone heading over Comparison shell; people pages use the scoped dark portrait-wall introduction.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the vertical list with an inline badge cloud, center the default opening, or put every portrait in a rounded card. Do not shrink the collage to an icon beside a large paragraph.

**Text style:** `display` — Monument Grotesk 400 · 60px/1.1

### Editorial promise and numeric proof

**Observed:** A soft-white band contains a 72px regular two-line promise with a tonal continuation. Five unboxed numeric groups spread across a 1152px span beneath it; figures use 48px/1 and labels use body text with a 4px gap.

**Transfer:** Pair a human benefit with a small set of defensible metrics; retain unboxed typography and broad spacing rather than a dashboard of colorful stat tiles.

**Text style:** `statement` — Monument Grotesk 400 · 72px/1.1

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Personal promise → tangible capabilities and people → product or comparative proof → uncertainty resolved → sculptural invitation → utility ending.

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Split audience-led opening with staggered portraits.
2. **Quantified proof** — Wide editorial statement and five unboxed metrics.
3. **Capability chapters** — Tabbed carousel with split text and product imagery.
4. **Human proof** — Expert portrait row and a separate assurance statement.
5. **Exploration** — Mixed-format editorial resource trio.
6. **Product demonstration** — Dark monitor theater with supporting tabs.
7. **Comparative proof** — Floating comparison table above pale folded art and a human-outcome reflection.
8. **Objection handling** — Narrow FAQ with one answer expanded.
9. **Closing call** — Dark sculptural panel followed by charcoal utility footer.

**Transfer:** Choose the proof chapters that fit the product; preserve the shifts between human warmth, functional proof and sculptural contrast.

### Alternative or competitive evaluation

1. **Positioning** — Centered two-tone comparison heading and concise support.
2. **Capability proof** — Feature availability table with a selected comparison option.
3. **Quantified proof** — Editable assumptions and aligned result columns with a dark total.
4. **Qualification** — Expandable disclosure row.
5. **Closing call** — Shared sculptural panel and full footer.

**Transfer:** Explain the alternative clearly, then separate qualitative availability from quantitative value.

### Focused product or service landing page

1. **Promise** — Centered hero over pale ribbon art and a large interface plane.
2. **Social proof** — Press-logo strip with faded outer edges.
3. **Offer explanation** — Short bridge copy and three service tiers.
4. **Qualification** — Disclosure inside the white offer panel.
5. **Closing call** — Product-specific invitation in the shared dark stage and utility footer.

**Transfer:** Use a shorter narrative with a large product demonstration and a clear choice of support levels.

### People and expertise directory

1. **Human promise** — Black introductory panel with a fading tilted portrait wall.
2. **Context** — Short explanation of collective expertise.
3. **Exploration** — Filters and search above a long four-column portrait grid.
4. **Closing call** — Shared sculptural invitation and charcoal footer.

**Transfer:** Let people dominate this page type; retain restrained card metadata and avoid making the directory look like a product feature grid.

## Writing

Warm, capable and direct. Confidence comes from describing the help offered and the mechanism behind it rather than from loud adjectives.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Compound names, logos, proprietary artwork, employee identities, press marks, financial claims, metrics, prices or client examples. Replace them with authorized assets and substantiated claims for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use you and your for outcomes, we and we'll for the service relationship; use the product name when describing specific capabilities.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are short statements or sentence fragments, often split into a lead and a completion. Support usually takes one short sentence; detailed explanations become compact paragraphs. Actions are brief and literal.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the service and the people or situation it serves. | 5–12 words | “Modern wealth management for”<br>“File your taxes where you manage your wealth” |
| Benefit heading | A plain helping phrase followed by a specific useful outcome. | 5–9 words | “We help you diversify your investments”<br>“Manage everything in one place” |
| Support | Explain what is combined or how access works; qualify eligibility without fanfare. | 10–25 words | “We combine the best of financial planning, investment management, and tax strategy.”<br>“Tax filing is exclusively available for [Brand] Planning clients.” |
| Action | Use a familiar verb and the concrete next step. | 2–5 words | “Talk to an advisor”<br>“Try the dashboard”<br>“Meet the team” |
| Closing statement | Frame the product as a personal, unified resource. | 4–10 words | “Your personal family office” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your; we help; personal; manage; experienced; in one place; support; learn more

**Avoid:** disruptive; revolutionary; crush it; ultimate; guaranteed; effortlessly 10x

**Rules:**

- Lead with a recognizable task or life outcome before explaining the machinery.
- Make numerical proof compact and attach a plain descriptive label.
- Use expertise terms only when they clarify a real capability, and explain the practical consequence.
- Keep limitations close to the offer rather than hiding them behind promotional copy.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening at 60px/1.1 with normal tracking and regular weight.
- Use one cream selection in the vertical audience list, echoed by the 48px announcement strip.
- Pair a 41px dark capsule with an unbordered secondary action at a 12px gap.
- Use 36px/40px benefit headings inside 28px-rounded capability cards.
- Keep editorial card grids at 24px gaps and directory grids at 32px gaps.
- Give the closing panel 24px corners, a 48px metallic heading and restrained black ribbon art.
- Keep FAQ text in a centered 650px column with 18px questions.
- Separate the closing panel and footer with 112px of breathing room.

### Don't

- Do not make all headings bold; the 400-weight grotesk is central to the tone.
- Do not turn cream into a full-screen brand background or a repeated feature-card fill.
- Do not make primary actions 56px-high rounded rectangles; the capsules are compact at 41px.
- Do not add colorful icon containers to the monochrome comparison table.
- Do not apply the 28px feature-card radius to every component; resource cards use 12px and table interiors 8px.
- Do not stage the main product interface on a vivid neon gradient; the theater is nearly black.
- Do not color every metric or wrap each one in a separate bordered tile.
- Do not turn footer utility links bright white by default; keep the gray hierarchy.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared interaction transitions use 0.15s cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) across color, opacity, background, border and related properties.
- Navigation links dim siblings to opacity 0.6 while the hovered item remains at 1.
- Secondary action hover adds a very pale gray fill; footer links brighten on hover.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the emphasis on small material and opacity changes in capsules and navigation, not dramatic button movement.
- Allow the audience highlight and sculptural scenes to remain visually coherent in a static implementation.

**Unknown:**

- Exact audience-selection timing, ribbon animation behavior and scroll-driven headline choreography are not established.
- No specific focus-visible or active treatment is established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 640px, 768px, 1024px and 1280px among other thresholds.
- Heading classes move from 36px through 48px and 52px to the 60px desktop opening scale.
- Directory grid declares two columns at the small breakpoint, three at the large breakpoint and four at the extra-large breakpoint; portrait sizes change from 90px to 130px.
- Desktop navigation is hidden below the large breakpoint; pricing declares one column before the large three-column layout.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the audience list and action hierarchy before the collage, stacking the two opening territories.
- Keep the closing fold clipped within its rounded panel and reduce side insets before reducing the central message's prominence.
- Use horizontal comparison overflow instead of squeezing text and check columns into unreadable widths.

**Unknown:**

- The narrow-screen menu, exact mobile collage placement and smallest directory presentation are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
