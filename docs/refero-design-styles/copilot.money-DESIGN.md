# Copilot Money — glossy midnight optimism

## Overview

A near-black, blue-toned world made approachable by oversized silver grotesk headlines and buoyant, candy-colored objects. Dense product interfaces sit inside carefully staged devices and rounded panels, while the surrounding marketing composition stays spacious. The feeling is polished, useful and warmly optimistic, suited to consumer software that turns complicated daily tasks into something manageable.

### Essentials

- The promise becomes a silver typographic monument: Jokker at 148px, weight 600, line height 0.9 and tracking -0.02em, centered across roughly two thirds of the opening width.
- A constellation surrounds the message instead of a conventional hero screenshot: tilted, glossy 40px-radius capsules with pictorial symbols frame the headline; the support line is 32px/1.2 and the action sits underneath.
- Actions feel like small illuminated hardware keys: a blue 56px-high inner button with 20px corners, inset highlights and a 2px darker blue outer rim; the compact navigation action is pale with 14px corners.
- Dark navy owns most of the page: #000814 canvas, #010d1e secondary bands and muted #597caa copy. Saturated colors belong to capsules, actions and product visualization; a single pale 32px-radius capability band supplies a strong tonal reversal.
- Product proof is composed, not wallpapered: use a 1200px stage, 16px grid gaps, 24px-radius feature panels and overlapping device silhouettes. When hardware is irrelevant, stage actual interface fragments with the same scale and depth.
- Every page ends with a tactile flourish: restrained link columns above an edge-to-edge pile of colorful, tilted capsules, about 80px high before rotation, with uppercase 28px labels and oversized pictorial symbols. Reinterpret the labels around the new product’s everyday outcomes.

## Typography

- **Jokker** — Marketing headlines, support copy, navigation and feature descriptions. Broad, friendly geometric grotesk; substantial lowercase forms and a single-storey a keep monumental text personable. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use weights 400, 500 and 600; preserve tight display tracking and broad line breaks. Font stack: "Jokker", "DM Sans", sans-serif.
- **Matter Variable** — Price numerals and testimonial text; lighter secondary voice rather than the main marketing font. Light, rounded sans with a delicate numerical texture. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use weight 100 for the numeric recipes; check apparent weight against surrounding copy. Font stack: "Matter Variable", "Inter", sans-serif.
- **Matter-TRIAL** — Uppercase lettering on decorative capsules. Compact, substantial uppercase sans with deliberately open tracking. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use weight 670 and 0.04em tracking. Font stack: "Matter-TRIAL", "Inter", sans-serif.

## Imagery and graphics

### Floating outcome capsules

**Use:** The opening’s framing art and the universal footer’s closing flourish.

**Observed:** Saturated, smooth, inflated lozenges carry small emoji-like dimensional objects and uppercase lettering. Around the opening they are softly defocused, tilted approximately 15–22 degrees and kept outside the central reading area; the footer is crisp, with more extreme rotations and partial clipping at the bottom edge.

**Transfer:** Use everyday outcomes relevant to the new product. The minimum is a carefully lit CSS capsule plus one high-quality pictorial symbol, not an unstyled emoji on a flat badge.

**Build:** Make unrotated lozenges roughly 200–286px wide and 80px high with the Glossy capsule recipe. Put a 48px pictorial symbol before the label. Use different palette fills and a pale top highlight with dark lower inset shading. Rotate wrapper layers independently; use negative angles around -20deg and positive angles around 15deg. For the opening, distribute around both sides of the headline and apply 2–4px blur; for the footer, align their lower edges into an overlapping 181px-high strip, vary rotation up to about 33deg and clip the strip at the page edge.

### Product devices and fragments

**Use:** Availability demonstration, capability cards, pricing and the closing product scene.

**Observed:** Dark app views are framed by realistic metal-and-glass laptop, phone and tablet silhouettes, with cool blue wallpaper visible around desktop windows. The large availability composition overlaps a phone against a laptop. The closing scene sets a laptop high on the right, tablet low toward the middle and a smaller phone between them, balancing a short text block on the left. Feature cards instead enlarge useful UI fragments until their edges are clipped.

**Transfer:** Use real views of the new product at readable scale. If no device photography exists, frame the interface in restrained rounded window chrome and retain the overlapping size hierarchy rather than inventing irrelevant hardware.

### Blue halo

**Use:** Low-contrast atmospheric support for product art.

**Observed:** Blue light is broad, dim and diffuse against the midnight background, not a visible hard-edged shape.

**Transfer:** Keep light behind the object so silver type remains the brightest neutral element.

**Build:** Place a full-stage radial ellipse behind the device layer using the Blue atmosphere surface. Its center may follow the main object; let its edges reach transparent before meeting surrounding text.

### Portrait ribbon

**Only for:** company introductions

**Use:** A human bridge between the mission statement and prose.

**Observed:** Small circular portraits are uniformly cool-blue duotone and travel in a rising diagonal ribbon across the width, with edge faces faded into the dark field. This is much lighter and more rhythmic than a conventional team grid.

**Transfer:** Use genuine portraits in consistent circular crops; preserve the diagonal, evenly spaced procession.

**Build:** Lay out circular portrait masks about 90px across in a horizontal flex ribbon with small gaps. Move the ribbon upward toward the right with an angled wrapper; counter-rotate faces to stay upright. Apply a blue duotone overlay and fade both ribbon edges with a transparent-to-opaque mask.

### Culture photo ribbon

**Only for:** recruiting introductions

**Use:** Immediately beneath the recruiting promise.

**Observed:** Full-color candid team, travel, meal and remote-collaboration images form a full-bleed, slightly overlapping row. Each has a thick 8px white rim, 16px corners and an individual playful rotation. Images differ in aspect ratio and extend past the viewport edges.

**Transfer:** Keep imperfect genuine moments and mixed framing, not uniform corporate portraits or a tiled stock-photo wall.

### Icons

**Observed:** Marketing uses simple pale pictograms, generally 24–32px, alongside larger emoji-like objects inside the decorative capsules. Product UI contains much smaller functional symbols. Blue actions carry a pale rounded-square app mark before the label.

**Transfer:** Separate functional pictograms from playful decorative symbols. Use a consistent small icon family for lists and a distinct dimensional treatment only in capsule art.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Silver promise opening

**Observed:** A 40px announcement strip and wide navigation occupy the top. Below them, the midnight field stages a centered three-line 148px silver headline across about 960px of a 1200px content stage. Back-to-front layers are blue-black atmosphere, defocused tilted capsules near the perimeter, sharp gradient lettering, then a centered 32px support line and Primary button. The large text starts around 208px down and visually fills the middle half of the screen. Within the first 900px the full headline, two-line support and blue action are visible; the capsules are the main object, not a device screenshot. Awards and the next narrative statement sit below this opening view.

**Transfer:** Write a compact outcome promise that can hold a similarly monumental three-line shape. Build custom outcome capsules when no hero media exists. Company pages instead open with a left-aligned 56px mission heading, 24px support and Light action above a Portrait ribbon; recruiting pages center the 56px heading above the Culture photo ribbon.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this opening with a small left-hand headline and a giant right-hand dashboard, or let bright capsule art cover the central letterforms.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → explanation and tangible proof → grouped capabilities or human story → trust and commitment → purposeful action → playful shared ending.

### Product marketing and conversion

1. **Promise** — Monumental silver headline framed by glossy outcome capsules.
2. **Explanation** — Centered manifesto and device-availability demonstration.
3. **Capability chapters** — Dark feature bento followed by a pale tonal-reversal chapter.
4. **Commitment** — Trial explanation, overlapping phone and transparent price.
5. **Social proof** — Varied-height testimonial field.
6. **Closing call** — Emotional outcome with multi-device scene and blue action.
7. **Navigation and brand memory** — Link columns followed by a colorful capsule pile.

**Transfer:** Use the same balance of atmospheric promise and concrete proof; adjust the number and order of capability chapters to the new story.

## Writing

Confident and reassuring without sounding institutional. Concrete conveniences carry the promise, while a little conversational warmth makes a complicated category feel friendly.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Copilot name, app mark, proprietary artwork, category labels as a branded set, testimonials, awards, financial claims, prices, user counts, employer logos or company-specific facts. Source quotations here illustrate voice only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your; use we and our when explaining the product’s care and intentions.
- **Rhythm:** Short outcome-led headlines alternate with one or two explanatory sentences. Parallel clauses and everyday verbs keep the cadence moving; longer company prose remains conversational.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the reader’s concern, then give it an emotionally desirable state. | 4–7 words | “Your money, beautifully organized.” |
| Capability heading | Pair an immediate action with its useful consequence. | 5–9 words | “Track your spending, boost your savings” |
| Feature explanation | Make the mechanism tangible, then explain the daily relief. | 1–2 short sentences | “Start your day with a quick look at your spending line, pending refunds, and upcoming bills.” |
| Action | Plain invitation with no grandiose promise. | 2–4 words | “Get started”<br>“We're hiring” |
| Closing promise | An emotional benefit stated as a concise imperative. | 3–6 words | “Gain peace of mind” |
| Culture heading | Human craft statement followed by a concrete detail. | Two short clauses | “Designed and built with care. From New York to Santiago.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** beautifully organized; confidence; a quick look; thoughtful; well-crafted; take it for a spin; peace of mind

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; unprecedented synergy; dominate; guaranteed wealth; enterprise-grade transformation

**Rules:**

- Lead with daily usefulness rather than an abstract technology claim.
- Tie automation claims to a visible task and its benefit.
- Use contractions and occasional playful metaphors sparingly.
- Let price and privacy explanations sound straightforward, not defensive.
- Keep headings in sentence case; reserve uppercase for decorative and interface labels.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main opening at 148px/0.9 with -0.02em tracking and a cool silver fill.
- Maintain the 1200px stage and 16px bento gutters at desktop.
- Use 24px panel corners and 28px top/32px side padding for feature content.
- Give blue conversion keys inset light and shade, plus the darker outer rim.
- Use #597caa for secondary copy on dark fields and #7f8ba4 on light panels.
- Preserve both the soft-focus opening capsules and the crisp, bottom-clipped footer capsule art.
- Use genuine interface fragments at useful scale instead of repeated full-screen thumbnails.
- Keep recruiting benefits unboxed with 48px column gaps.

### Don't

- Do not turn the whole palette into bright gradient section backgrounds; most of the page remains #000814.
- Do not give all actions full pill corners: the light key is 14px and the large blue face is 20px.
- Do not substitute white for the silver hero gradient or tighten every heading with negative tracking.
- Do not make the pale chapter the default page theme; it is a concentrated contrast band.
- Do not use huge black drop shadows under every card; the porcelain shadow is intentionally faint.
- Do not render decorative capsule labels as navigation or filter controls.
- Do not equalize every testimonial card height or strip away author context.
- Do not reuse the 20px/1.8 editorial paragraph recipe for compact feature descriptions; those use 16px/1.4.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Navigation color transitions are declared as 0.4s cubic-bezier(0.44, 0, 0.56, 1).
- The opening header becomes two compact glass islands in scrolled views; capsules occupy different positions and rotations across the visual sequence.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the functional navigation readable while its surrounding containers compact.
- Preserve the distinction between soft-focus opening art and a crisp decorative ending.

**Unknown:**

- Capsule trajectories, scroll interpolation, durations and other animation easings are not established.
- No focus-visible or active treatment is established for the conversion buttons.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- CSS width breakpoints cluster around 810px, 1200px and 1440px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, prioritize the silver promise and blue action; let perimeter capsules clip without obscuring the words.
- Collapse mixed-span panels into a reading-order stack and keep product fragments intentionally framed.
- Stack the closing text and device scene while preserving the relative laptop/tablet/phone size hierarchy.
- Keep footer capsule art as an edge-clipped horizontal flourish rather than wrapping it into badge rows.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, header controls and layout changes are not established; these narrow-screen rules are adaptations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
