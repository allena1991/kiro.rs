# Creative Giants — monumental type, tangible worlds

## Overview

An art-directed, image-led style that pairs enormous condensed word artwork with delicate, tightly set grotesk text. Warm ivory and absolute black form long contrasting stages, interrupted by pink and mint proof panels and richly colored documentary photography. It suits ambitious physical products, creative tools and experiential services, with sparse interfaces and a spacious editorial pace.

### Essentials

- Identity becomes architecture: a full-width condensed word sculpture occupies a 1376px × 305px field inside 32px side margins, then returns in ivory on black at the end. Use an original product name as the sculpture, even when no photographic media is available.
- The opening is a split material experience: an ivory upper field 503px high contains softly image-filled lettering, while full-bleed moving imagery below carries a 54px/1, weight 300 promise in the lower left. The first 900px shows both identity and promise rather than a conventional centered sales stack.
- Headlines feel like a single drawn line: Switzer weight 300, line-height 1 and -0.04em tracking at 54px, 64px and 84px; 16px uppercase labels sit 24px above them.
- Controls are small capsules against monumental content: the Menu pill is 86px × 46px; the 183px × 45px enquiry pill ends in a contrasting 32px circular arrow. Images and proof panels remain square-cornered.
- Color is concentrated rather than sprinkled: most of the page alternates #fffef7 and #000000; oversized proof cards pair #ffacea with #8a0467 or #a5ebd6 with #03624c. A new landing page retains at least one such bright editorial proof panel; deeper paired hues belong to case-study variants.
- Photography is evidence at bodily scale: close-up material details, people interacting with finished work, and behind-the-scenes making appear without device frames. Include a black, vertically ruled image constellation around an 84px two-line invitation; if video is unavailable use still material, user and process images.
- The ending is a designed destination: a black three-column information and action grid about 408px high resolves into the 305px-tall identity sculpture with 32px framing, not a small logo and legal strip.

**Notes:**

- Keep light display type distinct from the extremely heavy condensed identity artwork; the latter is a vector composition, not the heading font.
- Display tracking is consistently -0.04em; body tracking is less aggressive. Let long statements wrap into a considered editorial silhouette rather than reducing them to compact card headings.
- The root sizing rule is calc(0.599109rem + 0.445434vw); rem-based typography and spacing follow that rule. The listed recipes describe the 1440px desktop composition.
- Form entry text is visually much larger than its labels; use the 34px heading scale as design guidance for readable entries.

**Density:** Airy editorial pacing: deep section breathing room contrasts with tight labels and compact controls.

### Containers and grids

- **full-width editorial container**: max width 1920px · side padding 32px. Full-width sections with a shared inset; at 1440px the inner width is 1376px.
- **editorial thirds**: 3 columns · gap 32px. News, numbered process rows and the underlying modular image grid. Footer thirds are divided by 1px rules rather than gutters.
- **case-study reading column**: max width 907px · gap 64px. Centered case prose, video and large gallery images, inset substantially from the full-width hero.
- **contact split**: 2 columns · gap 12px. Asymmetric 5:7 split: invitation on the left, approximately 796px-wide form on the right at desktop.
- **contact fields**: 2 columns · gap 12px. Two equal field columns with 48px vertical gaps; the project message spans both.
- **project index** (only for project indexes): 3 columns · gap 33px. Three portrait-image columns approximately 437px wide on black.

## Imagery and graphics

### Monumental word sculpture

**Use:** The opening identity field and universal final band.

**Observed:** Extremely tall, tightly packed condensed capitals form an almost rectangular silhouette with sharp diagonal incisions and minimal counters. The opening version contains soft neutral photographic color; the final version is solid ivory. Both occupy a 1376px × 305px field at desktop.

**Transfer:** Create an original typographic silhouette for the new name. Minimum acceptable version: condensed extra-heavy lettering stretched into a coherent horizontal block, with a few intentional diagonal cuts; keep ordinary headings in the light grotesk.

**Build:** Create SVG text using a heavy condensed face such as Anton, convert to paths if available, and fit its viewBox to a roughly 4.5:1 word block. Cut a few narrow diagonal ivory channels using an SVG mask. For the opening, clip a blurred material photograph through the letter paths with a white 0.5-opacity veil; leave the surroundings ivory. For the ending fill the same paths with #fffef7 on #000000. Place inside 32px side margins.

### Tactile documentary media

**Use:** Full-bleed opening media, showcase images and editorial cards.

**Observed:** The opening gets extremely close to the textured eye and skin of a sculptural face. Other images show people meeting installations, artists working, equipment and colorful finished work. Color is environmental rather than an all-purpose brand tint; physical texture and human scale are central.

**Transfer:** Use macro product materials, people using the product and honest construction details. For digital products, stage real screens in use and tactile process imagery instead of a floating device render.

### Ruled image constellation

**Use:** The dark invitation interlude.

**Observed:** Five narrow image or video windows, approximately 215px wide and varying from 269px to 439px tall, are staggered across a black field around centered type. Faint vertical rules create six lanes; stills include faces, colorful work, workshop scenes and construction.

**Transfer:** Mix product detail, user, making and finished-result stills. Preserve unequal heights and staggered positions even without video.

**Build:** Use a six-column CSS grid inside 32px margins with 32px gutters. Add 1px vertical dividers using linear-gradient(rgba(255,255,255,0), rgba(255,255,255,0.2) 20%, rgba(255,255,255,0.2) 80%, rgba(255,255,255,0)) and difference blending. Position square-cornered image windows in separate lanes with object-fit:cover and varied vertical offsets. Place a centered two-line 84px invitation above the grid layer with enough black space to remain readable.

### Case-study image essay

**Only for:** case studies

**Use:** Document the result, audience and making process.

**Observed:** A full-bleed 1440px × 720px hero leads into centered 907px-wide images and a real video poster. Large 907px × 604px frames are separated by 64px. The final montage uses tall outer images, stacked landscapes and narrow portraits across the full inset width. One story uses cyan-lit immersive interiors; another uses detailed painting, gallery visitors and workshop scenes.

**Transfer:** Let the specific outcome determine the photographic palette. Include wide context, close material detail and people for scale; preserve the mixed-size montage.

### Inline portrait statement

**Only for:** people-focused process explanations

**Use:** A centered editorial statement interrupted by small photographic inserts.

**Observed:** Light black grotesk lines on ivory are punctuated by small rectangular portraits placed inline like visual nouns. Images are not circular avatars and remain subordinate to the oversized sentence.

**Transfer:** Use a small set of original people or process-detail images within a concise statement; keep the text readable as one sentence.

**Build:** Set an approximately 64px centered sentence in a broad text block. Insert inline-block rectangular images about 90px wide and 72px high, vertically aligned to the text baseline, with object-fit:cover. Use no border or corner rounding.

### Icons

**Observed:** Sparse arrows dominate: 12px northeast arrows in footer rows, 14px action arrows, and 22px horizontal carousel arrows. Larger quotation marks punctuate proof panels; social marks are small and monochrome.

**Transfer:** Use simple monochrome SVG arrows with slender visible lines, no colored icon containers except the action's ivory disc. Keep decorative pictograms out of the main content hierarchy.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Identity and material opening

**Observed:** The default first screen is layered: edge-to-edge material video at the back; an ivory upper mask containing the word sculpture; frosted media visible through the letters; a fixed sparse header above it all; and ivory promise text over the image below. The upper identity field is 503px high with 160px top inset and 32px side margins. Its word artwork spans nearly the entire width and stands 305px tall. Beneath it the close-up image fills the remaining first-screen width. The lower-left text block is about 645px wide: a 16px uppercase locator followed after 24px by a two-line 54px promise. Within 900px, the header, entire word sculpture, material detail and complete promise are visible. The hero continues below the fold; explanatory content starts later. Menu is the only first-screen button; there is no sales-button cluster.

**Transfer:** Use the new name as original word artwork and a meaningful macro material or in-use scene underneath. If no video exists, use one high-resolution tactile still with soft photographic color inside the letters. Process-led pages instead open with a left-aligned 54px statement, a narrow right support column and an asymmetric making-image strip. Case-study pages open with the colored title-and-metadata bands; indexes use a centered 84px title on black above photography.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the word sculpture with an ordinary headline, put the whole opening on a gradient, or push the promise and imagery beneath an oversized navigation bar.

**Text style:** `hero-promise` — Switzer 300 · 54px/1 · tracking -0.04em

### Editorial explanation and proof numerals

**Observed:** An uppercase marker leads a 64px multi-line statement by 24px. The statement spans about two thirds of the canvas; a gray 18px paragraph is indented beneath it after 64px. Three open stat groups use 54px numerals with small explanatory copy, no enclosing panels.

**Transfer:** Make a substantial plain-language statement, then support it with fewer, more meaningful quantities. Keep the indented relationship between proposition and explanation.

**Text style:** `manifesto` — Switzer 300 · 64px/1 · tracking -0.04em

### Dark project showcase

Four edge-clipped capability streams use 64px type, 89.6px line-height and bullet separators, with 80px breathing room before the standard enquiry section and ending.

**Transfer:** Use concrete making or delivery chapters and terse capability names. Let streams communicate breadth without turning every term into a card.

**Text style:** `capability-ticker` — Switzer 300 · 64px/1.4 · tracking -0.005em

## Page flow

**Storyline:** identity and promise → editorial explanation → tangible demonstration → human or quantified proof → invitation → monumental identity ending

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Word sculpture, tactile full-bleed media and a short lower-left promise.
2. **Explanation** — Large editorial statement, indented prose and three open proof numerals.
3. **Demonstration** — Scroll-led dark outcome showcase and a ruled image constellation invitation.
4. **Social proof** — Bright oversized quote rail followed by photographic editorial updates.
5. **Conversion and closure** — Open enquiry form followed by the black information grid and enormous identity artwork.

**Transfer:** Borrow the shifts in scale and material; adapt the selection and order of proof to the new product.

### Sensory, image-led case study

1. **Outcome** — Dark teal title, inverted ice metadata band and cyan-lit full-width photography.
2. **Explanation** — Prompt-and-paragraph dossier in a centered reading column.
3. **Demonstration** — Video and large contextual photographs, then a mixed-proportion montage.
4. **Continuation and closure** — Warm related-story band directly into the monumental black footer.

**Transfer:** Use one outcome's own visual atmosphere and explain the work clearly before the image essay.

### Outcome-led case study with measurable impact

1. **Outcome** — Dark green title and pale green metadata over full-width result photography.
2. **Explanation** — Concise dossier of motivation and delivery.
3. **Demonstration** — Poster-led video and large image details.
4. **Quantified proof** — Three unboxed numerals with short captions, then making and audience photographs.
5. **Continuation and closure** — Cool related-story bridge directly into the common black ending.

**Transfer:** Add measured outcomes only when meaningful and substantiated; retain the visual essay.

### Visual catalogue of outcomes

### Explain a method and reduce delivery uncertainty

1. **Promise** — Left editorial statement, right support and an asymmetric making-image strip.
2. **Human context** — Centered sentence with inline rectangular portraits.
3. **Capability chapters** — Numbered rows with a process image and detailed paragraph.
4. **Breadth** — Four clipped capability ticker lines.
5. **Conversion and closure** — The shared enquiry form and monumental black ending.

**Transfer:** Explain a believable workflow in concrete stages and support it with making images rather than generic diagrams.

## Writing

Confident, practical and warmly ambitious. The language connects creative possibility to concrete making and invites a conversation rather than pushing a hard sell.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the original identity artwork, company or collaborator names, addresses, project names, client claims, metrics or testimonial wording. Source examples illustrate cadence only; substitute the new product's truthful content and original assets.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use we for the maker and you or your for the reader. Headlines can use inclusive let's; explanatory case prose may speak impersonally about the work.
- **Rhythm:** Short invitation headlines contrast with long, flowing manifesto sentences. Supporting paragraphs are plainspoken and specific; uppercase is a visual label treatment rather than a shouting voice.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A compact list of disciplines ending in a phrase that opens possibility. | 7–12 words | “Production, design, and the art of the possible.” |
| Editorial statement | Name the people or practices behind the outcome, then explain what they make possible. | 25–40 words | “We are artists, designers, producers, creatives, clients and technicians. We work together to create immersive experiences and bring ideas to life in real-world spaces.” |
| Section invitation | Direct, conversational invitation with a short imperative or inclusive phrase. | 3–6 words | “Let’s make things happen.”<br>“Speak to us.” |
| Supporting line | Lower the barrier to contact by giving several natural reasons to start a conversation. | 12–25 words | “If you want to start a project, share an idea or simply say hi,” |
| Primary action | A practical verb plus a specific object. | 2–3 words | “send enquiry”<br>“View project” |
| Method heading | A clear chapter name, with occasional lightly irreverent parenthetical language in section-level headings. | 1–9 words | “The Play Book (it’s not rocket science)”<br>“Creative and technical development” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** make; bring to life; ideas; real-world; together; possibilities; discovery; impact; care

**Avoid:** revolutionary platform; seamless ecosystem; 10x everything; limited-time offer; unlock synergy

**Rules:**

- Tie ambitious statements to material examples or a clear description of the work.
- Keep actions conversational and concrete, with no urgency gimmicks.
- Use compact uppercase section markers to orient the reader; let the main statement carry the personality.
- Explain quantities with short descriptive captions rather than presenting numbers as self-evident proof.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the 32px desktop edge alignment consistent across identity, prose, media and footer.
- Set the principal grotesk display recipes at weight 300 with -0.04em tracking and line-height 1.
- Give editorial sections 112px top and bottom breathing room and use 64px for major internal separation.
- Retain the extreme contrast between the 305px identity artwork and 16px uppercase markers.
- Use square corners for images and proof panels, reserving 9999px rounding for controls.
- Let a bright proof rail reveal part of its next 813px-wide panel.
- Preserve the 45px icon-ended action capsule and its contrasting 32px arrow disc.
- End with practical information above a full-width identity sculpture, not instead of it.

### Don't

- Do not use Switzer bold for normal headings; the heavy identity artwork is a separate visual register.
- Do not spread proof pink and mint through every feature row or make them the default page background.
- Do not place documentary imagery inside rounded browser mockups or shaded floating cards.
- Do not center every paragraph; the default prose is left-aligned and often indented relative to the headline.
- Do not turn the 54px proof numerals into dense dashboard widgets with borders and chart chrome.
- Do not enlarge the compact Menu control into a permanent full-width navigation bar.
- Do not add drop shadows to the editorial panels; only the Menu uses a fine ring-like shadow.
- Do not apply the 114.1px case-title treatment or complementary title bands to an ordinary landing-page opening.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The invitation has a sticky centered text layer while narrow image windows pass through surrounding lanes; the project showcase progresses through numbered results.
- Declared button text transitions use color 0.3s; carousel controls use opacity 0.3s.
- Declared menu background, height and width transitions use 0.375s cubic-bezier(0.625,0.05,0,1).
- Capability marquee animations are declared at 70s linear.

**Transfer:**

- Keep motion tied to the large editorial stages: stable central text against passing documentary media, and slow horizontal capability streams.
- Preserve the composition using still images when motion is unavailable; motion must not be the only way to understand the outcome.

**Unknown:**

- Scroll progress mappings, exact parallax distances and complete menu open/close choreography are not specified.
- Reduced-motion behavior is not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints are 479px, 767px, 768px, 991px, 1440px and 1920px.
- Root sizing uses calc(0.599109rem + 0.445434vw); rem-based measures scale with it. Some process numbers and the index view switch are marked to hide on tablet.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the word sculpture as a fitted vector block, then retain a legible promise over the material image.
- Stack the enquiry invitation above the form and the process number/image/text sequence into a readable chapter; keep square imagery and fine dividers.
- Reduce the constellation's simultaneous image count rather than reducing its invitation to tiny type; keep the black stage and documentary fragments.
- Stack footer information zones while retaining the enormous full-width closing identity.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile typography, gutter sizes, ordering and breakpoint-specific column behavior are not established; the narrow-screen transfers are design guidance.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
