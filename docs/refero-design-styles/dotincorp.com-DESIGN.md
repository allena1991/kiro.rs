# Dot — tactile optimism, engineered

## Overview

A bright, approachable technology style that makes sophisticated hardware feel useful and human. Geometric sans-serif typography, soft grey fields and white rounded panels frame carefully lit product renders and everyday use photography. Orange supplies small bursts of confidence, while black cinematic demonstrations and an expansive black ending add weight to an otherwise airy page.

### Essentials

- The first promise is a panoramic product label, not a wall of sales copy: a 330px-high photographic banner with 30px corners, centered white 60px/1.5 weight-600 title and 15px/1.5 support; a separate image-and-explanation block follows.
- Headlines are rounded, sturdy and uncompressed: Plus Jakarta Sans, normally 46px/1.5 at 700 with normal tracking; use orange on a meaningful phrase rather than coloring the entire headline.
- Actions are compact lozenges beside expansive imagery: default orange buttons are 44px high, 30px radius, 12px 24px padding and 16px weight-600 text; secondary actions are grey, while the black closing uses 270px × 54px white-outline pills.
- A neutral technical gallery carries the story: #f8f8f8 canvas, white and #f5f5f5 cards, 1px grey edges, 20px–30px corners, 1200px content width and 80px chapter gaps. Orange stays chiefly in labels, actions and selected words; full orange bands belong to subscription modules.
- Features have physical layering rather than a flat tile treatment: a 580px-wide grey illustrated panel is paired with a narrower 480px white text panel, inset 50px each side and overlapping by 30px. Retain this layered device even when the illustration is a simple line drawing.
- The ending is a second visual event: a black 509px contact stage with an object breaking its top edge, two contact lanes, then a 50px/1.5 weight-800 mission line and an extended black social/footer area. Use a clean product silhouette or a composed abstract object if no hero-quality render is available.

## Imagery and graphics

### Panoramic product environment

**Use:** Default opening banner.

**Observed:** A wide, shallow photograph places the product on the right of a desk, with defocused furniture, books or plants elsewhere. Dark exposure behind the centered white title protects readability. The photograph occupies the full banner, with 30px clipping and no separate text card.

### Sculptural product study

**Use:** Split introductions, anatomy and conversion sections.

**Observed:** Dark hardware floats at a three-quarter angle above a pale plinth, backed by broad peach, cream and cyan light planes; another study shows a frontal device against soft window light. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Identification → product explanation → demonstration → audience or application chapters → technical and human proof → practical objections → conversion → staged contact invitation and visual footer.

### Flagship product marketing with detailed technical confidence

1. **Identification** — Shallow environmental panorama and centered product name.
2. **Product explanation** — Square object study beside prose and compact action row.
3. **Demonstration** — Low-key full-width product video.
4. **Audience and capabilities** — Miniature audience worlds followed by layered illustrated feature cards.
5. **Technical proof** — Large annotated product study and unboxed specifications.
6. **Human proof and objections** — Testimonials, independent video demonstration and narrow FAQ.
7. **Conversion** — Product image beside ordering details and paired actions.
8. **Closing invitation** — Overlapping object on a black contact stage, then extended mission/social footer.

**Transfer:** Use for a product whose form, mechanisms and compatibility need explanation; combine or shorten chapters while preserving the layered features and staged ending.

### Application-led product marketing centered on everyday outcomes

1. **Identification** — Shared panorama followed by an oversized operating-hand product scene and benefit-led text.
2. **Problem framing** — Centered two-tone heading, prose and an explanatory scale graphic.
3. **Application chapters** — Outcome heading, three benefits and task photography, followed by further environments in paired panels.
4. **Technical proof** — Annotated dark product stage and a three-column features list.
5. **Credibility** — Two partner cards and a collaboration explanation.
6. **Conversion** — Isolated product and accessories beside a readiness question and sales/demo actions.
7. **Closing invitation** — Shared black contact stage and full mission/social footer.

**Transfer:** Use when the same product improves several environments. Keep application photographs more prominent than specification decoration.

### Company storytelling and institutional trust

1. **Identity** — Abstract sphere panorama with a concise belief.
2. **Human impact** — Heavy orange/black statement, testimonial portrait and three tall use-case images.
3. **Principles** — Vision prose over a faint dot field and overlapping dimensional diagram panel.
4. **Track record** — Solid years, outlined months and strongly ruled milestone rows.
5. **Credibility** — Monochrome partner strip.
6. **Closing invitation** — Shared black contact stage followed by the same visual footer.

**Transfer:** Lead with lived outcomes before principles or chronology; reserve outlined figures and diagram circles for this editorial mode.

### Portfolio discovery and broad brand introduction

1. **Campaign promise** — Immersive environment photograph with lower-left white title and outlined actions under floating navigation.
2. **Brand explanation** — Broad introductory statement followed by a concentrated colorful technology story.
3. **Product discovery** — Large object studies, award proof and a black cinematic interlude.
4. **Capability exploration** — Pill tabs, photographic cards and a layered dimensional research gallery.

## Writing

Warm, earnest and benefit-led, with the confidence of an engineering company explaining useful progress. Technical facts support independence, ease and practical access rather than spectacle alone.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand or product names, logos, proprietary device imagery, award marks, partner identities, customer names or quotations, contact details, patents, market statistics, numerical performance claims or superlatives without the new project's own verified equivalents. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Product descriptions generally name the product in third person; action prompts address the reader directly. Company passages use we and our, while outcome headlines favor people and practical situations.
- **Rhythm:** Short noun or outcome headlines alternate with one-line support and fuller explanatory paragraphs. Two-part headings use a period or a color shift. Actions are concise verbs; questions soften the conversion and closing sections.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening title | Simple product identifier, supported by a sentence defining input, action and outcome. | 2–4 words for the title; one support sentence | “[Brand] Pad X”<br>“A multiline Braille display that converts images, PDFs and graphs into touchable content in real time.” |
| Outcome headline | Two compact clauses connect a practical improvement to a human benefit. | 4–10 words | “Safer Medication. Independent Patients.”<br>“Inclusion Without Barriers. Braille Labelling on Demand.” |
| Feature label | Name the capability in plain language before explaining the mechanism. | 2–6 words | “Seamless Device Integration”<br>“Zero Learning Curve” |
| Primary action | Direct verb plus destination or service. | 2–5 words | “Find a Reseller”<br>“Request a Demo” |
| Human proof heading | Balanced repetition links experience to outcome. | 4–7 words | “Real Stories, Real Impact” |
| Closing invitation | A friendly practical question with a short reassuring response. | 3–8 words per line | “Have any questions?”<br>“We'll get back to you right away.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** experience; independent; seamless; intuitive; on-demand; real-time; empower; connect; explore; for everyone

**Avoid:** disrupt or die; crush the competition; magic with no explanation; exclusive for the elite; hustle; jokey disability metaphors

**Rules:**

- Explain one concrete task or mechanism beneath every broad benefit claim.
- Use inclusive, capable descriptions of people rather than pity or rescue language.
- Keep specifications factual and scannable; put narrative persuasion in the surrounding chapters.
- Favor direct sales and demonstration labels over playful or cryptic CTA language.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 60px weight-600 white type for the shallow identifying banner and 46px weight-700 type for ordinary chapters.
- Retain 24px outer gutters even for near-full-width media.
- Give broad paired cards 32px gaps and layered features 40px gaps.
- Pair a grey illustration backing with a narrower white description overlapping by 30px.
- Keep body copy at 20px/1.8 and dense card answers at 16px/1.5.
- Use the full-width grey capsule to announce technical chapters.
- Make the black ending substantial: two contact lanes, a 50px mission line and a visual social area.
- Use orange in short phrases, labels and compact actions; reserve full-color surfaces for specifically scoped modules.

### Don't

- Do not tighten all heading tracking to a fashionable negative value; normal tracking is fundamental here.
- Do not substitute square 4px cards for the 20px and 30px rounded gallery language.
- Do not draw a visible orange outline around the default grey secondary CTA; it is a filled grey control.
- Do not give every card a deep shadow; most depth comes from hairlines and overlap.
- Do not set feature explanations as tiny 12px interface copy; the technical reading scale is 20px.
- Do not color every section title orange; keep the primary phrase black and the emphasized outcome selective.
- Do not replace the full-width chapter capsule with a small floating tab.
- Do not spread corporate pastel gradients into the ordinary audience and feature cards.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- FAQ surfaces declare a 0.3s transition.
- Some panorama artwork declares a 12s linear infinite animation.
- Language controls declare an orange hover border and a 4px orange glow; the circular subscription action declares a white 8% hover fill and translateY(-1px).

**Transfer:**

- Keep the moving panorama treatment confined to broad visual storytelling rather than animating every reading panel.
- Use the declared language-control glow as a compact utility cue; keep the larger product stages visually calm.

**Unknown:**

- Scroll animation timing, media playback behavior and focus-visible states are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles include width breakpoints around 480px, 640px, 750px, 768px, 1024px, 1280px, 1440px, 1536px and 1920px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, stack the image/text splits and paired cards while retaining the illustration/text overlap with proportionally smaller side insets.
- Keep the panoramic title readable by reframing the product toward an edge; do not squeeze the desktop support line into unreadably small text.
- Stack the two closing contact lanes and retain the overlapping object and mission line as the ending's main landmarks.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, navigation transformation and per-breakpoint spacing are not established; the narrow-layout rules here are design recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
