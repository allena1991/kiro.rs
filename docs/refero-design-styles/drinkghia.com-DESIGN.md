# Ghia — sunlit aperitivo theatre

## Overview

A convivial, slightly surreal editorial style for tactile products and everyday rituals. Warm cream and wine-colored type meet electric pink, Mediterranean blues and a deep olive-green finish. Tall condensed serif capitals, small friendly sans-serif copy, sunlit still lifes and glossy black product stages make the pages feel like a playful boutique rather than a conventional store; imagery is expansive while supporting information stays compact.

### Essentials

- Headlines feel like vintage café posters: FHA Condensed French NC at 92px/1 for the opening, 64px/0.95 for feature chapters and 48px/1.04 for shop headings, all regular-weight capitals with normal tracking.
- The first impression is a photographed scene, not a boxed card: a 1440px-wide, 691px-tall still life with a tall object on the right, a two-line cream headline on the left and a 180px × 42px pink action. Use a photographed object on warm textured cloth when bespoke campaign media is unavailable.
- Conversion controls are small rectangular tickets: 2px corners, 14px uppercase sans-serif labels, 42px height and 32px side padding; reserve capsule shapes for selectors rather than primary actions.
- Color arrives in theatrical blocks: cream carries browsing and explanatory content, black belongs to glossy product stages, blue fills a complete editorial chapter, and olive green with pale blue owns the closing area. Pink stays concentrated in actions, stars and small marks rather than the general canvas.
- Products look collectible: 320px square black-backed portraits use reflections and star-like highlights, contrasted with close, sunlit photographs of glass, fabric, hands and liquid. Carry both photographic modes into a new page; a carefully lit single object is the minimum black-stage treatment.
- Graphic punctuation is part of the identity: include a 56px wine-colored benefits ticker with 28px serif capitals and diamond separators, notched yellow labels, a sculptural badge or layered color-band composition, and a monumental pale-blue closing wordmark. Replace the lettering and symbols with the new product's own, retaining their scale and handmade geometry.

## Typography

- **FHA Condensed French NC** — Display headings, product names, numerical statements and ticker. Very narrow high-contrast serif with sharp flaring terminals and a vintage French poster character. If unavailable, use **Bodoni Moda SC**. Use regular capitals and condensed presentation; preserve tall letterforms rather than increasing weight. Font stack: "FHA Condensed French NC", "Bodoni Moda SC", serif.
- **Vivey 22 Positive** — Body copy, navigation, captions and controls. Friendly rounded geometric sans-serif with open counters and a casual editorial rhythm. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use weight 400 throughout most text; avoid heavy geometric headlines. Font stack: "Vivey 22 Positive", "DM Sans", sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- Use regular weight rather than bold to get the characteristic serif silhouette. The narrow letterforms, uppercase setting and tight line height provide emphasis without negative tracking.
- Keep the sans-serif subordinate: most support is 14px/20px even beneath 64px titles; oversized category words sit beside 15px uppercase descriptors only 132px wide.
- The giant closing lettering is artwork rather than another heading recipe. It spans 1344px × 569px at desktop; preserve its broad sweeping silhouette.

- **main-container**: max width 1440px · side padding 56px. Centered editorial and shop content with 1328px usable desktop width.
- **product-grid**: 4 columns · gap 16px. Four 320px square-media cards across; metadata is unboxed below each image.

- **faq-container** (only for detail and membership pages): max width 896px · side padding 56px. Centered 784px question column.
- **configurator** (only for bundle configurators): max width 1280px · 2 columns · gap 64px · side padding 56px. A wider 1.6fr option field beside a 1fr sticky summary; options form two visual columns.

## Imagery and graphics

### Sunlit tactile still life

**Use:** Opening and immersive lifestyle chapters.

**Observed:** A red glass bottle and clear sculptural glass rest on rippled beige cloth, seen close and slightly from above. Directional sunlight reveals fabric grain, liquid color and long shadows. Object occupies the right half, while low-detail left cloth holds cream type. Other scenes show hands, ice, fruit and condensation at an intimate scale.

**Transfer:** Photograph the new object on warm textured cloth with one tactile accessory and strong side light. Keep genuine material detail and enough calm texture for type; do not turn the scene into a clean floating render.

### Glossy black collectible portraits

**Use:** Product cards, product-detail opening and dark how-to demonstration.

**Observed:** Bottles, cans and designed objects stand or stack against deep black, grounded by mirror-like reflections. Strong rim lighting and small starbursts emphasize metal and glass; packaging colors stay saturated. Product fills much of each square, with headroom for a small flag.

**Transfer:** Use a black sweep and glossy floor, photograph a single object or precarious balanced stack, then add restrained highlight rays only where light catches edges. Minimum version is a crisply rim-lit object with a subtle reflection.

**Build:** For added sparkle, layer an SVG four-point star over an actual highlight: two narrow perpendicular tapered rays in cream at low opacity, with a tiny white center and a soft radial alpha glow. Keep rays local to metallic edges, not scattered as a background pattern.

### Sculptural offer plaques

**Use:** Graphic punctuation over paired full-bleed lifestyle images.

**Observed:** Two 720px-wide photo panels carry centered white 536px plaques: one is two joined horizontal ovals; the other joins an angular hexagonal upper lobe to an oval lower lobe. Wine headings, ruled price rows, small product thumbnails and citron flags sit inside.

**Transfer:** Use the same unexpected cut-paper silhouette for a focused offer or proof statement, simplifying contents if necessary. Carry at least one sculptural badge or layered-band graphic into an ordinary landing page.

**Build:** Draw an SVG with a 536 × 536 viewBox. Union two wide ellipses meeting at a narrow waist; for the angular variant replace the top ellipse with a six-point polygon. Fill white with no stroke or shadow. Place HTML content centered above it, with fine wine rules dividing the offer and inclusions.

### Overlapping variant bands

**Use:** A rich alternative to ordinary option cards.

**Observed:** A 648px-wide, 684px-tall stack overlaps 220px color panels so most show only a shallow upper strip. Large centered serif names and upright cropped cans share each strip; small repeated line-art ingredient motifs run behind. Colors step through cobalt, herb green, tomato, citron and wine.

**Transfer:** Stage product variants or capabilities as layered poster strips with a central object or simple dimensional silhouette, retaining the large labels and color collisions.

**Build:** Use a relative overflow-hidden container with 10px corners. Stack five absolutely positioned 220px panels with ascending z-index and roughly 116px visible steps; clip each next panel over the previous. Give upper corners 10px radius, set labels with band-display and center a narrow product cutout under the title. Add repeated simple SVG circles or subject-related line motifs near each panel's lower edge.

### Human proof portraits

**Use:** Review rows and small proof previews.

**Observed:** Vertical domestic face-to-camera videos show real people holding the product in ordinary rooms. They remain natural, without duotone grading or elaborate frames; white circular play controls are centered.

**Transfer:** Use candid portrait customer demonstrations with visible product interaction, not corporate headshots.

### Monumental identity and eye seal

**Use:** Every closing area.

**Observed:** A small outlined eye in pale blue crowns the olive field on a raised semicircular tab. Beneath newsletter and links, enormous sweeping italic identity lettering spans nearly the entire width, 1344px × 569px, with tiny legal links underneath.

**Transfer:** Create an original sweeping wordmark for the new name, or a large italic typographic signature if custom lettering is unavailable. Retain both the boundary seal and oversized closing artwork.

**Build:** Render original lettering as a single-color SVG with a tight viewBox, width 100% inside 48px desktop gutters. For the seal, place an olive semicircular cap centered over the section boundary; draw a pale-blue almond outline from two curved SVG paths, add a circular iris and a small off-center light mark. Keep the seal simple, thin-lined and symmetrical.

### Punchcard reward illustration

**Only for:** membership pages

**Use:** Explain recurring rewards in one playful illustration.

**Observed:** Cream artwork pairs a large condensed headline on the left with a slightly tilted wine card on the right. Eight pink circles form two rows of four, holding cream gift, percentage, currency and hourglass-like symbols; hand-drawn wine arrows connect uppercase annotations outside the card.

**Transfer:** Translate a real membership journey into a small set of reward symbols, keeping the informal diagram rather than making a software dashboard.

**Build:** Create an SVG with a rounded wine rectangle rotated a few degrees counterclockwise, eight evenly spaced pink circles and simple cream silhouettes. Draw irregular curved arrow paths around the perimeter with rounded ends; place short sans-serif uppercase captions at their tails. Use the cream canvas as the backing.

### Icons

**Observed:** Utility icons are compact and monochrome: 12–14px outlined arrows and pluses with approximately 1.4–1.5px round strokes; stars are filled at 14px, or 20px in detailed reviews. Account and bag are more solid pictograms. Footer social marks sit in 30px pale-blue disks.

**Transfer:** Use crisp small SVG controls in wine or cream, not a multicolor icon library. Allow larger flat playful symbols only in editorial diagrams and category links.

**Transfer:** Turn a short set of choices, ingredients or capabilities into an expressive text index rather than more cards.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Atmospheric invitation → browse or configure → tactile story and distinctive graphic punctuation → human or factual proof → reassurance → newsletter and monumental identity.

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Photographic campaign opening and benefits ticker.
2. **Selection** — Four-card shop rail.
3. **Editorial depth** — Blue object feature and layered variant theatre.
4. **Social proof** — Centered manifesto with alternating portraits and quotes.
5. **Offer comparison** — Paired sculptural offers and a further product rail.
6. **Exploration** — Large-word category index.
7. **Closing invitation** — Olive newsletter, link columns and monumental lettering.

**Transfer:** Alternate immersive material scenes with compact browsing chapters; preserve the signature devices without treating the entire sequence as fixed.

### Guided bundle configuration

1. **Task framing** — Centered title and introduction on a wine field.

### Recurring-membership persuasion

## Writing

Warm, sociable and sensory, with a dry wink. The tone celebrates an experience rather than lecturing about a problem, while purchase details stay unusually direct.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Ghia's name, logos, named ranges, proprietary artwork, customer quotations, ratings, savings, ingredient facts or health and alcohol claims. Write equivalent original copy grounded in the new product's real attributes. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to 'you' for choices and practical guidance; use 'we' for the maker's friendly invitations. Product descriptions can be short, confident fragments.
- **Rhythm:** Short poster headlines, often 3–8 words, followed by one or two conversational sentences. A rhythmic three-beat line can frame a practical offer; sensory fragments sit comfortably beside plain action verbs.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening headline | A clear product moment in emphatic poster capitals. | 5–9 words | “LE FIZZ IS FINALLY BACK IN STOCK” |
| Sensory support | Specific tastes or textures followed by a casual use cue. | 6–16 words | “Vibrant, fresh, and satisfyingly bitter. Just add bubbles.” |
| Primary action | One plain verb, sometimes with a possessive invitation. | 2–3 words | “Shop now”<br>“Build yours” |
| Membership promise | Three short verbs with a rhythmic full-stop beat. | 3 words | “Sip. Save. Repeat.” |
| Closing invitation | A witty contrast that turns a category truth into a signup benefit. | 8–12 words | “We’re booze free, but our emails are intoxicating” |
| Practical reassurance | A direct human question, answered without promotional fog. | 3–14 words | “Can I cancel?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** bright; ritual; together; your favorite; unfussy; extras; on your terms; made to be seen and used

**Avoid:** revolutionary solution; optimize your lifestyle; guilt-free perfection; disruptive platform; clinically superior

**Rules:**

- Anchor sensory language in an actual material, taste, action or setting.
- Give actions plain verbs even when the surrounding headline is playful.
- Explain savings, delivery and flexibility in direct language rather than burying them in whimsy.
- Use small uppercase captions as editorial asides, not long promotional paragraphs.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the opening headline at 92px/1 and normal tracking, with a materially rich image holding the right half.
- Use 64px/0.95 serif titles above 14px/20px support for strong poster-to-caption contrast.
- Keep primary actions rectangular at 42px height with 2px corners.
- Use the 56px desktop gutters consistently through rails and editorial chapters.
- Keep product media square and black-backed, with 16px gaps across a four-column rail.
- Introduce citron notched flags at 25px height rather than generic rounded badges.
- Carry graphic punctuation into each new landing page through sculptural plaques or layered bands.
- Make the olive and pale-blue closing identity a substantial chapter, including newsletter, seal and oversized original lettering.

### Don't

- Do not replace the narrow serif with a bold wide grotesk; regular-weight tall capitals are essential.
- Do not spread pink across the whole page; keep it concentrated in actions, small marks and reward artwork.
- Do not make every corner 16px or every button a capsule; the common commerce radius is 2px.
- Do not add generic floating shadows to the product grid; depth comes from photographic reflections.
- Do not substitute pale-gray cutout tiles for the glossy black product stages.
- Do not expand all supporting text to 18–20px; the editorial contrast depends on compact 14px copy.
- Do not make utility iconography oversized or multicolored; arrows and pluses are mostly 12–14px monochrome.
- Do not apply the wine configurator background or punchcard diagram to unrelated page types.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Primary actions declare all-property transitions at 0.15s cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1); many text and social links use opacity transitions at 0.2s with the same easing.
- Links commonly declare hover opacity 0.6. Outlined product-detail actions invert to wine fill and cream type; light outline actions invert to cream fill and wine type.
- The ticker and testimonial tracks loop horizontally; a Pause/Play control accompanies testimonial motion. CSS declares marquee-mobile at 40s linear infinite.
- Layered bands declare background-color 220ms, color 220ms with 40ms delay, and height 350ms cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1).

**Transfer:**

- Keep motion concentrated in horizontal proof/ticker movement, layered variant reveals and the navigation's change of material.
- Preserve the pause affordance for the moving review row and the high-contrast two-ring keyboard focus treatment.

**Unknown:**

- Exact desktop marquee speed, autoplay schedule and complete scroll choreography are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare many width breakpoints, including 480px, 768px, 1024px, 1280px and 1440px.
- Variant label size follows min(84px, 5.8vw). Desktop editorial splits use two columns; declarations indicate narrower layouts reorder editorial media and configurator summary.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, preserve object scale and text legibility by stacking the split rather than compressing both halves.
- Let shop rails remain horizontally browsable; keep notched flags and black-stage imagery recognizable.
- Scale the closing SVG to the available width and reflow newsletter and links above it, retaining its monumental role.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile viewport renderings, precise mobile gutters, hero framing and exact sticky-control behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
