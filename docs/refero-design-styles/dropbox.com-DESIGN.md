# Dropbox — content in motion

## Overview

A practical, personable technology style with warm off-white canvases, brown-black chapters and concentrated electric-blue actions. Broad grotesk headings sit above plainspoken sans-serif explanations, while real creative work and floating interface fragments make software feel tangible. The pacing is spacious and demonstration-led, with denser cards and tables reserved for decision-making.

### Essentials

- Headlines sound conversational rather than monumental: 40px/1.2 medium grotesk for the opening, 32px/1.2 regular wide grotesk for section introductions, normal tracking throughout.
- The opening gives the promise and the software equal presence: a 735px-tall warm canvas, 48px text inset, approximately 42% text and 58% media, with a focused floating control over softened context.
- Actions are blue rounded rectangles, not pills: the main action is 72px tall with 24px padding, a 2px border, 16px corners and a right arrow separated from the label by 24px.
- Color is concentrated in work and actions: most page area stays white, warm cream or brown-black; electric blue marks conversion and small interface accents rather than full-page washes.
- Software is staged as a collage of useful work: overlapping rectangular photographs, file previews and one legible floating control on a clean field. Include this device on a new page; when media is scarce, pair a real output sample with a simplified action panel.
- Dark chapters interrupt the light canvas with substance: use a full-width brown-black band or an inset graphite-to-black proof stage with 20px corners, pale text and an oversized work collage.
- End with useful navigation rather than spectacle: a pure-black footer has six desktop columns, 20px/1.5 headings and 14px/22px links, followed by social and language utilities.

## Typography

- **Atlas Grotesk Web** — Body, navigation, actions, utility labels and prices. Unembellished, readable grotesk with a human conversational texture. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use 400, 500 and 700; preserve normal tracking. Font stack: "Atlas Grotesk Web", "Inter", Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif.
- **Sharp Grotesk** — Opening headline, compact chapter headings and editorial card headings. Broad, sturdy grotesk with open counters and blunt, friendly shapes. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use 400 and 500; avoid heavy bold cuts. Font stack: "Sharp Grotesk", "DM Sans", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Sharp Grotesk 23** — Wide section introductions, feature-page headlines and closing calls. Distinctively extended grotesk, especially visible in short centered section titles. If unavailable, use **Archivo**. Use the variable width axis near 125 with weight 400. Font stack: "Sharp Grotesk 23", "Archivo", Arial, sans-serif.

## Imagery and graphics

### Focused software scene

**Use:** Opening demonstration and recurring benefit stages.

**Observed:** A large softly de-emphasized work context sits behind a crisp floating input or action control. Later scenes combine tall fashion photographs, a racing photograph or a real working desk with white file cards and small interface overlays. Images retain natural or editorially saturated color, while the surrounding page stays neutral.

**Transfer:** Use your own real outputs or working photographs, place two slightly offset rectangles together, then overlay one simple control that explains an action. Keep the control legible and the rest subordinate.

**Build:** Use a relative square stage, two rectangular output layers offset by about 48px, and a foreground white control spanning roughly two thirds of the stage. Give the control a fine neutral edge and restrained soft separation. For a focused-input variant, blur only the rear content, never the foreground label. Do not blur every scene.

### Dark work collage

**Use:** Signature trust or reassurance stage.

**Observed:** Colorful visual work stacks upward from the lower part of a graphite field: small green sculptural images, a tall blue-backed object composition and an oversized white permissions panel. Centered copy and actions remain well above the collage.

**Transfer:** Show a trustworthy operation being performed on visually rich work. Minimum version: two original output images and a simplified white settings panel, not a full application screen.

**Build:** Inside Inset graphite stage, layer a small square image on the left, a taller portrait-format image behind the middle and a wide white action panel in front toward the right. Stagger their top edges and overlap about a quarter of each image. Keep the lower scene close to the stage boundary; do not use colored gradients as a substitute for work.

### Editorial photography

**Use:** Resource and audience cards.

**Observed:** Rectangular, edge-to-edge images include people working, creative collages, vivid motorsport and product imagery. Horizontal cards use nearly square working photographs; reading cards use wide images.

**Transfer:** Use real situations and tangible outputs with varied color. Let the consistent card construction unify different subjects.

### Neutral interface boards

**Only for:** feature and audience capability chapters

**Use:** Alternating explanatory media panels.

**Observed:** Large interface views float on stone rectangles, often paired as desktop and mobile views or a primary window with a smaller dialog. Blue folders, light file previews and tiny avatars provide color; the board takes more width than the prose.

**Transfer:** Show only the interface needed to demonstrate the chapter benefit; maintain a 48px neutral mat around it.

### Audience documentary montage

**Only for:** industry and community campaigns

**Use:** Large hero media and documentary proof.

**Observed:** An edge-to-edge natural-color event photograph fills two thirds of the opening. A later large video frame presents a grid-like black-and-white montage of people making work.

**Transfer:** Use documentary imagery of the audience's actual environment rather than generic office stock; a simple editorial montage is an acceptable substitute.

### Monochrome portraits

**Only for:** company and leadership pages

**Use:** Leadership and governance grids.

**Observed:** 366px square head-and-shoulders portraits in black and white, with varied plain or softly blurred backgrounds. Three columns sit on warm-black for one group and coconut for another, with names and substantial biographies below.

**Transfer:** Match square framing, grayscale and human eye-level perspective; do not force every portrait into an identical cutout.

### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly 24px SVG symbols and 1.5px butt-ended outline arrows, in warm ink or pale text. Small navigation chevrons are 16px; some section marks reach 48–64px. Pricing checks use fine blue strokes.

**Transfer:** Use straightforward outline symbols with restrained stroke weight; preserve arrows as a repeated directional device and keep icons subordinate to the sentence.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Focused software opening

**Observed:** A white 72px Navigation sits above a 735px Coconut canvas opening; an announcement campaign can add a 56px pale-blue strip above the navigation. The left text region takes about 42% of the width with 48px insets and vertical centering. The Display headline occupies three lines at 40px/1.2, followed after 16px by a 20px/1.5 muted paragraph and after 32px by Primary button. The right region contains a 735px square Focused software scene: softened file context behind a crisp floating action bar, with no enclosing illustration box. The first 900px contains navigation, the complete promise, support, action and main scene; the trust-logo section starts below the fold.

**Transfer:** Keep the default light opening for a general product landing page. Build an original focused software scene around one useful action. Feature and audience variants use a 480px-wide dark text panel beside 960px-wide edge-to-edge media, 40px regular wide headings and Inverse button; pricing opens with a centered 32px title, short support and benefit chips above plan controls. Company storytelling can center its mission inside the dark text third.

**Avoid:** Do not turn this into a centered oversized headline above a full-browser mockup, or fill the right half with abstract decorative blobs.

**Text style:** `display` — Sharp Grotesk 500 · 40px/1.2

### Centered chapter introduction

**Observed:** Section heading sits in an 827px centered measure, often after 72px of top space. A sentence-case Action eyebrow can precede it by 16px; Body support follows by 16px and an Arrow link by 32px.

**Transfer:** Use the same construction on light or Dark chapter surfaces without changing its hierarchy.

**Text style:** `section-heading` — Sharp Grotesk 23 400 · 32px/1.2

### Integration benefit grid

**Observed:** On Dark chapter, three unboxed columns hold a small identifying symbol, Item heading, Body description and Arrow link, with 16px vertical intervals. Multiple rows share a 72px section rhythm.

**Transfer:** Use for integrations, capabilities or adjacent tools; preserve open columns rather than card containers.

### Centered demonstration chapter

**Observed:** A compact centered medium heading, muted support and outline action sit above a large open-white collage. The media is made from output photography and floating software controls; generous space separates the text from the scene.

**Transfer:** Lead with a tangible outcome, then show one action that creates it. Use Proof heading for the compact title.

**Text style:** `proof-heading` — Sharp Grotesk 500 · 26px/1.3

### Inset trust stage

**Observed:** Inset graphite stage carries a large pale outline symbol, centered Proof heading and muted Body support within a 776px measure. Outline button and Arrow link share a row above Dark work collage. Testimonial card strip follows inside the same dark scene, with 96px of lead-in.

**Transfer:** Include a dark tangible-proof scene somewhere in a new story, adapting the operation and proof to the product rather than requiring a specific topic.

**Text style:** `proof-heading` — Sharp Grotesk 500 · 26px/1.3

### Audience paths and reading shelf

**Observed:** Horizontal audience card forms a two-column grid on white. A later Coconut canvas shelf uses three Editorial resource cards below a centered title, followed by a centered blue resource link and a narrow explanatory note before the footer.

**Transfer:** Let use-case paths and useful reading provide a soft ending for a broad landing page instead of forcing another conversion banner.

### Utility ending

**Observed:** Black footer uses Footer columns with generous upper breathing room, six categories, a small social row, a 132px divider and language selector. The broad landing story ends with editorial resources; pricing ends after support comparisons; feature, audience and company stories use a dark final call before the shared footer.

**Transfer:** Keep the ending appropriate to the page's job while retaining the substantial black navigation base.

### Alternating capability chapter

**Only for:** feature and audience pages

**Observed:** A 720px-tall warm section pairs narrow vertically centered copy with a broad Stone media backing. Media alternates sides, uses 48px interior padding and often spans 831px. Feature heading, Body support and Outline button are separated by 16px and 32px.

**Transfer:** Explain one benefit per chapter and alternate sides only across substantial stories, not every sentence.

**Text style:** `feature-heading` — Sharp Grotesk 400 · 24px/1.2

### Pricing decision system

**Only for:** pricing and purchase-focused feature pages

**Observed:** Three Plan cards sit beneath billing radio controls; the recommended middle plan has a lime ribbon. A pricing page continues with two lower-emphasis free/custom options and a 1240px comparison table with four columns. Table rows are generally 57px tall, separated by stone rules, with white row headings and progressively warmer plan columns. Plan headers remain available while the long comparison is read.

**Transfer:** Keep the repeated order and comparison categories useful. A purchase-focused feature page can use the cards without the full table.

### Quote and quantified proof

**Only for:** audience and feature pages

**Observed:** A Dark chapter quote is either centered with attribution beneath, or paired with a large line-drawn editorial illustration. Below is a muted 20px slide counter and next-arrow control. Audience proof can then use four unboxed columns: 40px numerals, 20px/1.2 explanatory heading and 16px body, each separated by 16px.

**Transfer:** Use verified proof; distinguish the large figure, its meaning and its context rather than making a row of unexplained numbers.

**Text style:** `feature-display` — Sharp Grotesk 23 400 · 40px/1.2

### FAQ and closing action

**Only for:** feature and audience pages

**Observed:** A centered 40px FAQ heading precedes a wide FAQ row stack. After related-feature suggestions where relevant, Dark chapter forms a final band with 72px vertical padding: a 40px/1.1 statement at left and Inverse button at right, sometimes with a secondary sales link underneath. A 96px separation precedes Black footer.

**Transfer:** Use questions to remove practical objections, then make one clear next action. Company pages adapt the final band to a news or further-reading action.

**Text style:** `closing-heading` — Sharp Grotesk 23 400 · 40px/1.1

### Company narrative and people

**Only for:** company pages

**Observed:** A dark split mission opening leads to a centered section title and a roughly 665px prose column with paragraph breaks of 56px. Monochrome portraits then form three-column biography grids, first on Dark chapter and then on Coconut canvas. A dark news-action band closes before Black footer.

**Transfer:** Use a focused mission, a readable story and genuine people rather than applying this portrait-heavy composition to ordinary product pages.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible demonstration → capability context → human or quantified proof → decision support → useful next step → shared navigation.

### Broad product marketing and discovery

1. **Promise** — Light split opening with a focused floating software control.
2. **Social proof** — Registered-user statement and a widely spaced logo strip.
3. **Ecosystem context** — Centered dark introduction followed by unboxed integration columns.
4. **Product demonstration** — White centered outcome chapters with photographic software collages.
5. **Reassurance** — Inset dark stage and horizontally arranged testimonial cards.
6. **Audience discovery** — Two-column horizontal paths.
7. **Useful ending** — Editorial reading shelf, resource link, narrow note and black footer.

**Transfer:** Use the demonstration-led light story as the default, selecting the relevant discovery and proof modules.

## Writing

Confident, useful and conversational. Benefits are tied to recognizable tasks, and warmth comes from direct phrasing and occasional work-related wordplay rather than hype.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Dropbox names, logos, partner assets, customer identities, quotations, statistics, dates, prices or product claims. Create original equivalent content supported by the new product's facts. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to the reader as you and your; use we for company beliefs and the product name when clarifying what performs an action.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are sentence case, often one practical promise or two short clauses. Support lines explain the task and outcome in one or two sentences; button labels are short verbs.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening headline | Concrete task followed by a useful outcome; parallel verbs or two short sentences. | 7–16 words | “Keep your content in [Brand]. Put it to work in your AI tools.”<br>“Secure cloud storage for all your files” |
| Supporting line | Name the work, explain the mechanism, end with the reader benefit. | 15–35 words | “Store, share, and collaborate in [Brand], then use the context behind your files with supported AI tools to keep work moving.” |
| Capability heading | Verb-led everyday benefit, with occasional audience-specific wordplay. | 4–12 words | “Don’t lose the plot: Bring order to the creative chaos”<br>“Access your files from multiple devices” |
| Primary action | Direct verb and destination or commitment. | 2–4 words | “Get started”<br>“Compare storage plans” |
| Closing statement | Reassert the outcome by removing a familiar obstacle. | 6–10 words | “Don’t let complexity interrupt creativity” |
| Question | Plain practical question in the reader's words. | 5–12 words | “Can I work across multiple devices?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** keep work moving; store, share, collaborate; from first to final; all your work; safe; organized; together; no matter where

**Avoid:** revolutionary; unlock limitless potential; disrupt everything; enterprise-grade as empty decoration; effortless magic

**Rules:**

- Lead with an action or outcome, then explain the supporting mechanism.
- Keep section and category labels in sentence case; do not turn every eyebrow into uppercase.
- Make technical details specific when they help a buying decision, not as decorative jargon.
- Use a little audience-specific wordplay without sacrificing the literal benefit.
- Differentiate trial, purchase, sales and reading actions with distinct labels.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep default opening type at 40px/1.2 and weight 500.
- Use 16px/1.5 body text with 16px heading gaps and 32px action gaps.
- Keep main actions at 16px radius and reserve 12px corners for cards and compact controls.
- Maintain 48px desktop scene insets and 72px chapter rhythm.
- Include a real-work collage with one crisp foreground software action.
- Use warm-black narrative bands and a separate pure-black footer.
- Keep pricing recommendation color to a narrow 32px lime ribbon.
- Preserve six desktop footer columns and the social/language utility ending.

### Don't

- Do not apply negative tracking to imitate a condensed startup display; these headings keep normal tracking.
- Do not substitute 64–96px hero typography for the restrained 40px opening.
- Do not make primary actions fully pill-shaped; their 16px corners remain visibly rectangular.
- Do not wash the whole page in electric blue; reserve broad color for media and small action areas.
- Do not add elevation shadows to every marketing card; use flat neutral contrast and clipped 12px edges.
- Do not round the large alternating media boards into app-store tiles; their backing is rectangular.
- Do not place every integration or statistic inside a bordered card; open columns are part of the rhythm.
- Do not reduce the footer to a thin copyright strip or add a giant decorative wordmark.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared button transitions animate background-color and border-color over 0.10s with cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.6,1).
- Declared navigation color transition is 125ms linear.
- Testimonial cards declare all 500ms with 150ms delay and ease-in-out; their settled state is unblurred at scale 1.
- A logo strip provides an animation enable control; carousel controls include counters and next arrows.

**Transfer:**

- Keep button feedback short and color-led.
- Preserve explicit controls for moving proof and logo content rather than making motion the only access path.

**Unknown:**

- Exact playback and scroll-trigger choreography are not established; do not invent durations for the hero demonstration or collage reveals.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared shared width breakpoints include 480px, 648px, 768px, 1024px, 1280px, 1440px and 1920px.
- Opening heading classes specify 32px before the tablet 40px treatment; centered section headings specify 24px before 32px. Capability headings specify 20px before desktop 24px.

**Transfer:**

- For narrower layouts, stack the promise and its scene while keeping the foreground action legible; simplify the collage rather than scaling all interface text to unreadable size.
- Reduce multi-column shelves and footer groups into fewer columns, keeping the same text hierarchy and action construction.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen column counts, navigation replacement and image reframing are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
