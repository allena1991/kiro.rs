# Ecosia — everyday action, living color

## Overview

An approachable, purpose-led style that makes technology feel human and consequential. White and warm off-white space, charcoal grotesk headlines and fresh yellow-green actions surround practical interface demonstrations and richly green photography. Rounded panels and playful organic graphics soften a fairly spacious, factual product story; culture-led pages become more photographic and expansive.

### Essentials

- Compact, emphatic grotesk headlines do the persuading: Founders Grotesk 700 at 54px/1.1 for the opening and closing promise, 48px/1.1 for capability chapters, with normal tracking rather than fashionable negative spacing.
- The default opening is a white product stage: a 72px header, centered headline, 24px support line, 48px lime pill and a substantial device or interface scene beginning below the text. The upper part of that scene is visible within the first 900px.
- Conversion is friendly rather than boxy: 9999px-radius buttons, 18px medium labels with 0.1px tracking, 19px horizontal padding and a small leading icon. Lime is the default; charcoal reverses the action on the lime closing band.
- Color arrives as purposeful islands in a mostly white and #f8f8f6 page: lime #d7eb80 actions and a broad conversion band, with saturated hues concentrated inside photographs and organic artwork rather than across every section.
- Show the consequence alongside the mechanism: pair a real-world photo with a bold organic arc and dark translucent proof badges, using 20px bold numbers, 20px padding and blur(12px). Include this living proof device on new pages; use genuine workflow or user-outcome photography when landscape imagery is inappropriate.
- Product details feel like small editorial exhibits: 10px-radius hairline cards, 16px text insets, 24px grid gaps and large lower illustrations. Preserve a tactile, green atmospheric image somewhere in the page or product scene; a layered textured abstract field is the fallback.

## Imagery and graphics

### Atmospheric green field

**Use:** A signature natural material in product demonstrations and editorial conversion panels.

**Observed:** Abstract green imagery resembles dragged paint or a long-exposure forest: broad horizontal streaks, mottled fine texture, luminous yellow-green at left and deep blue-green at right. It is neither a smooth UI gradient nor a literal scenic postcard.

**Transfer:** Make a similarly tactile, organic field behind the product. At minimum combine a blurred original photograph with streaked texture; preserve readable foreground content.

**Build:** Layer a blurred forest or material photograph under translucent horizontal SVG brush bands. Alternate olive, yellow-green and deep teal irregular strokes at 10–35% opacity; add subtle fine noise and a broad brighter region on the left. Use cover sizing, keep strokes mostly horizontal and retain dark areas to support white text.

### Product stage

**Use:** The main opening object and lower feature illustrations.

**Observed:** A large frontal laptop with thin charcoal outline floats on white. Its screen carries an atmospheric green field and restrained white controls. Feature images enlarge isolated menus, toggles and panels against warm white with faint local shadows, often with a pale lime icon bubble and dark pointer.

**Transfer:** Show a real product view in a thin device outline; for a non-software product, use a carefully lit frontal object with the same uncluttered backing. Feature exhibits should show one interaction or mechanism at readable size.

### Photo and organic arc

**Use:** The signature bridge between practical technology and a tangible result.

**Observed:** Close photography of hands working among seedlings is framed in a 520px-wide rounded rectangle. A thick curved pink-and-cyan band crosses the left side, with two Photo glass proof badges attached to the right.

**Transfer:** Photograph real users, makers or outcomes with natural light and rich but plausible color. Carry the same rounded arc and proof overlays onto that image.

**Build:** Place a thick SVG quarter-ring over the left half of an object-fit cover photo, with rounded path ends and no outline. Split the band into two saturated color segments, leaving the photographed action visible near the lower center and reserving the right edge for Proof badges.

### Portrait claim tiles

**Only for:** impact-led editorial pages

**Use:** Four side-by-side brand-positioning arguments.

**Observed:** Tall 268×400px images use simple singular subjects: forest canopy, a hand holding a phone, a plant seedhead and solar panels. Rich blue and green color, clean close framing, 10px corners; titles and body remain below the image on white.

**Transfer:** Choose four distinct real objects or scenes that make abstract benefits tangible, maintaining uniform portrait proportions.

### Quantitative artwork

**Only for:** impact-led editorial pages

**Use:** Make financial or scale proof approachable.

**Observed:** A thick multicolor doughnut-like arc behind a white total card; elsewhere a black world-map silhouette on a stone panel is marked with lime dots and accompanied by a stat column.

**Transfer:** Use a simple diagram appropriate to the data, with a white total card or a clear secondary column. Do not let decoration imply precision unsupported by the numbers.

**Build:** For the arc, use an SVG circle with a very thick stroke, round caps and several colored stroke segments, leaving a white central opening. Overlay a 10px-radius bordered total card near the lower center. For coverage, use a flat SVG silhouette and small lime circles on stone.

### Human-scale photography

**Only for:** culture and recruiting pages

**Use:** Full-screen opening, video posters, people testimonials and workplace gallery.

**Observed:** Natural sunlit workplaces, smiling colleagues, plants and warm wood; the opening fills 1440×900px with a darkened photographic layer under centered white type. Video posters are broad 1440×800px scenes with a centered circular play affordance.

**Transfer:** Use candid environments rather than studio headshots. Retain natural light and lived-in detail; darken only enough for white text.

### Organic footer garden

**Only for:** culture and recruiting pages

**Use:** Distinctive warm ending above the informational footer.

**Observed:** A wide strip of flat, irregular plant-like lobes in forest green, lime, mustard, peach and cyan follows a rolling white edge. No outlines, texture or simulated depth; it occupies roughly 300px above the columns.

**Transfer:** Create an original family of friendly lobed shapes, not literal botanical branding, with varied heights along a soft wave.

**Build:** Draw 8–12 overlapping SVG blobs from rounded Bézier lobes and elongated ovals, varying scale and rotation. Position clusters across the width; mask their bases with a white undulating path. Use a small palette of flat warm and green tones with no strokes or shadows.

### Icons

**Observed:** Compact monochrome pictograms accompany actions and cards, generally 16–24px; photo counters use larger white symbols. Social marks form a separate muted row. Shapes are simple and rounded rather than intricate duotone illustrations.

**Transfer:** Use consistent simple glyphs with equivalent optical weight. Keep action icons near the label; keep decorative organic art separate from utility symbols.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** clear promise → concrete product or human demonstration → purpose and proof → capability or experience chapters → low-friction invitation → trust and utility navigation

### Convert interest into product adoption

1. **Promise** — Centered headline, short support, primary action and alternate route above a large device scene.
2. **Outcome proof** — Warm split band connects mission copy with a photograph and translucent metric badges.
3. **Capability chapters** — Three feature exhibits, a privacy demonstration, two performance exhibits and a customization sequence alternate white and warm bands.
4. **Closing call** — Wide lime invitation with a friction-reducing headline and charcoal action.
5. **Trust and utility** — Compact five-part footer followed by social and legal rows.

**Transfer:** Move from what the product does to why its use matters, then answer practical objections before a simple final action.

### Introduce an everyday utility through its broader value proposition

1. **Immediate utility** — An atmospheric green opening puts a usable search field below a white promise and above a glass proof strip.
2. **Positioning** — Four portrait-photo arguments with short captions and paragraphs.
3. **Accountability proof** — Colorful financial artwork and an explicit route to reports.
4. **Outcome proof** — A comparative photo and a large map-and-stat panel make the result concrete.
5. **Closing call** — A rounded atmospheric image panel repeats the invitation, followed by a restrained footer.

**Transfer:** Let visitors do the main job immediately, then show principles, accountability and scale with visible proof rather than slogans alone.

### Help prospective colleagues understand the organization and feel welcome

1. **Belonging promise** — Full-height candid photo with centered white headline and green action.
2. **Purpose** — An illustration-and-copy split followed by a large explanatory video poster.
3. **Lived experience** — A narrow culture paragraph and benefits list, another video, then three testimonials.
4. **Reassurance** — Inclusion copy, workplace images and a gallery show what participation feels like.
5. **Personal invitation** — Short hiring introduction and a named contact with portrait and green action.
6. **Warm utility ending** — A broad organic illustrated strip leads into resource and imprint columns, social icons and legal text.

**Transfer:** Use specific working conditions and real voices, then make the final action feel like contacting people rather than entering a sales funnel.

## Writing

Direct, optimistic and morally clear without sounding corporate. Practical explanations and explicit numbers support the confident claims; friendliness comes from everyday language rather than jokes.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Ecosia names, wordmarks, illustration assets, interface content, environmental claims, planting or financial totals, testimonials, certifications or staff identities. Replace them with the new product's own truthful content and licensed assets. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use we for commitments and accountability, you for the reader's practical benefit. Headlines can state a plain fact; actions name the thing the visitor will do.
- **Rhythm:** Short declarative headlines, often 4–9 words, above one or two compact explanatory sentences. Longer closing headlines pair the benefit with a friction reducer; culture copy allows contractions and an occasional warm exclamation.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the product and the beneficial consequence in one concrete sentence. | 6–10 words | “The browser that funds tree planting” |
| Supporting line | Turn the promise into an ordinary action the reader can imagine. | 5–9 words | “Be climate active while you browse” |
| Capability headline | Plain benefit, no abstract platform language. | 3–6 words | “Stay safe while browsing”<br>“Smooth and smart browsing” |
| Accountability | State a commitment and explain how the reader can verify it. | Short headline plus 1–2 sentences | “We're fully transparent”<br>“We publish financial reports every month so you can see exactly how much we made and how we spent it. Total transparency, every month.” |
| Primary action | Explicit verb plus product or destination. | 2–4 words | “Download [Brand] Browser”<br>“Open positions” |
| Closing call | Benefit followed by a concrete reduction in effort. | 10–16 words | “Switch to the browser that plants trees — it only takes 60 seconds” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** every day; simple ways; we respect; you can see; only takes; help; join; together; your preference

**Avoid:** revolutionary platform; unparalleled synergy; disrupt everything; enterprise-grade ecosystem; hype without a mechanism

**Rules:**

- Explain what happens and why it matters before introducing technical detail.
- Pair percentages, counts and speed claims with a clear unit and a credible basis.
- Use specific action labels instead of generic conversion language.
- A categorical eyebrow and a persuasive headline have different jobs; do not repeat the same phrase in both.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep product headlines at 54px/1.1 and section promises at 48px/1.1 with normal tracking.
- Use 96px chapter breathing room while keeping card text inset to 16px.
- Use 10px corners and 1px soft-gray borders for product exhibits.
- Make the final broad color moment lime with a charcoal action.
- Keep proof numbers in glass badges at 20px bold and their background translucent.
- Give the main product object a substantial centered stage rather than equal weight with minor decorations.
- Use 24px feature-grid gaps and 40px split-section gaps.
- Carry natural tactile imagery into at least one meaningful scene, even for an abstract product.

### Don't

- Do not apply a heavy drop shadow to every card; the card shell is flat and hairlined.
- Do not replace warm white with blue-gray panels; #f8f8f6 is the default tinted band.
- Do not use #008009 as the main product CTA; that green belongs to the recruiting variant.
- Do not set paragraphs in the display face; keep readable 16px/1.4 Inter.
- Do not make all actions charcoal; the main neutral-background conversion is lime.
- Do not turn footer links into navigation pills; keep muted 14px underlined text.
- Do not bring 80px culture headings into ordinary capability cards.
- Do not treat organic color as a generic rainbow page background; keep it inside focused visual devices.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Footer links declare color 0.1s ease-out transitions.
- Carousel progress declares width 1s.
- Actions have explicit lime, charcoal and translucent hover/active changes and blue focus-visible outlines.
- Other declared effects include short fades and counter-related animation; their complete visible choreography is not established.

**Transfer:**

- Retain the elongating dark carousel progress bar when using the customization sequence.
- Use the declared button color states as the main interaction feedback rather than adding large motion to every card.

**Unknown:**

- Exact runtime trigger order, autoplay behavior and complete section entrance choreography.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop compositions are centered and max-width constrained. Declared width breakpoints for the editorial system include 400px, 550px, 768px, 1080px, 1280px, 1440px and 1920px; culture styles declare 549px, 767px and 1199px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, preserve the promise → action → substantial object hierarchy rather than shrinking the entire desktop scene.
- Stack the text and photo proof scene while keeping badges attached to their media; move three-column exhibits to a readable single-column flow.
- Keep the illustrated culture-footer threshold full-width while allowing footer groups to wrap.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, navigation behavior and per-breakpoint art direction; the stacking rules are design guidance.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
