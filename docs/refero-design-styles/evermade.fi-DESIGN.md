# Evermade — serif with voltage

## Overview

A warm editorial identity interrupted by electric pink and deep wine-colored stages. Delicate, elongated serif headlines share space with monospaced labels and plain, approachable sans-serif paragraphs. Expansive product photography and carefully staged interface mockups give ambitious digital products a tangible presence; the overall density is low, with concentrated pockets of proof.

### Essentials

- The opening is a typographic light sculpture, not a conventional sales panel: a 900px burgundy stage holds a two-line magenta serif statement roughly 867px wide, with soft optical blur and a small cream editorial card at the lower left.
- Elegant language meets technical markings: Instrument Serif 400 carries 48px/1.1 leads and 128px/1.1 service words; DM Mono 300 carries 16px/1.6 uppercase section labels with a small square marker.
- Wine and electric pink frame a predominantly warm-paper story: #2d070b and #ff0389 concentrate in the opening and closing stages, while #fef0e6 supports the long middle. Full-color imagery supplies most other color.
- Actions are slender capsules rather than blocks: the default outlined button is 54px high, with 16px 32px padding, a 1px burgundy outline, uppercase 16px DM Mono and a fine 33px arrow.
- Proof is staged, not boxed: a 1316px-wide image leads into two smaller image-led stories; captions remain directly on the canvas, with 32px serif titles and 11px mono pink tags. Use a real interface in a simple angled device scene when bespoke photography is unavailable.
- The signature proof lattice and monumental sign-off anchor the identity: use a 1px dashed square-cell proof grid with an enlarged central message, and a burgundy closing field with pink action, sparse link columns and an oversized edge-clipped wordmark. Adapt these devices to the new product’s own proof and name.

## Typography

- **Instrument Serif** — Expressive headlines, leads, story titles, quotes and editorial numbers. Narrow high-contrast serif with tall, delicate letterforms; regular weight is expressive without bold. Font stack: "Instrument Serif", Georgia, serif.
- **Manrope** — Body paragraphs and concise explanatory text. Rounded contemporary sans-serif with an approachable, clear texture. Font stack: "Manrope", helvetica, arial, sans-serif.
- **DM Mono** — Navigation, labels, tags, actions and technical metadata. Light monospaced markings; predominantly uppercase, with medium-weight actions. Font stack: "DM Mono", monospace.

**Notes:**

- Keep the serif at weight 400: scale, narrow proportions and contrast do the work of emphasis. Most serif tracking is -0.01em; accordion headings retain normal tracking.
- The opening’s letter artwork should read at roughly 320px with a tight two-line composition at a 1440px viewport; use 22.22vw as a reconstruction guide, fitting the full statement to its stage. This is a separate artwork treatment, not the 128px editorial title.
- Small uppercase mono headings are navigation within the story, not substitutes for the expressive serif message.

## Surfaces and depth

### Surfaces

#### Warm paper · level 0

**Use:** Default content canvas.

**CSS:** `background: #fef0e6; color: #2d070b`

**Observed:** Long uninterrupted middle fields; story cards usually share the canvas rather than a separate painted box.

**Transfer:** Let spacing and image size separate stories.

#### Burgundy stage · level 0

**Use:** Expressive opening and monumental closing.

**CSS:** `background: #2d070b; color: #ff0389`

**Observed:** Flat wine-colored field makes saturated pink type feel luminous.

**Transfer:** Keep the dark area visually spare and typographic.

#### Navigation glass · level 1

**Use:** Subtle floating navigation shell.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(45, 7, 11, 0.02); backdrop-filter: blur(6px); border-radius: 32px`

**Observed:** A faint moving highlight outlines the rounded shell without a drop shadow. A darker shell is used over the culture collage.

**Transfer:** Keep the shell almost invisible against its stage; adapt foreground color to the background.

#### White editorial field · level 0

**Only for:** capability and case-study pages

**Use:** Alternative long-form backdrop.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #2d070b`

**Observed:** White image galleries and technical chapters contrast with warm-paper bands.

**Transfer:** Use white as an editorial variant, not a replacement for the default paper.

#### Rose title band · level 0

**Only for:** case studies

**Use:** Project-specific opening.

**CSS:** `background: #ffc7de; color: #2d070b`

**Observed:** A full-width title field gives way directly to an edge-to-edge image.

**Transfer:** Reserve the solid rose field for a focused project story.

#### Solution panel · level 1

**Only for:** capability detail pages

## Components

### Navigation shell

**Use:** Persistent site orientation.

**CSS:** `display: flex; align-items: center; justify-content: space-between; height: 52px; padding: 0px 21px; border-radius: 32px; background: rgba(45, 7, 11, 0.02); backdrop-filter: blur(6px)`

**Text style:** `navigation` — DM Mono 300 · 14px/1.6 · tracking 0.1em · uppercase

**Observed:** Logo and main links occupy the left rounded shell; language controls and a separate small contact capsule sit at the right. Cream text belongs on dark stages; burgundy text belongs on light stages.

**Transfer:** Keep the logo modest and links sparse; do not center a giant brand mark in the header.

### Outline action

**Use:** Default editorial and conversion action.

**CSS:** `display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; color: #2d070b; border: 1px solid #2d070b; border-radius: 9999px; padding: 16px 32px; height: 54px; box-sizing: border-box`

**Text style:** `action` — DM Mono 500 · 16px/1.25 · uppercase

**Observed:** Label is followed by a fine long arrow. Width follows copy; dark related-content fields use the pink outline and label variant.

**States:** Declared: hover unspecified; focus-visible outline 4px solid #ff0389.

**Transfer:** Use one clear verb-object action, with the arrow as a directional punctuation mark.

### Header contact

**Use:** Compact conversion link in navigation.

**CSS:** `background: #fef0e6; color: #2d070b; border: 1px solid #fef0e6; border-radius: 9999px; padding: 8px 24px; height: 32px; box-sizing: border-box`

**Text style:** `header-action` — DM Mono 500 · 14px/1 · tracking 0.1em · uppercase

**Observed:** A small filled capsule, visually distinct from the longer outline actions in the content.

**States:** Declared: hover background transparent, border and text #fef0e6; focus-visible outline 4px solid #ff0389.

### Story card

**Use:** Image-led proof, editorial stories and related content.

**CSS:** `display: flex; flex-direction: column; position: relative; border: 1px solid transparent`

**Text style:** `story-title` — Instrument Serif 400 · 32px/1.4 · tracking -0.01em

**Observed:** Wide image first, then uppercase category, serif outcome title and wrapped pink tags. No raised card backing; caption content has 16px vertical padding. Featured cards span the container; paired cards use the same hierarchy.

**Transfer:** Describe what changed, not just the product name.

### Taxonomy capsule

**Use:** Compact classification below stories.

**CSS:** `display: flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; gap: 8px; padding: 4px 12px; border-radius: 9999px; background: rgba(255, 199, 222, 0.5); color: #2d070b`

**Text style:** `tag` — DM Mono 300 · 11px/1.4 · tracking 0.009em · uppercase

**Observed:** Tiny dot-cluster or four-part glyph precedes the text; capsules wrap with 4px gaps.

**Transfer:** Keep two to four useful categories, not a second navigation bar.

### Proof lattice

**Use:** Trust block with an integrated central message.

**CSS:** `display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(5, minmax(0px, 1fr)); border-top: 1px dashed #2d070b; border-left: 1px dashed #2d070b`

**Text style:** `proof-message` — Manrope 400 · 22px/1.4

**Observed:** Monochrome marks sit centrally in square perimeter cells with right and bottom dashed edges; the central message spans three columns and two rows.

**Transfer:** Use credible partner marks, certifications or concise proof statements. With no logos, typeset short proof labels in the same cells rather than omitting the lattice.

### Rail indicator

**Use:** Understated horizontal browsing control.

**CSS:** `position: relative; height: 2px; background: rgba(45, 7, 11, 0.15); border-radius: 3px`

**Observed:** A burgundy active segment is 4px thick; the track spans the content width, below a clipped rail. The related-story dark variant uses pink.

**Transfer:** Retain the thin rail rather than pagination dots; segment length should reflect the visible fraction.

### Opening editorial card

**Use:** A small timely story anchored below the statement artwork.

**CSS:** `display: flex; max-width: 400px; border-radius: 8px; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** A roughly 200px-wide image forms the left half, cream copy the right half, with a 48px outlined circular arrow at the lower right. The card is about 157px high and sits at the lower-left content edge.

**Transfer:** Use a real announcement or product update rather than a second primary conversion button.

## Imagery and graphics

### Luminous type sculpture

**Use:** Signature statement in the opening.

**Observed:** Electric-pink tall serif lettering fills a centered two-line area about 867px wide and 528px high on burgundy. The upper line is relatively crisp, the lower line softly defocused; subtle vertical smears make the words feel optical rather than embossed.

**Transfer:** Typeset a short original promise using the display serif, preserving the two-line silhouette. A CSS text treatment is the minimum acceptable alternative to bespoke artwork.

**Build:** Use an accessible text layer with Instrument Serif 400, roughly 320px at 1440px width, tight line spacing and -0.01em tracking. Fill #ff0389 on #2d070b. Duplicate behind at low opacity with blur(8px), and soften only the lower line with blur(3px). Fit the complete phrase within an approximately 910px-wide stage; keep a clear reading layer.

### Staged product proof

**Use:** Large feature images and smaller project pairs.

**Observed:** Real screens are made tangible: two angled phones float over a diagonal pink-and-ivory plane, overlapping browser windows form a sharp collage, and a tablet sits on a black tabletop beside a red illuminated sculptural prop. Images are broad rectangles with little corner rounding; the media dominates its caption.

**Transfer:** Use the new product’s actual interface in a clean device composition. Alternate a bright pale scene with a dark tactile scene, keeping screen content readable. A single angled screen on a two-tone plane is sufficient.

**Build:** For a simple bright scene, divide a landscape SVG or CSS stage diagonally into pale rose and warm paper polygons. Place two device-framed interface images at opposing 12-degree rotations, one slightly higher and behind the other. Keep generous empty margins; shadows belong inside this composed image, not around the story card.

### Monumental wordmark

**Use:** Signature graphic at the very end of the page.

**Observed:** A saturated pink custom sans-serif wordmark expands past both viewport edges and is clipped along the bottom. It is far larger than the small multi-column footer links above.

**Transfer:** Use the new product’s own wordmark or a carefully tracked sans-serif rendering of its name. The giant edge-clipped sign-off remains part of the design even when the name is short.

**Build:** Place a single-line SVG wordmark on #2d070b, fill #ff0389, scale slightly wider than the viewport, center it and clip horizontal overflow. Make the visible letter height roughly 200px at 1440px, extending below the page edge.

### Human editorial photography

**Only for:** culture and company pages

**Use:** Alternating feature rows, loose hero collage and moving culture strip.

**Observed:** Warm natural light, beige interiors, textured walls and relaxed human gestures. Some portraits deliberately smear with lateral motion; the hero scatters rectangular images around a clear central serif statement. A horizontal strip uses broad 825×464 images with 20px gaps.

**Transfer:** Photograph real people in believable work settings. Reserve deliberate motion blur for atmospheric editorial portraits, not the contact directory.

### Expert studio portraits

**Only for:** expert and team directories

**Use:** People cards.

**Observed:** Consistent beige backdrop, soft light and grounded three-quarter poses. Clothes stay mostly dark or neutral; faces remain crisp.

**Transfer:** Repeat camera distance, backing and light across all people; do not mix circular avatars into this portrait grid.

### Technical collage

**Only for:** capability page visual introductions

**Use:** Full-width art strip following the title.

**Observed:** Burgundy code panel, a blurred human portrait, folded pink-to-lilac chevrons and warm-paper pixel squares on white. The strip mixes technical signs with humanity without resembling an application dashboard.

**Transfer:** Combine one relevant technical fragment, one human image and simple geometric art.

**Build:** Construct adjacent rectangular fields: a burgundy block with tiny light mono text, a warm portrait, then two SVG chevrons made of slanted quadrilaterals with pink-lilac gradients, followed by a sparse grid of cream squares. Keep the white negative space generous.

### Icons

**Observed:** Fine directional arrows, small square section markers and dot-cluster tag glyphs. Button arrows are 33×11px, accordion arrows 11×13px inside 48px circles; menu chevrons are 8×6px with 1px strokes. Social marks are compact 16px filled SVGs.

**Transfer:** Use fine, mostly unembellished directional symbols in the surrounding text color. Keep illustrative icons out of the paragraph hierarchy.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Optical statement opening

**Observed:** A 900px Burgundy stage is filled by Luminous type sculpture: a short two-line promise spans about three fifths of the viewport width and occupies the center, below the slim navigation. Background comes first, optical word layers next, navigation and Opening editorial card above them. The lower line falls toward the card’s top edge; the card sits at the lower left with about 40px breathing room below. Within the first 900px are the complete statement, navigation contact action and update card; the explanatory lead and proof begin below the fold. There is no conventional body-copy column beside the headline.

**Transfer:** Use a compact original promise as the main object, not a stock illustration. Rebuild it with live serif text if no artwork exists. Capability pages instead use editorial-display on white above a full-width collage; portfolio and case-study pages use project-display on paper or rose above a dominant image; culture pages use editorial-display amid scattered human photographs.

**Avoid:** Do not shrink the promise into a normal left-aligned SaaS heading with a paragraph and two filled buttons.

**Text style:** `service-display` — Instrument Serif 400 · 128px/1.1 · tracking -0.01em

### Paper introduction

**Observed:** Warm paper with a centered section-label, a square marker and two lead statements within Reading container. The first statement begins about 40.5px below the label; 130px top and bottom padding lets the prose feel like an editorial preface.

**Transfer:** Explain who the product is for and why it matters in two distinct thoughts, rather than a dense benefit list.

**Text style:** `lead` — Instrument Serif 400 · 48px/1.1 · tracking -0.01em

### Word-led capabilities

**Observed:** A centered small label introduces three stacked service-display links, each on a roughly 141px line with 7.6px gaps. The words dominate the otherwise unboxed field.

**Transfer:** Use two to four short capability names; keep them as a typographic scene rather than converting them into icon tiles.

**Text style:** `service-display` — Instrument Serif 400 · 128px/1.1 · tracking -0.01em

### Lattice trust field

**Observed:** A section-label sits above Proof lattice with 104px separation. The perimeter cells hold centered monochrome marks; a large merged central cell holds Proof-message prose.

**Transfer:** Make proof surround a reassuring statement, adapting cell content to available credentials.

**Text style:** `proof-message` — Manrope 400 · 22px/1.4

### Feature and supporting pair

**Observed:** Story card first fills Main container with a broad device composition; a later row holds two smaller stories. Captions remain on Warm paper, with caption-label above story-title and Taxonomy capsule beneath.

**Transfer:** Give one outcome visual priority, then let two supporting examples broaden the claim.

**Text style:** `story-title` — Instrument Serif 400 · 32px/1.4 · tracking -0.01em

### Editorial overflow rail

**Observed:** Section-label above three visible Story cards and a clipped continuation. Images are about 421×237px with 24px gutters; titles sit about 15px below images. Rail indicator spans the container below, followed by a centered Outline action about 78px later.

**Transfer:** Use a curated set of insights, use cases or learning material. Preserve the clipped continuation and thin progress track.

**Text style:** `story-title` — Instrument Serif 400 · 32px/1.4 · tracking -0.01em

### Monumental closing

**Observed:** Burgundy stage carries a centered pink contact marker and long outlined closing invitation. A broad empty interval separates that action from a low multi-column footer: legal and social information at left, sparse navigation groups at right. Monumental wordmark fills and spills beyond the page’s bottom edge. This same ending recurs after the editorial rail or archive.

**Transfer:** Close with one invitation in the product’s own language, then practical links and the product’s own giant name. Preserve the theatrical space and oversized sign-off without requiring a particular preceding section.

**Text style:** `action` — DM Mono 500 · 16px/1.25 · uppercase

### Capability chapter table

**Only for:** capability detail pages

**Observed:** Warm-paper two-column rows combine numbered 48px serif headings and 18px body text on the left with uppercase mono bullet lists on the right. Rows have 52px column gutters and subtle horizontal separation; stacked rows retain their paper backing.

**Transfer:** Pair a plain-language capability explanation with a concise list of practical deliverables.

**Text style:** `lead` — Instrument Serif 400 · 48px/1.1 · tracking -0.01em

### Case-study editorial proof

**Only for:** case studies

**Observed:** Full-bleed opening image leads to an intro and small service list, an asymmetric two-image gallery, then warm-paper narrative chapters with small labels at left and 18px prose at right. A later paired gallery is followed by a broad 60px serif quotation and three 120px mono percentages.

**Transfer:** Separate challenge, approach, implementation and outcome. Let actual product imagery and a credible quotation carry more weight than decorative cards.

**Text style:** `quote` — Instrument Serif 400 · 60px/1.1 · tracking -0.01em

### Scale as editorial type

**Only for:** company-scale proof

**Observed:** Giant 200px serif number-plus-noun statements occupy most of a 1316px row, with a narrow 18px explanatory paragraph at the right; later rows have 15% burgundy top rules and about 78px top padding.

**Transfer:** Use a small number of verifiable scale facts. The noun is as large as the number, rather than a tiny metric label.

**Text style:** `editorial-number` — Instrument Serif 400 · 200px/1 · tracking -0.01em

### Portfolio archive

**Only for:** portfolio indexes

**Observed:** After large featured stories, a centered label opens a six-column image grid with 40.5px gutters. Small 12px mono project names and dates sit directly under each thumbnail.

**Transfer:** Use this denser variant only for secondary browsing, keeping featured stories much larger.

**Text style:** `archive-caption` — DM Mono 300 · 12px/1.6 · tracking 0.04em · uppercase

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Expressive promise → plain-language positioning → capabilities and credible proof → image-led outcomes → deeper expertise or browsing → invitation and monumental sign-off.

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Dark optical type scene with a small current-story card.
2. **Positioning** — Centered serif introductory paragraphs on paper.
3. **Capabilities** — Oversized word links.
4. **Social proof** — Dashed proof lattice with central reassurance.
5. **Outcome proof** — One wide story and two supporting images.
6. **Continued discovery** — Editorial overflow rail.
7. **Closing call** — Dark invitation, sparse footer links and giant pink name.

**Transfer:** Use the strongest typographic promise and proof devices while adapting the narrative length to the new product.

### Detailed capability landing page

1. **Promise** — White centered serif title and full-width technical collage.
2. **Explanation** — Centered lead and numbered expandable arguments.
3. **Proof** — Large and paired product stories.
4. **Capability chapters** — Paper two-column stacked rows and split solution panel.
5. **Human trust** — Warm studio portraits with contact information.
6. **Continued discovery** — Editorial rail.
7. **Closing call** — Shared dark invitation and monumental sign-off.

**Transfer:** Move from broad promise to concrete delivery and a real person to speak with.

### Outcome-focused case study

1. **Promise** — Rose title band with a visit action above full-bleed product image.
2. **Context** — Short intro, service list and asymmetric gallery.
3. **Explanation** — Warm-paper challenge, solution and implementation chapters.
4. **Demonstration** — Two tall product details.
5. **Endorsement** — Full-width serif quote.
6. **Quantified proof** — Three large mono percentage metrics.
7. **Discovery** — Pink-on-burgundy related stories, then a white editorial rail.
8. **Closing call** — Shared dark invitation and giant name.

**Transfer:** Make the result visible before explaining its production; use only genuine metrics and endorsements.

### Portfolio browsing index

1. **Promise** — Compact paper title above a broad feature image.
2. **Proof** — Alternating full-width images and pairs of stories.
3. **Depth** — Six-column thumbnail archive with name and year.
4. **Closing call** — Direct transition into the shared burgundy sign-off.

**Transfer:** Use scale contrast between curated work and the dense long-tail archive.

### Company and culture story

1. **Promise** — Pink serif statement surrounded by scattered warm human photos on burgundy.
2. **Positioning** — Serif introduction followed by alternating portrait-and-copy rows.
3. **Principles** — Wide human photo strip and numbered accordion.
4. **Scale proof** — Giant serif number-and-noun rows.
5. **Human trust** — Three-column studio portrait directory.
6. **Discovery** — White editorial rail.
7. **Closing call** — Shared burgundy invitation and oversized pink wordmark.

**Transfer:** Tell an organization’s character through actual people and tangible scale, rather than abstract mission cards.

## Writing

Confident, warm and commercially grounded. Expressive headlines introduce a promise, while support copy explains practical value and reassures the reader about complexity and collaboration.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, logo artwork, customer marks, project names, proprietary platform names, rankings, numerical claims or testimonial wording. The examples illustrate tone and structure only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use we for the team and you or your for the reader. Address practical concerns directly without exaggerated familiarity.
- **Rhythm:** Short headline fragments contrast with complete explanatory sentences. Leads can be expansive but stay focused on one thought; actions are brief verb-object phrases. Mono metadata is visually uppercase, while editorial prose retains sentence case.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | A concise future-facing declaration or an outcome for the reader. | 4–9 words | “We build the next”<br>“Delivering impact for ambitious brands” |
| Positioning | State what the organization combines or builds, then connect it to lasting value. | One or two sentences | “[Brand] is a design and technology agency building business-critical digital services.” |
| Reassurance | Name the reader’s concern, then explain the process that resolves it. | Two or three short sentences | “Your project is in safe hands. Our clear, structured process makes your life easier.” |
| Story title | Transformation or experience, not a product feature inventory. | 6–12 words | “From a recipe tool to a food media”<br>“A museum visit that starts right on your screen” |
| Action | Plain verb plus destination or object. | 2–4 words | “more insights”<br>“visit website” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** impact; built for; human-centric; clear; trust; seamlessly; measurable; stand the test of time

**Avoid:** game-changing; 10x everything; crush the competition; magic; unlimited possibilities; disrupt or die

**Rules:**

- Pair ambitious language with an understandable practical consequence.
- Use technical terms where they clarify the offer; explain their value in plain language.
- Let project titles state a changed experience; use the small mono label for the organization or category.
- Use a short shared invitation at the end rather than adding a new argument.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep serif headlines at weight 400 and mostly -0.01em tracking.
- Use the 1316px image container against the narrower 960px reading width.
- Preserve the 1px dashed proof-grid treatment and enlarged central message.
- Use 54px outline capsules in content and 32px filled capsules in the header.
- Keep tiny taxonomy labels at 11px with 4px 12px padding.
- Give major editorial passages 104px–130px of vertical breathing room.
- Include the luminous type scene and edge-clipped oversized closing name.
- Use 24px rail gaps and show a clipped continuation beyond three cards.

### Don't

- Do not replace the narrow serif with a bold geometric sans-serif for marketing headlines.
- Do not spread electric pink into every long-form background; concentrate it in typographic stages and small accents.
- Do not add card drop shadows: the page uses flat fields, hairlines and photography for depth.
- Do not round project images into oversized 24px tiles; their broad rectangular edges are part of the editorial character.
- Do not turn 16px mono section labels into oversized bold headings.
- Do not shrink the central proof message to fit one square cell; it spans multiple cells.
- Do not use circular headshots where warm three-quarter portrait rectangles belong.
- Do not replace the thin 2px rail track and 4px active segment with large pagination dots.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Text reveal elements declare opacity and filter transitions of 0.75s cubic-bezier(0.39, 0.575, 0.565, 1).
- Navigation links declare background-color 0.25s cubic-bezier(0.19, 1, 0.22, 1); outline actions declare 0.25s with the same curve.
- Accordion controls declare transform 0.5s and border-color 1s cubic-bezier(0.19, 1, 0.22, 1).
- Culture media uses a 75s linear infinite marquee; capability rows use sticky positioning.

**Transfer:**

- Use optical soft-to-clear text reveals to support the luminous type identity.
- Keep hover response concentrated in capsules, arrow controls and subtle navigation shell changes.
- Restrict the continuous photo marquee to culture storytelling.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll triggers, pointer-tilt behavior and complete animation choreography are not specified.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- CSS width boundaries include 480px, 600px, 782px, 960px, 1080px, 1280px, 1440px and 1920px.
- Capability and result columns use repeat(auto-fit, minmax(250px, 1fr)) and are marked for stacking.

**Transfer:**

- Scale the optical statement to fit two legible lines before reducing surrounding space; retain the paper-to-burgundy contrast.
- Stack paired narratives on narrow widths and move the update card into normal flow beneath the statement.
- Reflow the proof lattice while preserving a merged central message and perimeter proof; retain horizontal overflow for editorial rails.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, mobile menu construction and proof-grid rearrangement are unspecified; these transfer rules are design recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
