# Farm Minerals — engineered earth theatre

## Overview

An earthy, cinematic style that pairs scientific confidence with the warmth of working landscapes. Cream and olive editorial panels alternate with immersive green product scenes, natural-light photography and tactile three-dimensional objects. A single contemporary grotesk carries spacious headlines and compact technical markings; the overall density is low, with information gathered into precise rows and small captions.

### Essentials

- Headlines behave like separate voices across a stage: the default opening uses Aeonik 86.4px/0.95 at weight 400 on the upper left, answered by 51.8px/0.9 on the lower right.
- The object is the spectacle, not a card illustration: a 900px-tall opening surrounds a roughly 316px product object with green cylindrical plinths, grazing light and deep shadows. Use an original product render or a carefully lit cutout on simple geometric plinths.
- Actions resemble small equipment controls: 39–42px high rectangles, 3.5px corners, uppercase 12.4–13.8px labels and tiny dots bracketing the words rather than oversized arrows.
- Color comes in whole scenes, not scattered UI accents: broad olive media chapters and a #596a33 closing band balance #f4ede6 editorial areas; ordinary text uses #404f1d and headings #636f3d. Bright colors belong mainly to photography and packaging.
- Proof reads as an open ledger: 53.9px regular numerals stand at the left of 1px ruled rows, while small uppercase explanations sit at the far right; compact proof variants use 97px green square badges.
- Every landing page carries an atmospheric product transformation and a human closing collage: use water, material or process imagery for the transformation, then offset rectangular documentary photos with olive square blocks. Where specialist media is unavailable, use a static before/process/after composition and original contextual portraits.

## Imagery and graphics

### Sculptural product stage

**Use:** The default opening and key product reveal.

**Observed:** A dark tactile tablet floats above glossy olive cylinders. Strong directional light produces cream highlights and deep green shadows; an enlarged blurred botanical silhouette softens the left edge. The set fills the screen, with the object held clear of the two text groups.

**Transfer:** Stage a new physical object with the same lighting and hierarchy. For an intangible product, use a single original physical metaphor with a credible material rather than a dashboard collage.

**Build:** Use three cylinders made from rectangular SVG bodies with elliptical tops, spanning roughly 280–460px in width. Shade bodies with deep olive-to-cream-to-olive horizontal gradients, add an elliptical contact shadow beneath the isolated object, and place a blurred leaf silhouette behind the plinths. Render the product at roughly 316px across, tilted slightly, with a soft shadow. Match the soft lighting; exact gradient stops are not essential.

### Transformation scene

**Use:** A signature process chapter on landing pages.

**Observed:** The object drops through a reflective waterline over a pale olive atmospheric field. Bubbles, refraction and suspended motion make the process tangible. Small translucent olive annotations sit to alternating sides; a very large low-contrast product-name line spans the bottom.

**Transfer:** Depict the new product's material change or process in one continuous visual language. Minimum version: three stills showing entry, interaction and result on the same backing, with the same side annotations.

**Build:** Use an olive gradient field, a thin irregular blue-green horizontal waterline near the upper third, a cutout or render at center and SVG bubble circles with translucent cream strokes. Layer soft reflections and a wide translucent text line behind the object; alternate compact annotation panels on the left and right. Use real liquid imagery where possible instead of flat clip art.

### Documentary image chapters

**Use:** Proof, use cases, application and editorial storytelling.

**Observed:** Sunlit fields, hands handling harvested produce, laboratory staff and people working with animals or crops. Images are warm, detailed and natural, often occupying one complete 720px half of a 900px section or a full-width interlude. Close framing favors hands, texture and practical activity.

**Transfer:** Show real users in the actual work setting, mixing establishing scenes with tactile close-ups. Keep colors natural and greens dominant without a uniform monochrome filter.

### Human closing collage

**Use:** Signature human counterweight to the product spectacle.

**Observed:** On an olive field, three differently sized rectangular photos of people and natural details cluster on the right, separated by generous open space and offset green squares. The largest portrait is lower and closer to the center than the two smaller images.

**Transfer:** Use original users, makers and material details; if photography is limited, combine one portrait with two tightly framed context details.

**Build:** Create a relative collage spanning about half the band. Place a roughly 280px square portrait lower-center, a 130px detail upper-left and a 145px portrait upper-right; attach 70px square blocks in proof green and deep olive at a few corners. Keep edges square and avoid a regular grid.

### Microscopic specimen art

**Only for:** science and material-explanation pages

**Use:** Immersive breaks in technical explanations.

**Observed:** Transparent rounded specimens float against a pale green shallow-focus background. Translucent edges and clustered botanical fragments suggest engineered natural material; one central specimen is sharp while others fall out of focus.

**Transfer:** Use a translucent cutaway of the new material or an original macro photograph with one sharp subject and softly blurred repetitions.

**Build:** For a simplified illustration, draw a rounded-square transparent vessel with a cream rim, fill it with small olive and cream circles and angular translucent fragments, and duplicate it behind at different sizes with 15–30px blur. Light the background with broad soft green shapes.

### Circular editorial triptych

**Only for:** company approach narratives

**Use:** Demonstrate a connection between disciplines or contexts.

**Observed:** Three 314px circular documentary images overlap slightly in a horizontal row, followed by two short body columns. Their subjects progress from controlled work to living detail to real-world use.

**Transfer:** Select three visibly related contexts for the new story; preserve the circular masks and close grouping.

### Packaging-led landscape

**Only for:** packaging-led product openings

**Use:** Alternate product-page opening.

**Observed:** An oversized package anchors the right half over real pasture or exaggerated produce, while identification and outcome copy occupy the left. Product and environment share plausible light, with darker lower regions helping tiny white text read clearly.

**Transfer:** Use a real package or product mockup with a relevant context and coherent light; do not replace it with a generic software window.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small olive botanical and mechanism symbols, usually 19–24px, support technical labels. Navigation uses a 12px four-part symbol; carousel controls use simple arrows inside 47px square buttons.

**Transfer:** Choose sparse organic or process-oriented symbols with similarly restrained detail. Use fine plus/minus signs for disclosures and plain arrows for navigation rather than colorful emoji.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** promise → tangible problem → product or process reveal → quantified proof → practical integration → availability → human invitation and contact

### Broad brand and product introduction

1. **promise** — Cinematic object opening followed by a small partner strip.
2. **problem framing** — Split media and consequence statement.
3. **product demonstration** — Long atmospheric transformation with benefit cards and side annotations.
4. **quantified proof** — Ledger split, then an environmental or operational value chapter.
5. **offering discovery** — Staggered product shelf followed by a map/update story and community image cards.
6. **closing call** — Human conversion band and cream Footer.

**Transfer:** Use the broadest promise first, then alternate spectacle with grounded proof; adapt the number and order of supporting chapters to the offer.

### Technical substantiation and reference

1. **orientation** — Short atmospheric banner and left-hand section index.
2. **mechanism explanation** — Introductory thesis, formulations, full-width microscopic image, staged diagrams and efficiency chart.
3. **practical use** — Two photographic method cards and short operational details.
4. **quantified proof** — Result badges, crop photograph and tabbed comparative chart.
5. **objection handling** — Sustainability disclosures, quality methods, specimen interlude and compatibility table.
6. **closing call** — Human conversion band and cream Footer.

**Transfer:** Separate reference navigation from the reading area and interleave technical density with material imagery.

### Explain a company's way of working

1. **promise** — Manifesto over sunlit documentary video.
2. **capability chapters** — Numbered split-media narratives moving between controlled work and real-world application.
3. **validation** — Circular image triptych, specimen glass chapter and human result image.
4. **closing call** — Human conversion band and cream Footer.

**Transfer:** Give each principle a concrete setting and a clear narrative rather than generic value cards.

### Introduce a practical product to new buyers

1. **promise** — Oversized package in a landscape with a pilot action.
2. **differentiation** — Three icon-led benefits followed by a full-width documentary image.
3. **product explanation** — What-it-is comparison and split-media mechanism steps.
4. **quantified proof** — Square result badges and category cards.
5. **practical integration** — Icon checklist beside a close-up of real use.
6. **availability** — Two format cards and request actions.
7. **closing call** — Product-specific human conversion band and cream Footer.

**Transfer:** Use a small trial or introductory action when that suits the new buying process; connect mechanisms to routine use.

### Tell an extended flagship technical product story

1. **promise** — Oversized package and short outcome statement in a green contextual stage.
2. **differentiation** — Three concise proof points and a split product-value chapter.
3. **product demonstration** — Large illustrated mechanism chapter with three reinforcing pathways.
4. **quantified proof** — Staggered photo result cards around a centered statement on olive.
5. **practical use** — Half-width application photograph beside ruled dosage and use rows.
6. **availability** — Format-card track.
7. **closing call** — Full-bleed landscape with separate upper-left and lower-right statements and actions.
8. **objection handling** — Long right-column FAQ, then olive Footer rather than the standard cream footer.

**Transfer:** Let technically demanding products earn a longer story, with proof and use instructions anchoring the extended visuals.

## Writing

Plainspoken confidence with a scientific backbone. Ambitious promises are expressed in everyday language, then supported with mechanisms, practical details and qualified results.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Farm Minerals names, logos, proprietary product names, package artwork, testimonials, regulatory assertions or performance numbers as claims for another product. Source examples illustrate cadence only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use you and your for outcomes and practical actions, we for the maker's convictions, and the product name for technical explanations.
- **Rhythm:** Short paired headlines, often two to seven words per phrase. Support lines are compact; technical paragraphs can be longer but lead with a direct statement. Periods and em dashes provide emphasis without exclamation marks.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the familiar category, then challenge its conventional form. | 2–6 words | “Fertilizer, Reinvented.” |
| Benefit heading | Two parallel outcomes or a compact three-verb action. | 3–9 words | “For Better Plants. For Healthier Animals.”<br>“Drop. Dissolve. Spray.” |
| Proof heading | Describe validation in plain, clipped language. | 2–6 words | “Field-Tested. Farmer-Approved.”<br>“We don’t trust theories alone” |
| Supporting line | A straightforward practical statement that connects a product behavior to familiar equipment or routine. | 10–30 words | “CropTab™ tablets are fully water-dispersible and designed for fast mixing, clean spraying, and precision delivery. Compatible with all standard agricultural equipment.” |
| Action | Verb plus concrete next step, not an abstract aspiration. | 2–4 words | “Explore Products”<br>“Start a Pilot”<br>“Read the science” |
| Closing invitation | Invite personal verification or turn a familiar cost into an advantage. | 5–16 words | “See the Difference for Yourself”<br>“Feed is your biggest cost. ElevateFeed™ turns it into your biggest advantage.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** engineered; tested; works; same routine; less waste; real results; designed for; available upon request

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; seamless synergy; unprecedented magic; game-changing solutions; hype without a mechanism

**Rules:**

- Lead with an outcome; explain the mechanism only once the reader knows why it matters.
- Pair quantitative claims with units, comparison conditions and qualifications.
- Use direct operational language for integration and availability.
- Let scientific specificity coexist with conversational headings; avoid turning the whole page into a paper.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening at 900px scene height with opposing headline groups.
- Use 49.8px/0.93 medium headings for cream-panel chapters.
- Maintain 26px desktop gutters and edge-to-edge 720px split media halves.
- Use 39–42px compact rectangular actions with tiny flanking dots.
- Give quantitative proof either ruled open rows or 97px green badges.
- Let packshots protrude above 5.5px-radius green product tiles.
- Include a tangible process scene and an offset human-photo collage.
- Keep ordinary surfaces flat and use 1px low-opacity olive rules.

### Don't

- Do not replace olive ink with generic charcoal across the page.
- Do not use pill buttons or 20px rounded corners on ordinary marketing cards.
- Do not introduce drop shadows beneath format cards; their separation comes from a 10% olive wash.
- Do not expand technical captions into oversized body text; retain the 12.4–15.6px compact register.
- Do not tint all photography green; retain natural skin, soil and daylight colors.
- Do not put every statistic into a dashboard widget; the main proof ledger stays open and ruled.
- Do not apply specimen-style 51.8px glass corners to standard editorial panels.
- Do not use multiple font families to imitate technical authority; this style uses one grotesk.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions declare 0.3s transitions; product-format cards declare 0.4s transitions.
- Scroll-led scenes reveal and reposition product imagery, words and annotations across extended chapters. The footer is arranged as a sticky reveal beneath the final section.
- Carousel controls declare an olive hover fill; many text actions only declare outline removal.

**Transfer:**

- Keep movement tied to the material process and the staged reveal of text, not unrelated decorative loops.
- For a static implementation, preserve the settled compositions and use a still sequence for the transformation story.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll timelines, transition-property assignments, most easing-to-element mappings and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- CSS includes breakpoint boundaries at 479/480px, 767/768px and 991/992px.
- Declared mobile side padding is 16px.

**Transfer:**

- At narrow widths, retain the opposing hierarchy by placing the opening promise above the object and the answer below it; keep the object dominant rather than making all elements centered and equal.
- Stack equal media/text halves while retaining full-width imagery. Convert the three-tile shelf and format track to a narrow track or vertical grouping with the same protruding silhouettes.
- Move the technical section index above the reading area so it does not consume the narrow text measure.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile compositions and exact type scaling are not established; these collapse rules are design guidance rather than verified layouts.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
