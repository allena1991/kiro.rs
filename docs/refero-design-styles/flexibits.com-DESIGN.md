# Flexibits — colorful app theatre

## Overview

Friendly, polished software marketing with a bright white foundation and bursts of candy color. A rounded humanist sans gives benefit statements an approachable confidence, while detailed interface imagery, floating icons and softly dimensional device scenes make the product tangible. Spacious chapters alternate with useful, text-rich cards; a charcoal variant brings the same playful language to dark product presentations.

### Essentials

- A product identity leads before the sales promise: center an icon-and-name lockup above a 40px/44px medium-weight benefit headline, with colorful oversized shapes cropped into both sides of the opening.
- Big chapter headings speak in paired tones, not tiny uppercase eyebrows: Effra 40px/44px muted lead-ins sit above 64px/64px dark statements, both at weight 500 and negative tracking.
- Actions feel like compact app controls: blue primary and pale-gray secondary pills are 44px high, have 24px corners and 31px horizontal padding, and sit 20px apart.
- Color arrives as theatrical intermissions: keep most of the landing page white or pale gray, reserve saturated yellow, violet and sky blue for discrete full-width proof or capability bands, and keep blue actions consistent throughout.
- The product floats rather than filling a browser template: stage real interfaces on isolated devices, layer small colorful symbols around them, and let selected media run beyond the 960px content width. When no device imagery exists, construct a legible interface fragment with floating capability icons.
- Feature explanations have a generous app-like shell: use two 460px cards separated by 40px, 20px corners and 50px padding; combine a colorful icon or device vignette, 40px title, 24px muted benefit and 18px explanatory copy.
- The closing is a miniature product theatre, not just another headline: place a centered icon, two-tone 44px identity, short summary, a 627px-wide rounded comparison or combination panel and paired actions before a black four-column footer. For one product, show two complementary modes or outcomes joined by a plus symbol.

**Notes:**

- The opening benefit headline is smaller than the major chapter claims. Preserve the identity lockup as a separate visual layer rather than turning the entire opening into a giant text heading.

### Containers and grids

- **content container**: max width 1024px · side padding 32px. Centered outer box yielding a 960px usable desktop content area.
- **feature grid**: 2 columns · gap 40px. Two equal 460px cards in the desktop content area; a lead card can span both columns.
- **opening stage**: max width 1800px. Oversized three-part stage with a centered information column and side artwork extending beyond the viewport.
- **closing panel**: max width 627px · 2 columns · gap 0px. Joined, symmetric product or mode presentation centered below a narrower summary.
- **plan grid** (only for pricing pages): 4 columns · gap 15px. Four equal plan columns within the content container.
- **portrait grid** (only for company pages): 6 columns · gap 0px. Six desktop portrait cells across, with names centered beneath.

## Components

### Global navigation

**Use:** Persistent compact orientation and conversion strip.

**CSS:** `position: fixed; height: 44px; background: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.8); backdrop-filter: saturate(1.8) blur(20px); z-index: 100`

**Text style:** `navigation` — Effra 500 · 17px/23px · tracking -0.006em

**Observed:** Brand mark at left, centered product and utility links, account action and smaller blue trial pill at right. Active product link gets a pale rounded background; product links include small app icons. Dark theme substitutes translucent charcoal.

**Transfer:** Keep this strip visually subordinate to the opening art.

### Primary button

**Use:** Main conversion action in opening and closing sections.

**CSS:** `display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; height: 44px; padding: 0px 31px; gap: 15px; border-radius: 24px; background: #408fff; color: #ffffff`

**Text style:** `action` — Effra 500 · 17px/28px

**Observed:** Usually paired with a quieter download action. Editorial or audience CTAs sometimes end in a right arrow; family and team choices use violet and gold fills.

**States:** Declared: hover underlines text, sets background-color #1a79ff and color #0d71ff; focus-visible behavior is unspecified.

### Secondary button

**Use:** Download, watch and learn actions beside or below primary actions.

**CSS:** `display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; height: 44px; padding: 0px 31px; gap: 15px; border-radius: 24px; background: rgba(116, 116, 128, 0.08); color: #408fff`

**Text style:** `action` — Effra 500 · 17px/28px

**Observed:** The video action has a leading play symbol. Learn-more variants use the slightly stronger neutral stage gray. Dark-theme download actions appear gray with white labels.

### Capability card

**Use:** Repeatable detailed feature explanation.

**CSS:** `background: #f7f7f7; border-radius: 20px; padding: 50px`

**Text style:** `heading` — Effra 500 · 40px/1.1 · tracking -0.016em

**Observed:** Icon or illustration comes first, followed by title, muted benefit line and paragraph. Title-to-benefit gap is 10px; explanatory paragraph follows the header by 30px. Media-rich variants reserve a broad top area.

**Transfer:** Pair a concrete capability name with an outcome, then describe a real use case.

### Joined offer panel

**Use:** Memorable closing product or mode comparison.

**CSS:** `display: flex; flex-direction: column; align-items: center; background: rgba(116, 116, 128, 0.08); border-radius: 50px 0px 0px 50px; padding: 48px 32px 41px`

**Observed:** This is the left half of a joined pair; mirror the corners on the right half. Each half stacks a name, short tagline and large app icon. A muted plus sits across the center seam.

**Transfer:** For a single-product offer use two complementary modes or benefits; preserve the equal halves and central connector.

### Footer

**Use:** Shared ending on every page type.

**CSS:** `background: #000000; padding: 40px 0px`

**Text style:** `body` — Effra 400 · 18px/28px

**Observed:** Four centered columns of company, product, account and contact links occupy the 960px content width. White medium-weight column headings, muted links and small social marks lead into a centered brand lockup, copyright and legal row below.

**Transfer:** Keep the bottom compact and utility-led; do not turn it into another large campaign.

## Imagery and graphics

### Translucent color wings

**Use:** Signature framing device around the opening identity and promise.

**Observed:** Oversized tilted rounded squares in cyan, violet, pink, green, yellow and orange overlap at the side edges, leaving the central copy white and legible. Translucent rim highlights and broad colored shadows make them feel like glossy app tiles. Side art is roughly 631px by 720px, partly offscreen.

**Transfer:** Use a small family of abstract rounded tiles that suggest the product's capabilities. The minimum version is layered translucent CSS shapes with soft colored glows; retain a clear central text lane.

**Build:** Create three rounded squares on each side, with varied sizes around 180px–350px, corner radii about one quarter of width and rotations near -15deg. Use separate cyan, violet, magenta, lime and gold translucent fills at 0.7–0.9 alpha, a thin rgba(255,255,255,0.25) inset rim and matching blurred shadows at 0.2 alpha. Offset an inner rounded outline by 12px; overlap and clip tiles beyond both viewport edges. Keep glow below the opaque center copy layer; exact gradient stops are not essential.

### Isolated device theatre

**Use:** Primary visual proof in the opening video and large capability chapters.

**Observed:** High-detail phones, tablets and laptops appear isolated on white, gray, black or saturated stages, sometimes at a shallow perspective with soft contact shadows. UI stays legible; small colored symbols and floating interface cards add depth. A 768px by 432px video sits beneath the opening actions; chapter devices can occupy most of the 960px content area.

**Transfer:** Use authentic product interfaces on one or two believable devices. Without device renders, compose crisp interface panels with restrained perspective, soft shadows and a few capability symbols rather than stock photography.

### Floating capability vignette

**Use:** Illustrated media at the top of feature cards and centered capability introductions.

**Observed:** Small app-style icons, pastel speech bubbles, interface fragments and directional arrows orbit a main product object. Card imagery is often about 360px wide; fades into the neutral card backing avoid a hard rectangular image edge.

**Transfer:** Compose a single readable interaction, not an entire dashboard. Use two or three small colored supporting symbols and leave room for the text below.

**Build:** Place a centered UI panel or rounded icon on a transparent canvas; distribute 3–5 smaller circles or rounded squares around its perimeter. Use soft low-opacity radial halos behind selected symbols and short curved SVG arrows only where they explain a real flow.

### Widget ribbons

**Use:** Wide visual proof of variety within a capability chapter.

**Observed:** Two horizontal rows of compact light and dark interface widgets extend beyond the viewport. Mixed square and wide formats have 28px corners and fine neutral outlines, with vivid interface accents against mostly black or white panels.

**Transfer:** Show multiple useful outputs from the new product, not decorative blank tiles. Keep real content readable, vary tile width and let both rows crop naturally at the edges.

**Build:** Use two nonwrapping flex rows of populated rounded panels, offset the second row horizontally and clip overflow at the page edges. Mix approximately 155px-tall square and double-width panels with 1px neutral borders.

### Capability backdrop

**Use:** Graphic context for selected saturated feature stages.

**Observed:** A violet stage carries a low-contrast dotted world-map silhouette behind a white interface card; another dark announcement uses concentric low-opacity rainbow pools beneath a laptop. These are supporting atmosphere rather than full-page wallpaper.

**Transfer:** Use a relevant diagram silhouette or soft colored pool beneath the main product object; keep the copy plain and high contrast.

**Build:** For a diagram, mask a repeated SVG dot pattern into a relevant silhouette at roughly 15% white opacity behind the media. For pools, layer several broad elliptical gradients under the product, with violet, blue and gold alpha fills fading outward into the dark stage.

### Friendly people illustration

**Use:** Audience-oriented shared-use offer.

**Observed:** Two stylized people in casual clothes interact around a large purple group icon with a pale circular backdrop. Simplified shaded forms, warm skin tones and long limbs make a relaxed explanatory scene, around 400px square.

**Transfer:** Use a similarly friendly small group vignette when the offer involves collaboration; do not substitute corporate stock-photo handshakes.

### Color-circle opening

**Only for:** dark product pages

**Use:** Alternate signature wing treatment.

**Observed:** Large overlapping flat circles in cyan, violet, green, pink and yellow carry bold white action glyphs. The circles extend off the page edges against charcoal and are less glassy than the light opening.

**Transfer:** Swap rounded translucent tiles for saturated circles with a few relevant solid white capability symbols.

**Build:** Place 3 circles of varied diameter on each edge, partly clipped, with overlapping solid color fields and oversized white glyphs centered inside selected circles. Leave the center lane charcoal.

### Confetti celebration stage

**Only for:** celebration-focused feature demonstrations

**Use:** A brief festive chapter on a black stage.

**Observed:** Colorful softly glowing dots scatter over black around a phone and paired celebratory symbols. The field supports the interface without becoming a page-wide texture.

**Transfer:** Reserve this treatment for an equivalent time-sensitive or celebratory benefit.

**Build:** Scatter small SVG circles in gold, violet, cyan and pink with varied opacity from 0.1 to 0.5; blur a minority of dots and concentrate them behind the isolated phone, leaving the text zone clear.

### Portrait wall

**Only for:** company pages

**Use:** Team and contributor section.

**Observed:** Natural-color casual headshots appear in circular white-rimmed frames with soft shadows on an unboxed white page. Six columns of people are followed by a smaller contributor group, names and subdued role captions.

**Transfer:** Use real human photographs and consistent circular framing, allowing informal backgrounds and expressions.

### Icons

**Observed:** Colorful app-like symbols rather than one uniform mono icon set: 48px capability icons, larger 64px–80px chapter marks, 24px action icons and small footer marks. Some are simple colored outlines, others are solid rounded app symbols; arrows are thin and blue or white.

**Transfer:** Choose a coherent friendly family of simple rounded glyphs, assign capability colors consistently, and reserve full-color app icons for product identity or integration proof.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Color-wing product opening

**Observed:** A 44px translucent Global navigation floats above a white stage. After 90px top padding, center the product icon-and-name lockup in a clear lane roughly 575px wide, framed by Translucent color wings cropped into both sides. The lockup is the largest identity object, followed by the 40px/44px heading and 24px/32px supporting statement with a second short reassurance. A 35px information-stack rhythm and 20px action gap keep this central block ordered. Primary button and Secondary button precede a centered 768px by 432px product video beginning around 624px down the page. Within the first 900px, the navigation, lockup, benefit headline, support, both actions and the upper part of the video are visible; the watch action and three award/rating badges continue below.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's icon and readable identity above a compact benefit statement. Frame it with the abstract tile wings even without proprietary art, and use a polished interface montage or static demonstration in the video position. Company pages open with a smaller arc of floating icons above a 40px mission statement; pricing pages open compactly with paired app icons and a two-tone offer identity before the plans.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a left-copy/right-browser split, a full-screen photograph or an oversized 100px headline that displaces the product identity.

**Text style:** `heading` — Effra 500 · 40px/1.1 · tracking -0.016em

### Paired chapter heading

**Observed:** Center a small capability mark above a muted 40px setup and 64px main payoff, or split a single 64px line into dark and muted phrases. Use Lead for the support line with about 10px–15px separation. Sections use 100px vertical breathing room and a centered 960px stage.

**Transfer:** Write a short contextual setup and a concrete payoff. Apply this device to the new product's main benefit, not necessarily its first section.

**Text style:** `display` — Effra 500 · 64px/1 · tracking -0.015em

### Proof intermission

**Observed:** Proof band holds either white 320px-wide review cards with 20px corners and gold stars, or a publisher-logo strip above a centered Lora italic quotation. The review row extends beyond the content container and has round black previous/next controls underneath; editorial attribution remains sans-serif.

**Transfer:** Use actual short user reviews and attributable press feedback. Preserve the bright band and change the proof content, not the rhetorical tone.

**Text style:** `quotation` — Lora 400 italic · 32px/38px

### Capability chapter and grid

**Observed:** Paired chapter heading and Isolated device theatre introduce a capability family. Capability cards follow in a two-column grid, sometimes preceded by Lead feature tint spanning both columns. Text cards and richer media cards share the same flat shell.

**Transfer:** Group related capabilities into a visual demonstration and supporting explanations rather than a wall of miniature cards.

### Split color stage

**Observed:** Violet stage or Sky stage places a 64px heading, benefit line and paragraph in one half of the content container, with a device or floating UI card in the other. A low-contrast backdrop stays behind the media. Some upright phones crop at the bottom edge.

**Transfer:** Use one saturated stage to isolate a distinctive capability, with a short readable explanation and one dominant product object.

### Trust statement

**Observed:** A centered line illustration above a muted setup and 64px statement creates a calm trust chapter. A short body paragraph sits below with 40px separation; one blue action follows with 60px breathing room. Light theme uses white, dark theme black.

**Transfer:** Use for a verifiable assurance or principled product decision; keep it short rather than turning it into a badge collection.

### Joined closing offer

**Observed:** A star-like product mark leads a centered Closing identity, Closing summary and a short muted body paragraph. Joined offer panel sits below as two equal halves linked by a plus, then Primary button and Secondary button repeat. Footer follows after a generous white or charcoal buffer.

**Transfer:** Close by making the offer concrete: summarize what the reader receives and show complementary product modes or benefits in the joined panel.

**Text style:** `closing-identity` — Effra 500 · 44px/48px · tracking -0.0268em

### Pricing comparison and questions

**Only for:** pricing pages

**Observed:** Compact identity opens into Billing selector and four tall plan cards. A centered feature heading and larger product selector introduce a 960px-wide comparison table with thin horizontal rules and a shaded premium column. Category rows and FAQ rows use small icons and chevrons. The plan grid and interval selector repeat after FAQs immediately before Footer.

**Transfer:** Put purchase choices first, detailed comparison second, practical objections third, then repeat the actionable choice rather than ending with only prose.

### Company introduction and people

**Only for:** company pages

**Observed:** Floating colored app symbols form a shallow arc over a centered 40px mission line with a gold phrase, muted 24px support and an 18px paragraph. A pale social card sits beside stacked pill actions. A pale-gray recruiting band leads to an unboxed portrait wall, contributor row and centered mailing address separated by a thin rule before Footer.

**Transfer:** Present mission, useful contact routes, an invitation to participate and actual people; preserve the compact editorial tone.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Product promise → tangible demonstration → external confidence → capability chapters → reassurance → concrete offer → utility footer; adapt the depth and ordering to the page's decision.

### Flagship light product landing page

1. **promise** — Color-wing identity stage, compact benefit headline and paired actions.
2. **product demonstration** — Centered video followed by watch action and three award/rating badges.
3. **social proof** — Yellow review band, cross-platform compatibility and editorial endorsement.
4. **capability chapters** — Large two-tone headings, isolated device scenes, paired feature grids and occasional saturated split stages.
5. **reassurance** — Shared-use offer and concise privacy statement interrupt the long feature narrative.
6. **closing call** — Joined offer panel and repeated actions, then black four-column footer.

**Transfer:** Use the flagship light treatment as the default for a new landing page, adjusting feature depth to the product.

### Dark product landing page

1. **promise** — Charcoal opening with flat colored action circles, identity and paired actions.
2. **proof** — Video, award badges and violet review and editorial bands.
3. **capability chapters** — Charcoal feature cards alternate with black demonstration stages, widget ribbons and warm-yellow feature stage.
4. **reassurance** — Privacy statement and shared-use illustration.
5. **closing call** — Inverse joined offer panel, paired actions and the same black footer.

**Transfer:** Use the dark variant as a complete product-theme decision, preserving the same hierarchy and warm icon language.

### Company and people introduction

1. **promise** — Colorful icon arc and centered mission statement.
2. **contact routes** — Social card beside press and contact actions.
3. **invitation** — Pale recruiting band with a single action.
4. **human proof** — Circular portrait wall and contributor group.
5. **ending** — Mailing address under a rule, then shared black footer.

**Transfer:** Use a short narrative with real people and practical contact information rather than the long product-feature structure.

### Purchase decision and comparison

1. **offer** — Compact paired icons, two-tone identity and brief summary.
2. **pricing** — Interval selector above four plan cards, one blue-tinted recommendation.
3. **comparison** — Product tabs and categorized feature table.
4. **objection handling** — Rule-separated FAQ list with icons.
5. **closing choice** — Repeated interval selector and plan grid, then shared black footer.

**Transfer:** Keep the decision accessible both before and after detailed questions.

## Writing

Warm, capable and benefit-first, with the enthusiasm of people who enjoy well-made apps. Confidence comes through concrete tasks and clear outcomes rather than corporate abstraction.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Flexibits product names, logos, illustrations, award marks, ratings, prices, platform commitments, subscription terms, privacy claims, user identities or quoted endorsements as new-product facts. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your; use we for the makers when explaining care or principles. Name the product directly when describing what it does.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are short benefit statements or rhythmic fragments. Support is usually one or two sentences, followed by a fuller practical paragraph when needed. Occasional exclamation marks and playful punch lines are welcome, but most explanations stay straightforward.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening benefit | A familiar product category gets an energetic upgrade metaphor. | 4–7 words | “Give Your Calendar Superpowers” |
| Chapter pair | A short setup leads to an emphatic payoff, often with parallel phrasing. | 2–6 words per line | “See Everything.”<br>“Miss Nothing.”<br>“Your Contacts”<br>“None of Our Business” |
| Support | State a concrete convenience in plain reader-oriented language. | 1–2 sentences | “Some prefer dark. Some prefer light. Whatever style you like, it’s your choice.”<br>“Your contacts have never looked so good!” |
| Feature explanation | Describe an action and its immediate result, using a small real-world example where helpful. | 2–4 sentences | “Instantly find who you’re looking for by typing a contact’s name or details.” |
| Primary action | Short verb phrase naming a low-friction next step. | 2–4 words | “Start for Free”<br>“Watch the Video” |
| Company statement | Use inclusive maker language and explain the human benefit. | Short claim followed by explanation | “We create apps”<br>“that are enjoyable and flexible.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** beautifully organized; quickly; easily; your choice; works with; powerful; intuitive; better together

**Avoid:** synergize; enterprise transformation; disruptive paradigm; revolutionary ecosystem; unlock unprecedented value

**Rules:**

- Name what becomes easier before explaining implementation.
- Use periods to make short parallel fragments feel conversational and assured.
- Keep feature titles literal; let benefit lines carry the charm.
- Pair enthusiastic claims with a concrete action the interface can perform.
- Use direct questions for objections and clear verbs for actions.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 40px opening benefits and 64px chapter claims at weight 500.
- Preserve the 1024px outer container with 32px side padding for a 960px content stage.
- Keep primary and secondary actions 44px high with 24px corners.
- Give capability cards 50px padding and 40px inter-card gaps.
- Let selected product media exceed the content width while keeping prose constrained.
- Include both a colorful proof intermission and the miniature joined closing offer.
- Carry a selected dark theme through charcoal canvas, translucent cards and inverse text.
- Keep the shared footer black, compact and four-column at desktop.

### Don't

- Do not make every heading 64px; the opening benefit and feature titles are 40px.
- Do not use heavy 700-weight marketing headlines in place of the medium-weight humanist voice.
- Do not put gradient fills behind every card; most capability shells are flat #f7f7f7.
- Do not apply portrait-style shadows to the flat feature-card system.
- Do not replace the pill actions with sharp-cornered rectangles or full-width bars on desktop.
- Do not color every paragraph; use dark body copy, muted benefit lines and restrained blue links.
- Do not treat the charcoal variant as a neon cyber aesthetic; its icons and typography stay friendly.
- Do not turn the utility footer into a giant wordmark billboard.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions declare hover underlines and blue color shifts; primary and secondary fill changes are specified in their component states.
- Some visual elements declare transform 0.25s ease-in-out; selected segmented controls declare width 0.2s ease-in-out with height and left included.
- Four elements declare a 32s linear infinite slide animation.

**Transfer:**

- Use steady horizontal movement for output ribbons when implementing their animated version; ensure the arrangement remains populated and readable when stationary.
- Keep segmented-control selection visibly anchored by the moving white active surface.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll-trigger timing, video sequencing and which visual elements use each transform transition are unspecified.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared layout thresholds include 576px, 640px, 768px, 835px, 992px, 1024px, 1200px and 1400px.

**Transfer:**

- Recommended adaptation: narrow the center text lane first and move the decorative wings farther offscreen so they do not compete with copy.
- Recommended adaptation: stack feature and plan cards, retain their content hierarchy, and keep the joined closing offer understandable as two complementary halves.
- Recommended adaptation: keep widget ribbons clipped horizontally rather than squeezing every widget into a tiny grid.

**Unknown:**

- Narrow-screen type sizes, exact stacking thresholds and mobile navigation behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
