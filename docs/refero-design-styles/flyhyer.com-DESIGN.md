# Hyer — altitude and assurance

## Overview

An expansive, editorial identity that pairs oversized black grotesk lettering with pale skies, natural-light photography and sharply divided color fields. White space carries the main narrative, while midnight blue, terracotta and muted sea blue give selected chapters a more tactile character. It suits premium services and considered products that want to feel personal rather than ostentatious; the imagery is immersive and the information density stays low.

### Essentials

- The identity is an object, not a small masthead: a 720px × 282px wordmark occupies half the desktop width, balanced by a compact 60px/0.91 two-line promise on the right. Use the new identity as equally monumental typography.
- The opening is an atmospheric stage: a full 1440px × 900px sky scene with a single isolated object across the middle, navigation above and a support line plus two actions at the bottom. A beautifully lit product cutout against cloudlike light is the fallback when no spatial render exists.
- Bold grotesk headlines behave like solid shapes: weight 700, 52px–63.3px at line height 1, with -0.01em to -0.02em tracking; a 187px/0.8 statement provides a second monumental typographic moment.
- Small actions counterbalance huge type: 48px-high dark pills with 16.9px bold labels, 21.6px horizontal padding, and 48px circular icon controls. Content panels remain square-edged rather than pill-shaped.
- Color arrives in architectural chapters, not decorative sprinkles: the default is a predominantly white page, interrupted by full-width midnight splits and a terracotta field occupying one third of a three-column band; pale blue atmosphere returns around the major invitation.
- Photography and diagram share the same stage: edge-to-edge portrait media sits beside flat color and a fine dashed curved path; feature symbols sit in 93px dashed frames with a rounded lower-right corner. Include both an atmospheric media moment and this fine-line diagram language.
- The invitation becomes the headline: a centered 130.8px bold text action over a 703px pale-blue cloud band precedes a 634px near-black footer. Preserve a monumental invitation and the dark editorial footer, wherever the narrative places its final conversion.

**Notes:**

- Use bold, sentence-case grotesk text, not all-caps display or serif accents. Large statements tighten to -0.02em; ordinary section titles use -0.01em.
- The oversized identity is vector artwork sized to a 720px × 282px box, not another measured text recipe. Fit a new wordmark into the same half-width visual role.
- Body leading is unusually close at 1.1. Keep paragraphs short and place breathing room between blocks rather than inside lines.
- Use the viewport formulas to retain the editorial proportions; do not replace every heading with one fixed desktop size.

### Containers and grids

- **page-grid**: max width 1920px · 24 columns · side padding 60px. A wide editorial grid; at 1440px each grid division is 60px. Content occupies 1320px inside the default side insets, while media bands go full bleed.
- **inset-stage**: 2 columns · gap 0px · side padding 120px. A 1200px-wide content composition at desktop, usually a 600px photograph beside an index or narrative block.
- **right-weighted-benefits**: 2 columns · gap 60px. A 720px statement on the right half with a 660px two-column benefit list beneath; the left half stays deliberately open.
- **category-band**: 3 columns · gap 0px · side padding 0px. Three equal 480px-wide, 806px-tall fields with text, photography and diagram roles.
- **feature-columns**: 2 columns · gap 60px. Feature groups use 360px-wide items beside a narrow left editorial statement.
- **catalog-media**: 2 columns · gap 60px. Two 399px × 506px portrait images occupy the left part of a wide row, leaving substantial right-side white space.

## Imagery and graphics

### Atmospheric object stage

**Use:** The default opening's dominant visual.

**Observed:** A pale-blue sky fades into warm white clouds and a white lower edge. One silver-white aircraft is seen in three-quarter view, floating across roughly the middle two thirds of the width with its nose pointing down-right. The subject is separated from the environment and no frame contains it.

**Transfer:** Use a single finely lit product, tool or symbolic object with an oblique silhouette. If a spatial model is unavailable, use a high-quality transparent photograph with controlled lighting over the same atmospheric backing.

**Build:** Make a full-bleed sky layer fading from desaturated blue at the top to white below. Layer broad irregular white cloud masks across the lower two thirds with feathered edges. Place one transparent object render above them, about 900px wide in a 1440px composition, keeping navigation and headline regions uncluttered.

### Monumental identity

**Use:** The opening's typographic anchor.

**Observed:** A heavy dark wordmark spans half the page width and reaches approximately 282px in height; it is much larger than the actual promise headline.

**Transfer:** Set the new identity or a short product noun as a single substantial graphic, maintaining its natural letterforms instead of imitating a particular logo.

**Build:** Use an SVG wordmark in a 720px × 282px view box at desktop, or fit bold grotesk text to that box. Align it to the 60px inset, fill with ink and avoid outlines, extrusion or shadows.

### Dashed journey graphic

**Use:** Flat graphic counterpart to category photography.

**Observed:** A thin, dark dashed curve rises from a small circular endpoint, bends into an elongated S and exits near the upper-right edge of a terracotta panel. A small solid object silhouette sits on the path.

**Transfer:** Represent a process or movement relevant to the new product with a small silhouette, fine path and endpoint rather than a full illustrated scene.

**Build:** In a 480px × 806px SVG field, draw a 1px ink cubic path from the lower-left interior toward the upper-right, with two broad bends and a 6px dash separated by 7px gaps. Add a roughly 16px endpoint ring and a small solid silhouette tangent to the curve. Clip the path at the panel boundary.

### Cloud closing layer

**Use:** Atmospheric backing for the large invitation.

**Observed:** Very soft, near-white cloud forms rise from both lower corners against a faint blue fade. Contrast stays low so the black lettering remains dominant.

**Transfer:** Retain the same pale material even when the product has no connection to skies.

**Build:** Start with Cloud invitation atmosphere. Add overlapping white radial-gradient ellipses in the lower 429px, feathered with broad transparent falloffs. Let them enter from both lower edges and leave the center mostly clear; an organic SVG alpha mask can break the ellipses into cloud contours.

### Natural-light editorial photography

**Use:** Category tiles, inset gallery, index selection and catalog images.

**Observed:** Architectural stone, garden greenery, coastline, transportation and expansive landscapes are photographed with daylight and believable color. Images are tightly filled with object-fit cover, generally square-edged; selected broad images and hero fields curve only at the lower-right corner.

**Transfer:** Choose a coherent set of real contexts and materials appropriate to the new product. Preserve natural light, muted color and confident full-panel framing rather than filling every area with isolated UI.

### Product in hand

**Use:** A dark half-width support chapter.

**Observed:** A hand holding a phone emerges from the bottom of a nearly black blue field. The device is slightly tilted; a restrained interface remains visible without becoming a dense marketing dashboard.

**Transfer:** Photograph a real product being used against the dark backing. For software, a simple legible screen in a hand-held device is the minimum equivalent; keep details secondary to the tactile silhouette.

### Monochrome human story

**Only for:** company-story pages

**Use:** Portraits, an environment photograph and the closing human image.

**Observed:** Black-and-white photographs sit directly on a continuous muted blue canvas. Portraits are tall, around 360px × 506px; the closing person-in-context image fills a 600px × 809px right panel.

**Transfer:** Use candid or lightly directed monochrome portraits with environmental context; pair them with restrained biographical copy.

### Membership object plates

**Only for:** membership catalogs

**Use:** Paired tier introductions.

**Observed:** White and charcoal membership cards are shown diagonally against dark photographic backings in portrait image slots; the object, rather than a web pricing table, introduces the tier.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small thin-line ink symbols appear at 37px in feature frames and 24px in circular social controls; arrow symbols mark downward movement and gallery controls. Feature frames are dashed with a rounded lower-right corner. The linework reads delicate rather than heavy.

**Transfer:** Use a consistent outline icon family and normalize visible stroke optically; do not apply the SVG's internal 8px stroke as an 8px screen-space line. Use symbols only for meaningful feature categories, not every paragraph.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Atmospheric promise → considered explanation → tangible offerings and contexts → human reassurance → signature invitation or objection handling → shared dark ending.

### Flagship marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Monumental identity and an isolated object share a sky stage with a compact offset headline and two small actions.
2. **Value explanation** — A right-weighted statement is supported by ruled benefits.
3. **Offering discovery** — Three contrasting material fields lead into a photographic index.
4. **Human reassurance** — A dark tactile product split is followed by a giant principle and framed-symbol benefits.
5. **Experiential proof** — A wide editorial gallery uses visible arrows and a large fractional counter.
6. **Closing call** — Cloud atmosphere carries an enormous text action, then the dark editorial footer closes the page.

**Transfer:** Balance identity spectacle with concrete explanations and real-world proof; preserve the material contrast without requiring the same section sequence.

### Multi-offering or catalog overview

1. **Promise** — A full-width environmental photograph carries a compact right-side introduction.
2. **Capability chapters** — Ruled chapter headers separate groups, pairing a left title with far-right explanatory text and portrait media below.
3. **Offer comparison** — Related object plates introduce two tiers, with plain benefits and price text beneath.
4. **Closing navigation** — A final rule and white breathing space lead directly to the shared dark footer.

**Transfer:** Reuse a consistent editorial chapter structure across product families; the ending need not add an unrelated conversion panel.

### Focused service-detail and lead-generation page

1. **Promise** — A photographic hero carries a large white left-side title, concise support and a reversed pill action.
2. **Value explanation** — A mixed-gray statement introduces a framed-symbol proof group.
3. **Capability demonstration** — Alternating dark and light image-and-copy splits expand the benefits.
4. **Supporting proof** — A larger framed-symbol grid gives practical details.
5. **Conversion** — A large terracotta statement accompanies a narrow two-field lead form.
6. **Objection handling** — Oversized ruled questions precede white breathing room and the shared dark footer.

**Transfer:** Use the focused photographic opening and local form variant for a service that benefits from personal consultation.

### Company narrative and trust building

1. **Identity** — A monumental wordmark sits on a continuous muted-blue field, followed by a right-weighted introduction and wide monochrome photograph.
2. **Human proof** — Founding narrative and two portrait columns establish the people behind the service.
3. **Principles and social proof** — Framed-symbol benefits and a concise client quotation carry the same tinted canvas.
4. **Track record** — Alternating year blocks sit around a fine dashed horizontal timeline.
5. **Closing belief** — A large editorial statement is paired with a tall monochrome portrait before the shared dark footer.

**Transfer:** Keep this continuous tinted, monochrome treatment scoped to company storytelling rather than applying it to every landing page.

## Writing

Confident, personal and service-minded, with premium positioning expressed through care and selection rather than superlatives. Technology is mentioned as an enabler, while reassurance and practical flexibility carry the persuasion.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Hyer name, logo, registered marks, travel-specific claims, client quotations, people, dates, prices, membership names or legal and operational assertions. Source examples illustrate rhythm only; new claims must belong to the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use we and our for the provider, you and your for the reader. Mix direct invitations with plain descriptions of what the service does.
- **Rhythm:** Very short display statements alternate with complete explanatory sentences. Benefit labels are usually two or three words; body copy is broken into short paragraphs, with occasional plain-language restatement.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A concise positioning statement, often with a full stop and a deliberate two-line break. | 2–4 words | “Beyond Travel.” |
| Supporting line | A short list of connected offerings, stated without hype. | 4–8 words | “Flights, stays and personalised experiences.” |
| Section statement | An outcome-driven sentence that joins quality, ease and personal service. | 8–18 words | “Carefully selected stays, seamless travel and a more personal experience along the way.” |
| Benefit label | A short adjective-and-noun or compact assurance phrase. | 2–3 words | “Flexible by Design”<br>“Personal Support” |
| Primary action | A calm, literal invitation or destination; no urgency. | 2–4 words | “Contact us”<br>“Get started now”<br>“Let's Start” |
| Question | A real first-person customer concern with a direct, explanatory answer. | 7–14 words | “Can I book accommodation for longer stays?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** carefully selected; personal; thoughtful; flexible; comfort; experience; trusted; designed around; we help you; from start to finish

**Avoid:** disruptive; game-changing; unprecedented luxury; hustle; act now; exclusive once-in-a-lifetime; technical jargon as a headline

**Rules:**

- Lead with the reader's desired experience, then describe how the product makes it possible.
- Use short labels for benefits and complete sentences for explanation.
- Follow a dense explanation with a plain-language summary when needed; 'In simple terms' is a characteristic bridge.
- Keep primary actions specific and understated; the size of the closing invitation supplies emphasis.
- Avoid exclamation marks and inflated urgency.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Give the identity a half-width 720px × 282px visual role at desktop.
- Keep the default promise at 60px/0.91 and the main section statements at 63.3px/1.
- Use 60px side insets and 120px for deliberately inset compositions.
- Pair 19.7px body copy with close 1.1 leading and short paragraph blocks.
- Use 48px action pills and 48px circular controls to counterbalance the display scale.
- Keep midnight, terracotta and photography as large flat neighboring fields.
- Include fine dashed diagrams and framed 37px outline symbols alongside the large media.
- Preserve the large fractional gallery counter and the text-only monumental invitation.
- Finish with the shared near-black editorial footer, including ample vertical separation between navigation and secondary details.

### Don't

- Do not turn the unboxed benefit groups into rounded, elevated feature cards.
- Do not spread terracotta across every link, button and small icon; it belongs to concentrated graphic fields and scoped emphasis.
- Do not replace the natural-light imagery with highly saturated stock collages.
- Do not give all image corners the same broad radius; the characteristic contour is selective and lower-right.
- Do not introduce a second decorative font to manufacture an editorial feel; the hierarchy comes from one grotesk's scale and weight.
- Do not use 8px screen-space strokes for the delicate feature symbols.
- Do not make the catalog media fill every available column; the open right-hand area is intentional.
- Do not reduce the closing invitation to a standard small button or put it inside a floating card.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared button transitions animate color, background-color and border-color over 0.3s with cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1).
- Declared navigation and footer-link color transitions use 0.5s with cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1).
- Question triggers declare background-color transitions over 0.6s.

**Transfer:**

- Keep interaction motion concentrated on the small controls and link color changes; retain the atmospheric opening's strong resting composition.

**Unknown:**

- Object-stage animation path, scroll choreography and gallery transition timing are not established.
- Focus-visible designs are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 640px, 1024px, 1200px, 1440px, 1620px and 1920px; the flagship also has a 900px breakpoint.
- Type and several spaces use viewport-relative sizing.

**Transfer:**

- For narrower layouts, preserve the hierarchy of identity, promise and object rather than reducing all three equally; stack the supporting baseline and actions when needed.
- Stack full-height split chapters and the three-material category band while retaining distinct color and media fields.
- Keep fine index contours and symbol frames at a legible size when editorial columns stack.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, menu behavior, column-stacking order and hero-object positioning are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
