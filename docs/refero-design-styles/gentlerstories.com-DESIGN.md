# Gentler Stories — human momentum

## Overview

An encouraging, image-rich product style that makes sophisticated personal technology feel companionable. Bold, tightly set grotesk typography sits on a mostly white canvas with pale gray panels, warm orange accents and occasional jewel-colored showcases. Natural outdoor photography, oversized device compositions and playful illustration provide the emotional texture; dense product detail is balanced by spacious promises and a generous final invitation.

### Essentials

- Human context and tangible product share the first screen: full-bleed warm photography, white 60px/1.2 bold Inter headline at -0.03em, left-aligned copy and a large handheld device on the right.
- Headlines are direct and closely knit rather than typographic spectacle: 60px weight 700 for the opening and chapter-scale statements, with the closing invitation enlarged to 80px/1.2 at -0.03em.
- Actions are soft rectangles, not pills: 60px high, 16px corners, contrasting white over photography and black on the pale closing stage; the final action grows to 252×84px.
- Color belongs to the product stages: white and #f2f2f7 carry the long page, while blue, coral, pink and deep violet stay inside 24px-radius feature panels. Orange punctuates phrases and small actions rather than flooding the canvas.
- The product is an object surrounded by evidence: use large sharply rendered devices, overlapping screens and small floating information cards; without device media, construct an equally prominent framed product view and orbit a few genuine outputs around it.
- A humane proof-and-invitation device anchors the experience: mix varied-height review cards in three 363px columns with 16px gaps, then give a 160px product emblem and an 80px two-line invitation their own spacious stage before the compact black footer.

## Imagery and graphics

### Warm lived-in hero

**Use:** Default first-screen emotional setting.

**Observed:** Full-bleed late-day field photography, yellow-green sky and golden grass; three moving people cross the upper half while a hand holding a sharp orange-edged phone dominates the right. Darkening toward the bottom gives white type and proof room to read.

**Transfer:** Photograph people using the new product in a believable environment with warm directional light. Without a custom shoot, use one strong contextual photograph and a carefully masked product view, not a collage of unrelated stock portraits.

### Device constellation

**Use:** Signature capability and portfolio stages.

**Observed:** Oversized phones and watches tilt, overlap and rise beyond panel boundaries; crisp UI fragments float around a main object. Light products use white screens and orange hardware edges; the performance variant uses black screens, saturated charts and metallic hardware.

**Transfer:** Make a large framed product view and surround it with a few useful output cards. Preserve recognizable hierarchy and readable focal detail rather than reproducing every interface control.

**Build:** Use a tall rounded device shell with a dark inner bezel and a clipped real interface image; layer two or three secondary screens behind it with small opposing rotations. Place 4–8 small output cards to either side with soft shadows, leaving the central screen unobscured. Clip the whole tableau at the stage bottom.

### Companion illustration

**Use:** Small emotional counterweight within product demonstrations.

**Observed:** Friendly orange heart-like character with a face and limbs appears in cheerful flat scenes, including a rainbow and fantastical ride; pastel blue or pink backing and rounded silhouettes keep it approachable.

**Transfer:** Use an original simple character or expressive product emblem tied to the new product. Minimum version is one warm rounded mascot with two-dot eyes and a single clear pose, not a complex copied scene.

**Build:** Draw a lopsided rounded SVG body in orange, simple curved limbs and a small smiling face. Place over a pale circular or cloud-like backdrop with two or three bright rounded shapes; keep hard outlines minimal and avoid gritty textures.

### Soft luminous reassurance

**Use:** A visually distinct trust or reassurance panel.

**Observed:** A broad rounded panel carries a lock symbol, centered headline and support above a phone emerging from the bottom. Pale cream and blush at the top intensify to orange and pink around the lower device; the dark campaign uses violet, electric blue and teal light.

**Transfer:** Use the glow to frame an honest assurance or product architecture explanation. The product remains crisp while the backing is diffuse.

**Build:** Layer two radial gradients over a pale neutral panel: orange at roughly 35% 90% and pink at 80% 85%, each fading to transparent across about half the panel. Add a white-to-transparent layer over the top third; keep exact stops secondary to the broad soft glow.

### Monochrome action chapter

**Only for:** dark performance campaigns

**Use:** High-energy hero and capability transitions.

**Observed:** Black-and-white cyclists and runners use strong panning blur, oversized environmental framing and dark fades behind vivid device UI.

**Transfer:** Use high-contrast documentary action photography relevant to the new audience; keep chroma concentrated in product output rather than tinting the photograph.

### Portrait quotation scene

**Only for:** founder or practitioner stories

**Use:** Personal authorship and design intent.

**Observed:** A 1120px-wide rounded woodland-running photograph places the person on the right and a 20px white quotation on the left. A huge translucent Georgia opening quote sits behind the text; directional background blur conveys motion.

**Transfer:** Use an authentic practitioner in context, keep one side calm enough for prose and apply localized shading rather than a heavy opaque text card.

**Build:** Clip a contextual portrait to 24px corners. Place a soft black radial gradient under the left half and a 400px Georgia opening quotation mark at low opacity behind a roughly half-width text column.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small monochrome social marks appear around 24px in footer groups. Laurel branches are taller decorative silhouettes; action symbols are solid rather than thin outline UI icons. A lock is the prominent reassurance symbol.

**Transfer:** Use familiar solid symbols sparingly and keep them subordinate to product imagery; reserve laurel-like ornament for genuine credentials.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Human product opening

**Observed:** A full-bleed photographic stage is 1060px tall with a fixed navigation wrapper above it. Back to front: warm contextual image, dark bottom and subtle top fades, left-aligned product identity and white copy, then a sharp handheld phone on the right. The main copy uses about the left half of a 1120px stage; display is 60px/1.2, wraps to two lines, and is followed by hero-support at a 16px gap and Download action after 32px. The device is about 492×656px including its visual composition and begins well above the headline. Within 900px the navigation, identity, full promise, support, action, main phone and first row of recognition are visible; secondary recognition continues below.

**Transfer:** Recreate the contrast between a believable human setting and a sharply tangible product. Without custom device photography, composite a framed product view on the right over one contextual image. Portfolio pages instead open with a centered icon, headline, credentials and paired actions on a pale gradient; dark performance campaigns keep the split composition but switch to monochrome photography.

**Avoid:** Do not substitute a centered text-only SaaS hero or place a small generic dashboard thumbnail beneath the headline.

**Text style:** `display` — Inter 700 · 60px/1.2 · tracking -0.03em

### Highlighted promise interlude

**Observed:** Centered multi-line statements occupy a narrow reading column on white with substantial vertical separation. One warm orange phrase starts each thought and flows into black continuation; the dark campaign uses pink and white. The surrounding section uses 160px top and 200px bottom breathing room.

**Transfer:** Express a few distinct user outcomes in one or two sentences each. Keep the color break semantic, not a gradient across all letters.

### Photographic capability chapter

**Observed:** A left-aligned bold chapter headline and short support sit near the top of an expansive contextual photograph. Oversized device imagery overlaps into the following product stage; Feature caption and three small panels complete the chapter.

**Transfer:** Introduce a capability through the situation it helps, then show the behavior at a tangible scale. Use the photograph to establish context rather than repeat an abstract claim.

### Heading and product panel

**Observed:** Major black headings can be left aligned above a bento or centered over a single object. Support is narrower than the 1120px stage, with a 44px grouping rhythm before media. Neutral feature panel supports one large object or a row of three smaller object-and-caption cards.

**Transfer:** Choose alignment according to the demonstration: centered for one iconic object, left for a family of related capabilities.

### Contained color bento

**Observed:** Color showcase panels form 552×440px pairs separated by 16px, with a full-width 1120×520px dark visual band between pairs. Feature caption sits at the top; large screens are cut by the bottom boundary.

**Transfer:** Use two related capabilities per row and one panoramic demonstration as a change of pace. Keep the surrounding page white.

### Mixed human proof

**Observed:** A centered bold section heading leads three independently stacked columns of Review card, colored editorial quotes, photos, email-style notes and a tall video. The group uses 100px vertical padding, 16px gutters and 24px card padding.

**Transfer:** Let real proof dictate card heights and media formats; preserve the mixture of concise endorsements and more personal narratives.

### Monumental closing invitation

**Observed:** Closing wash frames a centered 800px column: 160×160px product icon, 24px gap, two-line closing heading, 24px gap to 20px support. A short informal pointer line leads into the enlarged Download action. The section is about 864px tall with 140px vertical padding, then switches cleanly to Human sign-off footer. **Transfer:** Give the new product's own emblem and a short human invitation a complete visual stage. Without a recognizable app icon, make a simple original rounded product emblem. Keep the small conversational bridge before the action.

### Portfolio product stage

**Observed:** Portfolio light stage and Portfolio dark stage each center a product emblem/name, a 60px two-line headline, compact recognition and two actions. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Human promise → outcome statements → tangible capability demonstrations → independent and personal proof → reassurance → generous invitation → human sign-off.

### Long-form approachable product landing page; default marketing pattern

1. **Promise** — Warm full-bleed human setting, left promise and action, right handheld product, compact recognition.
2. **Outcome framing** — Centered orange-and-black statements articulate the user benefit.
3. **Capability chapters** — Photographic transitions, device constellations, pale object stages and colored feature pairs.
4. **Independent proof** — Oversized recognition carousel with a full editorial quotation.
5. **Product demonstration** — Single-object recommendation scene, overlapping screens and recap tiles provide practical detail.
6. **Objection handling** — Soft luminous reassurance scene explains an important trust boundary.
7. **Social proof** — Three-column mosaic of reviews, press, social media and video.
8. **Closing call** — Emblem, monumental invitation, informal pointer and enlarged action on pale gray, followed by black footer.

**Transfer:** Use the long-form rhythm to alternate emotional relevance with concrete product evidence; chapters and proof can move to suit the new story.

### Performance-oriented dark product campaign

1. **Promise** — Monochrome action hero with vivid phone and white action.
2. **Outcome framing** — Pink-highlighted centered statements on charcoal.
3. **Capability chapters** — Oversized angled screens, watch constellation, environmental photography and black detail panels.
4. **Personal proof** — Practitioner quote embedded in a blurred woodland portrait.
5. **Product depth** — Six compact demonstration cards with translucent captions.
6. **Trust and independent proof** — Violet-lit reassurance panel followed by editorial recognition cards.
7. **Social proof** — Blue-charcoal review mosaic.
8. **Closing call** — Large product emblem and 80px white invitation before the same black footer.

**Transfer:** Use when the new product needs intensity and deeper technical evidence, while preserving the humane language and rounded construction.

### Small multi-product portfolio gateway

1. **Product introduction** — Pale blue stage with centered identity, large promise, credentials, two actions and device constellation.
2. **Contrasting product introduction** — Plum-to-near-black stage repeats the hierarchy with a vivid dark-interface device.
3. **Utility ending** — The final media tableau meets the compact black link footer directly.

**Transfer:** Present a few distinct products as full-width scenes, not a generic tile directory; full-length detail belongs on product stories.

## Writing

Reassuring and practical, with confidence grounded in visible product behavior. Warmth comes from acknowledging ordinary constraints and personal pace rather than hype.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse product or company names, proprietary characters and logos, award claims, customer identities, quotations, performance claims or source-specific figures. Replace them with the new product's own substantiated content. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your situation. The product is a helpful companion; we appears mainly in authorship or design-intent passages.
- **Rhythm:** Short declarative headlines; one or two practical sentences explain a feature. Contrast clauses such as not X but Y clarify the point. A very short conversational bridge can soften the final conversion.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A clear category or outcome, not a cryptic slogan. | 5–9 words | “Award-winning Health and Fitness Tracker”<br>“Training Readiness for Endurance Athletes” |
| Capability headline | An active benefit grounded in a user's daily experience. | 4–9 words | “Work Out Smarter, Not Harder”<br>“Your Progress in Perspective” |
| Supporting explanation | State what happens and why it helps the reader decide. | 20–40 words | “It meets you where you are, adjusts when life gets in the way, and always keeps you moving forward at your own pace.” |
| Feature caption | A concise benefit lead followed immediately by specific function. | 5–12 words for the lead | “Your daily guidance, always on your wrist.” |
| Closing invitation | Connect a long-term possibility to one approachable first action. | 6–10 words | “A lifelong streak starts with day one”<br>“Go outside and put in the work.” |
| Action | Literal destination or next step. | 2–4 words | “Learn More”<br>“Download on the” |
| Human sign-off | A tiny personal statement after the utility links. | 3 words | “Made by humans.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your pace; clear; daily; guidance; personal; understand; consistent; in perspective; when to; so you can

**Avoid:** crush it; no excuses; revolutionary; unlock your potential; 10×; dominate; relentless optimization

**Rules:**

- Explain a feature through the decision it enables, not a list of technical nouns alone.
- Allow rest, uncertainty and real life into benefit writing; avoid moralizing the reader's behavior.
- Use a colored phrase to highlight the core promise, while the continuation explains it plainly.
- Keep action labels literal; put personality in the short line before them.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep primary headlines at 700 with -0.03em tracking and a 1.2 line height.
- Use 24px corners consistently across broad media panels and customer cards.
- Preserve 16px gutters in the two- and three-column product and proof groups.
- Give small captions a 17px/1.3 medium-weight recipe and a full-contrast lead phrase.
- Make primary actions 60px high with 16px corners; enlarge the final action to 252×84px.
- Leave 100px chapter padding and a 140px closing margin of breathing room.
- Keep a tangible product view larger than its supporting caption.
- Include the small human sign-off beneath the 800px-wide footer row.

### Don't

- Do not turn all actions into fully rounded capsules; that silhouette belongs to the navigation.
- Do not use Georgia for ordinary marketing headlines or body copy.
- Do not replace the predominantly white page with continuous saturated section backgrounds.
- Do not flatten the review mosaic into equal-height feature cards.
- Do not use a faint hairline weight for headlines; the emotional tone still needs bold type.
- Do not reproduce the miniature typography and chart internals of a device view as the marketing text scale.
- Do not make every product image fit neatly inside its own padded card; large scenes overlap and clip at stage boundaries.
- Do not make the 299px black footer compete with the much larger final invitation.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Navigation changes from a broad hero-aligned row to a compact floating capsule during the long scroll.
- Carousel controls provide three pages and a next control.
- Footer links declare orange color and underline on hover; no action or surface transition timing is established.

**Transfer:**

- Preserve the navigation's visual contraction and keep product-scene overlap intentional even without animated transforms.
- Keep the recognition panel as one readable scene at a time with compact pagination.

**Unknown:**

- Scroll choreography, animation durations, easing and focus-visible treatments are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Width breakpoints are declared around 800px and 1200px; product pages also declare a boundary around 354px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, stack the promise before its main object, preserving the warm contextual background and strong contrast.
- Reduce multi-column product groups to readable stacked scenes; preserve the large-object hierarchy rather than shrinking every screen to a thumbnail.
- Let the 800px closing column become fluid and retain the emblem, short invitation and single action.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen font sizes, navigation behavior and panel rearrangements are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
