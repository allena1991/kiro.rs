# Grammarly — work in focus

## Overview

An approachable work-tool identity with confident rounded grotesk headlines, readable sans-serif prose, and a predominantly white canvas. Deep green actions and expansive green proof bands give it authority, while colorful floating interface scenes make the product feel helpful rather than technical. The spacious marketing compositions suit productivity tools and services with a tangible everyday benefit; denser comparison tables retain the same plainspoken tone.

### Essentials

- Headlines sound like a capable partner and look compact rather than monumental: Matter at 46px/50px, weight 670, -0.01em tracking is the default opening; 36px/40px carries benefit chapters.
- The opening is a centered promise above a horizontal shelf of colorful product scenes: a 922px text measure, 40px gaps around the support and actions, then 417×381px cards with a partial next card visible.
- Actions are small-cornered work controls, not pills: 60px tall, 6px radius, 16px 24px padding, green fill and a small right arrow; a white outlined provider action sits beside the main action with a 16px gap.
- White space owns most of the page; saturated color is concentrated in media and a full-width green proof chapter. Use #027e6f for action and proof, #1f243c for normal marketing text, and pale gray only for selected grouped-content bands.
- The product is shown as assistance floating over familiar work: one background document plus a foreground suggestion panel, softly backed with teal, lavender-blue, lime or pink. Include this staged-help device even without finished product media by making a simplified, legible before-and-after interface.
- Proof is a number with a human underneath, not a chart: white 390×500px cards on green, 52px/58px statistics, 22px/32px explanations, customer mark above and a small monochrome portrait below.

## Typography

- **Matter** — Marketing headings, benefit titles, plan names and statistics. Rounded, open grotesk with sturdy soft curves; bold but not compressed. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use variable weights 570–670 with -0.01em headline tracking. Font stack: "Matter", "DM Sans", sans-serif.
- **Glyph** — Paragraphs, navigation, buttons, eyebrow labels and footer links. Plain, highly legible workhorse sans-serif with compact small-size proportions. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use regular and bold weights; retain generous paragraph leading. Font stack: "Glyph", "Inter", system-ui, "Segoe UI", Roboto, Oxygen, Ubuntu, Cantarell, "Helvetica Neue", sans-serif.
- **Inter** — Descriptions inside opening carousel tiles. Neutral interface sans-serif, slightly more utilitarian than the marketing headings. Font stack: "Inter", -apple-system, system-ui, "Segoe UI", sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- Use -0.01em tracking throughout the main heading scale, but normal tracking for body, captions and controls. The headline hierarchy is intentionally modest: section leads are often the same 46px size as the opening.
- Use sentence case by default. Eyebrows are ordinary 14px text, not uppercase, letter-spaced microtype.
- Carousel typography is a local variant: weight 600 titles and Inter descriptions. Do not spread that pairing to the rest of the page.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy marketing chapters with compact navigation and footer directories; media gets room to demonstrate one task at a time.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **rhythm** 8px, vertical — Main section, paragraph and component spacing; subordinate 4px and 12px increments serve compact controls.

### Containers and grids

- **marketing-container**: max width 1280px · side padding 80px. Centered desktop content; at 1440px it leaves equal side margins.
- **centered-lead**: max width 922px. Centered promise and support occupy about 72% of the main container.
- **benefit-split**: 2 columns · gap 32px. Equal 624px media and text tracks at desktop. Text receives an extra 80px internal inset away from the media.
- **footer-directory**: max width 1280px · 5 columns · gap 0px · side padding 80px. Five equal 256px tracks with 64px right padding inside each; longest directory determines the bottom row.
- **capability-trio** (only for enterprise pages and feature catalogs): 3 columns · gap 32px. Equal approximately 405px columns, image above left-aligned title and paragraph.
- **plan-group** (only for plan summaries): 3 columns · gap 0px. Three joined panels span the container; only the outer ends are rounded.
- **pricing-container** (only for pricing pages): max width 1208px · side padding 24px. Narrower centered pricing content with a 1160px comparison table.

## Imagery and graphics

### Floating assistance scenes

**Use:** Required signature product imagery in the opening shelf and benefit chapters.

**Observed:** Cropped documents, mail composers or chat windows form a familiar context, with a crisp suggestion panel floating in front. Some scenes sit directly on white with thin document outlines; others use rounded teal-to-pale backgrounds and translucent foreground cards. The main media track is 624px wide, balanced against an equally wide text track; the interface is selectively simplified rather than a dense full-screen dashboard.

**Transfer:** Show the new product intervening at a meaningful moment: a source artifact behind, a short actionable result in front. Minimum version is two overlapping HTML panels containing a short before-and-after task, not generic abstract blobs.

**Build:** For a 624px media stage, place a background work window in its upper-left region and a smaller result card overlapping the lower-right. Use 1px subtle gray or green edges, 6–10px corners, white or translucent white panel fill and short lines of real task text. A teal stage can use linear-gradient(135deg, #027e6f, #ffffff), with a soft white radial glow near the lower-right; keep the page outside the stage white. Match the soft glow rather than relying on exact gradient stops.

### Chromatic feature shelf

**Use:** Colorful upper panels of the opening carousel.

**Observed:** Each card has a distinct immersive treatment: deep teal for rephrasing, lavender-to-blue for tone, luminous lime for corrections and pale pink for another agent. Large translucent interface fragments and a smaller sharp result card overlap, with fine grain or dot texture. The color belongs to the media, not the tile's white label base.

**Transfer:** Assign each capability its own restrained two-color atmosphere while reusing the same panel construction. Keep UI text more prominent than the texture.

**Build:** Clip each scene to the upper 249px of its tile. Layer a two-tone linear gradient, one oversized translucent document, a centered white result card and a faint radial dot pattern. Use SVG circles or radial-gradient dots at low alpha; overlay a soft white haze at one edge. Preserve the teal, lavender-blue, lime and blush family contrast without tinting surrounding page text.

### Proof portraits and marks

**Use:** Customer proof cards and trust strips.

**Observed:** Customer marks are predominantly monochrome and visually small. Proof portraits are grayscale circular headshots placed beneath the attribution, never full-bleed lifestyle backgrounds. Trust strips use evenly spaced marks and white-to-transparent masks at the edges.

**Build:** Use an SVG or positioned elements in the 624px track: one central green circle, then smaller green disks distributed around an elliptical orbit, with reversed thin symbols or short labels. Leave most of the white stage unpainted; vary scale to create depth without 3D shading.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** A useful promise → work in context → focused benefits → trust or quantified proof → an appropriate commitment → a useful resource directory.

### Broad product marketing landing page

1. **Promise and demonstration** — Centered promise, paired signup routes and colorful feature shelf, followed by trust marks.
2. **Capability chapters** — White split sections progress from everyday output to tone, audience understanding and responsible use.
3. **Purchase choice** — Joined three-tier plan summary.
4. **Quantified proof** — Green chapter with human-backed metric cards.
5. **Broader context** — Split ecosystem announcement.
6. **Closing call and navigation** — White signup split then five-column white directory and bottom signature.

**Transfer:** Keep the broad promise tied to tangible work examples, and adapt the ordering of proof and purchase choice to the new product.

### Help a buyer select and validate a tier

1. **Promise and choice** — Centered lead, trust line and three joined plan cards.
2. **Detailed comparison** — Long category-based table with one continuous mint recommendation stripe.
3. **Social proof** — Dark forest band with three customer quotations.
4. **Objection handling** — Compact 700px disclosure group on near-white blue.
5. **Closing call and navigation** — Centered free-trial action on white, sales link, then a narrower five-column directory and bottom signature.

**Transfer:** Use the denser table only for decisions that need feature-level comparison; retain a simple final action.

### Explain an organization-wide offering and invite a sales conversation

1. **Promise** — Left-aligned eyebrow and promise beside a colorful work demonstration, then logo trust strip.
2. **Scope and capabilities** — Product-extension explanation and teal three-column capability band.

## Writing

Confident and warm, with the tone of a capable colleague who removes friction. Benefits are practical and reader-centered, with occasional compact wordplay and explicit reassurance around trust.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Grammarly or Superhuman names, logos, customer marks, portraits, proprietary interface art, customer quotations, adoption totals, ROI figures, certification claims or pricing promises. Source examples illustrate structure only; all claims and assets must belong to the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your team; use we when taking responsibility or promising help. Refer to the product as a partner or helper, not an omnipotent replacement for the reader.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines tend to be short benefit phrases or paired clauses. Support is one clear sentence or a compact paragraph; enterprise task stories may run longer. Contractions, parallel lists and occasional em dashes make the voice conversational without exclamation marks.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening headline | An ambitious reader goal followed by an assurance that the product handles the friction. | 6–12 words | “Think big. We’ll take care of the details.” |
| Benefit heading | A concrete outcome, often with a repeated word or balanced clause. | 4–9 words | “Better writing, better results”<br>“Keep your voice, make it clear” |
| Supporting line | State the practical improvement and contrast it with wasted time or effort. | 15–30 words | “Be perfectly professional, clear, and convincing in a few clicks, not a few hours.” |
| Workflow explanation | Describe a familiar situation, then what the product does and what the team can focus on instead. | 30–60 words | “A team thread is going in circles and needs a decision. Go surfaces the right context and schedules a meeting on the spot. Work keeps moving without breaking focus.” |
| Primary action | Direct verb and low-friction next step; a short qualifier can reassure. | 2–5 words | “Sign up It’s free”<br>“Get a demo” |
| Closing invitation | Return to the larger benefit or invite participation, not a manufactured deadline. | 4–10 words | “Great writing gets work done”<br>“Write the future with us” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** clear; confident; help; work; your voice; where you work; without breaking focus; trusted; ready to scale

**Avoid:** disruptive revolution; crush the competition; magic with no explanation; autonomous everything; urgent countdown language; dense model jargon

**Rules:**

- Name the reader's outcome before the implementation detail.
- Explain assistance through real tasks and familiar tools.
- Use specific reassurance for risks rather than vague superlatives.
- Make proof numbers interpretable with a plain-language explanation and attribution.
- Keep action labels literal even when headlines are playful.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Set default opening type to 46px/50px at weight 670 with -0.01em tracking.
- Hold desktop chapters to 1280px with 80px side insets.
- Use 36px/40px benefit headings with 16px to the 20px/30px paragraph.
- Keep major conversion controls 60px tall with 6px corners.
- Let media, not body-copy backgrounds, carry the lavender, lime and pink accents.
- Use 390×500px white cards for green-band numerical proof.
- Align joined pricing panels with shared rules and 40px inner padding.
- Preserve the small logo-and-copyright bottom row after the directory.

### Don't

- Do not turn 6px buttons into fully rounded capsules; reserve rounded tags for small billing information.
- Do not put heavy drop shadows beneath every marketing card; the faint integration-icon shadow is a scoped treatment.
- Do not substitute serif or monospace display headlines for the rounded grotesk hierarchy.
- Do not make eyebrow labels uppercase with wide tracking; they are 14px regular sentence-case text.
- Do not use bright mint as the default action fill; it belongs to a popularity ribbon.
- Do not style selected capability tabs as filled segmented pills; selection is a 2px underline.
- Do not reverse the sticky header onto green when the page enters a colored chapter; its surface stays white.
- Do not give every section an 8px enclosing card shell; ordinary benefits remain open on the canvas.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Product video scenes expose small pause controls; feature and testimonial shelves have directional controls, and trust strips have pause controls.
- Declared action/surface transitions include background-color 0.1s. Pricing actions declare darker green hover and active fills and green 2px focus-visible outlines with 2px offset.

**Transfer:**

- Keep product demonstrations task-focused with a visible pause route; shelf movement should support discovery of the partially visible next card.
- Reuse the documented short color transition for compact controls rather than introducing elaborate page-wide choreography.

**Unknown:**

- Playback durations, carousel speed and easing tied to specific scenes are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop composition uses 1280px content, equal split media tracks and a 922px centered lead.
- Pricing styles declare many width thresholds including 640px, 768px, 1024px and 1280px; no corresponding narrow-screen composition is established here.

**Transfer:**

- As a design adaptation, collapse benefit splits to a single readable column while retaining the overlapping work/result panel relationship.
- Keep horizontal feature and proof shelves swipeable with a visible next-card edge; do not shrink all desktop cards to fit one row.
- Adapt the dense directory and plan comparison to narrower layouts without compressing 14px labels into unreadable text.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile headline scale, menu behavior and table transformation remain unspecified.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
