# Graza — pantry pop editorial

## Overview

A sunny, slightly mischievous consumer style that mixes old-fashioned condensed serif headlines with typewriter labels and everyday product photography. Warm peach paper and olive ink keep the page grounded while sharp citrus bands and mint controls add grocery-shelf energy. It suits tangible products with tactile packaging, practical benefits and a friendly point of view; the composition alternates roomy storytelling with dense shopping information.

### Essentials

- Elegant headlines meet workaday labels: ITC Garamond Condensed at 72px/1 with -0.03em tracking pairs with GT Alpina Typewriter at 16px/1.5, not a conventional sans-serif marketing stack.
- The opening feels like walking into a real task: full-bleed 1440×900 moving photography, a 40% black scrim, a two-line cream headline anchored low left and a slim 353×34px citrus pill beneath it.
- Warm paper is the resting state, citrus is a whole scene: #f6e6d9 surrounds dark #3c422e text, while #d1e030, #fbd535 and #9eef80 occupy discrete promotional bands rather than tinting every card.
- Actions look like tiny printed objects: 9999px pill corners, 1px olive outlines and a fine offset checker-pattern underside; the default action is only 34px tall, with a 55px promotional variant.
- Products have weight and context: rounded 20px photographs place real objects against mottled pale stone with directional shadows; use an honestly lit physical prop or material study when no packaged object exists.
- A playful print-shop identity runs through the page: 1px dashed rules, oval category stamps, a repeating typewriter ticker and single-ink character drawings interrupt the refined serif. Carry these devices into new content with original line art and short utility phrases.
- The identity becomes architecture: a full-width olive word sculpture appears at monumental scale, including the closing footer at 1380×424px. Use an original heavy, soft-edged wordmark or a custom short word sculpture when no suitable logo exists.

## Typography

- **ITC Garamond Condensed** — Promises, product names, oversized editorial statements and expressive verbs. Tall, narrow old-style serif with soft brackets and an elegant high-contrast rhythm. If unavailable, use **Cormorant Garamond**. Use 400 and tighten horizontal proportions to retain the narrow silhouette. Font stack: "ITC Garamond Condensed", "Cormorant Garamond", Georgia, serif.
- **GT Alpina Typewriter** — Body copy, navigation, prices, actions and uppercase labels. Serifed typewriter construction, literary rather than technological; regular body and medium labels. If unavailable, use **Courier Prime**. Use 400 for prose and 700 sparingly for medium-weight controls. Font stack: "GT Alpina Typewriter", "Courier Prime", Courier New, monospace.
- **Apercu** (only for product category stamps and format labels) — Small utilitarian classification text. Plain compact grotesk that stays subordinate to the two editorial families. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Font stack: "Apercu", "DM Sans", Arial, sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- Keep the contrast between narrow, expressive serif headings and matter-of-fact typewriter text. Do not replace both with a single family.
- Default large serif tracking is -0.03em. Promotional and product-name recipes keep their own tighter measured values rather than inheriting one global letter spacing.
- The monumental identity is vector lettering, not another paragraph text size; treat it as the Word sculpture graphic.

- **page-container**: max width 1440px · side padding 30px. Centered desktop content with 1380px usable width; immersive photographs and colored scenes can bleed outside it.
- **editorial-inner**: max width 1104px. Inset storytelling and display statements; narrower than shopping rows.
- **promotional-split**: 2 columns · gap 35px · side padding 30px. Two equal 673px stages: centered text left, square product scene right.
- **product-trio**: 3 columns · gap 30px. Three 440px editorial product columns with photographs above centered serif names.
- **footer-halves**: 2 columns · gap 0px · side padding 30px. Equal newsletter and navigation halves; right half subdivides into three link columns with a 30px left inset.
- **copy-measure**: max width 400px. Promotional support copy; footer invitation narrows to 384px and email field extends to 448px.
- **catalog-grid** (only for catalog pages): 4 columns · gap 32px · side padding 30px. 321px cards, 50px row gap and 32px top/bottom inset.
- **lineup-grid** (only for purchase landing pages): max width 1136px · 3 columns · gap 16px. 368px lifestyle image columns with text below.

### Surfaces

#### Peach paper · level 0

**Use:** Default page and footer canvas.

**CSS:** `background: #f6e6d9; color: #3c422e`

**Observed:** Flat warm color without visible grain, gradients or floating depth.

**Transfer:** Let this be the continuous resting background between full scenes.

#### Citrus scene · level 0

**Use:** Promotional stage and animated utility band.

**CSS:** `background: #d1e030; color: #3c422e`

**Observed:** Full-width flat saturated fill. Separate promotional slides use sunflower or mint instead.

**Transfer:** Change an entire scene's backing, not each paragraph or tiny tile.

#### Dashed paper band · level 0

**Use:** Trivia interludes and footer subdivisions.

**CSS:** `background: #f6e6d9; border-top: 1px dashed #3c422e; border-bottom: 1px dashed #3c422e`

**Observed:** Print-like thin edges divide sections without raising them.

**Transfer:** Use for editorial side notes or the closing utility region.

#### Photo scrim · level 1

**Use:** Readable reversed copy over immersive video and photography.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.4)`

**Observed:** A uniform dark veil leaves the full photographic scene legible.

**Transfer:** Layer between media and cream text, not above the text.

#### Cream order panel · level 1

**Only for:** purchase configurators

**Use:** Grouped buying decisions within a yellow stage.

**CSS:** `background: #fff4ec; border: 1px solid #3c422e; border-radius: 20px; padding: 20px`

**Observed:** Flat panel with dashed internal divisions, no blurred shadow.

**Transfer:** Group summary, options, frequency and final action into one printed form.

## Imagery and graphics

### Everyday product film

**Use:** Default opening and large process demonstration.

**Observed:** Close framing of hands handling the object in a real kitchen; softly lit everyday surroundings, olive greens and warm neutrals. The opening fills the entire first screen, while a later demonstration sits inside 30px margins with rounded corners.

**Transfer:** Film the new product in a believable hand-led task. Without film, use a sharply composed still of hands and object; preserve the large uninterrupted scene and quiet background for copy.

### Stone-backed packshot

**Use:** Product promotion and shopping rows.

**Observed:** Glossy dark objects stand upright on pale gray-beige stone with a mottled wall behind; highlights make the silhouette tactile and sunlight creates clear diagonal shadows. Square promotional images and tall catalog images share 20px corners.

**Transfer:** Photograph the new object on a pale matte tabletop against plaster or stone, using a single directional key light. Keep real material texture and room for category and price overlays; avoid isolated cutouts on pure white.

### Word sculpture

**Use:** Monumental identity beat and the closing footer.

**Observed:** Heavy olive vector lettering with swelling curves, chunky softened serifs and close spacing occupies almost the entire content width. A mid-page version is roughly 376px tall; the footer allocation is 1380×424px including 24px vertical inset.

**Transfer:** Draw an original short name or word in heavy, soft serif lettering. If the logo is too thin, create a separate typographic sculpture with similar visual mass rather than merely enlarging body type.

**Build:** Use an SVG viewBox about 1380 by 376. Set a short heavy serif word, convert it to outlines, soften sharp corners and tighten the letters into one dark silhouette. Fill with #3c422e over #f6e6d9; scale proportionally to the content width without a box or shadow.

### Single-ink character drawings

**Use:** Benefit explanations and humorous trivia interludes.

**Observed:** Olive-only hand-drawn characters, fruit faces, leaves and workers use irregular outlines and a few solid fills. Two compact illustrated benefit columns contrast with a much larger trivia drawing that reaches off the left edge.

**Transfer:** Create original personified objects or task-related characters in one ink. Minimum version: a simple friendly line drawing with one expressive face and one solid silhouette, not a multicolor icon set.

**Build:** Use SVG paths with #3c422e fill and rounded irregular strokes on transparent backing. Combine loose curved outlines, two small eye dots, a curved mouth and broad filled clothing or object areas. Keep the drawing around 320px wide in benefits; enlarge a separate editorial drawing to occupy half a band.

### Utility ticker and oval stamps

**Use:** Kinetic activity section, product classifications and launch announcements.

**Observed:** Repeated uppercase typewriter phrases run across citrus. Category text sits in thin oval outlines; activity verbs become large serif words in loose oval positions. Launch announcements use a tilted citrus oval over a product photo.

**Transfer:** Repeat a short original task phrase in the ticker and replace activity verbs with uses relevant to the new product. Keep labels short enough to remain stamp-like.

**Build:** Make a clipped full-width flex track with duplicate uppercase text runs and generous horizontal gaps. For stamps use a transparent ellipse with a 1px olive outline. For a launch sticker use a roughly 254×185px citrus ellipse, rotated 15deg with centered stacked serif text and a few small four-point stars.

### Community photo rail

**Use:** Social closing bridge.

**Observed:** 200px-wide portrait frames of cooking, meals, hands and gatherings appear at varied heights, with rounded corners and about 30px between them. Colors are lively and ordinary rather than uniformly graded.

**Transfer:** Gather candid task, use and community photographs; mix details with people and finished outcomes while maintaining the consistent rail width.

### Source and process collage

**Only for:** brand story pages

**Use:** Explain materials, origins and craft.

**Observed:** A cutout botanical branch floats directly on peach with numbered factual captions around it. Other stories place rounded landscape and detail photographs at different scales, connected by a thin diagonal line and a small typewriter paragraph.

**Transfer:** Use a real material or component cutout with concise factual annotations. Pair one wide context view and one closer process detail, preserving the irregular editorial composition rather than turning it into a uniform grid.

### Icons

**Observed:** Olive filled pointed arrows at 19×8px recur in actions. Search and account use thin circular outlines at roughly 36px; small stars and flower-like asterisks decorate labels. Icons share the ink color rather than adding another palette.

**Transfer:** Choose simple thin utility line icons, but draw directional arrows with a distinctive tapered point. Use small asterisks as typographic separators, not a large decorative icon library.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Immersive task opening

**Observed:** A full-bleed 1440×900px film is the back layer; Photo scrim sits above it, then a compact 60px Navigation bar and low-left copy. The main object and hands occupy the center/right and much of the image height. The cream 72px headline spans about seven twelfths of the content width and breaks into two lines; a citrus Primary pill follows after 24px. The content has 30px side gutters and 40px bottom breathing room. Within the first 900px the navigation, object, headline and action are visible; no separate support paragraph appears. A slim origin-information strip and product promotion begin below the fold.

**Transfer:** Choose a close, authentic task image with a quiet lower-left zone and keep the promise and action on top of it. If no film exists, stage a still with the product or its material in use. Catalog pages instead open with a centered 72px heading, category bar and format filters; purchase pages open with Purchase folder tabs and a split buying stage; brand-story pages open with a centered promise above a large lifestyle image and an offset paragraph.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a centered text block above a small floating product render, or add a long supporting paragraph that pushes the action below the first screen.

**Text style:** `headline` — ITC Garamond Condensed 400 · 72px/1 · tracking -0.03em

### Bright split promotion

**Transfer:** Promote one offer at a time using one photographic set and one concise reason to care.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Immersive promise → tangible product invitation → playful explanation → choice or application → human context → useful community and identity close.

### Broad consumer marketing and product discovery

1. **Promise** — Immersive task opening with a short benefit and direct shopping action.
2. **Offer** — Slim contextual strip and a bright split product promotion.
3. **Explanation** — Staggered statement, illustrated reasons and monumental identity beat.
4. **Product choice** — Large editorial product lineup.
5. **Engagement** — Humorous fact interlude followed by a ticker-led activity playground.
6. **Human proof** — Photographic source call with a small contextual card.
7. **Closing** — Social rail, signup, directory and giant word sculpture.

**Transfer:** Use the visual contrasts to move between desire, explanation and choice without requiring every block or the same order.

### Efficient catalog browsing

1. **Orientation** — Centered serif category heading and full-width dashed category tabs.
2. **Selection** — Small circular format filters followed by a four-column product grid.
3. **Conversion** — Price-bearing pills repeated under consistent packshots.
4. **Closing** — Social rail, shared signup footer and giant identity.

**Transfer:** Favor uniform images and compact typewriter product names over the spacious editorial cards.

### Convert a specific purchase or recurring order

1. **Product decision** — Folder tabs, large media, announcement sticker, review line and configurable order form.
2. **Explanation** — Three named usage columns explain the alternatives.
3. **Demonstration** — Wide close-up process film within rounded edges.
4. **Closing** — Shared newsletter, directory and monumental identity.

**Transfer:** Bring buying controls forward, then explain use and show the product working.

### Make the brand's quality and craft believable

1. **Belief** — Centered promise over an everyday product photograph and offset introduction.
2. **Material proof** — Botanical cutout with numbered annotations and a large serif claim.
3. **Process proof** — Asymmetric landscape/detail collage connected by diagram lines.
4. **Further reading** — Three image-led editorial cards behind a dashed heading.
5. **Product invitation** — Rounded citrus bundle panel.
6. **Closing** — Social photo rail, shared footer and monumental identity.

**Transfer:** Substitute authentic source, material and production details; keep the main column asymmetric and the chapter navigation secondary.

## Writing

Warm, opinionated and a little goofy, with real enthusiasm for ordinary everyday use. Product confidence is expressed through concrete sensory benefits and practical distinctions rather than luxury language.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Graza's name, wordmark, character artwork, product names, proprietary packaging, source locations, prices, ratings, claims or quotations as facts for another brand. Examples below illustrate voice only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your habits; use we for the people making the product. The product can be described as a friendly kitchen helper rather than an abstract solution.
- **Rhythm:** Short declarative headlines contrast with compact conversational paragraphs. Use parallel phrases, occasional rhyme, contractions, an aside or a restrained exclamation; controls remain blunt and useful.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Make a broad everyday benefit feel obvious and appetizing. | 6–12 words | “Extra Virgin Olive Oil makes everything better.” |
| Editorial statement | A two-part sensory claim with a spoken cadence. | 7–12 words | “Olive oil so fresh, you can taste it.” |
| Supporting line | Define a use or contrast in plain language, often with parallel verbs. | 5–16 words | “Made for eating, never heating.”<br>“Use it every day, in every way.” |
| Eyebrow | Compact uppercase mnemonic or playful principle. | 3–6 words | “HAPPY OLIVES, HAPPY OIL”<br>“FRESH IS BEST” |
| Primary action | Verb plus an ordinary product or destination, no rhetorical flourish. | 2–6 words | “SHOP NOW”<br>“Shop The Trio” |
| Newsletter invitation | Talk like a friend sharing a recommendation. | 8–14 words | “Friends let friends know about fresh products and content.” |
| Purchase reassurance | Remove friction with short concrete verbs. | 4–7 words | “Skip, Edit or Cancel Anytime” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** every day; really good; fresh; punchy; mellow; best; made for; your new; do-it-all; a big pop; you guessed it

**Avoid:** revolutionary platform; unlock your potential; elevated lifestyle; disruptive ecosystem; unparalleled luxury; synergistic

**Rules:**

- Give each choice one memorable adjective and one practical use distinction.
- Prefer sensory or physical verbs to abstract superlatives.
- Let an occasional parenthetical thought or goofy fact break the sales pitch, but keep factual claims verifiable.
- Use quote marks around short product nicknames and uppercase only for labels or actions, not all prose.
- Pair a confident promise with ordinary supporting language rather than repeating the same claim in more formal words.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default serif heading at 72px/1 and -0.03em tracking.
- Use 16px/1.5 typewriter copy at roughly 400px measure in promotional scenes.
- Use 30px desktop side gutters and 20px media corners.
- Keep thin borders olive and prefer 1px dashed section rules to heavy dividers.
- Include an original single-ink illustration, repeating text device and monumental word sculpture.
- Give product photography a consistent pale stone setting, directional light and visible shadows.
- Use citrus 34px pills for ordinary conversions and the 55px variant for the split promotion.
- Close with a real signup and directory before the giant identity treatment.

### Don't

- Do not turn every paragraph into a rounded cream card; most content sits directly on peach.
- Do not replace the narrow serif headlines with broad geometric sans lettering.
- Do not use pure black as the main text color; olive ink carries the print-like identity.
- Do not add soft elevation shadows to flat outlined panels or utility controls.
- Do not make catalog names 46px serif headlines; dense grids use 20px medium typewriter names.
- Do not color each feature icon differently; drawings and arrows share the same olive ink.
- Do not turn the rectangular social rail into a wall of equal square stock portraits.
- Do not treat the closing word sculpture as a small centered footer logo.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Color, background, border, fill and stroke transitions are declared at 0.25s ease-in-out.
- Header transform and opacity transitions are declared at 0.4s ease-in-out.
- A repeated marquee is declared at 20s linear infinite; decorative spin at 3s linear infinite.
- Droplet animations are declared at 2s linear, one with a 1s delay; a blink uses 2s ease-in-out.
- A large vector identity uses a skewing wobble; a Pause animations control exists in the footer.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the ticker as a continuous printed-text track and constrain decorative motion to the word sculpture, small stamps and product-related motifs.
- Provide a pause control for the repeating decorative devices; preserve their static composition when paused.

**Unknown:**

- Exact movement distances and complete interactive playback sequences are not established.
- No additional hover transform timing is specified for the layered pill underside.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- CSS includes breakpoints at 640px, 768px, 1024px, 1200px and 1440px among others.
- Catalog classes declare two columns by default, three at the medium breakpoint and four at the large breakpoint.
- Promotional heading classes switch from smaller heading roles to the 102px desktop title; desktop navigation links are hidden outside the large layout.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens preserve the same paper/citrus scene boundaries and word-sculpture silhouette; stack the photographic stage and copy rather than reducing them to tiny side-by-side columns.
- Let social imagery remain a horizontal rail. Scale the vector identity proportionally to the usable width.
- Use the catalog's two/three/four-column progression for shopping layouts; do not apply that density to the immersive marketing opening.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile compositions and exact headline sizes were not visually established.
- Precise behavior of the mobile navigation and purchase tabs remains unspecified.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
