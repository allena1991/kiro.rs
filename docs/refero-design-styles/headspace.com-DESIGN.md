# Headspace — sunny shapes, human reassurance

## Overview

An upbeat, approachable style that makes a complex service feel like a friendly everyday companion. Mostly white and warm off-white space gives way to confident yellow, blue and pink stages, with a rounded humanist grotesk and cheerful geometric illustration. Product screens and candid portraits sit inside playful collages rather than clinical diagrams; the overall density is spacious, with a more practical, information-rich footer.

### Essentials

- Friendly headlines carry real weight: bold humanist sans at 52px/1.1 for the opening, 64px/1 for major section statements, with -0.03em tracking and sentence case.
- The opening makes two routes feel simple: a centered two-line promise above a 2.3:1 pair of off-white product stages, separated by 20px, with 24px corners and bottom-cropped product collages.
- Actions feel like soft physical tokens: 48px-high pills with 32px radii, blue in navigation and dark charcoal inside pale product cards; blue buttons have a restrained 2px downward shadow.
- Color arrives in chapters, not as a permanent wash: white reading sections alternate with broad yellow, royal-blue and sky-blue panels, usually inset 48px with 16px corners. Keep most reading space neutral; let a colored stage occupy most of a screen when it appears.
- Illustration supplies the emotional punctuation: simple circles, rounded blocks, clouds and expressive curves accompany real product screens and portraits. Every new page needs an original playful geometric scene; without product media, use a small original SVG object collage.
- A conversion can become a landscape: a 72px/1 white invitation inside a 750px-wide blue pill floats over a yellow field, with a large warm-colored object rising through scalloped cream foreground shapes. Preserve this outsized, illustrated invitation device rather than ending with only a routine button.

**Notes:**

- Use sentence case, not an uppercase eyebrow system. Bold headings have compact spacing, usually -0.03em; body copy is normal or -0.01em.
- Keep the 52px opening subordinate to the 64px later proof statements. The largest normal landing-page text is the 72px illustrated conversion invitation.
- The 48px topic-opening recipe is a scoped alternative, not a replacement for the centered product-card opening. Do not average its tracking with the standard section recipe.

## Imagery and graphics

### Geometric companionship

**Use:** Signature emotional illustration throughout feature stages, chooser symbols and the illustrated invitation.

**Observed:** Flat saturated circles, capsules, stars, clouds and chunky geometric objects have soft asymmetry and sparse expressive curves. They sit against clean yellow, pink or cream fields; small symbols punctuate headings while oversized shapes are cut by the panel edge.

**Transfer:** Create original friendly object metaphors related to the new product. The minimum version is a coherent SVG scene of rounded shapes and a few expressive strokes, not generic thin-line stock icons.

**Build:** Use SVG circles, rounded rectangles and rounded triangular paths in yellow, pink and blue. Add one offset curved patch in the same hue at lower opacity for simple volume, and 6px–10px round-ended charcoal curves for expression. For the invitation landscape, place a large warm-colored disc low in a yellow viewBox, put small cream cloud groups above it, then overlap the bottom with large cream circles that form a scalloped horizon.

### Product collage

**Use:** Opening routes and demonstrations.

**Observed:** Large upright phones have black or white rounded frames, readable colored screens and intentional bottom cropping. Floating thumbnail cards, speech bubbles, cloud shapes and small stars orbit the central object. Opening media is about 297px high within the pale cards; dedicated topic art is about 594px wide.

**Transfer:** Use real interface content in a simple device frame. Without interface media, use an original service object or process collage with two supporting cards; keep clear foreground-background layering.

**Build:** Layer a tall rounded device silhouette over a pale field; overlap two small rounded content tiles at its left, one white message chip at lower right, and two small geometric accents behind. Anchor the device to the bottom edge and clip the stage rather than shrinking everything to fit.

### People collage

**Use:** Professional trust sections and product reassurance.

**Observed:** Natural, warmly lit portraits are placed in unequal rounded rectangles over a flat blue field. Three people form a loose vertical collage, balanced by clouds and stars rather than a formal corporate photo grid.

**Transfer:** Use candid real users or practitioners in ordinary environments; avoid uniformly lit corporate headshots. Let the portraits occupy about half of a split panel.

### Cloud proof field

**Use:** Quantified adoption and trust proof.

**Observed:** Soft photographic clouds blend into a bright blue panel, behind a centered white headline and dark action. Small circular portraits hover around it, and a four-part stat row sits along the bottom.

**Transfer:** Keep the visible sky soft and light. When photography is unavailable, use a low-contrast cloud field rather than a glossy mesh gradient.

**Build:** On the sky-blue backing, position several oversized white radial-gradient ellipses at rgba(255,255,255,0.16) to rgba(255,255,255,0), overlap at the edges and blur about 24px. Keep the middle low contrast enough for white text and add four small circular portraits outside the title area.

### Night product theater

**Only for:** night-themed product pages

**Use:** Dark topic opening, audio demonstration and subscription conversion.

**Observed:** Deep navy image fields carry purple-toned screens and occasional tonal organic shapes. Two phones overlap at slight opposing angles; the larger audio demo pushes phones beyond both side edges of an indigo panel.

**Transfer:** Reserve this darker treatment for a genuinely nighttime or immersive product mode, preserving the white global navigation.

**Build:** Use a deep indigo SVG field with a few oversized low-contrast curved shapes. Center a white rounded player; place device images at opposing outer edges rotated in opposite directions, allowing them to extend beyond the stage.

### Illustrated editorial covers

**Only for:** resource and library sections

**Use:** Content cards and related reading.

**Observed:** Cheerful geometric scenes use strong single-color fields. Library covers are portrait-format with a dark lower gradient supporting white centered titles; article thumbnails are landscape, and related-category tiles are square.

**Transfer:** Make a consistent cover family, changing the central metaphor and backing hue while keeping the same shape language. Keep captions outside landscape and square images.

### People portrait rail

**Only for:** organization pages

**Use:** Expert or team introductions.

**Observed:** Circular cutout portraits are centered in pale rectangular artwork with a few cropped orange discs. Name and short biography sit below; cards continue beyond the right edge.

**Transfer:** Use natural expressions and a consistent head scale, with simple geometric backing instead of heavy photo filters.

### Impact shape wall

**Only for:** organization impact chapters

**Use:** Companion art beside a vertical list of giant pale numbers.

**Observed:** A wall of circles, capsules, rounded triangles and hexagons in warm yellow, cream and orange occupies the right half and extends beyond the page edge. Simple curved patches suggest volume without realistic 3D rendering.

**Transfer:** Arrange original rounded shapes as a playful modular sculpture alongside real impact data.

**Build:** Build a three-column SVG grid with narrow gutters. Mix discs, capsules, semicircles and rounded polygon paths; add one curved darker overlay within each shape. Vary orientation and clip the rightmost column at the viewport edge.

### Outcome posters

**Only for:** institutional outcome sections

**Use:** Research and measurable-results proof.

**Observed:** Four approximately 275px by 330px pale posters contain a colorful symbol above a large matching percentage or short number, then centered explanatory copy. Each poster has its own underlined source link below; a centered qualification and blue call follow.

**Transfer:** Use independently verifiable results, assign one accent hue per poster, and preserve a clear source link and qualification outside the artwork.

### Icons

**Observed:** Functional SVGs are mostly solid, using 16px navigation/disclosure marks, 24px action symbols and 32px utility icons. Chooser illustrations are much larger, around 58px, colorful and chunky. Circular arrows, play controls, checkmarks and small plus signs remain simple charcoal or white.

**Transfer:** Separate monochrome functional icons from expressive mini-illustrations. Choose filled, round-ended symbols; do not substitute a thin technical outline set for the playful pictograms.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Two-route product opening

**Observed:** A 58px yellow announcement strip and 80px white Navigation sit above a white opening. A centered two-line opening-recipe promise occupies the upper center; the first benefit phrase can change while the second line remains stable. A horizontal trio of checkmarked assurance lines sits 24px below. Under it, two Pale product stages use a 2.3fr:1fr split with a 20px gutter across about 1332px. Each has a centered 32px title, Dark action and small eligibility note, with product media fixed to the lower edge. The wide card has a central phone with orbiting content and illustration; the narrow card has a human service portrait within a phone. Within the first 900px, all headline, assurance, actions and primary collages are visible; the next chooser chapter begins below the fold. Layers are white canvas, pale rounded panels, geometric backing accents, device/portrait media, then text and action controls.

**Transfer:** For a new product, present a central benefit and two useful routes, prioritizing one with the wide card. If there is only one product, use the narrow route for a complementary service or guided entry point. Without interface art, build an original object-and-message collage. Focused feature pages instead use a 1160px split with a 64px headline on pale paper; topic pages use a 48px left headline, support, audio sample and one action beside large device art. Organization pages can open with a 92px headline and one monumental shape on yellow.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a full-bleed photographic hero, tiny dashboard thumbnail, or a pair of equal generic feature cards. Do not enlarge the opening headline to the 92px institutional scale.

## Writing

Warm, reassuring and direct, with confidence grounded in practical support and specific benefits. The product feels like a helpful companion rather than a performance coach or a medical authority talking down to the reader.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Headspace names, wordmarks, signature faces or character artwork, exact testimonials, partner marks, research claims, prices, eligibility assertions or adoption numbers. Source examples illustrate tone only; all product facts and claims must belong to the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your; use we for the people providing help. Introduce the product in everyday terms and explain what it helps the reader do.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often 3–8 words, followed by one or two concrete sentences. Longer paragraphs belong in education and FAQ sections. Gentle conversational questions and compact benefit phrases create movement without urgency.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | An ordinary desired change, expressed as a compact verb phrase. | 2–6 words | “Sleep better”<br>“Put your mind to sleep” |
| Feature headline | Reassuring availability or simplicity rather than a technical capability list. | 2–7 words | “Always-there support”<br>“Meditation made simple” |
| Section introduction | Invite self-selection or explain how the product fits everyday life. | 6–12 words | “What kind of headspace are you looking for?”<br>“The mental health app for every moment” |
| Support copy | Name a familiar situation, then describe the concrete help. | 1–3 sentences | “Stressed at work? Family drama? Trouble sleeping? Ebb can help you unpack what’s on your mind and move forward with more clarity.” |
| Action | A clear low-friction next step; trials, exploration and useful outcomes. | 2–5 words | “Try for free”<br>“Get better rest today”<br>“Check your coverage” |
| Proof heading | Translate scale or testimony into a human benefit. | 6–12 words | “Members are enjoying happier and healthier lives” |
| Closing invitation | Short possessive invitation that makes the product feel personal. | 3–5 words | “Get your headspace”<br>“Be kind to your mind” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** simple; support; everyday; your; help; practice; unpack; kind; thoughtful; find; start

**Avoid:** dominate; crush your goals; revolutionary breakthrough; optimize yourself; disrupt; hurry; guaranteed transformation

**Rules:**

- Name what the reader can do before explaining how the system works.
- Pair emotional reassurance with a concrete example of use.
- Keep technical or clinical qualifications plain, visible and separate from promotional claims.
- Write chooser labels as outcomes, filter labels as categories, and buttons as actions.
- Use conversational contractions in explanatory copy; avoid piling multiple exclamation marks or superlatives into the friendly voice.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the core headline weight at 700 and its tracking near -0.03em.
- Use the 2.3:1 pale-stage split to give the primary route clear priority.
- Hold default desktop chapter side insets at 48px and editorial width at 1160px.
- Anchor product artwork to the lower edge of the opening's 24px-rounded panels.
- Use 48px-high pills for everyday actions and 8px corners for rectangular inputs and goal tiles.
- Set quotations at 24px/1.3 with 48px inner padding and small attribution near the bottom.
- Give saturated stages a single dominant backing hue; keep surrounding reading sections white.
- Preserve the oversized 72px illustrated invitation and the final pale-to-dark footer contrast.

### Don't

- Do not spread pale pink or yellow behind every paragraph; color belongs to discrete scenes and supporting artwork.
- Do not use pure black as the default text ink; the core ink is warm charcoal.
- Do not turn all cards into 32px-radius pills; standard cards use 8px, 16px or 24px according to role.
- Do not add large diffuse shadows to ordinary quotation or resource cards; they are mostly flat.
- Do not replace the compact 16px navigation with bold uppercase labels or wide tracking.
- Do not make every headline 64px; opening, feature, FAQ and card roles deliberately differ.
- Do not turn an editorial rail into a table of small text links; preserve large illustrated covers and compact captions.
- Do not export the 220px watermark numerals or indigo nighttime mode into every marketing chapter.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared color, background-color and border-color transitions use 150ms cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.94, 0.6, 1) on many actions.
- Feature tabs declare 0.3s transitions; outlined goal tiles declare border 0.3s.
- Pressed blue and newsletter actions move down 2px and remove the lower shadow. The opening benefit phrase changes within an overflow-hidden slot; horizontal rails use translated tracks.

**Transfer:**

- Keep interactive feedback tactile and shallow: recolor the pill, then visually depress its lower edge on activation.
- For the repeated-message ribbon and feature rails, retain clipped horizontal tracks so continuation is visible without moving the page layout.

**Unknown:**

- Autoplay rates, ribbon velocity and opening phrase timing are not established.
- Focus-visible styling and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare width breakpoints including 768px, 1024px, 1280px and 1440px.

**Transfer:**

- Recommended adaptation: make tabs and content rails horizontally scrollable rather than compressing labels or illustrated covers.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
