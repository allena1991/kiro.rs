# Huddle — pastel blocks, human momentum

## Overview

A friendly, entrepreneurial style that makes ambitious work feel social and approachable. Warm off-white and white expanses meet dusty lavender, sage, rose and ochre blocks, divided by charcoal seams. Regular-weight grotesk typography, little portraits, conversational proof cards and oversized graphic lettering give service and community-oriented products a lively but spacious presence.

### Essentials

- A promise with people inside it: regular 69.1px grotesk type at 1.3 line height and -1.4px tracking, with overlapping 66px circular portraits nested in parentheses within the headline.
- An opening that behaves like a working noticeboard: an off-white two-thirds text column sits beside a one-third clipped stream of 360px pastel proof cards, separated by a 1px charcoal rule.
- Soft blocks have hard boundaries: full-width 40px-radius sections meet across 2–4px dark seams; white and off-white dominate the long-form space while lavender, rose, sage and ochre occupy proof cards and decisive closing bands.
- Actions are plump, not rectangular: primary pills use 22px regular text, 24px 40px padding and 100px radius; smaller chapter actions use 16px text and 12px 16px padding.
- Proof feels inhabited: flat 24px-radius project cards carry small status labels, dark tonal tags and occasional portrait overlaps. Include real people or tangible outcomes; use truthful outcome cards when photography is unavailable.
- The closing is a social junction rather than a sales billboard: a 79px rose activity ribbon, paired ochre and sage action panels with 44.1px headings, and an off-white footer with compact columns and a large condensed identity graphic. Adapt the ribbon to real product activity and the panels to two useful reader paths.

**Notes:**

- Headlines are regular weight, not heavy display type. Negative tracking is strongest on the opening promise; smaller labels keep normal tracking.
- Line heights remain open even at display sizes. The visual energy comes from composition and color rather than compressed leading or bold weight.
- The condensed identity artwork is a graphic, not the text family. Keep body and headline typography separate from that graphic language.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy in the opening and process chapters, compact inside proof cards and pricing tables.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **horizontal step** 8px, horizontal — A useful horizontal rhythm for common 16px, 24px, 32px and 48px gaps; broad panel insets also use independent fractional values.

| Value | Use |
| --- | --- |

| 24px | Pill vertical padding and ribbon padding. |
| 28.8px | Pricing panel horizontal inset and directory gap. |
| 32px | Editorial heading-to-body spacing and logo grid gaps. |
| 40px | Large card vertical inset and editorial inset-card padding. |
| 48px | Two-column process gap. |
| 57.6px | Horizontal inset of paired closing panels. |
| 64px | Opening text vertical padding and program band padding. |
| 80px | Inset identity-stage side padding. |
| 112px | Editorial manifesto top padding. |

### Containers and grids

- **opening split**: 2 columns · gap 0px. Two-thirds width promise column and one-third proof stream; text measure 720px with 144px left inset.
- **process spread**: max width 1536px · 2 columns · gap 48px · side padding 144px. Equal columns within a fluid page-width panel. At 1440px the content is 1150px wide, with roughly 551px for each side.
- **footer container**: max width 1200px · side padding 115.2px. Four-part footer: wider mission, two compact link groups, and identity artwork.
- **editorial measure**: max width 720px · side padding 40px. Centered long-form paragraphs and section statements.
- **proof grid** (only for people directories): max width 1280px · 2 columns · gap 28.8px · side padding 80px. People directory cards occupy two equal columns.
- **pricing measure** (only for pricing pages): max width 800px · 2 columns · gap 16px · side padding 144px. Narrow centered pricing field; plan pairs are equal while information rows divide 320px to 480px.

## Surfaces and depth

### Surfaces

#### Seam canvas · level 0

**Use:** Underlying frame around light sections.

**CSS:** `background: #232323`

**Observed:** Visible mainly through narrow gutters and rounded outer corners.

**Transfer:** Keep the seams thin so the page reads as predominantly light.

#### Paper block · level 1

**Use:** Opening, editorial bands and footer.

**CSS:** `background: #e5e6e1; border-radius: 40px`

**Observed:** Flat, opaque, edge-to-edge sections; the opening rounds only its lower corners and footer only its upper corners.

**Transfer:** Use as the main low-contrast ground rather than adding texture.

#### White chapter · level 1

**Use:** Large process explanation.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border-top: 1px solid #e5e6e1; border-right: 1px solid #e5e6e1; border-left: 1px solid #e5e6e1; border-radius: 40px 40px 0px 0px`

**Observed:** Later chapters use 57.6px upper corners; the final chapter also closes with 40px lower corners. Panels visibly overlap as a stack.

**Transfer:** Treat each capability as a large sheet rather than a small floating tile.

#### Tonal color block · level 1

**Use:** Proof and decisive reader-path panels.

**CSS:** `background: #e4b976; color: #65451d; border-radius: 40px`

**Observed:** Sage pairs with deep sage, lavender with deep grape, rose with wine; matching dark buttons reverse to the light panel color.

**Transfer:** Use one hue family per panel and keep fills flat.

#### Inset helper · level 2

**Only for:** pricing pages

**Use:** Workflow explanation and hiring note.

**CSS:** `background: #dcddd7; border-radius: 8px; padding: 32px 50.4px 24px`

**Observed:** A slightly darker paper inset within the broad pricing block.

**Transfer:** Reserve for useful clarifications, not promotional callouts.

#### Directory surface · level 1

**Only for:** people directories

**Use:** Biographical cards in a dark band.

**CSS:** `background: #303030; border-radius: 16px`

**Observed:** Only a small tonal lift above the charcoal canvas, without shadows.

**Transfer:** Use subdued portrait cards as a distinct dark chapter, not the default page theme.

## Imagery and graphics

### People in the promise

**Use:** Within the main promise and activity proof.

**Observed:** Real headshots appear as overlapping 66px circles inside headline parentheses, with roughly 12px overlap; smaller 29px circles punctuate the ribbon. Faces are tightly framed and naturally lit, with mixed color and monochrome portraits.

**Transfer:** Use real users, collaborators or representative product makers. If no portraits are available, insert compact circular product-detail images with the same overlap rather than generic stock faces.

### Outcome collage

**Use:** Right side of spacious process chapters.

**Observed:** Large flat lavender status cards float on white. Portraits overlap edges; a later result card overlays bright lifestyle photography of a physical product in use. The graphic takes about half the section and is balanced against a short text-and-testimonial column.

**Transfer:** Show one tangible outcome per chapter. Use your own product photograph or a constructed status card with honest result text; avoid needing a full application mockup.

**Build:** Create a 551px-wide graphic stage. Center a flat lavender rounded rectangle at about 75% of stage width, radius 24px, with a small deep-grape dot, short status label and a regular multiline title. Add two 66px circular portraits crossing opposite corners. For a delivered outcome, place a naturally lit rounded photograph behind the lower card. Use no glow or cast shadow.

### Conversational testimonial

**Use:** Below process explanation.

**Observed:** Short quotes sit in warm-paper speech bubbles with a small lower-left tail, followed by a circular author portrait, name and lighter role. A small Chapter pill sits to the right of the attribution.

**Transfer:** Use a brief, specific customer outcome, not a long testimonial wall.

**Build:** Use a paper-colored rectangle with subtly rounded corners and a triangular lower-left tail made with an SVG path or CSS pseudo-element; constrain it to roughly three-fifths of the text column. Place a 56px circular portrait below with two short attribution lines.

### Identity as architecture

**Only for:** editorial and program pages

**Use:** A large brand introduction stage.

**Observed:** Black condensed lettering spans a 1280px-wide paper backdrop behind a centered 480px lavender card. The type is tall, geometric and partly obscured by the card; it reads as architecture rather than a second readable headline.

**Transfer:** Use a short original identity or product word as a silhouette behind a readable introduction panel.

**Build:** Render an original short word as SVG text or outlined paths, scale its vertical strokes to fill most of a 1280px by 726px field, and color it near-black. Center a 480px panel with 16px corners and 40px padding above it. Keep the backdrop flat paper and omit shadows.

### Modular quarter-circle pattern

**Only for:** program pages

**Use:** One-third-width opening illustration.

**Observed:** A tall deep-purple and lavender geometric strip fills the right side. Circles, semicircles and concave four-corner diamonds form a repeating two-column tile field, cut by the section edges.

**Transfer:** Use abstract geometry when a program has no literal product image; preserve its bold, flat two-color treatment.

**Build:** Build an SVG pattern from square tiles in deep grape and lavender. Alternate full circles, paired semicircles and a central concave diamond made by subtracting quarter circles from all four corners. Repeat in two columns across a 480px strip; clip to the rounded opening.

### Orbit of people

**Only for:** pricing pages

**Use:** Centered explanatory graphic below the pricing title.

**Observed:** A 432px outlined circle encloses a dotted inner orbit and softly raised white rounded-square portrait tiles around a neutral central disc. One tile contains a plus symbol; the scene is light and spacious rather than a dense interface.

**Transfer:** Represent product modules or participants as a small orbit of tiles; keep the overall ground white.

**Build:** Use a 432px circular SVG outline at 1px with a smaller dotted concentric ring. Position four rounded portrait tiles and one add tile around the ring, with a pale central disc. Apply a diffuse low-opacity shadow only inside this illustration, not to marketing cards.

### Quiet logo proof

**Only for:** editorial and program pages

**Use:** Supporting organization or investor proof.

**Observed:** Centered multi-row logo grid on paper, with 32px gaps. Marks are predominantly black or desaturated; each retains its native silhouette with ample clear space.

**Transfer:** Normalize logos by optical size and dark monochrome treatment rather than enclosing each in a tile.

### Icons

**Observed:** Icons are sparse: text arrows on large actions, status dots, little chevrons before FAQ questions and tiny social symbols inside translucent portrait-overlap circles. The social symbols are 12px within 32px circles.

**Transfer:** Prefer text arrows and simple chevrons over a large decorative feature-icon system. Keep social icons subordinate to faces.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Human promise with live proof

**Observed:** A 54px white announcement strip precedes an 828px paper opening with 40px lower corners. The left two-thirds contains a 140px identity header separated by a 1px rule; beneath it, a 720px-measure three-line promise uses display type with portrait circles nested inside parentheses. The lower portion holds a two-line support sentence, a Primary pill and a Plain link. The right third is a clipped vertical stream of Noticeboard cards with a 1px left divider, showing partial cards at top and bottom. Back-to-front layers are charcoal seam canvas, paper split opening, structural rules, flat pastel cards and inline faces. Within the first 900px the full promise, support, both actions and card stream are visible; only the next white chapter's rounded top appears below.

**Transfer:** Use a concrete speed-or-outcome promise and put believable people or circular product details within its phrasing. **Avoid:** Do not turn the opening into a centered SaaS headline over a dashboard, or fill both columns with giant competing text. Do not place a generic full-width navigation menu above this composition.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Concrete promise → understandable process or participation → credible human proof → practical detail → useful next paths → compact identity sign-off.

### Convert interest in a service or product into an immediate first step

1. **Promise and social proof** — Split paper opening with embedded faces and a clipped stream of tonal example cards.
2. **Process demonstration** — Large overlapping white sheets pair a short explanation with an outcome collage and testimonial.
3. **Social momentum** — Rose activity ribbon.
4. **Closing choice** — Ochre and sage reader-path panels.
5. **Announcement and sign-off** — Lavender program-news band and paper footer with identity graphic.

**Transfer:** Use the opening and process sheets as the default landing-page language; tailor chapter count and proof to the product.

### Explain an organization's point of view and earn trust

1. **Mission and quantified proof** — Centered lavender statement with four deep-purple metric boxes.
2. **Problem framing** — Paired sage and rose editorial panels.
3. **Turning point** — Wide ochre statement.
4. **Introduction** — Large identity artwork behind a readable lavender card.

## Writing

Warm, direct and optimistic about getting useful work done. Confidence is expressed through concrete timing, practical mechanics and human proof rather than grandiose technical claims.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Huddle's name, logo artwork, people, customer names, quotes, program names, metrics, commercial terms or claims. Source examples illustrate rhythm only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to the reader as you; use we for the people behind the service. Reader-path headings adopt first-person identity.
- **Rhythm:** Short verb-led chapter headings meet conversational one- or two-sentence explanations. Longer mission statements are allowed; compact labels and action text keep the pace moving.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A direct verb, a desirable quality and a concrete time horizon. | About 10–16 words | “Get top designers ( ) working on your company by next week.” |
| Support | Name the resource, recognizable credibility and practical availability. | About 15–24 words | “Tap fractional experts from companies like Airbnb, Spotify and Uber available 1-5 days/wk.” |
| Chapter heading | A short action the reader can picture. | 2–6 words | “Tell us what you need”<br>“Start shipping” |
| Action | Plain, low-friction invitation; append a text arrow to larger pills. | 2–6 words | “Get started →”<br>“Apply to be a member →” |
| Reader path | First-person audience identification. | 3–5 words | “I'm a Company”<br>“I'm a Designer” |
| Practical explanation | Translate the offering into an everyday transaction. | One short sentence | “[Brand] is a flexible team of experts you can rent.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** get; build; make; ship; team; people; simple; flexible; by next week; things you love

**Avoid:** revolutionary paradigm; synergistic ecosystem solutions; enterprise-grade transformation; unmatched excellence; technical jargon without a practical outcome

**Rules:**

- Prefer concrete work, people and timing over abstract transformation.
- Pair a confident benefit with the practical mechanism that makes it believable.
- Use contractions and natural speech; reserve capitals for small labels.
- Make each proof card tell a different small story, not repeat the same generic benefit.
- Use first-person path labels only where they help readers choose a real next step.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the opening promise at regular weight 400 with open 1.3 leading.
- Pair lavender with deep grape, sage with deep sage and ochre with brown rather than default black for every colored panel.
- Use 40px outer section corners and 2–4px dark seams.
- Preserve 24px 40px padding on large pills and 12px 16px on chapter pills.
- Give process text a 16px heading gap and a 48px gap to its visual partner.
- Use tightly framed 66px people circles in the opening and 29px ones in the activity ribbon.
- Keep pricing explanations within an 800px measure.
- Finish with compact footer columns and a visually taller, narrower identity graphic.

### Don't

- Do not use heavy 700-weight marketing headlines; they lose the easy conversational tone.
- Do not put diffuse shadows under pastel cards; the system relies on flat tonal blocks.
- Do not shrink major section corners to generic 8px cards; 8px belongs to compact pricing panels.
- Do not make the default page dark from edge to edge; charcoal primarily frames the light sheets.
- Do not replace tonal tags with pastel outlined chips everywhere; small category tags are dark filled rectangles with 4px corners.
- Do not spread pricing into a full-width comparison dashboard; preserve the narrow, text-led field.
- Do not add gradients, glow or metallic 3D treatments to the flat lavender and sage language.
- Do not treat the oversized condensed identity as a substitute for the readable grotesk headline family.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The opening proof list is vertically translated within a clipped column. Process sheets visibly overlap during progression.
- No action transition durations, easing curves or animation timings are established.

**Transfer:**

- Maintain the clipped-stream composition and sheet-stack relationship even when rendered statically; motion must not be needed to read the promise or actions.

**Unknown:**

- Ticker speed, loop timing, scroll pinning details, hover treatments and focus-visible styling.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- The established composition is desktop at 1440px width; no measured narrow-screen layout is specified.

**Transfer:**

- Recommended adaptation: stack the promise above a shorter clipped proof stream, preserving the distinction between paper text and tonal cards.
- Recommended adaptation: stack process text before its collage and closing paths vertically; keep portrait overlaps and flat surfaces.
- Recommended adaptation: change directory and pricing pairs to a readable single column when necessary, keeping titles attached to their details.

**Unknown:**

- Exact breakpoints, mobile type sizes, navigation behavior and stream height.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
