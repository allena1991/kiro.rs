# ilovecreatives — editorial playground energy

## Overview

An editorial playground where serious practical information shares space with oversized serif lettering, friendly grotesk text and gleefully odd art direction. Mostly white and black, it releases bursts of lime, sky blue, pink and green through illustrated scenes, posters and colorful media. It suits personality-led products, communities and learning experiences, with spacious sections but busy, browsable shelves of work and people.

### Essentials

- Identity acts as the first piece of art: a high-contrast serif word sculpture spans 1300px inside 70px side margins, laid over full-bleed human photography. Use an equally expansive original product phrase when no identity artwork exists.
- Headings are conversations between typefaces: 54px/1.05 grotesk with -0.04em tracking mingles with 59.4px serif words on the same 56.7px baseline rhythm; section introductions pair 32px Arial with 35.2px romana.
- Links become circled words, not rectangular controls: 1px outlines, fully rounded ends, 1px 8px padding for headline links and 5px 20px for small browse buttons. A starburst and a directional arrow belong inside the verbal rhythm.
- Color lives in the exhibits rather than the furniture: white reading sections and a black closing area frame saturated artwork. Include a vivid full-width art-led interruption; keep navigation, ordinary typography and card chrome monochrome.
- The shelves keep going beyond the page: rounded 20px media, unboxed captions and partially visible next items make horizontal collections feel like an active directory, with 637px-wide landscape covers or 305px-wide portraits.
- Include a typographic broadcast device: 92px-high hairline-ruled strips combine 54px text, serif insertions and outlined word links. Pair this with a monumental typographic moment somewhere in the page, not necessarily in the same section.
- The ending feels like a generous invitation, not a tiny utility bar: a black field opens with a 54px white invitation and 18px support, a white 238×84px pill, then dense link columns and a final legal row.

## Typography

- **SuisseRegular** — Body, controls, oversized sans headlines and directory titles. Plain, compact neo-grotesk; regular-looking strokes even where declared at weight 100. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use regular 400 for the light-declared text; preserve the tight tracking. Font stack: "SuisseRegular", "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **romana** — Serif words inserted in sans headlines and descriptive section subheads. Tall high-contrast editorial serif with narrow proportions and elegant sharp joins. If unavailable, use **Bodoni Moda**. Use 400; tune horizontal width to keep the compact editorial silhouette. Font stack: "romana", "Bodoni Moda", Georgia, serif.
- **Arial** — Short directory section names. Unembellished familiar sans, deliberately less precious than the serif. Font stack: "Arial", Helvetica, sans-serif.
- **Junicode-two-beta-condensed** (only for condensed-serif product campaigns) — Chapter headings and italic verbal emphasis. Narrow literary serif, used with highly expressive condensed italics. If unavailable, use **Instrument Serif**. Regular and italic 400; retain the narrow proportions. Font stack: "Junicode-two-beta-condensed", "Instrument Serif", Georgia, serif.
- **ArialNarrow** (only for product campaigns and annotated company collages) — Narrow sans counterpart to condensed serif, uppercase labels and tight captions. Compressed utilitarian sans rather than an extended display face. If unavailable, use **Roboto Condensed**. Font stack: "ArialNarrow", "Roboto Condensed", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Brivela** (only for hand-lettered campaigns) — Brushy emphasis inside chapter headings. Heavy, liquid handwritten lettering with thick rounded strokes. If unavailable, use **Knewave**. Font stack: "Brivela", "Knewave", cursive.

**Notes:**

- Regular-looking type is the default: do not turn weight-100 declarations into wispy hairline text when substituting fonts.
- Large sans tracking is usually -0.04em, body -0.02em; outlined headline links tighten to -0.06em. Serif insertions share the surrounding line rhythm rather than becoming a separate text block.
- Wordmark lettering is artwork, not a 59.4px heading. Preserve its near-container-wide silhouette and its roughly 238px-tall image stage. For a replacement word sculpture, size original serif lettering to that stage and fit its width.

## Surfaces and depth

### Surfaces

#### White reading canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default page and shelf backing.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #222222`

**Observed:** Text and covers sit directly on white; panels are not all elevated cards.

**Transfer:** Let typography and media supply the identity.

#### Black invitation field · level 0

**Use:** Closing invitation and product proof bands.

**CSS:** `background: #000000; color: #ffffff`

**Observed:** An uninterrupted black plane without gradients or floating card outlines.

**Transfer:** Use as a deliberate contrast reset.

#### Outlined white card · level 1

**Use:** Small statements and audience cards.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border: 1px solid #222222; border-radius: 20px; box-shadow: none`

**Observed:** Flat, thin-edged and friendly, with no elevation.

**Transfer:** Keep the interior text short enough to preserve breathing room.

#### Ruled broadcast band · level 0

**Use:** Scrolling text strips and topical crosslinks.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border-top: 1px solid #222222; padding: 16px 0; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** Approximately 92px high; large type and pills cross the viewport edges.

**Transfer:** Use a short recurring phrase plus useful actions.

#### Illustrated chapter panel · level 0

**Only for:** hand-lettered campaigns

**Use:** Large chapter art with readable text and diagrams.

**CSS:** `background: #80c1e7; border-radius: 40px; color: #3f3838`

**Observed:** Alternates blue, yellow-lime and light neutral art grounds; thin hand-drawn diagrams and original cutout compositions occupy the right half.

**Transfer:** Use broad flat fields and a single strong explanatory illustration.

**Use:** Small utilitarian top navigation.

### Browse pill

**Use:** Compact section-level browsing action.

**CSS:** `border: 1px solid #000000; border-radius: 300px; padding: 5px 20px; color: #000000`

**Text style:** `body` — SuisseRegular 100 · 18px/1.4 · tracking -0.02em

**Observed:** Right-aligned opposite the two-line section heading; transparent rather than filled. Text is 18px regular weight 400 with normal line-height and -0.02em tracking.

### Inline word pill

**Use:** Make a headline's key phrase an actual link.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #000000; border: 1px solid #222222; border-radius: 1458px; padding: 1px 8px`

**Text style:** `inline-action` — SuisseRegular 100 · 48.6px/1.167 · tracking -0.06em

**Observed:** Flows within the sentence and stays close to the baseline; serif inline links use the section-description recipe at smaller sizes.

**Transfer:** Circle only a meaningful noun or verb phrase, not the whole heading.

### Metadata pill

**Use:** Categories and locations immediately below media.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #000000; border: 1px solid #222222; border-radius: 450px; padding: 1.5px 6px`

## Imagery and graphics

### Human identity stage

**Use:** Default first impression.

**Observed:** Full-bleed candid fashion-like portrait of a person turned away, pale wall, hard side shadow, gray clothing and visible grain. White serif identity stretches across the upper-middle; the image is larger than any paragraph.

**Transfer:** Photograph someone using or inhabiting the new product's world. Minimum version: a candid portrait against a pale wall, directional light and subtle grain, with original oversized serif lettering over it.

### Toy-world feature scene

**Use:** Signature saturated interruption among white reading sections.

**Observed:** Acid-green inflated landscape with a lavender sofa, miniature tables, flowers, a friendly retro computer and tiny graphic props. Soft plastic forms, fuzzy texture and dreamy depth fill the entire band; white type overlays the left.

**Transfer:** Make a playful still life from objects representing the product. Minimum version: a cutout collage of three original objects on a vivid tactile backdrop, not stock SaaS device mockups.

**Build:** Compose an oversized lime upholstered or inflated shape as the ground, one lavender focal object off center, and three smaller mint or checkerboard props. Use soft directional lighting, faint grain and overlapping foreground objects. A 2D SVG fallback uses swollen rounded organic paths, translucent highlight ellipses and photographic texture clipped inside them.

### Directory portraits and covers

**Use:** Browsable collections and proof.

**Observed:** People portraits are black and white and tightly framed in tall rounded rectangles. Course, shop and article covers remain vividly colored, often mixing editorial type, screenshots, cutout objects and stickers; their surrounding captions stay monochrome.

**Transfer:** Use real portraits for people and authored poster covers for products. Keep branding inside the cover, not as colored page chrome.

### Monumental social masthead

**Use:** A second identity-scale moment near media proof.

**Observed:** Black high-contrast serif social handle occupies a 1300×342px stage above a row of eight small rounded video or image thumbnails. White play triangles sit over selected thumbnails.

**Transfer:** Use a product-related collective phrase or handle as original oversized lettering above real work or short demos.

**Build:** Fit a single narrow high-contrast serif line across the content width, with a smaller sans prefix aligned near its baseline. Leave broad white space beneath, then lay out eight evenly spaced rounded thumbnails; apply object-fit cover and simple white triangular play overlays.

### Grainy light atmosphere

**Only for:** condensed-serif product campaigns

**Use:** Chapter transitions, teaching panels, perks and price art.

**Observed:** Pale blue cloudlike haze dissolves into white; lime-green grainy gradients anchor some sections. Spheres glow at the center, diagrams have fine outlines, and teaching cards use large rounded corners.

**Transfer:** Use atmospheric color as the support for useful diagrams rather than as a universal page gradient.

**Build:** Layer broad pale-blue radial gradients at the upper corners over white and let them fade to transparent near the bottom. Add a low-opacity SVG turbulence grain layer. For a green chapter use a lime oval fading into white; inset simple circles, timeline bars and black hairline connectors. Match the soft grain and broad fades; exact stops are not essential.

### Hand-lettered sticker campaign

**Only for:** hand-lettered campaigns

**Use:** Product identity, chapter panels, pricing and final lead stage.

**Observed:** Black-outlined wobbly lettering filled with green, yellow-lime and blue, pink audience backdrop, red instructor field, cutout people and cartoon stickers. Final price stage uses lime with multiple poses of a small cutout person; final form stage is green with a light panel edged in rough blue and a collage of tilted chapter covers.

**Transfer:** Create an original doodled title and illustrated chapter system for a deliberately youthful campaign; keep ordinary body copy plain.

**Build:** Draw letter outlines as slightly irregular SVG paths with dark strokes and flat color fills. Mix one brushy word with upright sans headings. Back chapters with broad rounded blue or lime fields; layer two or three tilted document thumbnails and a cutout person, plus small hand-drawn arrows. Build the final form panel as a light rounded rectangle with an irregular blue stroked SVG frame.

### Editorial company collage

**Only for:** company storytelling

**Use:** Explain an ecosystem or larger purpose.

**Observed:** A tilted orange poster is hemmed by huge black sans statements, some vertical. Later, a white 900px-wide manifesto poster sits in a blue cloudy sky, with stacked uppercase statements, paired body columns and a circled closing action. A loose cabinet of objects uses tiny numbered captions.

**Transfer:** Use a poster and annotated object collection to explain relationships between offerings instead of repeating feature cards.

**Build:** Rotate an original orange editorial diagram slightly counterclockwise and surround it with large horizontal and vertical sans labels. For the manifesto, place a flat white vertical panel on a cloud photograph or softly blurred blue-white SVG field; keep display lines narrow and centered, then end with two small text columns and one outlined phrase.

### Icons

**Observed:** Tiny social marks and an 18px search icon in the header, white triangular media-play symbols, directional arrows and text starbursts. Icons are monochrome and subordinate to typography.

**Transfer:** Use text arrows and starburst glyphs as punctuation; reserve familiar small line icons for navigation and simple filled triangles for media.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Portrait and word-sculpture opening

**Observed:** A 781px-tall full-bleed portrait stage sits behind a transparent two-tier header. Within 70px side margins a white serif identity image spans 1300×238px, beginning around 158px below the top. The person occupies the center-right while the lettering crosses the image, not a separate card. A short 18px support paragraph occupies roughly 522px at the lower left, with a small Inverse media button beneath. In the first 900px the full image stage, identity, support and video action are visible, followed by the beginning of a white 54px mixed-face promise. Media covers and browsing shelves fall below.

**Transfer:** Use the Human identity stage and original product wording fitted as a monumental serif word sculpture. If no custom footage exists, a well-lit still portrait with grain supplies the same presence. Product-focused variants can open with condensed serif title art on black and two small white pills, or a hand-lettered title, cutout person and compact support on a pale ground. Company storytelling opens on white with the same giant identity followed by a mixed-media statement.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a centered generic sans headline above a device screenshot, or a stack of floating rounded hero cards.

**Text style:** `display-serif` — romana 100 · 59.4px/56.7px · tracking -0.04em

### Mixed-face promise

**Observed:** A white strip combines heading, display-serif and Inline word pill within a sentence, with small inline images as punctuation; about 43px top breathing room follows the media hero.

**Transfer:** State the product's practical promise in an unusually typographic sentence. Give the reader one or two linked phrases, not a list of badges.

**Text style:** `heading` — SuisseRegular 100 · 54px/1.05 · tracking -0.04em

### Editorial shelf heading

**Observed:** Section-name sits above section-description at the left; Browse pill aligns with the lower line on the right. Supporting 14px copy and previous/next arrows precede the media shelf by around 14px–34px.

**Transfer:** Use the first line to name a category and the serif line to explain why someone should browse it.

**Text style:** `section-name` — Arial 100 · 32px/1.1 · tracking -0.02em

### Unboxed media shelf

**Observed:** Media cover leads, then Metadata pill, card-title, compact description or quote and a small status action. A partial next item stays visible. Sections generally start with 50px top inset and leave around 90px–105px below their content.

**Transfer:** Choose portrait, landscape or small poster proportions according to the content; never force mixed subject matter into one universal card height.

**Text style:** `card-title` — SuisseRegular 100 · 30px/1.2 · tracking -0.04em

### Art-led feature interruption

**Observed:** Toy-world feature scene fills a broad band between white shelves. A two-line 90px white title, Inverse media button, 32px quote and short attribution occupy the left; artwork remains large behind them.

**Transfer:** Choose one product story to celebrate with an authored scene and real proof, keeping text readable over a quieter portion of the art.

**Text style:** `feature-display` — SuisseRegular 100 · 90px/0.95 · tracking -0.04em

### Broadcast and social proof

**Observed:** Ruled broadcast band carries big repeated language and Inline word pills beyond both edges. Monumental social masthead then dominates a white area above eight small rounded media previews.

**Transfer:** Place the broadcast strip and monumental word moment where they best re-energize the narrative; use original topic words and actual product/community media.

**Text style:** `heading` — SuisseRegular 100 · 54px/1.05 · tracking -0.04em

## Page flow

**Storyline:** identity and invitation → practical promise → browsable work or product chapters → human proof → specific next step → generous relationship close

### Broad discovery and browsing

1. **Identity** — Full-bleed human portrait and monumental serif identity.
2. **Promise** — Mixed-face sentence with circled links.
3. **Discovery** — Alternating editorial shelf headings and overflowing media collections.
4. **Feature story** — Saturated full-width toy-world scene with title, action and quote.
5. **Community proof** — Ruled broadcasts, giant social masthead and small media previews.
6. **Closing invitation** — Black subscription field, link groups and legal row.

**Transfer:** Let one strong media stage introduce the brand, then give visitors multiple editorial ways to explore.

## Writing

A talented friend who can explain the practical stuff without becoming corporate. Warm, irreverent and reassuring, with jokes, plain outcomes and honest acknowledgement of uncertainty.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, wordmarks, course names, proprietary artwork, personal names, testimonials, prices, dates, performance claims or customer logos. Source examples illustrate voice only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Talk directly to “you”; “we” is a candid helpful team. First-person audience quotes mirror the reader's inner monologue.
- **Rhythm:** Brief headline phrases, chatty explanatory paragraphs and occasional abrupt asides. Mix lists of concrete outcomes with short reassuring sentences; exclamation marks, parentheses, arrows and starbursts are purposeful punctuation.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | Name the result, then remove the emotional obstacle. | 6–12 words | “Learn 2D & 3D Motion Design — without the overwhelm.” |
| Reassurance | Short contrast between approachability and practical value. | 3–8 words | “Beginner-friendly, but job-ready.” |
| Problem heading | Say the frustrating thing the reader is already thinking. | 8–14 words | “Designing for social shouldn’t be something you just “figure out”.” |
| Action | Direct verb plus urgency or direction, with a starburst or arrow. | 2–5 words | “Enroll ✹Today↘”<br>“Show all the Gems↗” |
| Objection handling | Ask the question plainly and answer in low-pressure fragments. | One question followed by 2–4 fragments | “Full access to everything. Your own timeline. No pressure.” |
| Closing invitation | A specific cadence plus the ongoing benefit. | 8–12 words | “Every Wednesday, creative happenings in your inbox.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** real-world; good vibes; Heck yeah; you’re not alone; make work you’re proud of; getta clickin’; practical; without the overwhelm

**Avoid:** revolutionary platform; unlock unparalleled potential; seamless end-to-end solution; world-class ecosystem; guaranteed success

**Rules:**

- Explain what someone can actually make or do before listing tools.
- Name the fear without making the reader feel incompetent.
- Put the joke in an aside, not in place of the useful answer.
- Use first-person concerns and real attributed proof instead of anonymous superlatives.
- Make the closing invitation concrete about what happens next.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main desktop reading width at 1300px within 70px margins.
- Pair the 32px section name with a 35.2px serif explanation, not two generic sans headings.
- Use 1px outlines and fully rounded pills for action phrases.
- Keep 20px corners on media and let captions sit directly on white.
- Retain partially visible next items on horizontal collections.
- Include original oversized serif lettering, a ruled broadcast and a saturated art-led interruption.
- Use 18px/1.4 tightly tracked body text, with 14px/1.4 captions.
- Give the black ending its own 124px opening space and oversized white pill.

### Don't

- Do not apply an eight-point grid retroactively; the gutters include 17px and the side margins are 70px.
- Do not color ordinary navigation or make all button fills green; color belongs to authored exhibits.
- Do not substitute heavy bold grotesk for the regular-looking 54px tightly tracked headlines.
- Do not put shadows beneath every card; the default surfaces are flat.
- Do not turn 15px metadata pills into oversized spacious badge bars.
- Do not grayscale the colorful product artwork along with the people portraits.
- Do not spread the brush font or condensed campaign serif across ordinary directory pages.
- Do not compress the full black ending into a thin copyright strip.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared action transitions include color, background-color and border at 100ms linear.
- A marquee is declared at 50000ms linear with infinite repetition.
- Full-width media stages and horizontal shelves support a moving editorial feel.

**Transfer:**

- Use the long continuous broadcast motion for short repeated language, with overflow clipped at the viewport.
- Keep action color inversions quick and simple; do not add springy card motion to the flat shelves.

**Unknown:**

- Hero media playback behavior and precise scroll-reveal choreography are not established.
- Focus-visible and active styles are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Width breakpoints include 640px, 767px, 768px, 900px and 1200px in the broad discovery layout; campaign styles add thresholds around 980px–1024px and 1250px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, fit the word sculpture to the available width while retaining its oversized presence; let long mixed-face promises wrap naturally.
- Preserve horizontal overflow for media shelves and audience cards instead of making their type illegibly small.
- Stack split story/form layouts and footer link groups while keeping the invitation visually distinct.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile font sizes, margin values and breakpoint-specific navigation behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
