# Kit — condensed creator energy

## Overview

A confident, people-first software style pairing muscular condensed sans headlines with straightforward grotesk body copy. Near-black ocean blue openings give way to bright white and warm ivory chapters, with cyan actions and concentrated lilac, orange and green accents. Large interface collages and candid working portraits make the product feel tangible; spacious sections alternate with dense, useful navigation and comparison content.

### Essentials

- Headlines have a compact, muscular silhouette rather than spaced-out luxury lettering: KitSansFont at 80px/0.95 for the opening and 56px/1 for chapter headings, weight 500 and normal tracking.
- The opening is a dark editorial stage with a bright product reveal: centered text within 768px, a cyan 60px action, and a 1312px-wide rounded demonstration beginning near the bottom of the first screen.
- Buttons are soft rectangles, not capsules: 14px corners with 20px padding for major actions; 12px corners with 16px padding for regular actions; 8px corners for compact navigation controls.
- Color is organized by chapter: white and ivory carry most reading, deep blue carries the opening, charcoal carries the ending; saturated cyan, lilac, orange and green stay concentrated in actions, proof and media rather than tinting every section.
- Interface imagery is an assembled scene, not a browser-window dump: rounded square or wide backings, subtle grain, overlapping white panels and selected enlarged details; use a simplified real workflow collage when finished product media is unavailable.
- People provide the visual proof: include candid working photography with dark bottom fades and white captions, plus a horizontally spilling portrait or capability rail with 32px gaps. Use authentic practitioner portraits or work-in-progress photography when customer media is unavailable.
- The ending is a substantial dark directory, not a tiny copyright strip: charcoal background, an action-bearing identity column, three compact link columns, then separated social and legal rows with 48px bottom padding.

## Typography

- **KitSansFont** — Display headlines, chapter titles, statistics and footer group labels. Heavy-looking condensed humanist sans with rounded joins, open counters and a narrow, lively silhouette despite its medium declared weight. If unavailable, use **Barlow Condensed**. Use 700 as a visual starting point; preserve condensed width and tune wrapping. Font stack: "KitSansFont", "Barlow Condensed", "Arial Narrow", sans-serif.
- **Libre Franklin** — Body paragraphs, card titles, controls, captions and navigation. Franklin-style grotesk with broad, sturdy lowercase forms; regular reading text and bold short labels. Font stack: "Libre Franklin", Arial, sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- Keep the condensed headline face distinct from Libre Franklin; substituting one wide geometric family for both destroys the hierarchy.
- Headlines use normal tracking even at 80px and 140px; compression comes from the face, not extreme negative letter spacing.
- Body tracking stays near -0.2px while action labels use -0.1px. Balance headline lines into short, assertive groups.
- The default opening is 80px; 64px split openings and the 140px photographic mission opening are scoped alternatives, not a blended scale.

- **split-chapter**: 2 columns · gap 80px. Headline on the left; short support and actions on the right, aligned toward the bottom.
- **media-pair**: 2 columns · gap 64px. Two 624px square demonstrations with deliberately staggered vertical placement.
- **three-cards**: 3 columns · gap 32px. Three 416px article or capability cards.
- **support-cards**: 3 columns · gap 48px. Three square pictures with titles and descriptions beneath.
- **plans** (only for pricing pages): 3 columns · gap 16px. Three roughly 427px plan cards.
- **team-directory** (only for company storytelling pages): 4 columns · gap 16px. Four centered portrait entries per row.

## Imagery and graphics

### Grained ocean field

**Use:** Signature backing for the opening and product showcase.

**Observed:** Very dark blue-black becomes muted ocean blue lower down. Fine mottled grain covers the field without adding large decorative shapes.

**Transfer:** Retain the grain even when using a static product collage instead of video.

**Build:** Use the Ocean stage gradient, then an absolutely positioned SVG feTurbulence fractalNoise layer with baseFrequency around 0.8, three octaves and low opacity around 0.08. Clip to the section; tune the grain so it reads as fine print texture rather than stars. This is a construction recommendation.

### Layered product scenes

**Use:** Major capability explanations and opening showcase.

**Observed:** Large white interface panels sit on blue, purple, peach or green backings. Selected workflow nodes, profile cards and metrics lift forward and overlap the base panel; square scenes are 394px–624px wide, while wide scenes fill the 1312px content spine. Corners are 12px–16px and grain is subtle.

**Transfer:** Use a real interface fragment plus two or three enlarged, legible details. Minimum version: one rounded backing, one partial app panel and one floating outcome card.

**Build:** Place a tonal backing in a clipped rounded wrapper, inset the base interface, then absolutely layer small white cards with modest shadows. Use fine connector lines only when demonstrating a real process. Keep the product's own UI typography inside these scenes rather than extending it into marketing copy.

### Working-life photography

**Use:** Trust rails, customer statements, audience tiles and closing conversion panels.

**Observed:** People are photographed in lived-in studios, kitchens, transit and workspaces rather than isolated corporate headshots. Wide panels are landscape and tiles are portrait or square. Color remains natural; dark fades let white copy sit near the bottom.

**Transfer:** Photograph people using or benefiting from the new product in authentic environments. If no customers are available, show the making process or a real practitioner rather than synthetic social proof.

### Staggered support triptych

**Use:** A human support or collaboration chapter.

**Observed:** Three square media blocks descend across the row instead of sharing a rigid top edge. Rich green interface illustrations precede a dark studio photograph; titles and descriptions sit outside the pictures.

**Transfer:** Keep the descending rhythm while mixing practical product artifacts with one real-world image.

**Build:** Use a three-column grid with 48px gutters and 12px picture corners; apply progressively greater vertical offsets to the second and third columns, resetting those offsets in a stacked layout.

### Flying accent illustration

**Only for:** reassurance conversion panels

**Use:** A small expressive interruption of the photographic callout edge.

**Observed:** A cyan-and-orange illustrated bird overlaps the upper edge of the dark migration panel, with textured shading and a pointed sweeping silhouette; it is much smaller than the photo.

**Transfer:** Replace with an original subject-relevant directional emblem, not a large mascot covering the copy.

**Build:** Construct a small SVG silhouette from two curved tapered wing shapes and a pointed body, fill with cyan, dark blue and a restrained orange highlight, then add clipped low-opacity noise. Position above the panel on the right so part projects outside its boundary.

### Team portrait directory

**Only for:** company storytelling pages

**Use:** Long staff listing after a photographic mission opening.

**Observed:** Small rounded-square portraits retain varied personal backgrounds, color and lighting. Names are centered in bold 24px Libre Franklin with 16px role captions. Four columns leave extensive white space around each portrait.

**Transfer:** Keep real individual character rather than forcing uniform cutout headshots. Scale the directory to the actual team.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small filled SVG symbols, mostly 20px or 24px, provide category marks, checks, arrows and chevrons. Chapter icons share the chapter tint; comparison checks use cyan. Social marks are compact and monochrome.

**Transfer:** Use simple filled or visually solid symbols with consistent optical weight; avoid a contrasting thin-outline icon library.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Ocean product opening

**Observed:** Back to front: grained Ocean stage; compact fixed Navigation; centered display promise in a 768px column; lead support after 24px; Primary button after 32px; small trial reassurance after 12px; then a rounded 1312px-wide product demonstration. The headline forms two dense lines at 80px/0.95 and occupies roughly the middle half of the width. Its text block begins 184px below the start of the dark field. Within the first 900px the entire headline, support, action and reassurance are visible, plus the top of the demonstration beginning around 634px. Most of the demonstration and the trust rail fall below the fold. A campaign strip, when present, precedes the navigation without becoming the main message.

**Transfer:** For another product, center an outcome promise above a bold scene showing the core transformation; use a static layered interface when video is unavailable. Detailed features can instead open on ivory with a 64px split headline and tall photograph-plus-interface composition, or a centered 56px title above a wide screenshot. Pricing opens directly with a 64px left title and plans; company stories use the scoped photographic mission opening.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the dark stage with a white dashboard hero, squeeze the complete demonstration into the first screen, or give every hero line a different color.

**Text style:** `display` — KitSansFont 500 · 80px/0.95

**Transfer:** Organize each chapter around one meaningful user result, then demonstrate it; rotate only among coherent chapter palettes.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → tangible product demonstration → human trust → capability chapters → specific proof → practical reassurance → objections or learning resources → substantial directory.

### Broad product landing page

1. **Promise** — Dark centered opening with a bright demonstration entering the fold.
2. **Trust** — Large trust statement and horizontally spilling portraits.
3. **Capability chapters** — Colored chapter accents, staggered scenes, a warm ecosystem band and support triptych.
4. **Breadth and social proof** — Capability rail, wide testimonial photograph and portrait mosaic.
5. **Reassurance and objection handling** — Photographic assistance callout then two-column questions.
6. **Ending** — Dark action-bearing directory, socials and legal row.

**Transfer:** Use the full visual vocabulary while choosing chapters around the new product’s benefits.

### Plan selection and purchase confidence

1. **Pricing** — Direct ivory title, billing controls and three nested plan cards.
2. **Comparison** — Trust statement followed by a ruled feature matrix with tier actions.
3. **Social proof** — Square video and large quotation.
4. **Reassurance** — Dark photographic conversion panel.
5. **Objection handling** — Split FAQ block.
6. **Ending** — Shared dark directory.

**Transfer:** Make the price model understandable before presenting supporting stories.

### Explain a complex flagship capability

1. **Promise** — Ivory split opening with creator photograph and floating workflow nodes.
2. **Benefits** — Three-column reasons followed by large offset interface demonstrations.
3. **Quantified proof** — Dark photographic statistics panel with lilac numerals.
4. **Demonstration and social proof** — Additional capability scenes, large customer quotation and workflow example rail.
5. **Breadth** — Six compact feature descriptions and photographic action panel.
6. **Education and objection handling** — Article tiles, related capability links and split FAQ.
7. **Ending** — Shared dark directory.

**Transfer:** Use layered scenes to explain a process in stages rather than placing the whole app in one picture.

### Explain a two-sided product benefit

1. **Promise** — Centered ivory title above wide interface panel.
2. **Capability explanation** — Split text and peach-backed interface, followed by customer quotation.
3. **Complementary outcome** — Ivory chapter with numbered steps and green-backed interface.
4. **Community proof** — Horizontal working-portrait cards with category tags and bottom captions.
5. **Closing call** — Dark inset centered conversion panel followed by a short practical demo invitation.
6. **Education and ending** — Three article tiles then shared dark directory; no FAQ block.

**Transfer:** Explain both sides of a mechanism through distinct chapters and distinct media colors.

### Make the organization tangible

1. **Mission** — Monumental white headline on a darkened group photograph.
2. **Human scale** — Centered team summary and action.
3. **People** — Long four-column portrait directory with names and roles.
4. **Ending** — Shared dark directory without an additional conversion banner.

**Transfer:** Allow people to be the primary material rather than reusing a product-demo sequence.

## Writing

Commercially confident but warm and practical. It speaks to ambitious independent operators, connects features to earnings or time, and reassures without sounding institutional.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Kit or ConvertKit names, logos, customer identities, proprietary artwork, quotations, audience counts, revenue statistics, discount terms or claims. Replace them with the new product’s own substantiated content. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your. Use we for human assistance and the product name for concrete capabilities.
- **Rhythm:** Short, punchy headlines balanced across two or three lines; support usually one sentence. Detailed explanations are plain spoken and longer, especially in step lists and FAQs.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Turn an existing resource into a valuable outcome. | 6–10 words | “Turn your audience insights into revenue” |
| Chapter headline | Contrast the work being automated with the human value preserved. | 6–10 words | “Automate your emails, not your relationships”<br>“Grow faster with a team, not just software” |
| Supporting line | One direct benefit in ordinary language. | 6–16 words | “Email automation that earns while you create.”<br>“You don't have to build your email strategy alone.” |
| Action | Verb plus clear commitment or destination. | 2–5 words | “Start free trial”<br>“Request free migration” |
| Reassurance | Name a practical worry, then say who handles it. | Two short sentences | “Switch without the stress. We’ll move your list for you.” |
| Feature explanation | Concrete action followed by how it helps a human outcome. | One or two sentences | “Create branched workflows based on clicks, purchases, and engagement so every email feels personal.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; grow; your business; on your terms; hands-off; get your time back; together; without the stress; actually works

**Avoid:** revolutionary paradigm; synergy; best-in-class ecosystem; unleash limitless potential; frictionless transformation

**Rules:**

- Lead with a reader outcome, then name the mechanism.
- Use product specificity in paragraphs rather than stuffing the headline with feature names.
- Pair ambitious results with practical operational reassurance.
- Number genuine setup steps; do not make every section a numbered framework.
- Keep trial requirements and support promises close to the relevant action.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main opening at 80px/0.95 with a 768px text width.
- Use 56px/1 condensed chapter headings over 18px/1.5 introductions.
- Pair 60px filled actions with 62px outline actions at a 16px gap.
- Use 1312px as the main desktop content width and 32px card gutters.
- Keep long reading sections white or ivory and concentrate rich color inside media.
- Include both layered product scenes and authentic working-life photographs.
- Give FAQs 12px corners, 28px summary padding and 16px row gaps.
- Carry the dark directory through a separate social/legal finish.

### Don't

- Do not set marketing headlines in Libre Franklin; reserve it for reading and controls.
- Do not apply -0.05em tracking to the condensed display face; its default is normal.
- Do not turn the 14px button corners into fully rounded capsules.
- Do not add drop shadows to every article or FAQ card; flat borders are the default.
- Do not spread lilac, green and orange indiscriminately across one chapter.
- Do not shrink major product scenes into icon-sized feature illustrations.
- Do not make the whole site dark because the opening is dark; most explanation chapters are light.
- Do not give the directory a giant decorative wordmark or monumental closing slogan; its drama comes from scale, density and contrast.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions declare all 0.15s cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1); directory links declare color-related transitions at 0.1s with the same easing.
- Article movement includes 0.3s ease-out transitions and translated tucked-away actions with .15s cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1).
- Some image cards declare a 0.6s ease-in-out infinite alternate animation. Staggered media uses motion-safe parallax classes.

**Transfer:**

- Keep action feedback quick and chapter-media movement subordinate to reading.
- Preserve the staggered media composition in a static implementation; animation is not required to make the scene intelligible.

**Unknown:**

- Exact parallax amplitudes, full keyframe paths and playback behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop content uses a 1312px spine with 32px container padding; header max-width is 1920px.
- CSS classes declare one-column to multi-column grids, full-width to auto-width actions, a navigation link threshold at 821px, and the three-plan grid at 841px.
- Opening headline classes reduce the desktop 80px recipe to 64px and 48px at smaller sizes; company mission classes include 100px below the desktop 140px tier.

**Transfer:**

- For narrower layouts, stack heading and support before the media, remove stagger offsets and keep images full-width within the gutter.
- Let capability rails retain a partially visible next item rather than compressing all cards into one row.
- Use the smaller rectangular action variants in navigation, retaining the main action's emphasis.

**Unknown:**

- Actual mobile renderings, exact breakpoint-to-size mapping and mobile navigation interactions were not observed.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
