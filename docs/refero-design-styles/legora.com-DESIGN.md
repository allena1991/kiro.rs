# Legora — glass systems, human judgment

## Overview

An expansive, pale enterprise style that gives complex software the physical presence of frosted glass and polished objects. Regular-weight grotesk typography, forest green accents and warm gray fields keep the tone assured rather than futuristic. Softly staged interfaces, architectural photography and witty monochrome drawings alternate between technical sophistication and human warmth; small text and controls sit within unusually spacious compositions.

### Essentials

- Cinema carries the promise, not giant typography: a full-bleed 900px media stage places a white 56.2px regular headline near its lower edge, with an 18px support line and a compact action beneath.
- Software becomes a material object: include a pale glass product sculpture or dimensional interface scene, using translucent layers, fine bright edges, dark reflections and soft gray studio light. A small stack of translucent SVG plates is the minimum equivalent when there is no rendered product film.
- The interface stays miniature against the stage: actions are 30px high with 48px radii, 14.4px labels and a separate 20px white arrow disk; section headings are 41.2px or 33.7px rather than oversized bold statements.
- Green punctuates an otherwise mineral page: #fafaf9 dominates, #005032 marks the 44px announcement strip, actions and footer links, and #003d26 fills the inset illustrated call card. Large saturated fields are concentrated in these devices, not spread across every section.
- The conversion device pairs institutional green with editorial humor: include a 1080px-wide, 15px-radius green panel with text and a low-anchored action on the left and a square black-line drawing on white on the right.
- The ending has its own scale change: five small green directory columns sit above a centered wordmark artwork about 487px wide and 94px high, with the utility row below. Use the new identity as the same substantial visual anchor.

## Typography

- **Legora Sans** — Default marketing headings, descriptions, navigation and actions. Open, compact grotesk with distinctive letter shapes; overwhelmingly regular, with medium reserved for small feature titles and primary actions. OpenType features: "blwf", "cv03", "cv04", "cv09", "cv11". If unavailable, use **Inter**. 400 and 500; retain the tight heading tracking and compact controls. Font stack: "Legora Sans", "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Aktiv Grotesk VF** — Large percentage numerals and editorial prose variants. Restrained neo-grotesk, with very light large numerals and tightly spaced editorial headlines. If unavailable, use **Roboto**. 300 for large percentages, 400 for editorial prose. Font stack: "Aktiv Grotesk VF", "Roboto", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Rhymes Display** (only for company scale statistics) — Large company-count numerals only. Light, high-contrast display serif with long elegant curves, providing a single editorial interruption to the sans system. If unavailable, use **Bodoni Moda**. Use the lightest available cut with normal tracking. Font stack: "Rhymes Display", "Bodoni Moda", Georgia, serif.

- Regular weight is the default even for the principal headline. Medium weight belongs to small labels, not large section statements.

- **page container**: max width 1680px · side padding 24px. Broad centered content frame; at 1440px, content spans 1392px.
- **centered heading**: max width 680px · side padding 30px. Centered section lead with short subtitle.
- **body column**: max width 480px. Readable feature copy within the broad page frame.
- **conversion panel**: max width 1080px · 2 columns · gap 15px. Inset green call card, approximately equal text and square-art halves.
- **innovation grid**: 4 columns · gap 32px. Portrait media tiles with titles and compact descriptions, without separate card shells.
- **feature sequence**: 4 columns · gap 40px. Four sequential capability or process tiles.
- **editorial thirds**: 3 columns · gap 10px. Narrow text third beside a media field spanning two thirds; reverse media and text in subsequent chapters.
- **footer directory**: 5 columns · gap 60px · side padding 24px. Five top-aligned small-text columns distributed across the page width.

## Surfaces and depth

### Surfaces

#### Chalk canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default marketing background.

**CSS:** `background: #fafaf9`

**Observed:** A pale warm field links most chapters; depth comes from the media rather than raised containers.

**Transfer:** Keep the majority of a new page on this surface.

#### White directory · level 0

**Use:** Footer and illustration backing.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff`

**Observed:** A subtly cleaner white distinguishes the final directory from the warm page field.

**Transfer:** Preserve the almost imperceptible shift rather than adding a strong footer divider.

#### Stone editorial band · level 0

**Use:** Human narrative and large use-case lists.

**CSS:** `background: #e1dfda`

**Observed:** Full-bleed, flat and square-edged; image and text form an asymmetric editorial composition.

**Transfer:** Use for a human or contextual argument, not as a repeating feature-card color.

#### Ink assurance band · level 0

**Use:** Trust and technical-assurance chapters.

**CSS:** `background: #0d1016; color: #ffffff`

**Observed:** One expansive dark chapter with restrained light text and fine low-contrast cell edges.

**Transfer:** Reserve the inversion for a serious objection-handling block.

#### Green conversion · level 1

**Use:** Illustrated action panel.

**CSS:** `background: #003d26; border-radius: 15px; padding: 15px; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** An inset horizontal composition, not a full-width green band. Workflow and recruitment versions use the deeper call green.

**Transfer:** Keep a square white illustration field beside a spacious text half.

**Only for:** workflow explanations and company narratives

**CSS:** `background: #0a0a0a; color: #ffffff; padding: 160px 24px`

## Imagery and graphics

### Pale dimensional system

**Use:** Signature product stage and product-tile illustrations.

**Observed:** Frosted transparent plates float in an exploded isometric stack, with polished dark rims, bright white bevels, tiny black labels and diffuse contact shadows. The completed object resembles a translucent block or keyboard. Neutral gray-to-white studio light fills most of a viewport; compact charcoal controls overlay its left edge.

**Transfer:** Translate a different system into physical layers, containers or modules. Keep the object pale and tactile rather than using colorful futuristic circuitry. Include at least a simplified layered sculpture if a detailed render is unavailable.

**Build:** Create 4–6 rounded square SVG plates in a 650px-wide group. Transform each into a shallow isometric diamond using a rotate/skew combination, offset layers vertically by 24px, fill with translucent white at 0.25–0.55 opacity, add thin white top edges and charcoal lower edges at 0.2–0.45 opacity. Place a broad gray ellipse blurred by 30px below the stack. Use a white-to-light-gray vertical backing, a few tiny dark labels and restrained glass highlights; a polished 3D render is preferable.

### Cinematic interface macro

**Use:** Default full-bleed opening and inset product film.

**Observed:** Extreme close-ups of pale software surfaces and floating translucent document objects fill the frame. Black text and files become spatial objects, sometimes partially outside the view. Shallow depth of field and muted gray light provide a soft technical atmosphere; a dark overlay creates legibility at the lower headline.

**Transfer:** Film or compose one close-up product operation, with deliberate off-frame scale and a readable lower text zone. If no film exists, use a still dimensional interface macro rather than a generic abstract gradient.

**Build:** Layer a pale interface plane and enlarged task text across a full-bleed stage, with partially clipped adjacent panels and a few blurred foreground fragments. Add a black gradient overlay at 0.5 opacity at top and bottom, dropping to 0.1 through the central area. Keep marketing text as a separate crisp layer.

### Interfaces in architectural light

**Use:** Capability chapters and product demonstrations.

**Observed:** Floating white or translucent interface panels appear over warm wood, leather, curtains, stone and directional window shadows. Other panels sit on soft neutral studio backings. Media is rectangular and square-edged, usually larger than the adjacent copy; surrounding photographs remain subdued and spacious.

**Transfer:** Place a real task interface over a calm material photograph with generous breathing room. Keep actual interface colors small; do not extend every colored UI badge into the marketing palette.

### Editorial line drawing

**Use:** Signature illustrated call card.

**Observed:** Black hand-drawn people and objects with light gray washes sit on a clean white square. Scenes show an exaggerated collaborative mechanism, such as people moving papers or sharing an implausible vehicle. The lightly comic image occupies half of the green card and contrasts with the technical media elsewhere.

**Transfer:** Commission an original visual metaphor for the new product's human benefit. Minimum version: a simple original black-line scene with two or three people and one shared object, not a stock multicolor vector character.

**Build:** Use an SVG with irregular 1–2px black strokes, white fills and a handful of translucent gray wash shapes. Draw a broad curved machine or table in the center and several simplified human figures around it; keep large white margins and avoid color fills or a heavy frame.

### Architectural and human photography

**Use:** Customer proof, audience carousel and editorial narratives.

**Observed:** Natural-light offices, façades, city architecture and professional portraits use subdued beige, gray, dark teal and green. Portraits feel candid or calmly posed rather than cut out. The audience carousel contrasts smaller 209×261px images with a larger 390×487px center image; customer stories can become full-width photographic stages.

**Transfer:** Use real environments and people connected to the new audience, with believable materials, shadows and restrained saturation.

### Footer identity anchor

**Use:** Signature footer artwork below directories.

**Observed:** A solid forest-green wordmark roughly 487×94px is centered in a broad empty field. It is substantially larger than the header identity but does not span the whole viewport.

**Transfer:** Use the new brand's wordmark as the final visual anchor, at comparable visual width and with generous space above and below.

**Build:** Place a single-color SVG wordmark in a centered 487px-wide, 94px-high contain box. If no wordmark asset exists, typeset the new name in a carefully spaced display face and convert it to an SVG; keep the surrounding directory and utility text much smaller.

### Company portrait and team imagery

**Only for:** company and recruitment narratives

**Use:** Team introduction, mission and quotation chapters.

**Observed:** A wide group photograph follows an offset long-form opening; square architecture and meeting photographs support the narrative. A centered quotation ends in a 414×518px upright portrait on taupe with attribution above.

**Transfer:** Preserve the changes in image scale and the human editorial tone, using the new team's own scenes.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small utilitarian arrows and plus signs, usually 10px inside 20px disks. Carousel arrows are 14×14px with a 1px square-ended stroke. Larger play triangles appear only on film controls.

**Transfer:** Choose simple line arrows, pluses and sparse solid play symbols; icons should guide actions, not replace the product imagery.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Cinematic promise opening

**Observed:** A full-bleed 1440×900px media stage starts behind the fixed header. From back to front: pale dimensional interface film, a black legibility gradient, then a crisp white promise near the bottom and the fixed announcement/navigation stack above. The 56.2px regular headline is a single centered line about two fifths of the screen width, not a huge top-aligned banner. A support line and Primary button share a centered row 30px below, with 15px between them and about 60px below the group. Within the first 900px, the header, dominant macro product object, complete headline, support and action are visible; the trust rail begins below the fold.

**Transfer:** Use a close-up staged operation from the new product, or the Cinematic interface macro still recipe, and retain the low, calm promise. A short product-name opening uses product-display centered on chalk, followed by a 24px-inset film beginning within the first screen. A detailed capability opening instead uses a one-third text column and two-thirds photographic demonstration with heading-lg. Company narratives open with an offset editorial-headline paragraph followed by a broad team image.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a giant bold heading above a tiny browser mockup, or center a generic illustration beside two oversized buttons.

**Text style:** `display` — Legora Sans 400 · 56.2px/1

### Centered heading pair

**Observed:** Heading-lg and support stack in a centered 680px maximum width with 30px separation. A 60px gap leads into a major product stage; larger 200px pauses can distinguish chapters.

**Transfer:** Use a concise category statement with one explanatory line; keep the supporting text smaller and quieter.

**Text style:** `heading-lg` — Legora Sans 400 · 41.2px/1 · tracking -0.01em

### Feature media grid

**Observed:** Feature media tile repeats in four columns with 32px gutters; a process version uses 40px gutters. Tall 4:5 images dominate, followed by feature-title and body. The section lead remains left-aligned or centered above the whole grid, rather than repeated within each card.

**Transfer:** Show distinct moments or outcomes in the new product. Keep the visual language coherent while changing the content of each tile.

### Alternating capability chapter

### Photographic proof stage

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → material product explanation → capability or context chapters → human and quantified proof → confidence → illustrated invitation → identity-led directory.

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **promise** — Full-viewport product macro with low centered promise and small action.
2. **social proof** — Compact monochrome trust rail.
3. **product explanation** — Centered heading introduces an extended glass-system stage with chapter controls.
4. **capability chapters** — Four-column media grid and center-weighted audience carousel.
5. **human and quantified proof** — Full-width film followed by split percentage rows.
6. **confidence** — Stone editorial narrative followed by a dark assurance chapter.
7. **closing call** — Green illustrated call panel and white identity-led directory.

**Transfer:** Use a similarly varied sequence of technical, editorial and human scales; shorten the long product stage to suit the content.

### Flagship product launch

1. **promise** — Centered short product name and small action above a broad inset film.
2. **product explanation** — Split text and square interface demonstration.

## Writing

Assured, professionally fluent and optimistic about what people can accomplish with capable tools. Technical language explains how the product works, while the human outcome stays close at hand.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Legora's name, product names, trademarks, illustrations, logos, customer identities, quotations, funding announcement, certifications or numerical claims. Source examples demonstrate phrasing only; use the new project's own substantiated content and original assets. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you or your team when explaining control and benefit. Use we for mission and intent; describe the product in the third person when stating capabilities.
- **Rhythm:** Short declarative headings, often 3–8 words; a single support sentence; compact two- or three-sentence feature descriptions. Lists of active verbs create momentum without exclamation marks.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero promise | Name the work or outcome, then remove its constraint. | 4–8 words | “Legal work, without limits.”<br>“Orchestrate complex legal tasks.” |
| Support line | Identify the audience and benefit without explaining every feature. | 5–20 words | “Collaborative AI for exceptional lawyers”<br>“The agentic operating system for legal work” |
| Capability heading | A concrete action or a concise system-level claim. | 1–9 words | “Run complex legal work from start to finish.”<br>“Every tool, working together”<br>“Plan” |
| Feature description | Explain what happens, then clarify the reader's role or control. | 20–50 words | “The Agent starts by analyzing the task, selecting the right tools, and proposing a plan.”<br>“No two lawyers work the same way. Skills let you encode your standard, processes, and preferences so the Agent works the way your team does.” |
| Primary action | Short verb-led invitation with a specific destination. | 2–5 words | “Book a demo”<br>“View roles” |
| Closing invitation | Invite a concrete next step or ask a benefit-led question. | 5–15 words | “Discover the future of legal work through a live product demo.”<br>“Ready to streamline your workflow?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** together; judgment; end-to-end; built for; review; deliver; confidence; your team; in action

**Avoid:** magic; crush it; 10x everything; effortless success; robot replacement; unlimited guarantees

**Rules:**

- Pair claims about speed or scale with a concrete task and a human benefit.
- Describe a process with active verbs rather than long adjective chains.
- Use domain vocabulary when it clarifies a real capability, not as decorative jargon.
- Keep actions specific and repeat the same conversion label for the same destination.
- Make trust statements factual and narrow rather than relying on superlatives.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep principal marketing headings at weight 400 and section leads near 41px or 34px.
- Make the opening media occupy the full 900px first-screen height while the promise stays low.
- Retain 30px action pills with 20px arrow disks instead of scaling controls with the hero.
- Use 24px page gutters and 32px gutters for the four-column innovation grid.
- Pair a 1080px green conversion panel with a square white line-drawing field.
- Use light 90px numerals with small captions for outcome proof.
- Let square-edged photographic and interface media dominate the feature tiles.
- Give the directory a centered identity artwork around 487px wide beneath the small links.

### Don't

- Do not make every surface pure white; the warm #fafaf9 canvas should remain perceptible.
- Do not turn each feature into a rounded elevated card; media tiles have no enclosing shadow or fill.
- Do not use a bright green gradient as the general page background; institutional green belongs to specific action and identity devices.
- Do not use the 88px serif count style for ordinary product headings or outcome percentages.
- Do not replace glass media controls with an opaque white sidebar; their charcoal translucency matters over the pale scene.
- Do not spread screenshot badge colors across marketing headings and buttons.
- Do not enlarge all body text to 20px; the dominant description recipe is 15px/1.3.
- Do not shrink the closing identity to the same scale as the navigation logo.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The header remains fixed while chapters scroll beneath it; the long glass-system explanation uses sticky chapter controls and changing dimensional layers.
- Editorial paragraphs use a per-word black/quarter-opacity text-clipped gradient to progress from pale to dark.

**Transfer:**

- Tie glass-layer changes to the corresponding chapter label rather than adding unrelated decorative movement.
- Keep the static product sculpture and all editorial prose complete and legible when motion is unavailable.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
