# Lottielab — playful tools, open space

## Overview

A bright, spacious software style that makes professional tools feel approachable and creative. Rounded geometric sans typography, violet actions and plum-black text sit on white and cool gray, with playful interface illustrations carrying most of the color. Sparse promises give way to detailed product stages, a dramatic dark device scene and friendly community proof; it suits collaborative creative products and self-service tools.

### Essentials

- A two-line promise separates product category from audience: 60px/1 semibold geometric sans, -0.025em tracking, plum first line and violet second line, centered over a single action.
- The opening earns its whitespace before revealing the tool: a 64px fixed white header, headline starting around 256px, trust line and eight muted marks below, then a full-width gray product stage beginning at 771px.
- Actions are small violet cushions, not pills: 12px corners, 1px matching border, 20px horizontal padding and 46px height for the main 18px action; compact actions are 38px high.
- Color is concentrated in working examples: most of the page remains white or pale gray, with short violet headline accents, warm gradient phrase endings and one plum-black demonstration band rather than alternating saturated sections.
- Product ability is pictured, not decorated: broad browser-like workspaces, isolated vector operations and a 3-column bento with two tall outer cards and stacked middle cards. Recreate real product outcomes with simple vectors when full interface media is unavailable.
- Community proof dissolves into the canvas: two offset rows of 384px-wide white quote cards, 16px gaps, 8px corners and 160px white edge fades inside a 900px window. Pair this device with a spacious centered closing call, dashed top rule and solid side rails.

**Notes:**

- Use the same geometric sans for both friendly display and practical interface-adjacent copy. Tracking tightens only on the 60px display and 18px feature titles.
- Split headings into a neutral setup and a colored payoff. The payoff retains its parent's font recipe; gradients do not introduce a new face or heavier weight.
- Sentence case is the norm. There is no recurring all-caps eyebrow system; section headings carry the hierarchy directly.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy outside demonstrations, compact inside cards and the directory; broad pauses keep the product illustrations feeling light.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **vertical rhythm** 8px, vertical — Most vertical layout spacing follows this rhythm; 10px footer row gaps and compact action padding are exceptions.

### Containers and grids

- **outer-frame**: max width 1400px · side padding 96px. Centered shared shell; at 1440px the side-gutter layout leaves a 1248px-wide content rail.
- **chapter-grid**: 2 columns · gap 32px. Equal image and text areas, with vertical text centering and alternating media sides.
- **feature-bento**: max width 1280px · 3 columns · gap 16px. Two tall outside cards span the rows; the center column holds two shorter cards. Desktop width is 1248px within page gutters.
- **quote-window**: max width 900px · gap 16px. Clipped horizontal quote rows with broad white edge fades.
- **reading-column** (only for story indexes and guided tools): max width 761px. Narrow centered story or tutorial content within a larger side-railed frame.
- **story-grid** (only for customer story indexes): 2 columns · gap 32px. Two thumbnail-and-caption columns inside the reading column.

**Use:** Default page ground.

**Use:** Full-width pale backdrop for a large working product scene.

**CSS:** `background: #f3f4f6; padding: 64px 96px 0px; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** The framed workspace rises from the pale band and meets its lower edge.

**Transfer:** Place a real multi-object product workflow on this stage rather than a floating decorative card.

**Use:** Feature and testimonial containers.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border: 1px solid #e5e7eb; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: none`

**Use:** One dramatic product compatibility or ecosystem scene.

**CSS:** `background: #1c1a2c; color: #ffffff; padding: 96px`

**Observed:** A large plum-black band interrupts the white canvas; its artwork is itself dark with lavender and small bright accents.

**Transfer:** Reserve the dark treatment for a convincing product story, not every other section.

#### Ra led chapter · level 0

**Use:** Subtle structural framing around illustration chapters and closing call.

**CSS:** `border-left: 1px solid #e5e7eb; border-right: 1px solid #e5e7eb`

**Observed:** Solid vertical rails sit inside the page gutters; full-width horizontal dividers are dashed.

#### Directory ground · level 0

**Use:** Shared long footer.

**CSS:** `background: #f6f6f6; border-top: 1px solid #e5e7eb; padding: 96px 96px 240px`

#### Warm phrase

**Use:** Short payoff within a split illustration heading.

**CSS:** `background-image: linear-gradient(to right, #facc15, #f97316); background-clip: text; color: rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)`

**Observed:** A second version runs from pink to coral; only a short phrase takes the gradient.

#### Quote edge fade · level 2

**Use:** Left edge of the clipped community strip.

**CSS:** `background-image: linear-gradient(to right, #ffffff, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0)); width: 160px; position: absolute; height: 100%; z-index: 10`

**Observed:** The right edge mirrors the direction, so central cards remain legible while peripheral cards dissolve.

**Transfer:** Overlay the fades above two offset rows, not over the entire text area.

## Imagery and graphics

### Working canvas

**Use:** Large early product demonstration.

**Observed:** A broad browser-like frame with a thin white top bar, pale gray workspace, artboards in several sizes and a bottom timeline. Visible art includes a payment card, a small shaded heart and a green vector mascot; colorful outputs are isolated on white artboards, not spread across the page.

**Transfer:** Show the new product's actual work in progress at several scales. A minimum version is a crisp browser frame with three populated output panels, useful labels and one plausible editing control.

**Build:** Use a wide rectangular gray workspace with a 24px white top strip and 4px top corners; arrange differently sized white artboards across it with 1px neutral outlines. Populate every board with an intentional output, add a restrained bottom control strip and apply the Workspace shadow.

### Isolated operation diagrams

**Use:** Alternating image-and-text chapters and bento cards.

**Observed:** Lightweight vector scenes float on white: paired tool symbols, a selected colored shape following a curved path, cyan selection handles, small colored collaborator cursors and violet size bars. The illustration commonly occupies about half the chapter width, with lots of unpainted room.

**Transfer:** Depict the corresponding product operation using one object, a path or state transition and a few meaningful controls. Keep the drawing clearer than a full interface.

**Build:** In an approximately 527px square SVG stage, center a simple product object; add thin guide lines, a curved dashed path and four small selection handles where relevant. Keep neutral scaffolding faint and put strong color only on the selected object and collaborators.

### Dark device family

**Use:** Signature full-width product delivery scene.

**Observed:** A large desktop monitor at left, two tablet or landscape panels stacked in the middle and a tall phone at right share one dark plum material. Screens repeat rounded abstract shapes, flowers, zigzags and hearts in lavender with sparing pink, cream and green. Beveled dark frames are understated rather than glossy photorealistic hardware.

**Transfer:** Carry one recognizable product result across several device proportions. A minimum version uses clean vector device silhouettes containing consistent output rather than expensive 3D renders.

**Build:** On the Dark demonstration surface, arrange a 512px-wide desktop group, two approximately 294px-wide landscape rectangles and a 186px-wide portrait phone. Draw thick low-contrast plum frames, dark screen insets and softly rounded edges; repeat a small family of circles, rounded blocks and starburst shapes across screens, with violet dominant. Do not add neon outer glows.

### Output mosaic

**Use:** A broad gallery of possibilities beneath a centered invitation.

**Observed:** Twelve square examples form two nearly edge-to-edge rows of six, each around 237px square with 4px gutters. Bright blue type compositions, product mockups, chair imagery, line icons and atmospheric campaign art coexist; color lives inside the cells.

**Transfer:** Show a varied selection of complete outcomes from the new tool: type, practical artifacts and visual compositions. Keep a consistent square framing despite different content styles.

**Build:** Create a two-row six-column grid with 4px gaps, square cells and hidden overflow. Alternate full-bleed outcome artwork with centered objects on pale backings; use original populated examples rather than empty preview cells.

### Story covers

**Only for:** customer story indexes

**Use:** Thumbnails that distinguish customer narratives.

**Observed:** Wide covers pair a bold customer identity with a product artifact against a faint technical grid; one is vivid red with a payment card, another nearly white with a black identity mark. The visual variety is contained inside the media frame.

**Transfer:** Use the customer's own palette and tangible artifact, keeping the title and summary outside the image. A simple original identity-plus-artifact layout is sufficient.

### Tool opener diagrams

**Only for:** single-purpose tool landing pages

**Use:** Small illustrative hook above the tool title.

**Observed:** A compact 300px-wide branching delivery diagram connects one violet source to platform symbols; a creation variant shows four playful marks spaced in a horizontal row.

**Transfer:** Use a compact schematic of input-to-output or a small set of representative results above the title, not a full-width billboard.

**Build:** Center a small source tile and connect it to three endpoints with thin gray forked lines; accent one route with short violet dashes. For an output-led workflow, use four equal-size original examples with equal spacing.

### Icons

**Observed:** Navigation is mostly text. Small external-link arrows are 20px outlines with 2px round strokes in muted gray; customer marks are monochrome and low contrast. Interface illustrations use their own colorful functional symbols.

**Transfer:** Choose simple round-stroke arrows for utility links; keep standalone feature icons subordinate to the actual product diagrams.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Open promise and workspace reveal

**Observed:** A white canvas sits behind Fixed navigation. The centered two-line Display promise occupies roughly 623px of width, starting around 256px down; plum setup above violet audience phrase. An 18px support sentence follows with a 32px margin, then Primary button after another 32px. After 96px, the Trust sentence includes Count badge; eight low-contrast customer marks sit below it with 64px spacing. In the first 900px the complete promise, support, action and trust row are visible, followed by the top of Workspace stage at 771px and its browser frame at 835px. Most of the large working canvas lies below the fold. Layers stay flat: white page, sparse type and action, muted proof, then gray stage behind the framed workspace.

**Transfer:** Keep this the default marketing opening: a category-and-audience promise, one concise outcome line, one action, understated proof and a peek of a populated product stage. If full product media is unavailable, construct the Working canvas from three representative outputs. Single-purpose tool pages instead use a 48px title, light 18px introduction and compact action; a small diagram or row of results can precede them. Story indexes open with pale customer marks above a left-aligned 48px title in the narrow reading column.

**Avoid:** Do not move a giant illustration above the default promise, use a split-screen sales hero or crowd the opening with multiple competing action buttons.

**Text style:** `display` — Plus Jakarta Sans 600 · 60px/1 · tracking -0.025em

### Split benefit chapter

**Observed:** Ra led chapter contains two-column media and copy, with 64px vertical padding and 96px inner side padding on desktop. A 36px Heading breaks into neutral setup and Warm phrase payoff; body follows after 24px. Media switches sides between operations and uses an approximately 527px-wide stage. Full-width dashed rules lightly divide the chapter from adjacent content.

**Transfer:** Explain one operation per row with an isolated diagram, alternating visual weight. Use a short colored phrase to express speed or control without turning every heading into a gradient.

**Text style:** `heading` — Plus Jakarta Sans 600 · 36px/40px

### Dark multi-device demonstration

**Observed:** A dashed top rule and solid side rails enclose a centered closing group with 224px vertical padding. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible product demonstration → capability chapters → range or community proof → spacious final action → useful directory; order and depth adapt to the visitor's intent.

### Broad product marketing landing

1. **Promise** — Centered two-tone audience promise, one action and muted adoption proof.
2. **Product demonstration** — Wide working canvas followed by alternating operation diagrams.
3. **Capability chapters** — Dark device scene and practical three-column bento.
4. **Range and social proof** — Colorful square-output mosaic and fading quote rows.
5. **Closing call** — Centered promise within lightly ruled whitespace, then a long neutral directory.

**Transfer:** Balance practical tool evidence with playful finished results; vary section order to suit the new product.

### Focused creation-tool landing

1. **Promise** — Centered 48px tool title, light introduction and direct opening action.
2. **Product demonstration** — Large working canvas on pale gray.
3. **Capability chapters** — Compact chapter heading above the familiar bento.
4. **Social proof** — Community quote rows with white edge fades.
5. **Closing call** — Shared spacious conversion block and directory.

**Transfer:** Shorten the narrative when visitors already know the category; foreground the working tool.

### Delivery or infrastructure feature landing

1. **Promise** — Small branching diagram above centered title, introduction and compact action.
2. **Product demonstration** — Dark cross-device scene with a smaller bold heading.
3. **Capability chapters** — Reusable bento expands the proposition beyond the single feature.
4. **Social proof** — Two fading rows of community endorsements.
5. **Closing call** — Shared centered call and neutral directory.

**Transfer:** Turn an abstract infrastructure benefit into a concrete source-to-many-contexts visual.

### Guided creation and acquisition page

1. **Promise** — Four example marks precede the centered title and tool action.
2. **Activation** — Downward cue introduces a numbered upload step, with an alternate creation path.
3. **Capability chapters** — Bold operation headings with gradient payoff phrases, followed by bento benefits.
4. **Social proof** — Beginner-friendly introduction above the quote strip.

## Writing

Friendly, practical confidence with a designer's enthusiasm for ease and craft. Benefits are expressed as actions and everyday outcomes, while community quotes bring the more exuberant emotion.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Lottielab name, logo, customer marks, quotations, adoption counts, enterprise claims or performance promises. Replace source-specific tool names and examples with truthful content for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to 'you' and 'your' in task-oriented copy; use 'our' sparingly in explanations. Product headings often omit pronouns and start with a verb.
- **Rhythm:** Short 3–8 word headings, a concise payoff on a second line and one or two plain sentences of support. CTA labels are short verbs; enthusiasm and emoji belong mainly to quoted users.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero promise | Product category followed by intended audience. | 6–10 words | “The motion design tool for product teams” |
| Section benefit | Action or capability followed by a short colloquial payoff. | 3–8 words | “Design to Motion in a snap.”<br>“Quick to learn, powerful once mastered” |
| Support | A concrete activity and its destination, with little preamble. | 10–25 words | “Create and edit lottie animations, ship to your apps and websites.” |
| Feature explanation | Specific functional benefit in plain language. | 10–20 words | “Export tiny, optimised files that load fast, without sacrificing quality.” |
| Primary action | A direct verb; remove friction with a truthful qualifier. | 2–5 words | “Get started for free”<br>“Open editor” |
| Guided instruction | Number the step and name the concrete action. | 4–7 words | “Step 1. Upload your logo” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** create; edit; ship; bring to life; in a snap; with ease; powerful; intuitive; built with care; your team

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; unlock limitless potential; synergize; disrupt everything; enterprise-grade transformation

**Rules:**

- Name the task before the technology; reserve specialized terms for readers already using that tool.
- Pair ease with capability rather than pretending the product is simplistic.
- Keep section support visually and verbally secondary to the illustrated operation.
- Use specific verbs in actions instead of vague invitations such as 'Discover more'.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main promise at 60px/1 and weight 600 with -0.025em tracking.
- Maintain 96px desktop outer gutters and lightly drawn 1px rails.
- Use 12px action corners and 8px card corners as distinct shape roles.
- Give feature-card text 40px insets and let diagrams occupy the remaining area.
- Retain the 16px bento gutters and the tall–stacked–tall composition.
- Keep short gradient payoff phrases at the same 36px recipe as their surrounding heading.
- Use 14px names and roles below the 16px quote text.
- Preserve the 224px closing padding and quiet 240px directory bottom space.

### Don't

- Do not substitute pill-shaped actions for the 12px rounded rectangles.
- Do not turn the inline 16px monospace count into a giant dashboard statistic.
- Do not add drop shadows to every white card; 1px outlines do the separating.
- Do not use fully black text as the general headline default; the main ink is plum.
- Do not apply warm text gradients to whole paragraphs or all headings.
- Do not tint every feature card violet; their surfaces are white.
- Do not place quote text directly on colored quote tiles; keep white cards with dark emphasis and gray prose.
- Do not scale utility navigation to 18px; its measured role is 16px medium. 

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Buttons declare a box-shadow transition of 0.5s cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1); hover changes violet background and border to the lighter violet.
- Community rows use horizontally translated marquee containers; their opposing positions vary through the page.
- Product illustrations show changing selection states, paths and small animated outputs.

**Transfer:**

- Preserve the softly dissolving horizontal proof strips and use product motion to demonstrate a task, not to animate every text block.
- Keep the fixed white navigation stationary while illustrated chapters change underneath it.

**Unknown:**

- Exact marquee speed, direction per row and looping duration are not established.
- Product animation timing, triggers and easing are unknown.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 640px, 768px, 1024px, 1280px, 1536px, 1920px, 2400px and 2800px.
- Navigation destination links are hidden below the 1024px breakpoint while signup remains.
- The default hero changes from 48px to 60px at 768px and becomes centered there; several section headings change from 24px to their desktop sizes.
- Split operation grids become two columns at 768px. The feature bento is hidden below 768px and becomes three columns at 1024px.

**Transfer:**

- Preserve text-first hierarchy on narrow layouts, with compact outer gutters and populated product scenes scaled to fit.
- Do not force the desktop three-column bento into tiny cards; keep its detailed diagrams for the larger layout.
- Keep the quote strip clipped with edge fades, but ensure a central quote is readable at the available width.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile menu behavior and actual mobile compositions were not observed.
- Large-screen sizing beyond the desktop window is not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
