# Mana — fizzy illustrated exuberance

## Overview

A sunny, irreverent consumer style that pairs oversized light grotesk lettering with exuberant illustrated packaging and tactile everyday photography. Cream and leaf green provide the breathing room, while yellow, sky blue, pink and tomato red arrive as whole scenes rather than tiny accents. Sparse copy and roomy compositions make the product feel like a playful object, with enough structured detail for shopping and education.

### Essentials

- The product is the opening spectacle, not a text-and-image split: a full-width leaf-green stage, illustrated edges, a tilted oversized object entering from below, and a 71px pill between white circular arrows.
- Light capitals become physical scenery: weight 300, normal tracking, 80.9px section questions and a 334.1px single-word chapter title; include a bent or individually rotated word sculpture as well as ordinary live headings.
- Color arrives in big, bounded doses: green dominates the opening scene, cream carries reading and conversion, and yellow supports a later social scene. Product imagery fills adjoining green, yellow and blue tiles rather than scattering accent colors through every paragraph.
- Buttons feel like cut-out objects: white 40px-radius navigation capsules, 49.5px-radius colored conversion pills with 1px outlines and a hard lower edge, and smaller black 37.7px-radius utility pills.
- Photography mixes convincing material detail with a visual joke: vivid products sit among dishes, dough, cosmetics and flowers, in full-bleed 720px half-width frames or colored studio stages. If a product cannot be photographed, use a clean dimensional mock-up and original flat motifs, not interface placeholders.
- Proof is playful but structurally legible: white benefit cards with broad colored caps, oversized single-color symbols, and 1px ruled factual rows. Carry the card-and-symbol device into each new landing page.
- The goodbye is a little playground: a 900px cream closing scene with an original oversized flat character, outlined clouds, a thin undulating horizon, yellow game control and tiny legal baseline. Keep this illustrated closing device even when the interaction is simplified.

**Notes:**

- Keep tracking normal throughout; the airy character comes from light strokes and scale, not widely spaced capitals.
- Do not make every heading monumental: the browse strip is sentence case at 28.5px, while chapter titles and curved word art supply the spectacle.
- Word sculptures use live SVG text along shallow arcs or separately rotated words. Keep their apparent stroke weight as light as the surrounding display lettering; treat them as illustration rather than body copy.

## Imagery and graphics

### Illustrated product world

**Use:** Hero and the transition into the benefit story.

**Observed:** A dimensional printed can tilts toward the viewer from the bottom center of a pale green field. Oversized flowers, leafy stems, a starburst-backed fruit and a cropped red patterned shape form an open frame around it. The center stays spacious and the illustrations remain flatter than the realistically lit metal object.

**Transfer:** Create a clean product render or isolated high-resolution photograph. Surround it with original motifs derived from the new product's form, material or effect. A static tilted object plus flat SVG edge motifs is the minimum acceptable version.

**Build:** Place a transparent object layer over the green field, sized to dominate the lower half and clipped by the bottom edge. In separate SVG layers, use five rounded flower lobes, pointed leaf paths and triangular rays. Crop two clusters against opposing edges; fill with deep green, red and yellow, using a yellow-to-red linear gradient only inside selected flowers. Leave the middle clear.

**Use:** Full-bleed interludes and smaller editorial proof images.

**Transfer:** Photograph the new product in one recognizable use context and one unexpected but harmless situation. Fill frames decisively with hands, texture and product rather than stock-photo scenery.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** object-led delight → conversational explanation → tangible proof → product choice or deeper detail → renewed invitation → playful goodbye

### Immersive consumer marketing landing page

1. **Product spectacle** — An illustrated color world and dimensional object introduce the offering with minimal copy.
2. **Category explanation** — A large conversational question becomes a sequence of orbiting benefit cards around the product.
3. **Sensory proof** — A full-width photographic diptych makes the product tangible and unexpectedly domestic.
4. **Product choice** — A monumental chapter word gives way to adjoining color-coded product tiles.
5. **Repeat engagement** — A tilted word sculpture resolves into a subscription action; a yellow social collage broadens the invitation.
6. **Closing delight** — An illustrated game scene ends on cream with discreet legal navigation.

**Transfer:** Borrow the shifts between object, typography, photo and choice, not a fixed number of scenes.

### Browse a family of purchasable products

1. **Invitation** — Enormous split-word capitals and a short service-oriented support line on cream.
2. **Product choice** — Three-column zero-gap product tiles wrap into a second row.
3. **Family demonstration** — A full-width macro video shows several packages together.
4. **Closing delight** — The same cream playground and legal baseline.

**Transfer:** Make this the more direct shopping variant while retaining expressive type and a sensory interlude.

### Understand one item and complete a purchase

1. **Purchase decision** — Equal image gallery and centered buying panel with price, description, quantity and yellow action.
2. **Factual proof** — Editorial lead, checked ingredient-style rows, then an image paired with a ruled data table.
3. **Benefit explanation** — Arched word art introduces the single-card presentation.
4. **Lifestyle demonstration** — Two large photographs show material and human context.
5. **Alternative choice** — A three-tile recommendation strip.
6. **Closing delight** — The same character playground ends the page.

**Transfer:** Preserve the compact buying panel and put technical facts into clear ruled structures.

### Explain the product's category, origin and process

1. **Origin promise** — Large reversed capitals over an origin image, ending at a curved text-bearing edge.
2. **Material explanation** — An offset photo-and-illustration composition leads into a substantial editorial paragraph.
3. **Benefit chapters** — Arched word art and staggered red-capped white cards.
4. **Process proof** — An image-only macro material scene.
5. **Objection handling** — Large reassurance paragraph, black FAQ action and a smaller right-offset qualification.
6. **Closing delight** — The shared illustrated playground and small legal row.

**Transfer:** Replace origin and process material with the new product's real construction story; maintain the alternation of facts and expressive graphics.

## Writing

Friendly, sensory and a little mischievous. Simple benefits are stated confidently, then softened by a conversational aside or a small joke rather than by corporate language.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Mana's name, wordmark, character, package illustrations, proprietary slogans, social handle, exact product names, health claims, caffeine quantities, certifications, prices, savings or shipping thresholds. Source quotations illustrate voice only; replace every factual claim with substantiated information for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your everyday needs; we appears when the brand is being candid or making a practical offer.
- **Rhythm:** Short uppercase display phrases alternate with sentence-case explanatory prose. Two-sentence benefit explanations often end in a brief conversational fragment; parenthetical asides and asterisk footnotes carry the jokes.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Section question | Ask the reader's basic category question without technical vocabulary. | 6–12 words | “[Brand] ? Yerba Maté ? What are we talking about?” |
| Chapter caption | A compact list of sensory qualities, followed by a direct invitation. | 8–14 words | “Fresh, fruity, sparkling, beautiful colours, awaken your taste buds.” |
| Benefit explanation | One tangible feeling, one practical outcome; understated ending. | 7–18 words | “A gentle wave of energy. To get you going without the crash.”<br>“Richer in antioxidants than tea. Not bad.” |
| Conversion support | A clear action and practical reward in a single sentence. | 8–15 words | “Sign up for automatic delivery* and save 10%.” |
| Primary action | Direct verb or recognizable shopping destination. | 2–5 words | “Subscribe”<br>“Add to cart”<br>“See all our products” |
| Playful footnote | An everyday limitation exaggerated into an absurd aside. | 8–14 words | “* We don't deliver in space yet, but who knows...” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** a gentle wave; your everyday; fresh; a dose of; we have what you need; not bad; who knows

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; unlock your potential; enterprise-grade; synergistic; clinically proven without support; luxury exclusivity

**Rules:**

- Make the ordinary product benefit understandable before adding a joke.
- Reserve uppercase for display, labels and brief facts; write paragraph explanations in sentence case.
- Use precise numbers only when they help a real purchase decision or can be verified.
- Let a small aside supply the wink; do not fill every headline with puns.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep major display lettering at weight 300 with normal tracking, alongside regular-weight body text.
- Use the 38.9px desktop gutter consistently for text, controls and fact columns.
- Let colored product tiles meet edge-to-edge in three desktop columns with 1px ink rules.
- Keep white navigation capsules separate above full-bleed scenes, at roughly 51px high.
- Give benefit symbols the visual weight of a 173px graphic, not a small decorative icon.
- Make at least one chapter feel physically typographic through a bent phrase or tilted word composition.
- Use cream as the breathing space between large green, photographic and yellow scenes.
- Keep the illustrated closing scene dominant, with the legal links small and below the horizon.

### Don't

- Do not substitute bold 700-weight headlines for the light uppercase display voice.
- Do not add letter spacing to the giant capitals; their tracking is normal.
- Do not put soft drop shadows on product tiles or benefit cards; their separation is color, whitespace or a 1px rule.
- Do not make all components rounded: product tiles and ruled fact tables have square edges.
- Do not apply the red benefit-cap treatment indiscriminately to the default green immersive story.
- Do not darken lifestyle photography with a text-legibility overlay when the section is image-only.
- Do not shrink every photograph into a padded card; major visual pauses span the page.
- Do not turn the closing scene into a dense black sitemap or a generic email-signup panel.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The product-led story changes object orientation and moves angled cards around a persistent low focal object as the reader progresses.
- The closing character appears in different walking poses and carries an explicit Space-to-jump control.
- No action-specific transition durations or easing prescriptions are established.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the product as the visual anchor while rotating or replacing the surrounding benefit cards.
- When using a static presentation, preserve the angled compositions and provide a simple working character reaction in the closing scene.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll pinning implementation, animation timing, easing and game behavior beyond the displayed jump invitation.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- The defined composition uses a 1440px desktop canvas with three product columns and paired editorial panels.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, stack paired purchase and editorial content in reading order; preserve the colored tile backgrounds rather than reducing them to small thumbnails.
- Fit word sculptures to their container without losing the distinction between light display type and ordinary reading text; simplify edge motifs before reducing the focal product.
- Keep the closing character, action and legal baseline distinct; provide a pointer-accessible equivalent to the keyboard invitation.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile navigation construction, tested breakpoints and exact type interpolation are unspecified.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
