# Mercury — atmospheric everyday ambition

## Overview

A cinematic, humane treatment of serious software, pairing misty landscapes and luminous abstract objects with practical, restrained interface language. Charcoal framing gives way to pale, faintly lavender editorial chapters before returning to dark proof and closing sections. Rounded contemporary sans typography, small pill actions and spacious product vignettes make complex products feel approachable rather than technical or institutional.

### Essentials

- Ambition is staged as a place, not an oversized slogan: a full-width 900px opening places a 49.3px, weight 480 centered headline above a small working object in an expansive atmospheric landscape.
- Headings feel conversational rather than monumental: display sans at 42px/1.15 for centered promises, 32px/1.15 for ruled chapter leads and 28px/1.2 for feature titles; tracking opens to 0.01–0.02em on smaller display roles.
- Conversion stays compact inside the scenery: 40px-high blue pills with 20px side padding, paired with translucent secondary pills; the email-and-action capsule is 48px high with a 32px radius.
- Color belongs to the atmosphere, not to every section: charcoal #171721 frames the story, pale neutral chapters occupy its middle, and #5266eb is concentrated in actions and tiny emphasis. Artwork carries muted cyan, lavender and dawn warmth across much larger areas.
- Software becomes a tactile object: 496×331px feature images and 496×620px portrait stages isolate pale interface fragments, blurred duplicates, fine orbital lines or soft luminous forms. Every new page includes both an atmospheric opening and a luminous conceptual product vignette; use a real product object in a landscape and simple SVG light geometry when custom film is unavailable.
- Trust is given breathing room: centered 28px quotations and a dark quantified-proof strip with 65.3px numerals, 8px label gaps and 1px vertical dividers interrupt the capability chapters. Carry this device with verified metrics or equally concise proof statements.

**Notes:**

- Use sentence case throughout the main narrative. Display weight 480 is substantial without looking bold; body weight 360 supplies the contrast.
- Do not apply negative tracking as a universal software-site convention: the 42px heading uses 0.01em, the 32px and 28px roles use 0.015em and the 24px role uses 0.02em.
- Centered headings typically occupy 672px; wider specialist openings use 848px. Keep line breaks compact rather than stretching headings across the entire canvas.

## Imagery and graphics

### Landscape workstation

**Use:** Signature atmospheric opening.

**Observed:** A solitary desk, chair and small laptop stand on a grassy ridge above a forested valley. Mist, a winding river and low dawn light produce deep cyan shadows, muted peach sky and a spacious horizon. The scene fills the screen, while the physical object occupies only the lower central portion and leaves sky for copy.

**Transfer:** Photograph or composite a real working object associated with the new product into an expansive, credible landscape. Minimum version: a high-quality landscape with one carefully scaled foreground object and matched lighting, not a generic landscape behind a floating dashboard.

**Build:** Layer a full-bleed landscape with object-fit: cover, a foreground object with a soft contact shadow and matching cool shadows, then a top-to-bottom black veil using rgba(0,0,0,0.6) at 0%, rgba(0,0,0,0.3) at 40% and transparent at 100%. Keep haze behind the object and the clearest light near the horizon.

### Luminous conceptual objects

**Use:** Signature capability and assurance vignettes.

**Observed:** Abstract light paths, orbiting points, overlapping glassy discs, concentric fingerprint-like arcs and glowing bars turn speed, visibility and protection into physical metaphors. Dark graphite stages support soft cyan-white light; forms have blurred halos rather than hard neon outlines. Portrait stages balance short text, while smaller image grids use a landscape frame.

**Transfer:** Include at least one luminous conceptual object alongside concrete product imagery. The minimum is a carefully composed SVG line-and-glow study with a small number of focal points.

**Build:** Within a 496×620px or 496×331px rounded stage, layer three to seven thin SVG ellipses or curved paths in pearl at 15–35% opacity. Duplicate selected paths underneath with Gaussian blur of 8–20px, add two or three 4–8px pearl points, and a broad cyan-white radial glow at 8–15% opacity. Use a charcoal backing and substantial negative space; avoid equal-intensity glow everywhere.

### Tactile interface vignettes

**Use:** Feature image grids and product explanations.

**Observed:** A few legible interface fragments float on warm ivory, pale cyan or lavender backings: cards with out-of-focus duplicates behind them, a bill with lifted metadata pills, a small workflow with curved connectors. Soft shadows, blur and subtle grain make the fragments feel like objects rather than flat full-screen documentation.

**Transfer:** Show one comprehensible task per image. Rebuild the smallest real interface fragment that explains the benefit, leaving most small interface content subordinate.

**Build:** Place one crisp white or graphite interface card at the center of a 496×331px stage; place two duplicates 12–24px above or behind it with lower opacity and 6–12px blur. Add at most three floating labels with diffuse shadows. For portrait specialist stages, add a faint SVG grid over broad lavender and peach radial washes; match the soft glow rather than exact gradient stops.

### Human editorial photography

**Use:** Customer proof and editorial storytelling.

**Observed:** Customer portraits use a broad 1024×512px frame with a person to one side and short overlaid copy on the other. Warm ambient daylight, natural offices and subdued color connect the product to everyday work. Company stories add portrait-format laptop, tea and people photography rather than generic corporate headshots.

**Transfer:** Choose candid, composed working environments with tactile detail and space for copy. Culture-specific still lifes belong only to a company story.

### Orbital systems diagram

**Only for:** company or ecosystem explanations

**Use:** Make a complex product family feel like one system.

**Observed:** Thin overlapping ellipses form a flower-like orbital diagram on a pale lavender portrait field. Small numbered labels occupy the lobes, with a tiny figure caption in a lower corner.

**Transfer:** Use real relationships and short labels for a new ecosystem, maintaining the delicate diagrammatic tone.

**Build:** Draw a fine outer SVG circle and five or six rotated elliptical loops in muted purple at 25–40% opacity. Keep fills transparent, set stroke to 1px, distribute short labels within the petals and place a small figure identifier in the lower right. Back with a low-contrast ivory-to-lavender wash.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small monochrome arrows, chevrons, play/pause glyphs and functional category symbols, generally 14–18px. Circular media controls are 40px; larger illustrative symbols appear in benefit rows.

**Transfer:** Use simple rounded functional glyphs, aligned to the label baseline. Do not turn each feature into a colorful icon badge.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** atmospheric promise → recognizable product → human or quantified proof → capability chapters → decision support → context-appropriate invitation → dark directory and disclosures

### Broad product and brand landing narrative

1. **promise** — Immersive environmental hero with compact centered conversion.
2. **product demonstration** — Large product reveal with narrow explanatory tabs.
3. **social proof** — Customer logo line and wide photographic testimonial.
4. **capability chapters** — Ruled image grids, alternating portrait explanations and a centered quote pause.
5. **quantified proof** — Dark divided stat strip followed by visual assurance cards and editorial destinations.
6. **closing call** — Centered promise, two actions and two audience-choice cards.
7. **ending** — Dark four-column directory followed by separately ruled disclosures.

**Transfer:** Borrow the alternation of cinematic scale, small task-level proof and spacious trust moments; shorten or reorder chapters to fit the product.

### Commercial comparison and price transparency

1. **promise** — Centered pale opening with a short reassurance line.
2. **pricing** — One white shell for three plans, billing control and benefit ribbon.
3. **decision support** — Ruled expandable comparison categories.
4. **social proof** — Centered testimonial without a card.
5. **objection handling** — Narrow FAQ column as final commercial content.
6. **ending** — Dark directory, then long two-column disclosures; no separate closing call.

**Transfer:** Put purchase information first and let structured detail replace the cinematic landing narrative.

### Explain a specialist product and qualify demand

1. **promise** — Pale centered headline, eligibility action and large interface scene.
2. **benefit summary** — Centered two-line promise above three compact icon-led benefits.
3. **capability chapters** — Several alternating portrait stages with concise text and occasional ruled points.
4. **cross-sell** — Three related-product cards under a centered heading.
5. **social proof** — Testimonial with slim carousel progress indicators.
6. **objection handling** — FAQ immediately precedes the dark footer.
7. **ending** — Shared directory and shorter product-specific disclosures.

**Transfer:** Keep this variant practical and pale, using soft lavender media stages rather than the environmental hero.

### Establish purpose, credibility and a human company character

1. **promise** — Centered headline above a wide illustrated brand film.
2. **explanation** — Alternating portrait photography and a delicate systems diagram.
3. **external proof** — Investor logos and two rows of text-led press cards.
4. **human context** — Culture montage and tactile editorial still life.
5. **closing contact** — Centered invitation with two equal contact cards.
6. **ending** — Shared dark directory and compact disclosures.

**Transfer:** Replace conversion-heavy product detail with editorial artifacts that make the organization's character tangible.

## Writing

Confident and helpful, with an occasional dry turn of phrase. Big promises are immediately grounded in concrete tasks, familiar language and practical constraints.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Mercury's name, identity artwork, customer logos, quoted endorsements, financial rates, pricing, eligibility promises or numerical claims. Source examples illustrate voice only; all new claims require product-specific substantiation.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your business. Use we for commitments and a product name when explaining a specific mechanism; avoid institutional third-person distance.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often two linked clauses. One or two explanatory sentences follow. Reversals and paired contrasts supply energy without exclamation marks or hype.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | A concise category promise with one sharp differentiator. | 3–8 words | “Radically different banking”<br>“Pricing that fits your business” |
| Chapter headline | Turn a familiar frustration into forward momentum, often using a two-sentence contrast. | 6–13 words | “Banking’s been a headache. Now, it’s a head start.”<br>“Standard protection stops short. [Brand] goes further.” |
| Supporting line | Name the concrete action or outcome, then explain the friction removed. | 12–30 words | “Apply online in 10 minutes to experience banking unlike anything that’s come before.”<br>“Free checking and savings accounts — no in-person visits or paperwork.” |
| Primary action | Direct verb and object, describing the next step rather than a vague promise. | 2–3 words | “Open account”<br>“Check eligibility” |
| Editorial invitation | A useful invitation softened by a small human detail. | 5–10 words | “Send us questions, comments, or a poem.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** built for; all in one place; get started; in seconds; no minimums; grow; head start; clear view

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; unlock limitless potential; disrupt the industry; best-in-class solutions; synergize; !!!

**Rules:**

- Make a benefit legible before explaining its mechanism.
- Use specific task verbs such as create, send, approve and pay.
- Pair any numerical benefit with its conditions nearby, without forcing legal language into the headline.
- Use occasional wordplay in a headline, then return to plain explanatory prose.
- Prefer direct descriptions to strings of adjectives.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep ordinary primary pills at 40px high with 20px side padding.
- Use 32px gutters across two- and three-column content grids.
- Pair 496×620px visual stages with a narrower 408px text measure.
- Keep narrative headings around weight 480 and paragraphs around weight 360.
- Let dark cards differ from the canvas by a small tonal step, using #1e1e2a over #171721.
- Use 128px chapter pauses and 72px media-introduction gaps to separate different scales of content.
- Give proof numerals their own 65.3px scale and short labels rather than embedding them in paragraphs.
- Finish on a continuous dark directory and disclosure ground.

### Don't

- Do not turn the 49.3px hero headline into 100px heavy grotesk type.
- Do not make every section an accent-blue band; #5266eb belongs chiefly to small actions.
- Do not apply pill radii to editorial panels; their corners are 12px.
- Do not put deep drop shadows beneath every dark card; tonal fills provide the separation.
- Do not add uppercase mono eyebrows above every heading; the main narrative uses sentence-case sans type.
- Do not introduce saturated multicolor feature badges or thick cartoon icons.
- Do not let a three-column card grid spread to the entire 1376px outer width; the narrative width is 1024px.
- Do not represent all product benefits with full application windows; mix small task fragments with luminous conceptual imagery.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions declare color and background transitions of 0.3s cubic-bezier(0,0,.2,1). Theme surfaces declare .5s cubic-bezier(0,0,.6,1).
- Media exposes compact play/pause and audio controls; testimonials use three slim progress bars.
- Some explanatory areas use expanding product rows and visual changes associated with the selected topic.

**Transfer:**

- Preserve the feeling of settled, slowly revealed scenes; action feedback remains a restrained color shift.
- Provide the same compact media controls when using ambient film or animated conceptual art.

**Unknown:**

- Exact film playback, scroll choreography and testimonial timing are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare width breakpoints at 640px, 768px, 1000px, 1024px, 1280px, 1536px and 1952px.
- Layout rules include single-column feature grids becoming two columns, a two-column footer becoming four, and vertical proof-cell rules changing orientation.

**Transfer:**

- Recommended: stack portrait media and text into one reading order while keeping the scene's focal object clear of the hero copy.
- Recommended: wrap the email capsule and secondary action vertically before shrinking their 40px control height; keep fine diagram labels legible by simplifying the drawing.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen hero framing, menu construction and type-size changes are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.

4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
