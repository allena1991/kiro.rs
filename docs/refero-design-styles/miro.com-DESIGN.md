# Miro — ideas in company

## Overview

An optimistic, light workspace aesthetic makes complex software feel social and tangible. Rounded grotesk headlines sit above airy canvas compositions, with charcoal text, electric blue actions, sunny yellow highlights and small pastel collaborator markings. Interface collages, softly elevated panels and candid workplace photography balance expressive invention with practical product detail. It suits collaborative tools and multi-capability product stories without feeling like a dense enterprise dashboard.

### Essentials

- A headline is a human promise, not a typographic stunt: centered Roobert PRO at 56px/1.1, weight 500, normal tracking; section statements use 48px/1.15 with -0.03em tracking.
- The opening behaves like a shared work surface: a 740px text column and 360px signup stack sit amid connected floating documents, with a soft glow beneath the central object and collaborator cursor labels around the scene.
- Conversion controls are small rounded rectangles, not capsules: 8px corners, 40px navigation actions and 48px hero actions; electric blue is for conversion, charcoal for capability exploration.
- Color lives inside the work rather than flooding the page: pale gray canvases cover most of the story, yellow selects a 40px tab or backs a substantial product panel, and pastel chips punctuate otherwise neutral scenes.
- Make collaboration visible with fine curved connectors, diamond endpoints and named cursor pills. Include this shared-canvas device on every landing page; when interface media is unavailable, connect three simplified input, processing and output panels instead.
- Proof has a human face: portrait-format 304px × 482px photo cards carry white 48px semibold outcomes near the top, with a small handwritten nudge nearby; supporting scale figures use 48px semibold italic.
- End with useful next steps and a substantial charcoal directory: white resource cards with pastel category pills and circular arrow links lead into six compact footer columns, then trust marks and a separated utility row.

## Typography

- **Roobert PRO** — Headlines, navigation, card titles and numerical proof. Rounded geometric grotesk with open counters and a friendly, almost monoline silhouette. Medium cuts remain light rather than aggressively bold. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use 500 for Medium and 600 for SemiBold; retain the tight section-heading tracking. Font stack: "Roobert PRO", "DM Sans", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Noto Sans** — Paragraphs, actions, category labels and footer links. Neutral, readable sans with a less geometric texture than the display face. Font stack: "Noto Sans", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Nanum Pen Script** — Small informal annotations beside proof and interactive media. Loose pen lettering, paired with a tiny hand-drawn arrow rather than used as a headline. Font stack: "Nanum Pen Script", cursive.

**Notes:**

- The display face stays at Medium for nearly all headlines; only outcome figures introduce SemiBold. Medium cuts visually render at weight 500 even when their family-specific declaration uses weight 400.
- Tracking changes by role: the default opening headline is untracked, section headings tighten to -0.03em, and product gallery titles tighten further to -0.05em.
- Keep text hierarchies independent of the tiny interface lettering embedded in product imagery. Handwritten text is a single small aside, not a competing type system.

## Imagery and graphics

### Connected canvas collage

**Use:** Signature opening illustration and capability stories.

**Observed:** Floating white documents and tables surround a central prompt-like panel. Thin violet curves join diamond endpoints, side objects partially leave the viewport, and tiny pastel avatar labels make the scene feel occupied. A very pale dotted grid and soft yellow-pink light sit behind the central object; the art remains lighter than the headline.

**Transfer:** Recompose the product's own inputs and outcomes as a legible connected scene. Minimum version: three clean white panels with simple rows and diagrams, two curved connections and two collaborator markers.

**Build:** Use an SVG layer across a full-width stage. Draw two or three 2px cubic Bezier connectors in a muted violet such as rgba(100,80,210,0.85), with 8px diamond endpoints. Place white input panels to either side and a larger white output panel low in the center, with 12px–20px corners and soft gray shadows. Add a 20px-spaced radial dot pattern using rgba(28,28,30,0.035). Beneath the central panel place a 476px × 327px layer with overlapping yellow and pink radial circles fading to transparent at 60%, blur 60px and opacity 0.24. Match the soft glow; exact stops are not essential.

### Collaborator markings

**Use:** Signature social punctuation around product scenes.

**Observed:** Small peach, mint or lavender name capsules sit next to triangular cursors, sometimes with circular face thumbnails. Thin matching outlines and slight offsets make them read as live presence rather than testimonials.

**Transfer:** Use short fictional participant names or meaningful team roles. Keep the labels sparse and away from primary copy.

**Build:** Draw a small triangular SVG cursor with a 1px colored outline and a pale matching fill. Offset a rounded name pill by 8px from its tip; use 4px 8px pill padding, 12px–16px sans lettering, and a 24px circular portrait when available. Distribute two or three around the edges of a scene; never cover conversion controls.

### Human proof photography

**Use:** Portrait outcome cards.

**Observed:** Natural indoor light, real work settings and close product details: people reviewing physical material, clothing on rails, and someone working at a screen. Muted photographic colors support large white metrics and bottom-aligned identities.

**Transfer:** Commission or select documentary-feeling work scenes relevant to the new product. Use a tight vertical crop and enough dark tonal area for the white outcome.

### Product demonstrations

**Use:** Explain capabilities with actual interface substance.

**Observed:** White canvas interfaces show documents, sticky-note clusters, diagrams and contextual tools, often on pale gray backing. Centered hero media is 720px × 405px; feature scenes are framed within larger white or yellow panels. The interface is simplified enough to read as a workflow at a glance.

**Transfer:** Use real interface media when available; otherwise draw representative panes and one meaningful transformation, not decorative empty windows.

### Slide-like product chapters

**Use:** Expressive long-form platform explanation.

**Observed:** A large white presentation-like card extends beyond the right edge beside a stacked left headline. A tiny playback toolbar, collaborator marker and handwritten pointer reinforce the canvas metaphor. Inside, bold italic emphasis, avatar clusters and colored diagram lines communicate relationships.

**Transfer:** Use this device for a large product concept rather than another conventional card grid. Minimum version: one readable relationship diagram in a white slide card with a toolbar-like cap.

**Build:** Place a white rounded card in a horizontal overflow track with the next card partly visible. Use an inner light-gray slide field, a large centered statement, three outlined relationship boxes and one strong italic emphasis. Add a small white toolbar above the card and one collaborator capsule on its upper edge; preserve clear reading order.

### Integration constellation

**Only for:** ecosystem or platform stories

**Use:** Demonstrate compatibility without a dense logo wall.

**Observed:** A broad white rounded panel holds a centered 32px statement and dark action; soft square logo tiles orbit the margins, fading toward the side edges.

**Transfer:** Use actual partner or format marks when appropriate; otherwise show labeled format or workflow tiles with the same light, low-contrast perimeter treatment.

**Build:** Reserve the central third for copy. Place two uneven rows of white rounded square tiles around it with shallow shadows, then apply white-to-transparent edge masks so the outer tiles soften. Keep colored marks small within the squares.

### Cool closing light

**Only for:** technical product closing calls

**Use:** Soft final conversion section before the dark footer.

**Observed:** A pale blue-lavender glow washes the top behind a centered closing question and fades into near-white below; there is no enclosing card.

**Transfer:** Carry the product benefit into a short question and a single action, leaving ample open space below.

**Build:** Position an oversized elliptical radial gradient above the section: rgba(122,144,254,0.25) at the center, transparent by about 70%. Stretch it beyond both side edges and fade it over a #fafafc field; keep the text charcoal and the action blue.

### Icons

**Observed:** Navigation chevrons and inline utility marks are generally 16px with 2px round strokes. Circular arrow links on resources are charcoal disks of 32px–40px, with white northeast arrows. FAQ plus signs are small relative to the 24px question text.

**Transfer:** Choose simple rounded-stroke utility icons; reserve colorful filled marks for product objects or integrations. Keep arrows directional and subordinate to the card title.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Connected canvas opening

**Observed:** A 72px white Navigation sits over an 856px pale Opening stage with 40px lower corners. Back-to-front layers are the faint dotted working field, soft central glow, curved violet connectors, white floating input/output panels, then collaborator markings and the central copy/form stack. The 56px display headline spans roughly half the viewport in two centered lines within a 740px column, starting after 64px of top breathing room. A two-line 20px support statement sits 24px below. The 360px Signup stack anchors the middle, followed by a central prompt panel and tilted tool tiles; input documents flank it and an output diagram is clipped at the right edge. In the first 900px, headline, support, email field, blue action, the whole main composition and the start of a muted customer-logo row are visible; the capability selector falls below.

**Transfer:** Give the new product an equally legible relationship between inputs and outcomes. Without finished product media, construct the three-panel Connected canvas collage with collaborator markings and the soft glow, keeping the headline and signup strongest. Focused product pages use product-display, a compact 16px-spaced text stack, one or two 48px actions and a centered 720px × 405px demonstration 80px below; they retain the presence labels while simplifying the surrounding collage.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a giant centered dashboard underneath a billboard headline, or push the meaningful product object entirely below the fold.

**Text style:** `display` — Roobert PRO 500 · 56px/1.1

**Observed:** Centered 48px heading and 22px slate lead are separated by 24px and held to a roughly 792px readable measure. Product pages often use a 16px gap. Left-aligned resource leads use the same heading scale.

**Transfer:** Make each pair explain one narrative claim; align it with the media or rail below instead of introducing a separate eyebrow at every section.

**Text style:** `heading` — Roobert PRO 500 · 48px/1.15 · tracking -0.03em

**Observed:** Section heading pair leads into Capability tiles on a 12-column grid with 16px gutters. A wide half-width card sits beside two quarter-width cards, followed by a reversed rhythm. Titles and support sit above cropped pale interface illustrations; larger rows can use two half-width cards.

**Transfer:** Vary spans according to explanatory importance and illustration shape. Keep tile materials uniform while the content changes.

**Text style:** `gallery-heading` — Roobert PRO 500 · 24px/1.15 · tracking -0.05em

### Alternating feature rows

**Only for:** technical product feature explanations

**Observed:** 611px × 344px demonstrations alternate sides with short 32px benefit headings and slate descriptions. Rows use a 48px horizontal gap and 64px separation; no outer card is needed around the whole row.

**Transfer:** Use clear single benefits, each backed by one observable interaction.

**Text style:** `product-subheading` — Roobert PRO 500 · 32px/1.15 · tracking -0.03em

### Accordion with yellow demonstration

**Only for:** interactive product benefit overviews

**Observed:** A centered heading introduces a 1270px-wide white outlined panel with 14px corners and 32px inset. Three benefit toggles occupy one half; the expanded item reveals a short explanation. The other half holds a yellow-backed square product scene that clips at its outer edge.

**Transfer:** Use when several related benefits share one demonstration. Change the media with the selected benefit rather than displaying unrelated decorative art.

### FAQ ending variant

**Only for:** product pages with practical adoption objections

**Observed:** Centered 48px section heading, then stacked FAQ rows spanning the product container. Questions remain single-minded and the closed state is very light. This can be the final content block before breadcrumbs and the charcoal footer, without a separate closing call.

**Transfer:** Answer the few objections that block trying the product; do not manufacture an extra sales section afterward.

**Text style:** `faq-question` — Roobert PRO 500 · 24px/1.429

### Cool closing call

**Only for:** technical product landing pages

**Observed:** A centered 40px closing question sits above a short 18px line and one 48px blue action, with 64px space before the control. Cool closing light fades downward before the charcoal breadcrumb strip and directory.

**Transfer:** Restate the shared outcome, not the product name, as an invitation.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise and shared-canvas demonstration → concrete capabilities → human or functional proof → deeper product chapters → helpful next steps or objections → substantial directory and trust ending.

### Expressive broad-platform marketing

1. **Promise** — Centered headline and signup inside a connected canvas collage with collaborators and muted trust logos.
2. **Capability orientation** — Tabbed user-job stories pair benefit copy with small workflow scenes.
3. **Quantified social proof** — Three photographic outcome cards followed by italic scale figures.
4. **Product model** — Large slide-like visual chapter conveys the broader system.
5. **Helpful next steps** — Illustrated resource rail on a cooler pale field.
6. **Utility ending** — Dark six-column directory, trust strip and utilities.

**Transfer:** Use the fullest collage-led expression for a product with several connected jobs; keep proof and practical resources as distinct modes.

### Platform category explanation with a feature ecosystem

1. **Promise** — Centered title, two actions and elevated product video framed in lavender.
2. **Product demonstration** — Large yellow-backed canvas scene and three explanatory columns.
3. **Applied examples** — Horizontal image-led playbook rail.
4. **Capability chapters** — Large split panel followed by a two-column grid of image-led feature panels.
5. **Compatibility** — White integration constellation surrounding a centered statement.
6. **Adoption support** — Text-led resource cards answer readiness concerns.
7. **Utility ending** — Breadcrumb transition into the charcoal directory and trust area.

**Transfer:** Use the calmer centered-media opening for an abstract category; explain the category through a large concrete workflow before listing capabilities.

### Technical feature landing page

1. **Promise** — Centered product title, short explanation, single trial action and product media.
2. **Social proof** — A developer-feedback introduction precedes the benefit story.
3. **Capability demonstration** — Alternating text/video rows make separate benefits observable.

## Writing

Warm, direct and optimistic about getting useful work done together. Claims connect a concrete mechanism to a team-level outcome rather than relying on abstract prestige.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Miro names, proprietary product labels, customer logos, quotations, audience counts or performance claims. Replace all examples, proof and participant identities with material appropriate to the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to 'you' and 'your team'; use the product name sparingly as the enabler. Shared action and inclusive 'together' language are more characteristic than a corporate 'we'.
- **Rhythm:** Brief benefit headlines alternate with one- or two-sentence explanations. Use parallel verbs and occasional short sentence fragments; longer section statements can wrap over two balanced lines. Questions are practical or invitational, not theatrical.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening headline | Connect a human activity to a sharper outcome or new capability. | 4–10 words | “Human collaboration at the speed of AI”<br>“The AI platform for teamwork” |
| Supporting line | Name the workspace or mechanism, then describe what the team does with it. | 15–35 words | “The Intelligent Canvas where your team and agents think, plan, and build together” |
| Capability heading | Verb-led movement from an input to a useful shared result. | 3–8 words | “Turn research into shared direction”<br>“Capture and connect insights” |
| Product explanation | Short, specific actions with the result built in; parallel fragments can replace a long sentence. | 15–35 words | “No auto-layout collisions. No manual node dragging. Mermaid syntax renders correctly on the first pass.” |
| Action | Plain verb plus destination or low-friction start. | 2–4 words | “Get started free”<br>“Book a demo” |
| Resource lead | Friendly question that offers practical help rather than pressure. | 4–7 words | “Need help getting started?” |
| Annotation | Small conversational aside that nudges attention toward a visual. | 2–4 words | “Check this out” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** together; your team; turn … into …; build; think; plan; shared; context; get started; next steps

**Avoid:** synergize; best-in-class; revolutionary; unprecedented; unlock limitless potential; corporate transformation jargon without a mechanism

**Rules:**

- Describe what changes in the reader's work before listing implementation details.
- Pair ambitious statements with a visible workflow, supported outcome or practical example.
- Keep action labels functional and sentence case; title case belongs only to the wide signup variant.
- Use technical vocabulary when it reduces ambiguity, and immediately connect it to a task.
- Let the occasional handwritten aside supply playfulness; keep main copy composed.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the opening promise at 56px with a restrained Medium weight and a two-line measure around 740px.
- Use 8px corners on functional actions and inputs, reserving capsule shapes for labels and tabs.
- Hold the signup field and action to the same 360px width with a 16px gap.
- Make at least one connected scene visibly social with named cursors, fine connectors and a soft central glow.
- Use 24px gutters between the three portrait proof cards and keep metrics at 48px.
- Keep yellow concentrated in selected tabs and meaningful media mats, surrounded by pale neutral fields.
- Use 20px white resource cards and small pastel category chips for practical next steps.
- Finish with the dark six-column directory and a separate trust/utility zone.

### Don't

- Do not turn all controls into pills; conversion actions need the 8px rectangular silhouette.
- Do not set headline paragraphs in heavy 700-weight type; Medium carries the primary hierarchy.
- Do not use blue as the default background for full narrative sections; its main role is conversion.
- Do not replace white-on-pale resource cards with hard charcoal outlines or deep floating shadows.
- Do not expand the handwritten annotation into a repeated headline or paragraph style.
- Do not make collaborator labels as large as headlines or arrange them like testimonial quote cards.
- Do not give every capability tile the same footprint in an asymmetric product gallery.
- Do not make the footer another spacious pale marketing block; preserve the charcoal field and compact 14px directory links.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Primary actions declare all-property transitions of 0.15s cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1). Navigation items declare the same duration and easing for color-related properties.
- Three floating scene animations are declared at 5s ease-in-out, infinite alternate, for top-left, bottom-left and bottom-right elements.
- Additional surface transitions include 0.2s and 0.4s cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1); the large canvas chapter uses a scroll-led presentation treatment.

**Transfer:**

- Use subtle alternating drift for peripheral collaborator or canvas fragments, keeping the headline and signup stack stationary.
- Keep hover changes compact and material-based; do not animate the whole page into a different color theme.

**Unknown:**

- Exact float distances and scroll choreography are not specified.
- Expanded accordion timing and all reduced-motion behavior are unknown.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop compositions use a centered 1366px product container with 48px side padding and large multi-column galleries.
- Declared responsive families include 576px, 768px, 992px, 1200px and 1400px; some implementations add further intermediate widths.

**Transfer:**

- On narrower screens, preserve the headline → support → action → meaningful product object order and move peripheral collage fragments away from the signup stack.
- Collapse the 12-column gallery to readable stacked cards while retaining a distinction between primary and secondary capabilities.
- Let resource rails expose one readable card and a preview of the next rather than squeezing four tiny cards into view.
- Convert the dense footer directory into grouped narrow-screen navigation without losing the trust and utility hierarchy.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile type sizes, padding values and breakpoint-specific navigation behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
