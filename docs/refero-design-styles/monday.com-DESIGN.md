# monday.com — playful work in motion

## Overview

An optimistic software style that pairs rounded geometric sans typography with colorful, humanized interface scenes. Mostly white and pale gray, it opens into bursts of violet, pink and bright product color, balancing approachable playfulness with enterprise credibility. Airy compositions and oversized verbal statements make a complex platform feel easy to enter.

### Essentials

- A modest promise opens into monumental chapter statements: Poppins 56px/1.2 at weight 400 leads the opening, while 128px weight 500 chapter type uses -0.04em tracking and deliberately compact lines.
- The opening behaves like a working scene, not a poster: a 580px text column faces a 673×397px interface composition, with 32px-high choice chips and a compact action beneath the promise.
- Actions are rounded capsules with directional momentum: 160px radius, 14px 32px padding and 50px height; violet leads the opening, black leads capability sections, and a 12×10px arrow follows the label.
- Color lives in demonstrations and proof, not all over the canvas: white and #f3f4f5 carry most sections; #6161ff marks conversion and selection, while a #f4cdff proof card interrupts the neutral rhythm.
- Interfaces become approachable little performances: float crisp white panels, circular human portraits and colorful character tiles above subdued tables; use a simplified real workflow with a result bubble when rich character art is unavailable.
- Make at least one expansive typographic moment and a lively closing ribbon: 128px text, inline portrait or object inserts and clipped horizontal continuation create scale without requiring a giant logo.

**Notes:**

- Use regular weight for most promises; scale and tight tracking carry authority rather than heavy bold text.
- The 128px chapter family has purposeful line-height variants. Use 1 as the default, 0.8 only for very short stacked words, 0.95 for centered statements and 1.3 for the horizontal ribbon.
- Keep dense interface text inside imagery visually subordinate to marketing copy; do not promote its tiny labels into the page hierarchy.

### Surfaces

#### Canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default marketing ground.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #000000`

**Observed:** Broad untextured white spaces separate colorful demonstrations.

**Transfer:** Let content and objects create the drama rather than texturing every section.

#### Neutral stage · level 0

**Use:** Capability bands and broad product cards.

**CSS:** `background: #f3f4f5; border-radius: 24px`

**Observed:** Rounded inset stages coexist with full-width square-ended bands.

**Transfer:** Use the inset version for one demonstration and the band for grouped capabilities.

#### Trust outline · level 1

**Use:** Formal reassurance and enterprise proof.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border: 1px solid #cacbcd; border-radius: 24px`

**Observed:** Flat white interiors with restrained edges, not elevated dashboard widgets.

**Transfer:** Group evidence with a visible boundary while preserving the light canvas.

#### Floating context tile · level 2

**Use:** Small floating pieces surrounding a central product or character scene.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(26, 36, 46, 0.06); border-radius: 10px; backdrop-filter: blur(5px)`

**Observed:** A very pale gray visible wash supports miniature files, records and charts.

**Transfer:** Use real excerpts of the new product as a sparse constellation.

#### Pastel feature stage · level 0

**Only for:** single-feature landing pages

**Use:** Narrative demonstration cards.

**CSS:** `background: #f7f3ea; border-radius: 24px`

**Observed:** Warm stone, ice blue and sky blue distinguish adjacent chapters without changing construction.

**Transfer:** Choose one pastel per chapter; keep the interface panels white.

#### Blue mint media stage · level 0

**Only for:** single-feature landing pages

**Use:** Square media backing for a process demonstration.

**CSS:** `background: linear-gradient(135deg, #cee5fe, #e8fff4); border-radius: 24px`

**Observed:** Soft diagonal color transition behind overlapping white calendar panels.

**Transfer:** Use a simple two-stop gradient and a clear interface focal point.

#### Inverse chapter · level 0

**Only for:** platform-capability and product-suite landing pages

**Use:** A high-contrast product chapter.

**CSS:** `background: #000000; color: #ffffff`

**Observed:** Centered full-width treatment for platform demonstrations; an inset 32px-radius split panel for suite cross-sell.

**Transfer:** Reserve for a major change in capability, not every other section.

## Components

### Navigation

**Use:** Persistent global orientation.

**CSS:** `height: 72px; padding: 16px 40px; background: #ffffff; position: sticky; top: 0px; display: flex; align-items: center; justify-content: space-between`

**Text style:** `navigation` — Poppins 300 · 14px/1

**Observed:** Brand at left, compact menu group next, utilities and outlined sales action at right followed by violet primary action. Dropdowns use tiny downward chevrons. Suite navigation substitutes product identity and black actions.

**Transfer:** Keep the marketing header visually lighter than the product scene.

### Primary button

**Use:** Opening conversion action; violet is the default lead action.

**CSS:** `background-color: #6161ff; color: #ffffff; border: 1px solid transparent; border-radius: 160px; padding: 14px 32px; height: 50px; display: flex; align-items: center; gap: 8px`

**Text style:** `action` — Poppins 400 · 16px/1.25

**Observed:** Label precedes a small right arrow. A warm radial sweep passes across the opening action; it is usually seen as solid violet. Capability sections use a black fill with a black outline. Closing actions return to violet; inverse chapters use white.

**States:** Declared: hover opacity 0.9; focus-visible unspecified; active outline 0px.

**Transfer:** Keep labels short and preserve the capsule rather than stretching it into a full-width bar.

### Secondary button

**Use:** Sales route beside or opposite a primary action.

**CSS:** `color: #000000; background: transparent; border: 1px solid #000000; border-radius: 160px; padding: 14px 32px; height: 50px; display: flex; align-items: center; gap: 8px`

**Text style:** `action` — Poppins 400 · 16px/1.25

**Observed:** A wider companion capsule with a right arrow. Header version is smaller and violet-outlined in the global navigation.

**States:** Declared: hover background-color rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.05); focus-visible unspecified; active outline 0px.

### Choice chip

**Use:** Compact audience or task selection below opening support.

**CSS:** `background: #f3f4f5; color: #323338; border: 1px solid #f3f4f5; border-radius: 32px; height: 32px; padding: 8px 12px; display: flex; align-items: center`

**Text style:** `small-label` — Poppins 400 · 14px/1.43

**Observed:** Wraps into short rows. Selection uses a lilac fill, violet label and a small checked circle. The later capability selector is a single white capsule rail with a soft shadow.

**States:** Declared: hover background-color #e6e7ea and opacity 1; focus-visible unspecified.

### Capability card

**Use:** Broad split demonstration paired with explanatory copy.

**CSS:** `background: #f3f4f5; border-radius: 24px; padding: 32px 32px 32px 48px; display: flex`

**Observed:** Small icon or integration cluster sits above a short title and paragraph in the left half; a large crisp interface panel fills the right. Several cards overlap as sticky layers.

**Transfer:** Show one workflow per card rather than filling the panel with unrelated features.

### Proof card

**Use:** Results in a horizontally clipped customer-story carousel.

**CSS:** `background: #f4cdff; border-radius: 24px; padding: 12px; display: flex; max-width: 736px`

**Text style:** `result` — Poppins 400 · 64px/1.2 · tracking -0.03em

**Observed:** Text and photograph share a wide card. A small category tag occupies the top, result and explanation the lower left, with an underlined story link. The paired black version swaps the metric for a quote and pink highlighted words; photographs have rounded corners.

**Transfer:** Use a substantiated result and authentic context photography; keep both within one card.

### Trust card

**Use:** Security, external recognition or business validation.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border: 1px solid #cacbcd; border-radius: 24px; padding: 48px 32px; display: grid; gap: 24px`

**Text style:** `trust-card-heading` — Poppins 500 · 32px/1.3 · tracking -0.03em

**Observed:** Wide left card uses left-aligned prose and an insignia row along its foot. Narrow cards center a large result, explanation, link and authority mark.

**Transfer:** Use only proof relevant to the new product; retain the unequal-width grid.

### Footer directory

**Use:** Quiet, comprehensive end navigation.

**CSS:** `display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr 1.25fr 1fr 1fr 0.75fr; column-gap: 16px; row-gap: 36px; padding-bottom: 48px; background: #ffffff`

**Observed:** Six columns: identity and general links, audience links, capabilities, related products, company and resources. Small gray links use a relaxed vertical rhythm. A hairline separates language, social icons, trust marks, legal links and store badges below. An event promotion is a scoped campaign insert rather than a universal footer requirement.

**Transfer:** Retain the generous directory treatment but reduce column count to fit the actual information architecture.

## Imagery and graphics

### Humanized product scene

**Use:** Opening scene and key capability demonstrations.

**Observed:** Pale full interface tables recede behind sharply focused floating status bubbles, rounded character portraits and real circular headshots. Vivid blue, magenta and yellow character clothing punctuates an otherwise white scene; the opening object takes roughly half the hero width.

**Transfer:** Use a real interface fragment with one enlarged result and one human presence. When custom 3D art is unavailable, use portrait discs and simple colored avatar tiles rather than generic stock device mockups.

**Build:** Layer a subdued white interface at the back, soften its outer edges with white-to-transparent masks, then place one white rounded status bubble with a thin violet-to-pink edge in front. Add 2–3 portrait discs or colored square avatars at different depths with restrained soft shadows; leave broad white space around the group.

### Floating context constellation

**Use:** Expansive visual chapter explaining connected information.

**Observed:** Small semi-transparent gray panels containing files, integrations, charts, documents and records orbit a central outlined-to-color character. The objects are spaced widely rather than packed into a dashboard.

**Transfer:** Surround one central product object with a sparse collection of recognizable input and output fragments.

**Build:** Use a relative full-width stage with five or six absolutely positioned 170–260px panels, 10px corners and rgba(26,36,46,0.06) backing with 3–5px blur. Stagger heights and overlap sparingly; put a simple line illustration or circular portrait at the center as the minimum version.

### Inline portrait language

**Use:** Oversized support lines and horizontal closing ribbon.

**Observed:** Tiny rows of circular people and rounded character tiles replace part of the normal text spacing. The images sit on the line rather than in a separate testimonial block.

**Transfer:** Embed small teams, product objects or output examples in a short verbal statement.

**Build:** Create overlapping portrait discs in an inline-flex span aligned to the text baseline, with small white rims. In the closing ribbon use 224px-wide image clusters between 128px text phrases, 32px gaps and a clipped horizontal flex track.

### Customer context photography

**Use:** Proof carousels, enterprise reassurance and editorial resources.

**Observed:** Real people working with colleagues, retail locations and business environments appear in natural light and full color. Photos fill the right half of wide proof cards or the top of smaller testimonial cards; some enterprise photos use a repeating scalloped mask.

**Transfer:** Use believable work context and varied people rather than isolated cutout portraits; keep photography confined to proof and supporting stories.

### Pastel interface collage

**Only for:** single-feature landing pages

**Use:** Process explanations and benefit chapters.

**Observed:** Overlapping white calendar panels, transcript windows and video-call grids are staged on soft blue, mint or warm stone backings. Small chat cards break the edges of the main call window.

**Transfer:** Compose two or three interface fragments that show cause and effect, with the result enlarged.

**Build:** Use a 24px-radius pastel or blue-to-mint gradient stage. Center one white application panel, overlap a second smaller panel toward a corner, and float a compact message or result card above the foreground edge. Preserve readable hierarchy without reproducing a whole application.

### Closing activity field

**Only for:** single-feature and product-suite landing pages

**Use:** Illustrated conversion ending.

**Observed:** Single-feature endings surround a clear centered call with horizontal rows of portraits, pastel labels and meeting icons. Suite endings instead use very pale workflow nodes and a faint square grid around the central statement.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's people, outputs or task names in the surrounding field, leaving the headline and button unobstructed.

**Build:** For portrait rows, repeat circles and rounded rectangular labels along four clipped horizontal tracks while masking a clear central zone. For a workflow field, draw a faint SVG orthogonal grid, place small white node cards at outer edges, connect them with thin lines and fade everything toward the center using white gradient masks.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small solid right arrows are 12×10px with an 8px label gap; navigation chevrons are 10×7px. Capability icons are simple 24px pictograms, often violet. FAQ chevrons use 32px boxes with 2px round strokes.

**Transfer:** Keep arrows compact and horizontal. Use simple outline symbols for explanatory capabilities; reserve colorful app marks and avatar imagery for demonstration and integration contexts.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Split working-scene opening

**Observed:** A white 72px Navigation sits above an airy split stage. The left promise uses hero type in a 580px column, usually two lines; a narrower support paragraph follows after 20px. Choice chip rows follow with 6px gaps, then a violet Primary button after 24px and reassurance after 16px. The right half holds a 673×397px Humanized product scene: subdued interface at back, avatars and a sharp status bubble in front. The hero itself is 592px tall below navigation. Within the first 900px the complete promise, support, selector, action, main object and a centered trust line with logo strip are visible; capability chapters begin below.

**Transfer:** Use a product promise and real interface activity in the same balanced split. With no rich art, build a simplified table or output panel plus an enlarged result and portrait disc. Single-feature pages use feature-hero type and a pale blue-backed call collage with three small outlined benefit cards at its foot; platform pages center platform-hero type above a 1280px-wide demo; suite pages center suite-hero type above selectable audience tiles and a logo rail; pricing starts with pricing-hero and comparison controls.

**Avoid:** Do not substitute a full-bleed photo hero, a giant centered black display statement or a dark canvas for this light split first impression.

**Text style:** `hero` — Poppins 400 · 56px/1.2 · tracking -0.04em

### Capability introduction

**Observed:** Centered section-heading over a white capsule selector, followed by a narrow left explanation and a larger right interface window. A short completion word can turn violet. Major heading blocks start 64px into the section.

**Transfer:** Let a short choice set switch between concrete workflows rather than presenting a generic wall of feature cards.

**Text style:** `section-heading` — Poppins 400 · 56px/1.2 · tracking -0.03em

### Monumental split chapter

**Observed:** Monument or context-heading occupies the left half; a compact explanatory paragraph, small icon cluster and black Primary button occupy the upper right. Neutral-stage cards or a Floating context constellation carry the chapter below.

**Transfer:** Use a short benefit statement large enough to act as an object; let demonstrations, not a longer headline, explain the mechanism.

**Text style:** `monument` — Poppins 500 · 128px/1 · tracking -0.04em

### Open control grid

**Observed:** A pale gray band begins with compact-monument type. Six small icon-led explanations form three columns and two rows, without individual card borders. Labels are bold relative to 16px body paragraphs.

**Transfer:** Present cross-cutting assurance or control principles as an open grid rather than six heavy tiles.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** promise → visible product activity → capability chapters → human and quantified proof → reassurance or objection handling → confident ending → useful directory

### Broad platform marketing

2. **social proof** — Centered trust sentence with monochrome customer-logo rail.

## Writing

Optimistic, direct and work-focused. Big promises sound approachable because they are immediately paired with concrete actions, outputs and reassurance.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse brand names, logos, character artwork, customer identities, proprietary product names, numerical claims, certifications or quoted endorsements. Replace all with truthful assets and claims belonging to the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to you and your team. Describe what the product does in plain active language; use we mainly inside attributed customer stories.
- **Rhythm:** Short benefit headings alternate with one or two explanatory sentences. Monumental phrases often use only 2–4 words; support explains the mechanism without piling on adjectives.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | People-centered outcome, not a technical specification. | 6–12 words | “People and agents working as one team”<br>“The intelligence core of your business” |
| Chapter statement | A compact, memorable benefit that can carry monumental type. | 2–5 words | “Work in context”<br>“Consider yourself limitless”<br>“Let work flow” |
| Supporting line | Name the inputs, action and practical result. | 12–30 words | “Turn work data into real-time clarity and shorten the path from signal to action.” |
| Feature headline | Use a verb-led promise with an everyday work consequence. | 5–11 words | “Close more deals by cutting the manual work”<br>“Decisions land straight in the workflow.” |
| Action and reassurance | A short next step followed by a friction-removing fact. | 2–10 words | “Get Started”<br>“Free to start. No credit card needed.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your team; progress; in context; real-time clarity; flow; together; on its own; from signal to action

**Avoid:** revolutionary paradigm; unparalleled synergy; disrupt everything; effortless magic without explanation; formal procurement jargon in the hero

**Rules:**

- Pair the aspirational headline with a concrete product action nearby.
- Explain automation as a sequence of familiar tasks and results.
- Keep reassurance factual and small; do not let disclaimers compete with the promise.
- Use ordinary sentence case, even for monumental type; uppercase belongs to scoped feature eyebrows.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening at 56px, weight 400 and -0.04em tracking.
- Use 128px medium chapter statements to change pace after the opening.
- Give broad feature and proof cards 24px corners.
- Keep desktop section tops near 64px and major bottoms near 96px.
- Use white and #f3f4f5 as the dominant spatial rhythm.
- Pair a pastel result card with a black quote card for colorful proof.
- Make interface result bubbles sharper and more prominent than their supporting tables.
- Retain the clipped text-and-image ribbon as a signature scale moment.

### Don't

- Do not make every heading bold; the default hierarchy depends on regular and medium weights.
- Do not replace 160px-radius actions with small rounded rectangles.
- Do not spread violet across all large background bands; it is primarily an action and selection color.
- Do not turn the open capability grid into a set of heavily shadowed boxes.
- Do not put an entire dashboard at full contrast behind the opening text.
- Do not use dark glassmorphism as the default page material.
- Do not apply 128px chapter type to pricing columns or ordinary FAQ headings.
- Do not remove the quiet multi-column directory in favor of only a final CTA. 

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The opening action declares a 6s ease-in-out radial sweep with a 2s delay; its base transition is 0.1s.
- Closing horizontal text tracks declare 20s linear infinite motion; other logo and integration marquees declare 10s or 40s linear loops.
- Sticky stacked cards and pinned visual chapters create progression; FAQ chevrons declare transform 0.3s.

**Transfer:**

- Use the closing ribbon as a continuous horizontal composition; keep its message understandable at any loop position.
- Animate demonstration depth and chapter progression rather than introducing unrelated decorative motion.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll-linked timelines and complete animation trajectories are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop layouts use wide 1376px and 1280px containers. Declared breakpoint families include 479px, 767–768px, 991–992px and 1280px.
- Only the two explicitly fluid text recipes have established viewport formulas.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve promise → action → product-scene reading order in a single column; let choice chips wrap.
- Reduce monumental type enough to preserve short expressive line breaks while keeping it distinct from ordinary headings.
- Keep horizontal proof cards and ribbons clipped within their own tracks rather than allowing page-level overflow.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile type sizes, navigation collapse, sticky-card behavior and exact breakpoint-specific spacing are unknown; narrow-layout guidance is a recommendation.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
