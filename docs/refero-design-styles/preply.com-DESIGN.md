# Preply — pink powered people

## Overview

An upbeat, people-first style that combines bold grotesk headlines with approachable sans-serif explanations. White space and near-black type dominate, punctuated by bubblegum pink, mint and small primary-color markers. Natural photography, echoed image layers and neatly staged interface fragments make digital products feel personal and tangible; it suits outcome-led services and platforms with a moderate, easy-to-scan density.

### Essentials

- Headlines feel like emphatic spoken promises: Platform 700 at 64px/68px with -0.005em tracking, growing to 96px/1 for major proof and reassurance statements.
- The default opening is a pink split stage, not a centered software billboard: a roughly 391px text column faces a 446×326px photograph with smaller offset echoes; one dark 320×56px action sits 48px below the headline.
- Actions are sturdy little rectangles: 8px corners, 2px borders and 56px height; dark on pink, pink with an ink outline on white. Labels use Figtree 18px/24px at 600.
- Color arrives in complete, flat moments: pink fills the opening and a monumental reassurance band, while the longer body stays white. Mint belongs to a distinct audience-conversion panel; yellow and blue mostly remain compact markers.
- People appear in layered moments of activity rather than generic cutouts: natural interiors, small call insets and two receding repetitions of the same frame. Include this echo device in a new page; a single authentic product-in-use photograph repeated at diminishing sizes is the minimum equivalent.
- Proof has two speeds: compact 32px/36px numbers in an unboxed horizontal strip, and 96px/96px figures or promises for slower emphasis. Pair these with a small explanatory line, never an ornamental chart.
- The page resolves into a substantial near-black directory: four columns, 20px/24px bold headings and underlined 14px links on a 24px rhythm. A small pink square up-arrow overlaps its upper right edge.

## Typography

- **Platform** — All headline tiers, prominent figures, card titles and footer group headings. Heavy, compact grotesk with friendly rounded shapes and forceful short lines. - **Figtree** — Body copy, metadata, navigation and action labels. Open, friendly sans-serif with a softer everyday voice than the display family. Font stack: "Figtree", Arial, sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- Large display tracking is slightly negative, but 32px and smaller Platform titles use positive tracking. Do not apply one tight tracking value to every tier.
- The 96px tier serves statements and figures, not every section. Use 64px for major headings, 48px for benefit chapters and 32px for compact titles.
- Paragraphs remain regular weight; weight 600 identifies actions and emphasized labels. Headlines are sentence case, even when highly emphatic.

- **Feature chapters**: 2 columns · gap 64px. Alternating image and copy pairs; artwork takes roughly half the content width.
- **Footer grid**: max width 1280px · 4 columns · gap 0px. Equal 320px directory columns, with 32px internal right padding.

### Surfaces

#### White canvas · level 0

**Use:** Long-form marketing body.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #121117`

**Observed:** Most of the page is an uninterrupted light field; images and thin card outlines create structure.

**Transfer:** Keep the content field predominantly white between strong colored moments.

#### Pink band · level 0

**Use:** Opening and short oversized reassurance moments.

**CSS:** `background: #ff7aac; color: #121117`

**Observed:** Flat full-bleed color with no gradient, texture or darkening overlay.

**Transfer:** Make the color itself the stage for the headline and image.

#### Outlined card · level 1

**Use:** Process and explanatory cards.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; border: 1px solid #121117; border-radius: 4px; box-shadow: none`

**Observed:** Fine dark outline with content clipped neatly at the card edge.

**Transfer:** Use when an ordered explanation needs a clear boundary.

#### Soft panel · level 1

**Use:** Feature media, reviews and case studies.

**CSS:** `background: #f4f4f8; border-radius: 16px`

**Observed:** A cool near-white fill, normally flat. Review cards use 8px corners with a 2px light edge; company-principle blocks use 12px corners.

**Transfer:** Back interface fragments with this calm field rather than saturated gradients.

#### Support inset · level 1

**Use:** Compact help or mission summary.

**CSS:** `background: #ebebf1; border-radius: 16px; box-shadow: 0px 0px 8px 0px rgba(9,15,25,0.1)`

**Observed:** Centered inset panel narrower than the content container.

**Transfer:** Offer a low-pressure route after the main explanation or questions.

#### Dark directory · level 0

**Use:** Page ending.

**CSS:** `background: #121117; color: #ffffff; padding-top: 60px`

**Observed:** A substantial multi-row directory, not a thin legal strip.

**Transfer:** Use grouped navigation to make a useful, definitive ending.

#### Mint conversion half · level 1

**Only for:** secondary-audience conversion sections

**Use:** A distinct audience pathway.

**CSS:** `background: #3ddabe; padding: 64px; color: #121117`

**Observed:** One half of a large portrait-and-copy panel within a shared 1px ink outline and 8px corners.

**Transfer:** Reserve this color for a separate audience or participation opportunity.

## Imagery and graphics

### Photographic echo stack

**Use:** Opening and selected benefit or proof scenes; the core signature image treatment.

**Observed:** Warm, naturally lit people at laptops or phones, with everyday furniture, curtains and notebooks. A dominant rectangular image has two smaller copies tucked behind one side, offset downward or upward like successive moments; a small video-call portrait can overlay the foreground. The opening foreground is 446×326px, with 357×261px and 286×209px echoes.

**Transfer:** Photograph a real user doing the product's core activity. If a product has no people-led medium, show hands using the product in a lived-in setting. Repeat that same image, rather than inventing unrelated decorative assets.

**Build:** Place three copies in a relative stage. Put the full-size frame at the front; scale the rear frames to 80% and 64%, shifting each toward the exposed side by about 68px and centering them vertically. Use 4px corners and no heavy shadow. Add a small rounded call or identity inset only when it explains the interaction.

### Interface and human collage

**Use:** Feature demonstrations and capability grids.

**Observed:** Portraits or phone/tablet outlines sit over pale lavender-gray squares, with white cards showing a few useful metrics, notes or scheduling details. Thin dark device frames and tiny pink or mint highlights make the interface legible without turning the whole section into a dashboard.

**Transfer:** Show one task, result or feedback moment per scene. Use actual product details but keep marketing text outside the interface.

**Build:** Use a roughly 300px square pale panel for grid scenes or a 500px-wide stage for feature pairs. Layer one device or person as the main object, then two or three white rounded interface cards with subtle shadows, aligned at uneven heights. Keep accent marks small and preserve ample light backing.

### Documentary story media

**Use:** Customer stories, company introduction and case-study sections.

**Observed:** Bright, natural interiors and gardens; subjects are seated, speaking or casually engaged. Large landscape video is paired with simple tabs; the play control is a translucent white circle with a pink triangle. Story portraits use rounded rectangular frames, while call inset portraits are smaller.

**Transfer:** Choose relaxed, credible situations instead of dramatic studio hero shots. Let natural surroundings provide warmth.

### Secondary-audience portrait panel

**Only for:** secondary-audience conversion sections

**Use:** Large portrait paired with a colored participation invitation.

**Observed:** A waist-up smiling subject fills the left half from edge to edge. Repeated narrow image slices extend into the left edge; the right half is flat mint with black type and a full-width dark action.

**Transfer:** Use a participant, expert or partner portrait in neutral surroundings so the mint half carries the color.

**Build:** Clip both halves inside an 8px rounded, 1px ink-outlined panel. Fill the image half with object-fit cover; tuck two horizontally offset copies behind its lead portrait. Set the copy half to mint with 64px inset.

### Team portrait wall

**Only for:** company profiles

**Use:** People and leadership directories.

**Observed:** Consistent square portraits on warm neutral studio backgrounds, natural clothing colors and direct expressions. Names and roles sit below, with a small plus control at the top-right of each portrait.

**Transfer:** Keep backgrounds, framing and lighting consistent across the group; use real people rather than decorative avatars.

### Editorial object collage

**Only for:** cross-selling and editorial campaign sections

**Use:** A lighter, playful illustration of a secondary use case.

**Observed:** Overlapping photographed objects carry small yellow rectangular labels with fine black outlines; the collage sits opposite compact copy and paired actions.

**Transfer:** Choose one meaningful everyday object for the new proposition and repeat it with a few short descriptive labels.

**Build:** Overlap three object photographs with roughly 24px negative horizontal spacing. Place yellow labels in the upper region with 1px ink borders, 4px corners and 6px 10px padding.

### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly monochrome filled SVGs at 16px, 24px and 32px; category drawings reach 48px and mix outline and solid shapes. Arrows, chevrons, stars and external-link marks are functional and compact.

**Transfer:** Choose simple ink-colored functional symbols, use 24px arrows beside large actions and reserve illustrated 48px icons for category tiles.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Pink photographic opening

**Observed:** A 72px navigation bar merges into the flat Pink band. Beneath it, a 454px split hero has 64px top/bottom inset: a narrow left text column with a three-line 64px headline, followed by a Dark button after 48px, faces the Photographic echo stack. The front image occupies about a third of the viewport width, with smaller frames receding toward the right and a small call inset on top. Back-to-front layers are pink field, echoed images, primary photograph/inset, then headline and action in the adjacent column. Within the first 900px, the complete hero, five-item compact proof strip and the first category-tile row are visible. There is no supporting paragraph in this default opening; further categories and the large proof chapter fall below.

**Transfer:** Use one concise outcome promise and authentic product-in-use photography with the same echo geometry. An explanation-led opening adds a support paragraph on pink. Enterprise openings use a white split stage, dark fixed navigation and a photo with a small interface overlay; research-led openings center a 96px claim over evidence media; company profiles center a 96px mission line above wide documentary video.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the asymmetric split with a giant centered app window, add a second equal-weight hero action, or use a gradient behind the photograph.

**Text style:** `hero` — Platform 700 · 64px/68px · tracking -0.005em

### Compact proof strip

**Observed:** Five compact proof items spread across the Main container. Each uses compact-heading numbers above navigation-weight labels; a rating item adds stars. White surrounds the row with 64px separation from adjacent chapters.

**Transfer:** Select a small set of credible scale, satisfaction or availability measures; keep the strip unboxed.

### Heading and support pair

**Observed:** Major chapter title at 64px/68px followed by body-lead copy after 16px; the more celebratory centered proof chapter uses monumental type and a 24px gap. Most headings stand directly on white, without an eyebrow.

**Transfer:** Make the headline name an outcome or reader question; use the support line to explain the mechanism.

### Echo proof carousel

**Observed:** Centered monumental heading and support pair lead into a two-column story. A tall photographic echo stack occupies the left; a compact-heading statistic or quote, body-size research credit and small pagination dots sit right. Small Previous/Next controls sit at the outer edges.

**Transfer:** Pair a single human story with one meaningful proof statement; use a short methodology credit.

### Illustrated process

**Observed:** A hero-sized title sits 48px above a Process grid of Process cards. Step counter, feature-heading, body copy and image read top to bottom. Explanation-led pages instead use three open columns with pink square numbers and compact-heading titles; enterprise onboarding expands to four open columns.

**Transfer:** Use the variant that matches the complexity of the process, preserving one task per column.

### Alternating capability chapters

**Observed:** White two-column chapters alternate image and text sides. Roughly 400–540px-wide collages face 48px feature headings and 20px explanatory paragraphs; many chapters use 96px vertical inset. Product-grid variants place three 300px illustrations above centered 32px titles and short 20px copy.

**Transfer:** Explain one outcome per chapter and let the artwork show the corresponding action or feedback.

### Monumental reassurance

**Observed:** Pink band with 96px vertical padding, centered monumental statement and body-lead support after 32px. It works without imagery or a button.

**Transfer:** Include a short, confident reassurance or commitment as a distinct colored pause, using a truthful promise for the new product.

### Objections and endings

**Observed:** Question rows appear near the end of explanation-led pages, followed by a narrow Support inset and the Footer. Enterprise conversion includes a two-column portrait/form chapter, resource cards, compact two-column FAQs and related-link directories before the Footer. Research-led pages finish with four unboxed review columns before the Footer. Company profiles finish with a pink contact band: a centered 96px two-line invitation and a 32px contact detail after 24px.

**Transfer:** Resolve the page's actual remaining decision with questions, a human contact route or credible final reviews; then use the shared Dark directory. For press/contact variants, retain the monumental invitation rather than replacing it with a small email link.

### Secondary audience conversion

**Only for:** secondary-audience conversion sections

**Observed:** Mint conversion half and Secondary-audience portrait panel form a 1280px-wide outlined composition. A monumental title, compact body, three bold benefit lines and a full-width Dark button occupy the copy half; a text action sits below.

**Transfer:** Use for a clearly separate participation path, not as the default feature-card treatment.

### Research metric trio

**Only for:** research-led proof sections

**Observed:** A heading and support pair introduce three Proof cards with 40px gaps. The 96px figures and 34px explanations are unusually large relative to the restrained outline. A centered Pink button follows.

### Company principle explorer

**Only for:** company profiles

**Observed:** Three columns with 20px gaps connect a category summary, a stack of 68px option buttons and a matching explanatory panel. Pale 12px-corner panels carry 12px colored rails at the left of each category and the right of the selected option. Yellow, blue and cyan distinguish rows.

**Transfer:** Adapt to a set of operating principles or categories with concise explanations; keep this interactive organizational treatment out of ordinary product chapters.

### Editorial and press modules

**Only for:** company and resource pages

**Observed:** News uses three image-led columns with compact titles. Press coverage switches to horizontal rows: publisher mark left, headline and publication/date middle, Read more right, with light rules between rows. A centered heading and support pair introduce the list.

**Transfer:** Use cards for visual stories and rows for a long archive; keep publication metadata easy to scan.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → compact credibility → human or product explanation → process and capability proof → reassurance or conversion → objections and useful resources → substantial directory.

### Broad consumer discovery and conversion

1. **Promise** — Pink split stage, echoed photograph and one dark action.
2. **Credibility and discovery** — Compact numbers followed by three-column category tiles.
3. **Human proof** — Monumental introduction and photographic proof carousel.
4. **Process** — Illustrated outlined cards.
5. **Reassurance** — Oversized promise in a pink band.
6. **Audience expansion** — Mint portrait invitation and a separate business cross-sell.
7. **Ending** — Related-resource directory, then multi-tier dark footer.

**Transfer:** Start with the easiest action, then support discovery without losing the people-first proposition.

### Explain a service and reduce uncertainty

1. **Promise** — Pink split opening with explanatory support and a dark action.
2. **Credibility** — Compact stats strip.
3. **Process** — Open numbered columns plus an inset proof summary.
4. **Capabilities** — Alternating photographic feature pairs and a three-column tool gallery.
5. **Social proof** — Tall review cards and a grid of available experts.
6. **Objection handling** — Roomy FAQ rows followed by a compact help panel.
7. **Ending** — Shared multi-tier dark footer.

**Transfer:** Let the explanation answer how it works before asking readers to choose a provider or commit.

### Enterprise service evaluation and lead generation

## Writing

Warm, direct and optimistic, with confidence grounded in specific mechanisms and proof. Human outcomes lead; technology supports rather than displaces the person.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Preply names, wordmarks, proprietary artwork, customer identities, study claims, numerical results, testimonials, guarantees or contact details. Source examples demonstrate tone only; replace them with truthful product-specific content. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your; use we for help and commitments. Product descriptions can use the product name in third person when explaining evidence or organizational context.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often ending in a period. Support copy is usually one or two plain sentences; feature explanations are fuller but avoid dense jargon. Paired short clauses and concrete verbs create momentum.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | An action or outcome made personal with your. | 6–12 words | “Learn faster with your best language tutor.” |
| Support line | Name the audience and scale, then return to a personal result. | 12–25 words | “Millions of learners. Over 100,000 tutors. Progress that’s personal (and proven).” |
| Feature heading | A practical benefit in ordinary language. | 4–9 words | “Learn on your budget and schedule.” |
| Primary action | Verb plus a concrete next step; personal possessive when it reduces distance. | 2–5 words | “Find your tutor”<br>“Talk to our team” |
| Reassurance | Two compact statements, promise then confidence. | 4–9 words | “Lessons you’ll love. Guaranteed.” |
| Proof headline | Short paired clauses that turn scale into a result. | 4–8 words | “Global teams. Real results.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** real progress; personalized; confidence; built around you; step by step; keep moving forward; goals; flexible; support; measurable

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; disruptive paradigm; effortless mastery; crush the competition; unlimited potential without proof

**Rules:**

- Connect each feature to a change the reader can recognize in daily life.
- Place research credits and qualifications near measured claims.
- Use practical questions for objection handling, not sales slogans disguised as questions.
- Keep enterprise explanations concrete: time, visibility, setup, adoption and outcomes.
- Let reassurance be clear and brief; do not soften every sentence with promotional adjectives.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Preserve the 64px/68px opening headline and its compact three-line wrapping at desktop.
- Use the 96px tier for a few proof or reassurance peaks, including a colored typographic pause.
- Keep 56px conversion actions rectangular with 8px corners and visible 2px edge construction.
- Use a 1280px centered body container and 64px or 96px chapter breathing room.
- Stage real activity photography with smaller echoed frames rather than unrelated ornaments.
- Pair category titles with 14px metadata and a 48px monochrome symbol.
- Use 1px ink outlines for explanatory cards and lighter #dcdce5 edges for directory tiles.
- Finish with a useful four-column dark directory and compact underlined links.

### Don't

- Do not turn every card into a 24px-radius floating panel; 4px and 8px corners carry the core geometry.
- Do not make the main marketing body a dark theme; near-black primarily anchors type, actions and the footer.
- Do not spread mint, blue and yellow into alternating full-page chapter backgrounds; pink is the main large-area accent.
- Do not apply negative tracking to the 32px Platform tier; its tracking is positive 0.01em.
- Do not render supporting paragraphs in the heavy headline family; use regular Figtree at 16px or 20px.
- Do not give every card an elevated shadow; thin outlines and flat pale fields do most of the organizing.
- Do not inflate metadata and directory links into headline-sized navigation.
- Do not replace the natural interior palette of photography with hard duotone filters or neon rim lighting.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared action transitions use color, border-color and background-color at 220ms cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1). Text links use the same color timing.
- Some media layers declare opacity and transform transitions at 0.3s ease-in-out; selected story tabs declare color and border-color at 0.4s.
- Two tickers declare a linear infinite animation with duration calc(var(--ticker-width) * 1s / 120).

**Transfer:**

- Use the declared action transition consistently across the dark, pink and outline action family.
- If a logo ticker is used, keep it a supporting proof strip rather than a headline effect.

**Unknown:**

- Precise carousel autoplay, scroll entrance choreography and video behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 400px, 640px, 700px, 781px, 880px, 960px and 1200px; informational and business modules also declare 1024px.

**Transfer:**

- Recommended adaptation: stack the opening text above the echoed photo while preserving the dark-on-pink action contrast and one visible receding edge.
- Recommended adaptation: collapse multi-column cards into fewer columns without changing their outline and counter geometry; retain labels above form controls.
- Recommended adaptation: keep large statements short enough to wrap cleanly and turn the four-column footer into vertically grouped directories.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile font sizes, navigation behavior and exact breakpoint-to-layout mappings are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
