# Prime Intellect — luminous systems wilderness

## Overview

A dark technical identity that places humane, cinematic landscapes beside austere engineering diagrams. Regular grotesk text and compact uppercase monospace labels keep the interface restrained, while green light, fine particle fields and occasional acid-yellow proof panels supply energy. It suits developer platforms and ambitious technical products with a mixture of spacious visual stages and dense, ruled capability grids.

### Essentials

- The scene is monumental, not the headline: a full-width atmospheric opening sits behind a left-aligned 36px/1.1 regular Geist promise and an 18px/1.5 support line limited to 480px.
- Controls resemble equipment labels: square white buttons are 28px tall with 8px padding and 12px/1 uppercase ABC Favorit Mono; numbered translucent navigation tabs are 124×30px.
- Nearly everything is charcoal: #0e0e0e canvas and 1px #202020 rules dominate; green appears in tiny status marks, light and data art, with a single concentrated acid-yellow proof surface rather than a repeated accent fill.
- Capability chapters read as illuminated instruments: 320–360px image bands pair a white 28px category with a muted same-size explanation, followed by zero-gap two- or three-column ruled cells.
- Technical media is a stage, not decoration: wireframe geometry, sparse luminous points and small figure labels occupy large dark panels. When no equivalent media exists, use an original geometric process diagram and a restrained generated particle field.
- The ending is a typographic light sculpture: a low-contrast dotted two-line promise occupies a 666×205px field on #191919, separated from a tiny white action by 80px, with small uppercase link columns beside it. Recreate the device with original words.

## Imagery and graphics

### Atmospheric threshold

**Use:** The primary opening visual.

**Observed:** A dark forest gorge reflected in still water surrounds a small luminous vertical doorway on a central island. Cool haze descends from above; moss-green light and the portal reflection are the bright focus. Image fills the opening behind text, fading into black at edges and bottom.

**Transfer:** Stage an original product metaphor as a small lit threshold or object in a vast, believable environment. Minimum version: licensed forest or architectural landscape with a simple original luminous rectangle, atmospheric haze and reflection; preserve the scene-to-text scale.

**Build:** Use a full-bleed cover image with a black left-to-right vignette and bottom fade. Place a narrow upright SVG rectangle near the horizontal center, filled with translucent pale green and edged with rgba(133,237,117,0.5); add a blurred halo and a vertically mirrored, softened reflection below. Keep the text area distinctly darker.

### Particle chapter fields

**Use:** Signature capability bands and featured technical art.

**Observed:** Low-luminance point clouds form sweeping sheets, curved rows and expanding vertical data bars. Colors are green, icy cyan and faint violet on near-black; marks accumulate toward the right while the upper-left copy remains readable.

**Transfer:** Translate a relevant process into a field of points or narrow light bars. The minimum is an SVG perspective grid with sparse colored highlights, not a generic multicolor gradient.

**Build:** On a 1400×340px SVG viewBox, draw repeated 1–2px dots along perspective curves, or 1px vertical lines whose height and spacing increase from left to right. Use white at 10–25% and green at 15–40%, with occasional cyan points; mask the left edge and lower boundary into black. Layer a screen-blended radial green glow at 72% 38% with alpha 0.17 and a cyan glow at 48% 52% with alpha 0.1.

### Wireframe instruments

**Use:** Large feature illustrations inside ruled cells.

**Observed:** Thin grey isometric cubes, layered planes, network arcs, orbiting nodes and process triangles on nearly black backing. White nodes and occasional green values draw attention; most marks are intentionally faint, with ample air around them.

**Transfer:** Draw an original diagram explaining the product's flow using 1px lines, a few small white nodes and tiny mono annotations. Keep it visually subordinate to the promise but larger than a conventional feature icon.

**Build:** Construct isometric planes as SVG polygons with white strokes at 10–20% opacity. Connect 3–6 nodes with fine arcs or straight lines; highlight only one plane or node in white and one live value in green. Reserve a large empty perimeter.

### Dotted closing words

**Use:** Required identity device in the closing region.

**Observed:** An original short ownership-oriented statement appears as two lines of very low-contrast dot-matrix letters in a 666×205px field, beneath a small wordmark and well above the white action.

**Transfer:** Choose a short product-specific promise with a strong two-line silhouette. Reproduce as dots even without interactive rendering; this is artwork, not a conventional bold footer heading.

**Build:** Render the chosen phrase into an offscreen canvas in a substantial monospace face. Sample its alpha mask on a 3px grid and draw 1px dots in white at roughly 10–25% opacity on transparent backing. Scale the field to 666×205px; retain the dark charcoal ground.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Atmospheric promise → operational capabilities → human and quantified proof → deeper technical detail → invitation → dotted identity closing.

## Writing

Confident and technically literate, with an emphasis on ownership, direct action and practical outcomes. Claims are explained through mechanisms and concrete evidence rather than exuberant adjectives.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand identity, customer logos, product names, ownership slogans, customer quotations, investor names, model claims or numerical outcomes. Source examples illustrate syntax and tone only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to the reader with 'your' in promises and actions; use product-led descriptions for capability explanations and third-person narrative for customer stories.
- **Rhythm:** Short imperative actions and one-word chapter labels alternate with clear one- or two-sentence explanations. Editorial stories allow longer technical sentences, restrained subheadings and explicit comparisons.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | A compact imperative about agency and ownership. | 3–5 words | “Own Your Intelligence” |
| Supporting line | Start with concrete verbs and name the integrated workflow. | 18–28 words | “Train, deploy, and continuously improve your own models on an integrated compute, training, inference, and sandbox stack.” |
| Chapter heading | One-word category with a period, followed by a useful capability statement. | 1-word label plus 6–18-word explanation | “Lab.”<br>“Post-train your own self improving agents” |
| Primary action | Verb plus the immediate technical task, not a vague emotional invitation. | 2–4 words | “Run your first eval”<br>“Create Environments” |
| Case study title | How a customer uses the product to accomplish a concrete task. | 12–18 words | “How Extropic Uses [Brand] to Train a Thermodynamic ML Research Agent” |
| Article section heading | Plain description of the narrative job or implementation step. | 1–5 words | “Designing the environment”<br>“Results” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** own; build; train; deploy; run; evaluate; integrated; open-source; workflow; reliable; your

**Avoid:** magic; revolutionary; game-changing; effortless everything; supercharge; unleash limitless potential

**Rules:**

- Name the actual operation before describing its benefit.
- Attach quantified claims to their task, baseline and context.
- Use technical terms when they explain the mechanism; do not use them as texture.
- Keep uppercase styling for interface labels, not for paragraphs or quotes.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the opening promise at 36px and weight 400 against a much larger scene.
- Carry 1px #202020 rules continuously across adjacent cells.
- Use 20px desktop gutters and standard cell padding.
- Set conversion labels in 12px uppercase mono inside 28px square-corner controls.
- Separate white chapter names from muted explanations through brightness, not font weight.
- Include a concentrated bright proof moment while keeping the wider canvas near-black.
- Give figures generous dark staging and tiny labels rather than oversized icon treatments.
- Close with original dotted words on #191919 and a small white action.

### Don't

- Do not make green the fill color of all buttons; primary actions are white with black text.
- Do not replace zero-gap service grids with widely separated rounded cards.
- Do not increase ordinary display weights to 700; regular grotesk is central to the hierarchy.
- Do not brighten every secondary paragraph to white; the half-white layer is essential.
- Do not apply the article's yellow-and-burgundy figure treatment to the default page canvas.
- Do not use prominent drop shadows as the main way to separate panels; borders do that work.
- Do not turn customer logos into full-color badges in the monochrome opening grid.
- Do not render dotted closing words as a solid high-contrast conventional heading.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

**Transfer:**

- Let light move subtly across the opening words without changing the modest headline hierarchy.
- Keep action motion short and tonal; do not make the small controls bounce or expand.

**Unknown:**

- Exact video loop timing, particle behavior and dotted-word interaction are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints are 560px, 640px, 768px, 960px, 1024px, 1280px and 1536px.

- The case-study summary rail is hidden outside the large-screen layout; dotted footer canvas is hidden at the smallest sizes.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
