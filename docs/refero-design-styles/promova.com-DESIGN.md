# Promova — pastel blocks, fearless play

## Overview

A bright, encouraging style that makes a useful digital product feel approachable rather than technical. Rounded pastel chapters sit against a black ground, with chunky geometric sans headlines, readable humanist body text and playful toy-like objects orbiting real interfaces. It suits consumer products and service platforms that need both warmth and tangible proof, with airy storytelling and denser discovery sections.

### Essentials

- Soft chapters meet hard actions: nearly full-width panels have 30px corners and 20px outer gutters, while main black buttons remain square, 340px wide and 60px tall.
- A blunt, reassuring promise shares the stage with a playful product object: 70px/1 geometric display type, a 500px copy measure and a roughly 636×516px interface collage on a periwinkle field.
- Color belongs to whole chapters, not rainbow text: black lettering sits on broad periwinkle, butter, mint, lilac and sand surfaces; white and pale gray provide extended reading intervals. Yellow is concentrated in a 60px announcement strip and a vivid conversion band.
- The typographic personality is substantial without compressed spacing: Nekst headlines at 40px, 50px, 70px and 100px use matching line heights and normal tracking; Manrope explanation text is 24px.
- Every landing page needs a tactile visual interruption: tilted interface frames with floating achievement-like objects, soft plastic symbols or dimensional punctuation. When no app exists, use a real product view with two oversized rounded tokens rather than generic abstract wallpaper.
- Proof becomes part of the playground: 70px metrics and a 120px spotlight number are paired with small dimensional symbols, while white 30px-radius review cards spill horizontally across a pale lavender band.
- The closing invitation is a designed destination: a split pastel promise-and-product composition leads into a yellow action shelf with a rectangular web action and circular alternate-channel icons, followed by a substantial white directory footer.

## Typography

- **Nekst** — Headlines, numbers, short titles, navigation and action labels. Broad geometric sans with single-storey forms and a round, confident silhouette; regular display cuts look substantial. If unavailable, use **Outfit**. Use 500–600 for the regular display look and 700 for the opening; preserve generous width and normal tracking. Font stack: "Nekst", "Outfit", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Manrope** — Descriptions, paragraphs, metadata and footer links. Open, readable sans that is lighter and less emphatic than the display face. Font stack: "Manrope", Arial, sans-serif.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy storytelling inside closely stacked rounded chapters; dense navigation and directories contrast with broad product stages.

### Containers and grids

- **page panels**: max width 1400px · side padding 20px. At 1440px, rounded panels occupy the width between narrow black gutters. Panels often touch vertically; the curved corners expose black seams.
- **feature stage**: max width 1160px · 2 columns · gap 100px. A 460px explanation column balances a 600px image stage; reverse the order in the next feature group.
- **hero stage**: max width 1280px · 2 columns · gap 80px. A 600px copy column beside a product collage; headline and supporting copy are limited to 500px.
- **discovery grid**: 6 columns · gap 10px. Two compact rows of 215×92px selector tiles inside a lilac-gray panel.
- **review rail**: gap 20px. 570px slide tracks contain 540×468px white cards; the next card remains partially visible rather than shrinking all reviews into equal columns.
- **footer directory**: 4 columns · gap 0px · side padding 30px. Four 335px columns in each of two tiers; the upper tier is primary navigation and download channels, the lower tier is the detailed directory.

## Imagery and graphics

### Interface and toy collage

**Use:** Opening and feature stages; the core visual signature.

**Observed:** Real phone interfaces have black device edges, white screens and mild perspective tilts. Oversized soft-plastic symbols, speech fragments and rounded white achievement cards float around them. The hero collage fills roughly half the available width, while the background remains flat periwinkle.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's actual interface or physical product as the anchor. The minimum version is one carefully framed product image plus two tactile tokens that illustrate outcomes.

**Build:** Place a transparent product cutout in a roughly 600px stage. Tilt the principal device about 8deg, keep a crisp black rim, and layer two white rounded cards at opposing corners. Add a small colored flame, star or speech token with rounded extruded edges, a broad upper-left highlight and soft contact shading; do not add a shadow to the chapter itself.

### Dimensional punctuation

**Use:** Metrics, quotes and feature explanations.

**Observed:** Objects include inflated quotation marks, a rocket, cursor, lightbulb and chunky rounded blocks. Smooth matte-to-satin material and soft lighting make them look like friendly desktop toys; they overlap headings or sit in deliberately open areas rather than becoming full backgrounds.

**Transfer:** Translate the new product's ideas into one recognizable object per visual pause. For a metric, let a star or result symbol partially overlap the number without hiding its meaning.

**Build:** In SVG or 3D, start with simple thick rounded geometry, one light-facing plane and one shaded edge. Use lilac, sky blue, pink and lime family colors with low-contrast highlights; render on transparency. A fallback is two or three rounded, rotated pastel blocks with a soft inset highlight, not a flat line icon.

### Article and tool previews

**Use:** Discovery and resource collections.

**Observed:** A large featured preview and white text card sit beside a vertical thumbnail list. Compact tool grids place interface imagery above white rounded text bodies, with an arrow near the lower-right corner.

**Transfer:** Show useful content or concrete output, with consistent preview proportions and no stock-photo filler.

### Blue edge sculptures

**Only for:** course and institutional landing pages

**Use:** Lavender invitation bands.

**Observed:** A glossy blue ring enters from the upper left and a thick blue spring rises from the lower right. Both are clipped by the panel; the centered title and black button remain isolated.

**Transfer:** Reuse the edge-entry logic with dimensional shapes relevant to the product, keeping the clear central field.

**Build:** Draw a 220px outer ring with a thick rounded tube and a 280px-tall coil using wide round SVG strokes. Shade the strokes from pale cyan highlights to medium blue on the far edge; position parts outside opposing corners and clip at the 30px panel radius. Exact gradient stops are not essential.

### People and credibility

**Only for:** institutional, partner and editorial sections

**Use:** Expert profiles, endorsement splits and video panels.

**Observed:** Natural-color portraits sit in rounded rectangles rather than isolated floating headshots. Expert profiles combine a tall central portrait with a large quote on one side and biography/action on the other; purple toy-like squiggles overlap a lower corner. Wider photography uses ordinary people in bright real environments. Video titles are oversized white type over a full-bleed image.

**Transfer:** Commission approachable portraits and real-use scenes; retain the rounded framing and asymmetric text balance, not a formal executive grid.

### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly solid black or white SVG marks at 16px and 24px; selector images are 30px. Chevron and arrow navigation stays minimal. Yellow review stars are small filled shapes; alternate-channel icons use black 60px circles.

**Transfer:** Use simple filled monochrome symbols for navigation, keeping colorful dimensional objects separate from functional icons.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Playful product opening

**Observed:** A 60px yellow announcement strip tops a white sticky navigation bar, occupying about the first 129px. Under it, a 1400×694px periwinkle Pastel chapter sits in 20px black gutters. The hero stage has two balanced columns: the left contains a two-line 70px/1 promise within a 500px measure, a 24px support paragraph 20px below it, and a Primary button after 60px. The right presents the roughly 636×516px Interface and toy collage with a tilted phone and floating white proof fragments. Back-to-front layers are flat field, product frame, floating objects and badges. A muted single-line press-logo strip spans the bottom of the chapter. Within the first 900px the navigation, complete promise, support, action, main object and logo strip are visible; only the top of the following proof panel appears.

**Transfer:** Make the promise about overcoming a recognizable barrier. If no interface exists, stage the product or one concrete output with two dimensional outcome tokens at equivalent scale. Course pages can open with a 630px copy/action column beside a 770px illustration panel; institutional pages use either an asymmetric character composition or a narrow vertical title tile next to two explanatory tiles.

**Avoid:** Do not center all hero content in a generic stacked layout, replace the product with an abstract gradient, or put the primary action below the first screen.

**Text style:** `hero` — Nekst 700 · 70px/1

### Numbered benefit stage

**Observed:** A Reading chapter begins with a centered chapter-display headline. Beneath it, feature stage rows pair two stacked benefits with a 600px product scene; each benefit has an em-dash number, a 40px title and 24px explanation after 30px. The next row reverses media and text. Section buttons sit beneath benefit groups.

**Transfer:** Group related benefits into coherent pairs and let each image demonstrate the adjacent claims.

**Text style:** `feature-heading` — Nekst 400 · 40px/1

### Bright proof interruption

**Observed:** A butter Pastel chapter isolates a 120px number with a dimensional symbol and a centered 40px statement. Ordinary proof rows instead use 70px numbers with 18px explanatory labels on white; company variants form a three-column, two-row grid.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Reassuring promise → concrete experience → proof → benefit or process chapters → human credibility → objections → invitation and practical directory.

### Broad consumer product landing page

1. **Promise** — Periwinkle opening with product collage and a muted press strip.
2. **Proof and choice** — White metric row and lilac-gray selector grid.
3. **Benefits and demonstration** — Large neutral chapters alternate numbered explanations with oversized interface scenes; butter spotlight proof interrupts the rhythm.
4. **Credibility and activation** — Partner split, mint quote, green onboarding pair and review rail.
5. **Discovery and objections** — Tools, awards, featured resources and a ruled question panel.
6. **Closing call** — Split pastel invitation, yellow channel shelf, two-tier white directory and legal line.

**Transfer:** Borrow the alternation of product, proof and relief rather than reproducing the full inventory.

### Editorial methodology and authority page

1. **Purpose** — Three touching colored tiles: a vertical title and two explanatory blocks with dimensional objects.
2. **Principle** — Butter statement with monumental heading and overlapping rocket.
3. **Authority** — Expertise introduction followed by alternating portrait, quote and biography spreads.
4. **Method** — Butter principles chapter with large topic headings and compact explanations.
5. **Expectation and contact** — Split expectation panels, then a compact lavender contact band and the shared directory ending.

**Transfer:** Use this variant when the reader needs to trust the people and methods behind the product.

### Company identity and trust page

1. **Identity** — Purple asymmetric heading, clustered dimensional characters and a lower-right introduction.
2. **Quantified proof** — Six metrics on white followed by a lime definition panel.
3. **Purpose and range** — Monumental mission statement and a horizontally overflowing offer collection.
4. **History and leadership** — Narrow timeline spine, centered invitation and a photographic leadership split.
5. **Public credibility** — Community project carousel, awards, wide photographic video, partner split and featured press.
6. **Contact** — Purple help invitation, paired gray form/green contact panel, common directory and legal ending.

**Transfer:** Replace corporate boilerplate with readable proof, people and a visually legible progression.

### Focused product-category conversion page

1. **Category promise** — Asymmetric copy/action stack beside a large illustration panel.
2. **Capabilities** — Metrics followed by a tabbed feature stage with an active black left rule.
3. **Method and differentiation** — Partner demonstration, pink process spine, invitation band and practical use-case collection.
4. **Depth** — Real-context carousel, numbered benefit constellation and level/skill collections.
5. **Proof and closing invitation** — Review rail and lavender invitation with blue edge sculptures.
6. **Alternatives and objections** — Outlined destination chips, ruled questions, then the common directory footer without another yellow closing shelf.

**Transfer:** Use deeper explanation for a focused offer while preserving flat pastel chapter changes and repeated square actions.

## Writing

Encouraging, conversational and practical. The product acknowledges hesitation and imperfection, then answers them with a tangible action or real-world benefit rather than grand technical claims.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Promova names, wordmarks, partner identities, character assets, proprietary product screens, review quotations, awards or numerical claims. Source wording here demonstrates tone only; write fresh copy and use substantiated claims for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you. Use we when describing the team’s responsibility; let the reader own the effort and outcome.
- **Rhythm:** Short, punchy promises and benefit titles contrast with relaxed explanatory paragraphs. Contractions, em dashes, rhetorical questions and brief answers create a friendly spoken rhythm; occasional exclamation marks appear in invitations.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | An imperative that makes starting feel possible before perfection. | 4–7 words | “Speak before you're ready” |
| Benefit title | A compact practical outcome or memorable pairing. | 3–6 words | “Built for real life”<br>“Human-made, AI-powered” |
| Supporting explanation | Describe an ordinary action first, then explain how repetition helps. | 2–4 short sentences | “You learn something, you practice it, you use it in a conversation. Doesn't matter if you started five minutes ago or five years ago — you'll sound a little better every time.” |
| Primary action | A direct verb with minimal commitment language. | 2–3 words | “Try Now”<br>“Get started” |
| Objections and contact | Use familiar questions or a warm invitation, not formal institutional labels. | 3–12 words | “People often ask”<br>“Want to get in touch?”<br>“Have something to share? Bounce us a message, don't be shy!” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** real life; start; your pace; confidence; step by step; practice; goals; support; you don't need to

**Avoid:** revolutionary ecosystem; unparalleled excellence; effortless mastery; guaranteed transformation; enterprise jargon in consumer promises

**Rules:**

- Name a familiar situation instead of describing an abstract capability alone.
- Reassure without promising perfection or instant expertise.
- Follow emotional encouragement with a concrete explanation of what happens next.
- Keep action labels shorter than benefit titles; let the support paragraph carry nuance.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the 20px black side frame visible around 30px-radius chapters.
- Make the first-screen promise 70px/1 with a roughly 500px text measure.
- Give primary actions a 60px height and square corners even when surrounding cards are rounded.
- Use 24px Manrope for prominent explanations and 18px for compact context.
- Stage actual product imagery at roughly half the feature width, with a small number of tactile overlapping objects.
- Reserve 100px–120px display type for chapter pivots and standout proof.
- Preserve a clipped continuation in 540px-wide review cards rather than making every card fit the viewport.
- Carry the white four-column directory all the way to a small centered legal ending.

### Don't

- Do not turn the black structural ground into a full dark reading theme.
- Do not make the main buttons pills to match the chapter corners.
- Do not apply tight negative tracking or condensed typography to the broad display face.
- Do not add a shadow or glass blur to every pastel card; the surfaces are flat and opaque.
- Do not color each headline word differently; most marketing lettering stays black.
- Do not replace the dimensional symbol vocabulary with a uniform set of tiny outline illustrations.
- Do not use the saturated culture-purple opening as the ordinary product landing default.
- Do not shrink 24px support copy to navigation scale just to fit more cards above the fold.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Navigation opacity transitions use 0.2s ease-out; action and surface transitions also include 200ms background-color and opacity changes.
- The opening decorations use 4s ease-in-out infinite floating animations. Some later floating objects use 4.5s, 5.2s and 3.8s durations with staggered delays.
- The press-logo marquee uses a 40s linear infinite animation with a 3.44s delay.
- Some tool elements declare a 600ms bounce-in animation using cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1).

**Transfer:**

- Keep motion attached to the floating-object and horizontal-proof vocabulary rather than moving all text.
- Let drifting tokens retain their relationship to the product frame; the static composition must still communicate the offer.

**Unknown:**

- Exact translation distances, scroll triggers and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Width breakpoints are declared at 1100px; the company variant also declares 940px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, retain the pastel chapter stack and square actions, but place copy before the main product scene.
- Reduce the number of selector columns while preserving 10px inter-tile gaps; keep the review collection a horizontal rail.
- Move the two closing channel groups into a readable stack rather than compressing the button and circular icons together.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile sizes, navigation behavior, exact stacking rules and viewport-relative type formulas are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
