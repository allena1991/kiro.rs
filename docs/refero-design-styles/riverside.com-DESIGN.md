# Riverside — studio scale, human energy

## Overview

An expansive, creator-led software style that makes sophisticated tools feel approachable and immediately useful. Wide regular grotesk headlines, violet actions and near-black product stages alternate with pale neutral chapters. Real people, detailed interfaces and warm workspace photography provide the richness; the surrounding marketing layout stays sparse and direct.

### Essentials

- Big promises have a wide, unforced voice: Sharp Grotesk at 60px/1.25, weight 400 and normal tracking for the default opening; 50px/1.25 for major chapters.
- The opening is a centered production stage rather than a split sales banner: a 730px headline, short support line, two rows of translucent category pills, a 210×52px violet action and a 1060px-wide demonstration entering the first screen.
- Conversion controls are unmistakable capsules: #9671ff fills, white labels and fully rounded ends; compact editorial actions are 207×47px with 18px semibold text, while larger demonstration actions are 58px tall with 16px medium text.
- Color belongs to the work, not the whole canvas: near-black #111111 occupies long capability chapters, #f6f6f6 provides relief, and violet is concentrated in actions, small labels and a soft glow behind the opening object.
- Human output breaks the software grid: include an edge-clipped mixed-format gallery with portrait and landscape work, using 8px corners. If finished customer work is unavailable, show original sample outcomes with equally varied aspect ratios and real human context.
- Physical atmosphere anchors the digital tool: include a warm, full-bleed workspace scene carrying a centered closing promise at 56px/1.25 and a compact violet action. Without elaborate photography, use one well-lit practical workspace photograph with clear negative space for the text.
- Chapters switch scale deliberately: 1280px-wide demonstrations and 16px-gap card grids sit beneath spacious headlines, while quotes compress into a centered 777px column with 30px regular text.

## Typography

- **Sharp Grotesk** — Display headlines, major section headings and footer category titles. Broad, geometric grotesk with rounded bowls and a relaxed regular weight. If unavailable, use **Unbounded**. Use weight 400 for display; tune width and line breaks before changing size. Font stack: "Sharp Grotesk", "Unbounded", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Instrument Sans** — Body copy, actions, subheads, quotes and utility navigation. Open contemporary sans with a practical, friendly voice and readable small text. Font stack: "Instrument Sans", Arial, sans-serif.

- Keep the display face regular and broadly proportioned; hierarchy comes from width, size and line breaks rather than compressed tracking or heavy weight.

- The 800-weight pricing title is a localized contrast, not the default heading weight.

### Surfaces

#### Light canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default opening and light product chapters.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #1d1d1d`

**Observed:** Clean pale canvas with no enclosing page card.

**Transfer:** Let the full-width background continue beneath the opening media.

#### Studio stage · level 0

**Use:** Long capability and proof chapters.

**CSS:** `background: #111111; color: #ffffff`

**Observed:** Flat near-black field; major transitions can use the chapter entrance or chapter exit radius.

**Transfer:** Group related demonstrations into one continuous dark chapter rather than striping every row.

#### Relief canvas · level 0

**Use:** Light capability breaks and footer.

**CSS:** `background: #f6f6f6; color: #1d1d1d`

**Observed:** Subtle off-white rather than a strongly tinted gray.

**Transfer:** Use as a pause between dark demonstrations and as the utility destination.

**Use:** Atmosphere behind the opening media.

**Observed:** Broad flattened light spreads outside the demonstration; it does not become a solid violet panel.

**Transfer:** Center the light behind the object and clip it at the page edges.

**Use:** Short categories between opening support and action.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.45); border: 1px solid rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.3); border-radius: 36px; backdrop-filter: blur(5px); padding: 12px 20px`

**Observed:** Two centered wrapping rows, 44px tall, with small outlined square markers.

## Imagery and graphics

### Human product demonstration

**Use:** Opening object and dominant media throughout capability chapters.

**Observed:** Dark browser or editor interfaces surround close, naturally lit people. Panels include real transcripts, timelines, waveforms, participants and controls. The interface occupies substantially more area than the adjacent copy; floating UI details explain one action at a time.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's real interface with believable populated content. If no interface exists yet, create one coherent task demonstration with realistic data and a clear before-and-after, not a collage of generic dashboard widgets.

### Mixed-format output gallery

**Use:** Required proof device between broad promises and detailed capabilities.

**Observed:** Portrait clips, landscape interviews and square cover art share a horizontal centerline on the Studio stage. Different heights create an informal skyline, 8px corners keep it tidy, and work extends beyond both page edges.

**Transfer:** Use original output samples in three aspect ratios. Keep human subjects, varied crops and finished-looking captions or cover designs rather than identical generic cards.

**Build:** Use a horizontal flex track with 16px gaps and center alignment. Mix 200px-wide portrait panels, 416×232px landscape panels and square artwork; clip the track at viewport edges and repeat enough work to form a continuous gallery.

### Pale dot field and violet halo

**Use:** Required atmospheric backing for the default pale opening.

**Observed:** A very faint neutral micro-dot texture covers the pale stage. A flattened lavender light grows behind the lower interface, while category pills remain nearly white above it.

**Transfer:** Keep texture subordinate to the black promise and violet action. The halo must read as illumination, not a violet rectangle.

**Build:** Layer a low-contrast SVG dot pattern over white, then place the Opening violet glow behind the media. For a practical dot field, use 1px neutral dots with opacity around 0.08 on a 12px pattern; softly vary opacity with a mask. These dot dimensions are implementation guidance; preserve the nearly invisible texture.

### Warm physical workspace

**Use:** Required immersive brand moment and large closing scene; also supports product capability overlays.

**Observed:** Photoreal dark laptops, wood grain, soft window light, notebooks and working people provide warm brown and purple counterweights to the black interface. A top-down closing scene follows a person in headphones and a violet sweater across a long workspace image while a white two-line promise remains centered.

**Transfer:** Photograph a real place where the new product's work happens. Preserve tactile material, natural directional light and negative space around text. Minimum acceptable version: one high-quality workspace photo with a gentle dark overlay, the same centered promise and compact action.

### Orbit diagram

**Use:** Explain a central tool connected to several external destinations.

**Observed:** A dark central circle is surrounded by several thin dashed circular orbits, with small colored destination marks distributed at different radii. The diagram balances a right-hand benefit stack on black.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's real ecosystem marks or simple labeled nodes. Keep circles subdued and reserve saturated color for the nodes.

**Build:** Draw three concentric SVG circles with 1px white strokes at low opacity and a short dash pattern. Place a charcoal hub at the center, then distribute six small circular nodes along the paths. Build within a roughly 632×510px illustration area without adding a filled colored background.

### Technical portrait mesh

**Only for:** technical identity or transformation demonstrations

**Use:** Explain fidelity or technical integrity around a human subject.

**Observed:** A warm shoulder-up portrait fills the width; a subtle rectilinear background grid and white triangulated face-and-shoulder wireframe add a technical layer. Four Technical photo glass panels flank the face without obscuring it.

**Transfer:** Use a relevant subject or object and annotate only the areas the technology actually transforms. Avoid a futuristic neon character aesthetic.

**Build:** Dim a warm portrait with a translucent black layer. Overlay an SVG square grid with thin white lines at roughly 0.06 opacity and an irregular polygon mesh following the subject at roughly 0.15 opacity. Keep all lines fine and place glass cards in balanced pairs at the outer sides.

### Violet corner light

**Only for:** compact product closing bands and broadcast conversion panels

**Use:** Softly energize a short conversion panel without introducing a new color theme.

**Observed:** A localized violet light fades into charcoal; closing bands emphasize the lower-right corner, while an inset conversion panel lights from the left.

**Transfer:** Match the soft glow; exact stops are not essential. Keep most of the band dark so white text remains clear.

**Build:** Add an oversized blurred elliptical radial gradient behind the content, using rgba(150,113,255,0.5) near its center and transparent at the edge. Offset it below and to the right of the band, clip to the rounded boundary, and keep the action itself a flat violet fill.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small marketing icons are mostly 20×20px; navigation chevrons are filled 13×8px. Fine monochrome utility marks coexist with occasional colorful miniature pictograms in card titles and larger violet use-case symbols.

**Transfer:** Use simple literal icons at the text scale, with consistent optical weight within each group. Keep colorful symbols confined to examples, integrations or short benefit headings.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Centered production opening

**Observed:** A fixed navigation bar occupies the top 64px. The pale stage begins its centered content after 120px: a 730px-wide two-line black promise at 60px/1.25, then an 18px gap to a one-line 18px support statement. Two centered rows of Translucent category pill surfaces sit below, within a 700px group with 10px gaps. The Opening action follows after 32px. A dark 1060×596px Human product demonstration begins 40px beneath the action, over the Pale dot field and violet halo. Within the first 900px, the headline, support, category pills, action and upper part of the demonstration are visible; a narrow translucent logo strip crosses the bottom of the visible stage. The rest of the demonstration and the next dark chapter fall below the fold. Layer order is pale texture, halo, dark media, category/action content, then fixed navigation and the slim proof strip.

**Transfer:** Use a single concrete promise, a compact set of output categories and one dominant product object. Without production media, build a populated task demonstration with real-looking outputs and retain the mixed human/software emphasis. Focused product pages open on black or charcoal with a 56px/1.25 white promise, shorter support, one action or a matched sales pair, and an 810–1070px-wide demonstration. A comparison product can use two adjacent human results; a workflow product can show its interface against a warm workspace.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a left-copy/right-illustration hero, a giant decorative wordmark, or an all-violet canvas. Do not compress the demonstration to fit its entire height above the fold.

**Text style:** `display` — Sharp Grotesk 400 · 60px/1.25

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible demonstration → human/output proof → capability chapters → practical reassurance → a confident invitation → utility directory; depth and ordering vary with the buying task.

### Introduce a broad creative platform and make its breadth feel like one coherent tool

1. **Promise** — Pale centered production opening with category pills and a dominant demonstration.
2. **Product breadth** — Dark workflow overview with rail and synchronized media.
3. **Social proof** — Mixed-format output gallery.
4. **Capability chapters** — Light quality demonstration, dark automation chapter, immersive workspace editing scene, then distribution and live-workflow demonstrations.
5. **Human proof** — Centered portrait quotes interrupt detailed capability explanations.
6. **Business conversion** — Warm photographic panel with sales actions, small customer marks and a compact rating rather than giant vanity metrics.
7. **Closing call** — Long physical-workspace scene with an anchored two-line promise and action, then pale directory footer.

**Transfer:** For a broad platform, alternate breadth, tangible task proof and human reassurance; keep the physical closing scene as the emotional resolution.

### Explain a high-control real-time workflow while keeping the result approachable

1. **Promise** — Black centered opening with a white headline and demonstration.
2. **Capability proof** — Two-column gradient cards with large illustrative interface views.
3. **Workflow explanation** — Large verb-led rows explain the production cycle in one framed panel.
4. **Capability chapters** — Alternating customization and interaction demonstrations.
5. **Use cases and collaboration** — Violet-icon tiles, concentric capsule call-to-action motif and photographic role cards.
6. **Closing call** — Short conversion banner followed by the pale directory footer.
7. **Advanced capability appendix** — A final dark split demonstration with a selected control panel, supporting copy and paired actions appears after the footer.

**Transfer:** Use the continuous charcoal variant for control-heavy products. Place advanced detail near the relevant capability in a new page rather than treating the unusual post-footer appendix as a required ending.

### Introduce a new technology through a concrete human result and a short creation journey

1. **Promise and comparison** — Dark opening with a brief eyebrow, paired actions and adjacent human comparison media.
2. **Social proof** — Restrained monochrome customer marks.
3. **Capability chapters** — Two alternating interface rows followed by an annotated technical portrait scene.
4. **Use-case demonstration** — White rail-and-results chapter contrasts practical output variations.
5. **How it works** — Numbered rows beside a video walkthrough on black.
6. **Closing call** — Compact glowing invitation with paired actions, then pale footer.

**Transfer:** Prove the technology with an understandable result first; use the technical illustration only after its practical value is clear.

### Present an end-to-end workflow with enough commercial detail to support purchase

1. **Promise** — Dark centered opening with broad interface hero and subtle surrounding data imagery.
2. **Output proof** — Horizontal square-cover gallery.
3. **Capability chapters** — Alternating publishing and analytics demonstrations, then two promotional capability cards.
4. **Reassurance** — Small conversational support assurance and repeated action.

6. **Pricing** — Introduction plus two tall plan cards.
7. **Objection handling** — Narrow FAQ stack.
8. **Closing call** — Short violet-lit dark band with rounded bottom corners, followed by pale footer.

**Transfer:** For an integrated service, connect outcomes to workflow, pricing and practical objections without switching to a different commercial visual language.

### Explain a sophisticated tool to beginners and experienced users at once

1. **Promise** — Centered white promise over a warm workspace stage with a dominant editor demonstration.
2. **Social proof** — Mixed-format creator-work gallery.
3. **Capability definition** — Large heading and two-column demonstration cards.
4. **Breadth** — Light horizontal rows of small use-case chips.
5. **Depth and control** — Selected feature rail, detailed tool demonstrations and creator testimonial cards.
6. **How it works** — Numbered journey beside walkthrough media.

8. **Objection handling** — Dark FAQ stack with a repeated action.
9. **Learning support** — Two large tutorial thumbnails above three smaller editorial resources.
10. **Closing call** — Flat charcoal rounded-bottom invitation and pale directory footer.

**Transfer:** Balance automatic outcomes with visible manual control; move the dense feature inventory later, after visual proof establishes relevance.

## Writing

Confident, conversational and production-minded. Benefits sound achievable rather than futuristic; light wordplay makes complex software feel familiar.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Riverside name, logos, proprietary product names, customer identities, quotes, ratings, subscriber counts, prices, performance claims or other brand assets. Replace them with truthful content for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your work. Use we sparingly for service reassurance; name the product when explaining what it does.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are often two short clauses across two lines. Support usually takes one or two sentences; feature descriptions start with an action and finish with a practical result.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Describe an expanded capability or remove a familiar constraint, without technical preamble. | 5–11 words | “Create like you have a full production team”<br>“Camera off. You, fully on.” |
| Chapter headline | Pair a desirable standard with a disarming simplification. | 5–10 words | “Studio-level quality. No studio required.”<br>“Powerful editing. Purposefully simple.” |
| Feature headline | Short outcome, occasional conversational inversion or gentle pun. | 3–9 words | “Hours of editing, done in seconds.”<br>“All chats. One place.” |
| Supporting description | Start with a concrete action, explain the mechanism briefly and end with the user's advantage. | 15–40 words | “Get clear insights into your podcast performance, episode success, and audience behavior with built-in, simple analytics.” |
| Action | Plain verb phrase describing the immediate next step. | 2–3 words | “Start for Free”<br>“Book a Demo” |
| Closing promise | A compact invitation that contrasts more possibility with less effort. | 6–10 words | “Your all-in-one studio. Ready when you are.”<br>“More to say. Less to shoot.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** create; ready; your; all in one; built for; with ease; right in; look and sound; one place; no studio required

**Avoid:** revolutionary disruption; unlock limitless potential; synergize; next-generation paradigm; enterprise-grade in every headline; exclamation-heavy hype

**Rules:**

- Lead with the finished result; describe automation only after the reader knows what it saves or improves.
- Use parallel short phrases for related capabilities and reserve full sentences for explanation.
- Ground strong claims in visible demonstrations and truthful specifications.
- Use playful phrasing sparingly; keep pricing, instructions and answers literal.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 60px regular broad grotesk type for the default opening and 50px for major chapters.
- Keep a 1280px main content width and 16px default card gutters.
- Let product demonstrations exceed the visual area occupied by adjacent copy.
- Use 8px card corners and fully rounded capsule actions as distinct shape families.
- Keep dark-stage body copy #d2d2d2 beneath white headings.
- Include mixed-format human output proof with edge clipping rather than a uniform logo-only wall.
- Carry the warm physical-workspace device into the closing invitation.
- Set quotes at 30px regular in a centered 777px column.

### Don't

- Do not tighten the display into condensed, negatively tracked billboard type; the broad regular letterforms carry the tone.
- Do not spread #9671ff across every chapter background; it identifies actions and local illumination.
- Do not turn every card into a heavily shadowed floating panel; most depth comes from adjacent neutral values.
- Do not use 18px corners for ordinary feature cards; that softer shape belongs to pricing cards.
- Do not enlarge header links to match the 18px body lead; navigation remains 14px.
- Do not use the inactive #383838 rail color for normal paragraph copy on black.
- Do not turn the 16px-gap card grids into widely separated isolated tiles.
- Do not replace natural people and tactile workspaces with generic glossy three-dimensional software cubes.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Filled demonstration actions declare background-color transitions of 0.25s; compact editorial actions declare 0.3s transitions.
- Footer links declare color 0.4s cubic-bezier(0.68, -0.55, 0.265, 1.55).
- Continuous horizontal tracks declare slideLeft at 100s linear infinite and carousel at 120s linear infinite.
- The photographic closing promise uses sticky positioning within a much taller image scene.

**Transfer:**

- Use slow continuous horizontal movement for the edge-clipped work gallery, keeping individual examples readable.
- Tie selected workflow labels to the displayed demonstration rather than animating unrelated decoration.
- Keep the closing promise anchored while the physical environment provides the change of view.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll-trigger timelines, video behavior and most on-scroll easing are unspecified.
- A universal keyboard focus system is unspecified; retain visible accessible focus when implementing new controls.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width boundaries include 479px, 767px, 768px, 991px, 992px, 1149px and 1299px; the desktop composition uses both 1280px and 1160px max-width containers.

**Transfer:**

- Recommended adaptation: wrap the category pills, preserve the centered promise and retain the media as a single dominant object.
- Recommended adaptation: stack feature rails above their media, reduce multi-column grids progressively, and keep the mixed-format gallery horizontally clipped rather than shrinking every sample.
- Recommended adaptation: place technical side annotations below the subject when the central face or object would otherwise be obscured.

**Unknown:**

- Narrow-screen typography, navigation collapse behavior and exact breakpoint-specific spacing are unspecified.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
