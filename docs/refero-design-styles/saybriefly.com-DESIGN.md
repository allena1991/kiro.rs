# SayBriefly — pencil trails and relief

## Overview

An exuberant, hand-drawn software identity on warm paper, with forest-green lettering, lemon highlights and occasional lavender or aqua stages. Heavy, characterful grotesk headlines sit above airy explanations, playful editorial illustrations and tightly framed interface details. It suits productivity and creative tools that want to feel capable without sounding corporate; the overall pace is spacious, punctuated by dense comparison tables.

### Essentials

- Big promises sound like remembered conversations: Bricolage Grotesque 800 at 80px/1.05 for the opening, 72px/1.2 for major chapters and 60px/70px for supporting section leads, all with normal tracking.
- The opening is a lavender illustrated workspace, not a generic split hero: a centered two-line headline across about 1180px, a 768px support line, one glossy action, and dark-green pencil drawings curling around the lower edges.
- Buttons are tiny polished objects against flat paper: forest-green pill actions with lemon text, a translucent white top sheen, yellow inset edges and a soft green drop shadow; the opening action is 54px high.
- Color works as stationery: warm #fcfaf5 dominates the long body, #efd3fa fills the opening, #a8e5e5 backs the closing card, and #ffeb5b appears in marker strips, short highlights and small badges rather than a succession of saturated full-width bands.
- Human drawing interrupts software polish: include dark-green irregular line illustrations, looping connector trails and broad dry-marker swashes. Frame real product detail on cream or pale tinted paper; if there is no interface, use a simple illustrated before/after workflow in the same materials.
- The ending is an illustration-led encore: an aqua conversion card with 60px/64px, 0.1em-tracked lettering, then a ruled utility footer and a nearly full-width giant wordmark above an illustrated human scene. Recreate the scale and linework with the new identity, even when only a few original drawn objects are available.

## Typography

- **Bricolage Grotesque** — Hero, major section headings and closing call. Heavy, friendly grotesk with broad bowls and slightly eccentric letter shapes. Font stack: "Bricolage Grotesque", sans-serif.
- **Inter** — Body copy, action labels, quotes and secondary feature headings. Clean, compact contemporary sans; light paragraphs contrast with strong headings. Font stack: "Inter", sans-serif.
- **Source Sans 3** — Navigation labels. Open, humanist sans with a softer texture than the body family. Font stack: "Source Sans 3", sans-serif.
- **Roboto Mono** — Short comparison annotations and compact navigation promise. Unpretentious typewriter-like markings that echo labels on stationery. Font stack: "Roboto Mono", monospace.
- **Reenie Beanie** — Personal vignette paragraph. Loose handwritten script, large but lightly drawn. Font stack: "Reenie Beanie", cursive.

**Notes:**

- Normal tracking is the rule even at large sizes. The deliberately loose 0.1em tracking belongs to the closing card alone.
- Bricolage Grotesque carries the emphatic brand promises; Inter 600 carries detailed feature claims and the large quotation. Preserve that difference rather than assigning every heading one family.
- Body weight 300 leaves room for the very heavy headings. Do not turn long paragraphs into bold display copy.
- Marker strips can sit behind a short phrase or numeral in a heading; keep the phrase on a coherent line and the type legible.

## Imagery and graphics

### Connected pencil drawings

**Use:** Opening frame, capability chapters and visual transitions.

**Observed:** Dark forest linework with slight hand tremor depicts paper, calendars, pencils, task cards, people and small expressive objects. Most objects are unfilled or paper-filled; lemon and aqua appear as flat scribbled accents. Thin looping lines connect separate objects. In the opening, drawings sit around the outside edges and lead into a cream product panel at the bottom, leaving the headline clear.

**Transfer:** Draw a few recognizable objects from the new product's working world, linked by a wandering line. A minimum version is three original line objects, one expressive human gesture and a large looped connector around a small product example.

**Build:** Use SVG paths with forest strokes around 1–3px at display size, round joins and slightly irregular contours. Layer doubled contours selectively, add short hatch marks for shading, and leave interiors transparent or warm paper. Draw connector paths as long cubic curves with two or three loops; place the largest objects outside the central copy area.

### Dry-marker trails

**Use:** Chapter breaks, emphasized phrases and the underside of the testimonial.

**Observed:** Wide lemon and aqua loops sweep across the page; a forest zigzag about 814px wide sits below the quotation. Their edges are scuffed and internally streaked, not blurred. Headline highlights are flatter, irregular rectangular strokes fitted to a short phrase.

**Transfer:** Include a broad drawn trail and short marker emphasis as recurring visual punctuation. They should imply a hand moving through the page rather than a decorative geometric background.

**Build:** Create an SVG bezier loop or zigzag; repeat it as 8–15 slightly offset strokes, mixing widths around 2–10px to make a broad 30–60px dry band. Use lemon, aqua or forest ink at full color with small transparent breaks, a light turbulence/displacement filter and sparse paper-colored cuts. For text highlights use a shallow wavy polygon behind the words, extending 4–12px beyond the text edges.

### Stationery label strips

**Use:** Short benefit or audience labels above headlines and quotes.

**Observed:** A roughly cut horizontal colored strip carries small typewriter-like lettering. A dark square at the left contains a simple pale icon. Lemon dominates the default strips; aqua distinguishes the feature-led opening. Pricing presents a cluster of multicolored strips in two loose rows.

**Transfer:** Use a short useful label rather than decorative gibberish; keep it physically separate from the large headline.

**Build:** Use an SVG wavy-edged rectangular ribbon about 36–44px high with small paper-colored flecks. Overlay a 38–40px forest square with softly rounded corners at the left, a simple line icon, and a mono label. Keep the strip flat; no shadow or realistic tape gloss.

### Product detail on tinted paper

**Use:** Software proof adjacent to explanatory copy.

**Observed:** Cream interface panels sit inside pale yellow, mint or terracotta frames, often cropped to a meaningful conversation, alert or next step. Their styling shares dotted or dashed rules, small icons and hand-marked highlights. Paired panels can fill most of the content width; individual feature panels are about 596px wide.

**Transfer:** Show one credible product operation, not an entire illegible dashboard. Match the cream backing and restrained tint, and keep marketing typography independent of UI internals.

### Aqua closing illustration

**Use:** Right-hand half of the closing conversion card.

**Observed:** A roughly circular target-like character, leaning in perspective, is drawn in forest lines with small yellow pencil details, hatching, a solid ground shadow and sparse grass marks. It occupies about 480px by 451px beside the text.

**Transfer:** Create one expressive object that makes the benefit tangible; retain the irregular linework, minimal color and grounded silhouette. A simpler original tool or character is preferable to polished stock 3D.

**Build:** Construct an original main object from irregular SVG ellipses or rounded polygons, add forest contour hatching on one edge, a few lemon inserts and a flattened solid forest shadow beneath. Use a 480px by 451px viewBox and no photographic lighting.

### Monumental identity encore

**Use:** Final visual after the utility footer on every page type.

**Observed:** A 1408px by 780px composition nearly touches the viewport sides. A huge forest wordmark fills the upper third; below it, a looping line links paper and pencil objects above a ground-level gathering of people and animals. One broad lemon loop rises behind the figures. The paper background stays continuous.

**Transfer:** Render the new product name at a similarly monumental width, coupled to an original human scene. If detailed figure artwork is unavailable, use a small set of original line-drawn people and working objects across a baseline, but retain the oversized name, connector and lemon gesture.

**Build:** Use a 1408px by 780px responsive SVG stage with 16px side clearance at desktop. Fit a heavy grotesk wordmark to almost the full width in the top third; draw a forest looping connector through the middle and baseline figures in the lower third. Put one thick lemon loop behind the central figures. Keep negative space between the lettering and scene.

### Icons

**Observed:** Marketing icons use forest outlines or solid forest silhouettes, usually 20–28px. Interface-style outlines have round caps and 1.5–2px strokes. Header dropdown triangles are tiny and solid; carousel arrows are 14px with 2px round strokes. Hand-drawn editorial icons remain less regular than interface glyphs.

**Transfer:** Choose simple, friendly outline icons for controls and redraw a small set with irregular contours for the paper labels and illustrations. Do not mix elaborate multicolor symbols into ordinary navigation.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Illustrated lavender opening

**Observed:** The Glossy navigation beam floats near the top inside a 68px sticky header. A continuous Lavender stage fills the first screen. Centered below the header is a yellow Stationery label strip, then an 80px/1.05 Bricolage Grotesque 800 headline occupying roughly four-fifths of the width in two lines. A 768px-wide black hero-support paragraph sits 24px below it, followed by a centered 54px Primary button and a small helper link. Connected pencil drawings enter from both sides below the support copy, while a cream product panel begins at the bottom of the 900px viewport. Within that first screen the label, complete headline, support, actions, side drawings and upper portion of the product panel are visible; the full illustrated system and video band fall below.

**Transfer:** Default new marketing pages use this lavender, centered promise-and-drawing composition. Without existing artwork, create the minimum Connected pencil drawings scene around a focused product example. Pricing pages open on Paper canvas with a 60px/66px promise, label strip, short explanation, two rows of colored stationery labels and one action. Feature-led pages instead lead with an approximately 479px by 408px expressive line illustration, then category, aqua label and a 60px/60px headline; support can fall below the first fold.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the illustrated lower frame with a stock photo, put a dashboard beside the headline, or shrink the opening headline to ordinary section-heading scale.

**Text style:** `display` — Bricolage Grotesque 800 · 80px/1.05

**Text style:** `closing-heading` — Bricolage Grotesque 800 · 60px/64px · tracking 0.1em

### Pricing plans

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Human promise → visible product behavior → specific capability chapters → differentiated proof → lived outcome → social proof → conversion → illustrated identity encore.

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **promise** — Lavender illustrated opening with a direct memory-of-work promise and one action.
2. **product demonstration** — Dark-green wide video stage, then illustrated explanation and paired interface details.
3. **capability chapters** — Large statements alternate with small drawn metaphors, integration rows, an assistant interaction and text/media feature pairs.
4. **outcome** — A marker-emphasized contrast leads into a large drawn scene and narrow handwritten personal vignette.
5. **differentiated proof** — Outcome-based comparison matrix, followed by oversized customer testimony.
6. **closing call** — Aqua card, ruled utility footer and monumental illustrated identity.

**Transfer:** Use the same alternation of big human promises, small visible operations and expressive drawing without inheriting the original feature inventory.

### Pricing and purchasing decision

1. **promise** — Warm-paper opening lowers the perceived risk with a short value promise and colored label cluster.
2. **pricing** — Annual/monthly selector, three outlined plans, one yellow recommendation and enterprise strip.
3. **value comparison** — A highlighted cost contrast introduces a large illustration and handwritten outcome vignette.
4. **differentiated proof** — Comparison matrix and oversized customer quote.
5. **closing call** — Aqua card, subscription/legal footer and the full illustrated wordmark ending.

**Transfer:** Explain the actual charging model and limits early, then use the shared expressive proof and closing language rather than making a purely transactional grid.

### Focused capability landing page

1. **promise** — Large expressive line character above a category, aqua strip and centered feature headline.
2. **product demonstration** — One long aqua-and-cream gallery contains paired demonstrations and a full-width focused tool view.
3. **quantified proof** — A page-wide aqua loop and small line figure lead into a highlighted stat with a human interpretation.
4. **comparison** — Open matrix contrasts concrete capabilities.
5. **objection handling** — Dashed disclosure rows answer practical questions.
6. **social proof** — Illustrated outcome vignette, oversized customer quote and a compact guide-link cluster.
7. **closing call** — Aqua conversion card, utility footer and monumental illustrated identity.

**Transfer:** Start from the user's practical need, use a deeper grouped demonstration than the broad landing page and provide specific adoption answers.

## Writing

Warm, plain-spoken and lightly mischievous. Confidence comes from describing exactly what the product does and the small frustration it removes, not from grand technological language.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the SayBriefly or Scribbble names, logos, proprietary illustrations, competitor judgments, customer identities or quotations, prices, numerical claims or category-first claims. Replace them with the new product's own identity, substantiated facts and permissions-backed testimony. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to the reader as you and your; use we sparingly for integrations and reassurance. Refer to the product by name when explaining a concrete action.
- **Rhythm:** Short complete sentences and fragments alternate. Major headlines often pair a promise with a specific ordinary situation; support uses a few conversational clauses. Three-beat lists and plain repeated verbs recur; punctuation is restrained.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Specific benefit followed by an unexpectedly ordinary detail. | 8–14 words | “Remember every client meeting. Even the one from March.” |
| Pricing promise | Permission to begin, then a fair exchange in everyday language. | 7–12 words | “Start free. Pay when it earns its keep.” |
| Feature heading | Describe the finished state or relief, not the technology. | 3–9 words | “Today, sorted before you start.”<br>“Ask anything. Months later.” |
| Support | Concrete actions in a short sequence, ending in a human outcome. | 12–35 words | “Know what was said last time. Know what changed. Walk in prepared.” |
| Primary action | Literal, brief verb; no sales theatrics. | 1–2 words | “Download”<br>“Get started” |
| Human interpretation | Translate a measure into useful time or freedom, with a modest joke. | 8–15 words | “That's 3 projects done. Or one really good sabbatical.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** already; what changed; just ask; one place; ready; before; your; done; no card; when you need it

**Avoid:** revolutionize; unlock unparalleled potential; seamless synergy; game-changing AI; next-generation platform; supercharge everything

**Rules:**

- Attach a benefit to an observable action or artifact.
- Use everyday objects and moments to make abstract capabilities concrete.
- Reassure with actual limits and conditions rather than vague trust language.
- Make playful asides brief enough that the practical point remains dominant.
- Use sentence case for labels and headings; avoid a blanket all-caps technical voice.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the opening at 80px/1.05 and weight 800 on lavender, with a much narrower 768px support line.
- Use forest ink for ordinary text and dashed 1px rules.
- Keep the primary action glossy and pill-shaped; use 12px 24px padding in the opening and 16px 32px in broader conversion sections.
- Leave comparison cells transparent with 38px vertical padding.
- Frame product operations on cream paper and let a single meaningful detail dominate each panel.
- Give the closing card 56px insets, 16px corners and the unusual 0.1em heading tracking.
- Include hand-drawn loops, dry-marker strokes and an oversized illustrated identity ending.
- Use a 32px gap between the three pricing cards and emphasize only one card with lemon.

### Don't

- Do not set the whole site in black-on-white; forest lettering and warm paper are structural choices.
- Do not extend 0.1em tracking to ordinary 60px headings; it belongs to the closing card.
- Do not spread soft shadows over every content block; open paper and dashed rules organize most of the page.
- Do not use glossy pills for plan selection; plan actions have 11px corners and a flat 2px outline.
- Do not substitute a smooth blurred gradient for a dry-marker stroke; retain visible gaps and rough edges.
- Do not color every comparison cell yellow; highlight short answers only.
- Do not turn the aqua closing card into a dense centered pricing panel; keep text and one expressive object in dialogue.
- Do not end with a small legal strip alone; preserve the near-full-width identity artwork after it.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
