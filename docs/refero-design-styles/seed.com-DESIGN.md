# Seed — living science, domestic ritual

## Overview

A research-minded but tactile style that brings laboratory credibility into everyday life. Forest green and warm ivory alternate with softly lit domestic photography, translucent objects and organic scientific imagery. Light grotesk typography and small technical annotations keep spacious consumer pages approachable, while more documentary layouts suit research and professional audiences.

### Essentials

- An everyday scene carries the promise: a full-width 844px photographic opening places a 48px/1.1, weight 350 headline on the left and sharply rendered objects across the lower right; support is 16px/1.3.
- Headlines sound conversational rather than monumental: the default section lead is 48px/1.1 at weight 350 with -0.015em tracking; secondary leads are 40px/1.1 with -0.01em tracking.
- Navigation floats in separate capsules, not a full-width toolbar: a 56px-high left capsule and a compact right action cluster sit over the media; primary actions are 52px-high pills with 16px 24px padding.
- Green acts as architecture, not decorative confetti: deep #1c3a13 anchors the product shelf and footer, #fcfcf7 carries most editorial space, and vivid #d3fa99 is restricted to a 40px announcement strip and small 24px badges.
- A technical idea becomes a tangible exhibit: pair a translucent or exploded object with fine dashed callouts and a compact bordered result panel, on a 32px-radius frosted stage with 37.5px backdrop blur. Without a physical product, use a layered process model or material study rather than a generic dashboard.
- Organic lettering provides the memorable release: a wide ivory word sculpture sits over a green, cellular-textured closing field beneath the functional footer. Rebuild this device with an original short phrase and rounded, gently distorted letterforms; retain it even when no proprietary artwork is available.

## Typography

- **Seed Sans** — All headlines, prose, navigation and controls. Open, lightly weighted contemporary grotesk with a friendly rounded rhythm and compact headline spacing. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use variable weights 300–500; preserve light display weights and tune line breaks to the intended text measure. Font stack: "Seed Sans", "Inter", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif.

**Notes:**

- Consumer headings retain the same light weight as the supporting copy; hierarchy comes from scale, line breaks and breathing room rather than bold emphasis.
- The opening tightens tracking more than ordinary section headings at the same size. Keep their roles separate.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy at section scale, compact within labels and controls; substantial media blocks carry the visual weight.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **spacing step** 8px — Declared 8px, 16px, 24px, 32px and 48px spacing family; retain deliberate 12px, 28px, 50px and 100px exceptions.

### Containers and grids

- **marketing container**: max width 1440px · side padding 32px. Full-width bands with a centered inner grid; opening text uses a deeper 56px inset.
- **editorial grid**: 12 columns · gap 32px. Organize paired half-width images, split features and the footer; copy often spans fewer columns than its media.
- **product shelf**: 4 columns · gap 16px. Four equal 348px-wide tiles across the desktop; each places a 264px square object study centrally.
- **support measure**: max width 426px. Default opening support paragraph; visually subordinate to the image and short headline lines.
- **bookend pair**: 2 columns · gap 32px. Two 672px square media calls across the main container.
- **research container** (only for research narrative pages): max width 1440px · side padding 50px. 1340px-wide exhibits with inset two-column explanations.
- **advisor wall** (only for research narrative pages): 4 columns · gap 0px. 335px columns with 25px inner side padding and thin right dividers; portraits are tall and captions compact.
- **professional container** (only for professional audience pages): max width 1440px · gap 48px · side padding 48px. Equal media/text splits, four-column advisor gallery and broad left-copy/right-form closing.

### Surfaces

#### Ivory canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default consumer editorial field.

**CSS:** `background: #fcfcf7; color: #1c3a13`

**Observed:** Large continuous fields, with no box shadows.

**Transfer:** Let whitespace and real media create hierarchy.

#### Forest band · level 0

**Use:** Product presentation and full functional footer.

**CSS:** `background: #1c3a13; color: #fcfcf7`

**Observed:** Full-width dark anchors interrupt the light editorial canvas.

**Transfer:** Use a dark field for concentrated product choice or identity, not every section.

#### Leaf tile · level 1

**Use:** Product shelf backing.

**CSS:** `background: #3d5b34; border-radius: 24px; box-shadow: none`

**Observed:** Tonal green backing is applied behind a centered object, not as an outlined white card.

**Transfer:** Keep neighboring tiles materially consistent even when the products differ.

**Use:** Annotated technical feature staged over organic macro imagery.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(87, 94, 85, 0.35); backdrop-filter: blur(37.5px); border-radius: 32px; padding: 80px`

**Observed:** Broad green-gray translucent panel softens the detailed backdrop while the exploded object stays sharp.

**Transfer:** Place sharp labels and an object above a genuinely textured image, with texture still visible around the outside.

#### Model backing · level 1

**Use:** Large educational object study.

**CSS:** `background: #eeeee9; border-radius: 32px; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** Warm gray square carries a translucent pale-green anatomical model.

## Components

### Floating navigation

**Use:** Persistent compact brand and navigation cluster.

**CSS:** `height: 56px; max-width: 375px; border-radius: 9999px; background: rgba(87, 94, 85, 0.314); backdrop-filter: blur(17.1px); display: flex; align-items: center; gap: 16px; padding: 0px 32px`

**Text style:** `navigation` — Seed Sans 400 · 14px/1

**Observed:** Separate left navigation and right account/action capsules leave the center of the image open. Text reverses on dark fields; the first light scene presents the navigation without a conspicuous dark box.

**States:** Declared: hover color follows the section theme; focus-visible outline-offset 0.3ch and outline-style solid. Glass background, color and blur transition over 0.3s ease-in-out.

**Transfer:** Keep only a few short navigation nouns and let the media remain continuous behind them.

## Imagery and graphics

### Domestic product photography

**Use:** Default opening and supporting routine scenes.

**Observed:** Translucent dark-green and frosted jars sit on a reflective wood table in front of a softly blurred daylight window. The opening is nearly a whole screen of photography: products occupy the lower-right half, while pale upholstery and diffuse daylight provide left-side copy space. Detail scenes show hands, packaging and plants with warm natural skin and wood tones.

**Transfer:** Photograph the new product in a believable daily-use setting, with a restrained green cast, tactile reflections and deliberately empty bright space for text. For nonphysical products, construct a photographed workspace or a material metaphor tied to the actual task.

### Translucent technical exhibit

**Use:** Signature mechanism explanation and educational feature.

**Observed:** A glossy green capsule is separated vertically into shell and granular contents against a blurred gray-green panel; small white labels connect through short dashed leaders. A related visual uses a pale translucent human profile with branching green structures on a warm-gray square. Both mix scientific explanation with soft, almost botanical materiality.

**Transfer:** Use a cutaway or exploded structure that explains the new product. Minimum acceptable version is a clean vector sectional object with translucency, grain or inner layers and two precise callouts.

**Build:** For a vector fallback, draw two vertically separated rounded shell paths in forest green with translucent ivory highlights; place a dense stippled interior between them. Back the assembly with oversized overlapping green translucent circles and capsule shapes, blur only that background, and overlay the Frosted exhibit. Keep 70px dashed ivory leaders with 1px strokes and small uppercase labels outside the object.

### Organic closing word sculpture

**Use:** Signature full-width release after functional footer content.

**Observed:** A short ivory phrase appears as thick, irregular, softened connected lettering across roughly two thirds of the page width. Beneath is a dense green microscopy-like texture: rounded cell islands, branching edges and scattered dark dots with a muted olive-green veil. The reveal occupies about 316px below a 629px functional footer.

**Transfer:** Create an original two- or three-word emotional coda with the same liquid, living contour. Minimum version is a custom SVG phrase with rounded joins and subtly warped outlines, over an original procedural cellular field.

**Build:** Create an SVG word group with heavy rounded custom letter paths, fill warm ivory, and gently displace its outline using low-frequency feTurbulence plus feDisplacementMap; keep counters open. For the backdrop, cluster several sizes of dark-green circles on a pale green field, vary opacity and density using noise masks, soften cluster edges slightly, and overlay rgba(59,82,40,0.48). Center the lettering with ample top and bottom air; place a small copyright line below.

### Human stories rail

**Use:** Consumer social proof and editorial texture.

**Observed:** Wide documentary interview videos use warm human faces and domestic environments, with an adjacent slide peeking in. A second rail mixes tall hand-held product videos, a pill-shaped horizontal still and a restrained press quote card; media corners are softened, not all forced into identical rectangles.

**Transfer:** Commission candid use stories and detail photography with consistent natural light. Mix portrait, wide and capsule-shaped frames while keeping text overlays sparse.

### Research diagrams and microscopy

**Only for:** research narrative pages

**Use:** Evidence-led full-width exhibits and paired technical explanations.

**Observed:** Large rectangular scientific density plots and cool aqua microscopic cell imagery are followed by circular green contour fields, circular genome-style diagrams and a sparse row of labeled vessels. Color intensity belongs inside the scientific figure rather than the surrounding page.

**Transfer:** Use genuine evidence graphics with captions when available. For an explanatory diagram, draw only the stages or measurements that the product can substantiate.

**Build:** For a conceptual contour illustration, clip irregular nested SVG paths inside a 470px circular pale-green field, deepen forest-green opacity toward two unequal centers and leave an off-center bridge between them. For process diagrams, use fine forest outlines, flat translucent fills and dotted connectors; label below each vessel or stage. Do not present synthetic contours as measured data.

### Advisor portraits

**Only for:** research and professional pages

**Use:** Credibility through identifiable people.

**Observed:** Natural-color, vertically cropped individual portraits in real outdoor, office and lab settings; no monochrome filter or decorative cutout. Small names and role captions sit directly below. Research pages use thin column dividers, while professional pages use 300px by 450px portraits with 48px gutters.

**Transfer:** Use real experts in varied but credible environments, standardize aspect ratio and caption position rather than forcing identical backdrops.

### Professional split film

**Only for:** professional audience pages

**Use:** Audience-specific immersive opening.

**Observed:** Equal halves pair aerial forest/river imagery with a close-up of human hands in a care context. An ivory headline spans the lower portion across both halves; a pale announcement band stays above.

**Transfer:** Pair the broader system or environment with its human consequence, using footage of equal tonal weight and no intervening border.

### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly small functional arrows, plus signs and play controls: 13–16px marks with 1–1.5px strokes, butt caps and forest or ivory paint. Larger inline footer symbols interrupt the mission statement like pictorial words rather than sitting in colored icon boxes.

**Transfer:** Choose spare line symbols with consistent stroke weight. Use small arrows to finish links, fine plus/minus controls in ruled rows, and a few subject-specific outline glyphs inside the footer statement.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Domestic product opening

**Observed:** A 40px Signal lime Announcement strip sits above an 844px edge-to-edge domestic photo with softly rounded lower corners. Floating navigation overlays its top edge in separate left and right clusters. The photograph is the back layer, pale left-side negative space protects forest text, and large jars sit sharply across the lower-right half. A three-line Opening-headline occupies approximately the left third, with a 426px support measure 16px below and actions 28px farther down. A Primary button and understated arrow link share a row. Within the first 900px, the announcement, navigation, complete headline, support, both actions and the full product group are visible; only a narrow hint of the dark next band appears below.

**Transfer:** Create an equally intentional photographic or rendered scene around the new product, preserving bright copy space on the left and a substantial tangible object group on the right. If no object exists, stage a relevant workspace or layered material metaphor rather than placing an isolated interface on a blank gradient. Research pages instead open with a 32px thesis on white and abundant empty space; professional campaigns use Professional split film with the 108px overlay headline.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the scene with a centered giant slogan, abstract neon glow or a small product card under a generic navigation bar.

**Text style:** `opening-headline` — Seed Sans 350 · 48px/1.1 · tracking -0.025em

### Product shelf heading pair

**Observed:** On Forest band, a 48px Section-lead sits left while a short Body explanation and small arrow link occupy the right. Four Product shelf tiles follow with 16px gutters and matching object scale; the section uses 56px vertical padding.

**Transfer:** Use for a compact set of alternatives or capabilities, keeping the header asymmetrical and the repeated units visually equal.

### Split routine feature

**Observed:** Rounded section transition follows a dark field. Left copy stacks Status badge, Secondary-lead, Body and Primary button at 24px intervals. Right media combines one wide product still with three smaller routine details below; the main grid gutter is 32px.

**Transfer:** Explain a combined offer or workflow with a primary staged image and supporting moments, rather than more text cards.

### Annotated mechanism stage

**Observed:** Frosted exhibit floats within 32px side insets over full-width organic macro imagery. Left column uses Exhibit-label, Secondary-lead and Result panel; the right carries a 400px object animation with two external callouts. Fine dashed leaders keep the annotation visually lightweight.

**Transfer:** Make a real mechanism legible through an exploded model, with one specific outcome and just enough labels to orient the viewer.

### Educational split

**Observed:** Ivory canvas pairs a vertically centered 48px lead, brief paragraph and capsule action on the left with a large Model backing square on the right. Small technical wayfinding sits toward the lower left, separate from the text stack.

**Transfer:** Give one unfamiliar concept a visual model and a low-friction way to explore it.

**Transfer:** Develop a sequence of method, validation or production topics. Vary the exhibit geometry while repeating the header so long-form detail feels navigable.

### Future-facing circular gallery

**Only for:** research narrative pages

**Observed:** A repeated 48px light headline spans beyond the viewport like a ticker. Five 223px circular images sit in one row with compact labels below; a short text block and arrow link return to the left margin beneath.

**Transfer:** Use to widen the frame from the core product to adjacent applications. Preserve the moving-line/circular-exhibit contrast without borrowing the same subject areas.

### Professional resources and conversion

**Only for:** professional audience pages

**Observed:** The professional route uses a 72px product introduction beside large product media and Ruled accordion, then a white partner-access split, Resource band, four-portrait credibility gallery, and a broad organization introduction. The ending pairs a left-aligned human-support statement with Lead form on white, followed by a low 98px legal-gray strip rather than the consumer footer and coda.

**Transfer:** For specialized audiences, prioritize qualification, technical reassurance and human contact. Keep the functional legal ending specific to this conversion route.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Everyday promise → product or mechanism explanation → documented credibility → wider context → a next step suited to the audience.

### Consumer product discovery and routine selection

1. **Promise** — Photo-led domestic opening with short health-routine headline and paired actions.
2. **Product choice** — Dark tonal shelf of equally staged alternatives.
3. **Practical value** — Light split feature combining a staged offer with routine detail imagery.
4. **Mechanism proof** — Frosted technical exhibit, exploded model and compact multiplier panel.
5. **Education** — Short concept explanation beside a translucent model.
6. **Social proof** — Interview carousel followed by mixed-format human and editorial stories.
7. **Next step** — Two atmospheric square media invitations.
8. **Brand closure** — Useful forest footer and organic word-sculpture reveal.

**Transfer:** Let the new product determine which proof and selection blocks are useful, while preserving the alternation of real-world use, tangible science and human experience.

### Long-form method and trust building

1. **Thesis** — Light 32px introductory prose on white, followed by a small section marker and short summary.
2. **Process chapters** — Numbered ruled headers alternate wide exhibits, diagram/text rows and testing accordion.
3. **Expert credibility** — Introductory heading pair and extensive four-column portrait wall.
4. **Wider potential** — Repeating headline and circular application gallery.
5. **Conversion** — Full-width dark product packaging photograph with a modest side-aligned prompt.
6. **Brand closure** — Shared forest footer and cellular word-sculpture field.

**Transfer:** Use as a research or craftsmanship route: expand the substantiation, not the sales pressure, and conclude by returning to a concrete product invitation.

### Professional audience education and qualified contact

1. **Audience promise** — Split environmental/human film under a large bottom-aligned headline and pale fixed announcement.
2. **Mission and invitation** — Tinted horizontal mission band with outlined contact action.
3. **Product reassurance** — Large product media and 72px introduction beside ruled benefit disclosures.
4. **Enablement** — Partner-access split and subtle resource band with two educational/support offers.
5. **Authority** — Four portraits, compact credentials and a show-more link.
6. **Organization context** — Broad introduction paired with a scientific/environmental collage.
7. **Qualified conversion** — Human-support copy and restrained lead form.
8. **Compliance closure** — Low light-gray legal strip; no consumer navigation footer.

**Transfer:** Adapt for expert buyers or partners by surfacing access, credentials and real support before asking for contact information.

## Writing

Confident, research-literate and unusually human for a science-led brand. Plain-language outcomes lead, then concrete mechanisms, qualifications and caveats earn the claim.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Seed names, product codes, logos, slogans, proprietary illustrations, member quotations, scientific claims, trial outcomes or numerical results. Source wording below demonstrates form and tone only; replace it with substantiated facts for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use you and your for consumer outcomes; we and our for methods and responsibility. Professional language speaks directly to the reader's practice and the people they support.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are complete conversational thoughts, commonly 5–12 words with purposeful line breaks. Consumer support is one concise sentence; technical explanations can become denser paragraphs without turning the headlines into jargon. Exclamation marks are absent from the key messaging.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A meaningful everyday improvement, followed by a phrase tying it to the underlying system. | 8–12 words | “A life-changing health routine, built for your microbiome.” |
| Editorial lead | A short reframing of the reader or the scale of the problem. | 5–9 words | “You are more than human.”<br>“Whole body health starts in the gut.” |
| Mechanism headline | Name a common limitation, then contrast it with a specific product behavior. | 8–12 words | “Most probiotics don't survive digestion—DS-01® does.” |
| Research thesis | State a standard, then explain the collaboration or method that makes it credible. | One substantial opening paragraph | “The science of probiotics demands precision.” |
| Professional reassurance | A direct human assurance with a clear service role. | 5–8 words | “Real clinicians, here to support.” |
| Action | Plain verb plus object when needed; curiosity actions stay short. | 2–5 words | “Take the Quiz”<br>“Read More”<br>“View Taxonomy” |
| Signup invitation | A specialist topic plus a warm, slightly nerdy description of what arrives. | One short sentence | “Science with [Brand]—nerdy reads for your inbox.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** built for; designed for real results; clinically studied; validated; precision; research-backed; care; support; whole body; discover; R+D

**Avoid:** miracle; magic cure; biohack everything; crush your goals; revolutionary without explanation; corporate superlatives; urgent countdown language

**Rules:**

- Translate technical language immediately or place it alongside a clear visual explanation.
- Put the reader's benefit before the technical process in consumer copy; reverse the emphasis for expert documentation.
- Keep labels utilitarian: a short product identifier, a benefit name, a place credit or a figure number.
- Tie every quantitative result to a defined outcome and place the necessary caveat nearby.
- Balance institutional confidence with small moments of warmth; do not turn every line into a joke.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening headline at 48px, weight 350 and 1.1 line height with deliberately short line breaks.
- Use 32px editorial insets and 32px gutters as the main desktop framework.
- Make primary consumer actions 52px-high fully rounded pills with 16px 24px padding.
- Let deep forest bands anchor a mostly ivory reading experience and restrict lime to slim strips and chips.
- Use the 32px-radius frosted stage for one clear mechanism, with sharp foreground labels over a genuinely textured backdrop.
- Retain fine 1px separators and 12px annotations when developing research content.
- Preserve a custom organic word sculpture as a memorable brand device, using original language and contours.
- Stage real faces at generous media scale and keep their captions visually small.

### Don't

- Do not turn the 108px professional campaign typography into the default consumer heading size.
- Do not use weight 700 to make ordinary section headings prominent; their identity depends on weight 350 and scale.
- Do not replace tonal green product shelf tiles with white cards and floating drop shadows.
- Do not use lime as the main button fill; forest and ivory actions carry conversion.
- Do not spread backdrop blur across every panel; the distinctive blur belongs to navigation capsules and the technical exhibit.
- Do not force the mixed human-story rail into a uniform three-card feature grid.
- Do not give research portraits circular avatars; the credibility wall uses tall natural-color photographs.
- Do not inflate the compact 32px multiplier treatment into a row of giant KPI tiles.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Navigation glass declares background, text color and backdrop-filter transitions of 0.3s ease-in-out.
- Accordion headers declare padding-bottom 0.25s.
- Some visual surfaces declare transform 0.3s linear; a repeating research heading declares a 20s linear infinite animation.
- The closing textured field is fixed behind the functional footer, creating a reveal as the document ends.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the functional footer moving over a separate closing-art layer so the coda feels uncovered rather than merely appended.
- Use the continuous heading treatment only for the wider-context research gallery; keep action and navigation feedback restrained.

**Unknown:**

- Scroll-trigger timing, object-video choreography and exact image animation sequencing are not defined.
- The repeating heading's travel distance and mobile behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles include width breakpoints at 400px, 480px, 600px, 719px, 896px, 960px, 1024px and 1440px, with additional route-specific values.

**Transfer:**

- As a design adaptation, stack copy and media when the 426px support measure and large object can no longer coexist; preserve clear image space behind the headline.
- Retain the product shelf's equal object scale through horizontal scrolling or stacked tiles rather than shrinking four columns into unreadable cards.
- Keep the organic coda fluid in width using SVG artwork; do not substitute plain small text.

**Unknown:**

- Actual narrow-screen compositions, navigation collapse and typography scaling rules are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
