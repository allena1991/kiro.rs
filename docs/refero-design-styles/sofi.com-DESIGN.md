# SoFi — luminous connected frontiers

## Overview

A cinematic technology style that pairs near-black stages with white interludes and cool blue light. Medium-weight geometric sans typography and compact explanations give complex platforms an approachable, assured presence. Floating interface compositions, luminous orbital forms and blue-lit human photography keep the spacious layout from feeling purely corporate. It suits technical platforms and enterprise products that need both capability and human credibility.

### Essentials

- A centered promise floats inside a cinematic environment rather than above a conventional illustration: a 749px-tall blue atmospheric opening, a roughly 697px text measure, 41.6px medium headline and one white action.
- Headlines feel conversational, not monumental: TT Norms 500, 41.6px/1.1 for the opening, 34.7px/1.3 for section leads, and tightly negative tracking; tiny 10.4px uppercase labels supply the contrast.
- Controls are small rounded rectangles, not capsules: 6.9px button corners, 13.9px padding, 13.9px medium labels, with solid white or black and 2px outlined variants.
- Color arrives as environments, not a rainbow of cards: near-black and white carry most reading areas; deep blue, pale blue and cyan light occupy bounded hero, proof and demonstration stages. Bright blue marks links and selected tabs, while violet stays in technical art and specialized support sections.
- Product imagery is a layered object: overlapping dark interfaces or frost-blue panels, an orbital violet line or paired luminous connectors, and spacious backing. Every new landing page needs this connected-light device; use simplified UI planes and SVG connectors when finished product imagery is unavailable.
- Proof and conversion get their own sculptural staging: a 494px-wide deep-blue ruled statistics card contrasts with a pale lavender editorial card, while an opaque 863px dark conversion panel sits over a full-width luminous dot field. Carry both the contrasted proof treatment and the dotted-light conversion device into new marketing content.

**Notes:**

- Use medium rather than bold display type; bold is reserved for compact footer group labels.
- The default body is markedly smaller than section headlines. Preserve the contrast between 13.9px explanations, 27.8px split headings and 34.7px section headings.
- Heading tracking grows more negative with size; uppercase eyebrows instead hold 1px positive tracking. Keep sentence case on headings and actions.
- Fluid sizes and tracking follow their scales formulas on desktop; do not scale the whole page as a bitmap.

## Imagery and graphics

### Orbital atmosphere

**Use:** Signature opening environment and luminous conversion staging.

**Observed:** The opening is an immersive dark teal-blue space with a broad cyan light arc along the edges, soft cloudy light and a small translucent sphere near the lower edge. In other moments the scene contains glossy abstract objects. The text occupies the calm upper-center area; artwork fills the whole stage rather than a separate illustration slot.

**Transfer:** Use abstract light and one simple product-related object; a still atmospheric composition is a sufficient minimum. Keep the central text area dark and low-detail.

**Build:** On a deep-blue stage, layer a near-black translucent center over two oversized elliptical outlines partly outside the left and right edges. Render the outlines with rgba(74,143,232,0.6) and rgba(80,220,240,0.5), blur 30px–60px, and add a low-opacity radial mist. Place a roughly 170px translucent sphere near the lower left with an inner cyan rim. Match the soft glow; exact stops are not essential.

### First screen: Atmospheric promise opening

**Observed:** A full-width 749px blue atmospheric stage sits behind the Floating navigation. Back-to-front layers are the Orbital atmosphere, centered white copy and Primary button, then navigation. The two-line display headline takes roughly half the page width; its final audience word occupies a centered line of its own. A 697px-wide support paragraph sits about 20.8px below, then the compact action. The headline begins around the upper quarter of the stage; the large open lower half lets the light environment breathe. A bottom trust rail uses a thin pale separator, small claim at left and six white logos across the right. Within the first 900px the complete hero, action and trust rail are visible, plus the beginning of the next dark introduction. The full product scene belongs below the opening rather than competing with the centered promise.

**Transfer:** Use the same centered promise, compact explanation and single action over a new orbital light composition, with an original trust signal at the base. When no cinematic object exists, the glow, mist and simple sphere provide the first-screen artwork. Product-detail openings instead use the Product opening field: left-aligned display and dark action facing a 559px-wide frosted product scene in a 635px stage. Recruitment openings use the blue geometry at left and white promise at right; manifesto openings center the company-display over dark blue architectural art.

**Avoid:** Do not turn the default opening into a white split SaaS hero with a giant 80px slogan, many badges and several competing actions. Do not put high-detail UI directly behind the central text.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** promise → system reveal → credibility → capability or human chapters → focused next step → deep directory

### Broad platform marketing landing

1. **Promise** — Centered cinematic opening with trust rail.
2. **System reveal** — Dark text-and-interface platform introduction.
3. **Credibility** — Staggered lavender editorial proof and deep-blue metrics.
4. **Human connection** — White narrative interval with cool circular photographs.
5. **Capability exploration** — Dark icon grid joined to light tabbed demonstrations.
6. **Focused promotion** — Small product object over a dotted violet wave.
7. **Brand depth** — Full-width cinematic video scene with centered heading.
8. **Closing call** — Dark card over dotted blue light, then deep footer directory.

**Transfer:** Balance promise, proof and tangible exploration; preserve the signature devices but adapt the sequence to the new offering.

### Specific enterprise product explanation

1. **Promise** — Pale-blue split hero with frosted product composition.
2. **Product demonstration** — Centered dark system explanation and large interface.
3. **Social proof** — Deep-blue quotation with portrait attribution.
4. **Capability chapters** — One dark inset series explains challenge, modular mechanism and solution with alternating media.
5. **Further reading** — Three editorial cards on white.
6. **Closing call** — Compact product-image invitation band into shared footer.

**Transfer:** Use a narrower technical story with enough detail to resolve adoption concerns.

### Product family breadth and benefits

1. **Promise** — Pale-blue split opening with conceptual connected-object graphic.
2. **Breadth** — Large blue vocabulary ribbons on deep blue.
3. **Problem framing** — Pale gradient report panel with heading opposite argument and download.
4. **Capability chapters** — Continuous dark alternating text and media sections, divided by thin rules.
5. **Closing call** — Short image-and-invitation band, then footer directory.

**Transfer:** Use the vocabulary texture to communicate range, then explain a few distinct outcomes in detail.

### Recruitment and culture storytelling

1. **Promise** — Blue circle-and-orbit opening with right-aligned narrative column.
2. **Credibility** — Mission explanation beside a giant gradient percentage and badge.
3. **Human proof** — Pale panel with employee quote carousel.
4. **Benefits** — Dark triptych of oversized line pictograms.
5. **Reach** — Dotted geographic map and centered place names.
6. **Principles** — Pale split panel with blue vertically masked statements.
7. **Closing call** — Filterable opportunities and department rows into shared footer.

**Transfer:** Move from purpose to lived experience to actionable opportunities; retain the people-first variant rather than using sales-page claims.

### Company credibility and explanation

1. **Manifesto** — Centered white statement over dark blue architectural light.
2. **Quantified proof** — Light band with blue-gradient ruled numbers.
3. **Organizational pillars** — Dark triptych of product, people and platform imagery.

## Writing

Assured technical fluency with a warm invitation to build together. Benefits lead, while cloud, API and operational language give the promises substance.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand's names, logos, proprietary artwork, customer marks, product names, awards, account totals, employee counts, geographic claims, certification claims or quotations. Replace every assertion with original, supportable material for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your; use we for the partner who enables progress. Product names appear as concrete mechanisms rather than as the subject of every sentence.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are usually short benefit statements or paired clauses. Support expands into one compact paragraph, sometimes two in technical chapters. Actions are brief and plain; exclamation marks are not part of the voice.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Future-facing outcome, expressed simply and often with the reader's organization in view. | 6–16 words | “Future-ready scalability for your bank.”<br>“Expanding the financial frontier with” |
| Section heading | A compact contrast, question or benefit that introduces a specific explanation. | 4–9 words | “One platform, tailored experiences.”<br>“What can we achieve together?”<br>“Customers expect more. Deliver more.” |
| Capability heading | Lead with the result and name the mechanism in natural language. | 5–10 words | “Help customers access their pay sooner.”<br>“Boost profitability with direct deposit.” |
| Support | Explain what enables the outcome, balancing technical adjectives with human consequences. | 25–55 words | “A next-generation, cloud-native core banking platform designed to accelerate innovation, enhance customer experiences and boost operational efficiency.” |
| Primary action | An approachable conversation or direct exploration verb. | 2–4 words | “Let's talk”<br>“Explore our platform”<br>“View open positions” |
| Closing invitation | Return to the opening ambition with an active next step. | 6–13 words | “Start expanding your financial frontier.”<br>“Let’s make deposits work smarter for you and your customers.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; launch; scale; modernize; enable; together; cloud-native; API-first; secure; seamless; customer experience

**Avoid:** disrupt everything; 10x magic; crush the competition; frictionless as an unsupported blanket claim; dense acronym chains; playful slang in technical explanations

**Rules:**

- State an outcome before explaining architecture.
- Use paired adjectives selectively, then name the practical mechanism behind them.
- Keep proof labels short enough to sit under a number.
- Make technical chapters specific without turning the marketing page into API documentation.
- Use inclusive questions to introduce human partnership, not as a substitute for a clear opening promise.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening at 41.6px/1.1 and weight 500, with a compact centered support block.
- Preserve 43.2px desktop outer gutters around the white navigation and inset stages.
- Use 6.9px corners for default actions and 13.9px for broad dark panels.
- Separate the 13.9px body from 34.7px section headings with about 20.8px of stack spacing.
- Give interfaces shallow layering and blue-violet connectors inside approximately 434px feature scenes.
- Pair a pale qualitative proof card with a deep-blue vertically ruled quantitative treatment.
- Keep the dotted-light conversion backing outside an opaque centered dark panel.
- Use four compact footer columns and a fine 0.5px legal separator.

### Don't

- Do not replace medium geometric headings with heavy 700-weight display typography.
- Do not turn the default rectangular buttons into 9999px pills.
- Do not elevate every card with shadows; the consistent CSS shadow belongs to navigation.
- Do not fill each capability with a separate bright card; white copy shares a near-black stage.
- Do not recolor all body text blue; luminous blue belongs to links, selected controls and scoped display devices.
- Do not expand the small uppercase labels into oversized section titles; keep the 10.4px contrast.
- Do not use warm lifestyle photography or white cutout portraits where the layout calls for cool silhouetted human scenes.
- Do not apply the purple newsletter band, dense support grid or recruitment principles treatment indiscriminately to ordinary product pages.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The opening pairs video with a changing audience word; trust logos and product vocabulary occupy clipped horizontal tracks.
- Product tabs declare background 0.2s, color 0.2s transitions. Footer links declare opacity 0.25s and hover opacity 0.75.
- Navigation category labels declare color 0.2s cubic-bezier(1, 0, 0.5, 1). Opportunity rows declare border-color 0.2s, background 0.2s.
- People proof has three testimonial indicators; the active dot is 9px white and inactive dots are 7px at 30% white. Principles use a masked vertical text sequence.

**Transfer:**

- Keep changes localized: a selected tab recolors while its demonstration changes; do not animate every paragraph.
- A static orbital atmosphere must retain the same dark text-safe center and edge lighting when video is unavailable.
- Preserve clipped tracks for vocabulary and a single emphasized line for principles rather than exposing every repeated item at once.

**Unknown:**

- Video loop timing, word-change cadence, ticker speed, carousel autoplay behavior and scroll choreography are unspecified.
- Focus-visible appearance is not established for the shared action system.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare width breakpoints at 376px, 428px, 429px, 768px, 1024px, 1025px, 1200px, 1660px and 1661px.
- Desktop typography, many spacings and corners use viewport formulas; the product selector explicitly allows wrapping.

**Transfer:**

- For a narrow adaptation, stack text and product scenes while preserving their order of explanation and the dark/light chapter grouping.
- Keep the orbit and dot field as clipped background layers, not content that forces horizontal overflow.
- Reduce proof-card staggering when stacking; preserve lavender-versus-deep-blue contrast. Let the directory and support columns become sequential groups.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen compositions, navigation behavior and breakpoint-specific type overrides are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
