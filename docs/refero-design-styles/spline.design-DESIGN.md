# Spline — a stage for making

## Overview

A black-box workshop with soft charcoal interfaces, tightly fitted grotesk headlines and small technical mono labels. Color arrives through playful dimensional scenes and finished work rather than through the page furniture. The feeling is capable, collaborative and hands-on, suited to creative tools and technical products with something tangible to demonstrate; spacious chapters surround compact, useful detail.

### Essentials

- Headlines feel like editable objects, not posters: Geist 450, 48px/56px in the centered opening and 56px/64px for chapters, both tracked -0.06em; bright first statements hand off to #8a8a8a continuations.
- The opening is an invitation to make something: a compact two-line promise above a 680×116px translucent charcoal composer, a row of 36px suggestion pills, and a 1320px-wide product stage entering the bottom of the first 900px.
- Actions are pale little capsules on a black workbench: #eeeeee on the primary fill, black lettering, 44px height, 20px side padding and fully rounded ends; outline companions stay black with a 1px #2e2e2e edge.
- Color lives inside the work: keep the canvas, navigation, typography and nearly all cards neutral; reserve blue #8eb2f2 for selection or technical cues and let a contained scene carry the concentrated warm or pastel color.
- Show a real tool and a delightful result: pair charcoal application frames with a dimensional demonstration in 16px-rounded stages. When no interactive scene exists, use a polished static rendering of a real output inside a credible interface, never an empty stage.
- Small workshop markings and live progress make the page specific: 12px/16px uppercase Geist Mono with 0.06em tracking labels chapters; include a stack of 270×54px progress capsules over a faint dot field, with one bright active row and dim neighboring rows.
- Scale comes from pauses, not giant letters: use 256px chapter lead-ins on the main marketing treatment, with 16px between labels and headings and compact 14px/20px detail beneath. The directory and customer-story variants are denser and remain scoped alternatives.

## Typography

- **Geist** — Default marketing headings, paragraphs, actions and navigation. Compact contemporary grotesk; light-medium display weight and unusually tight headline fitting. Font stack: "Geist", "Helvetica Neue", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Geist Mono** — Chapter labels, numbered steps, directory categories and footer headings. Small regular monospace with widely spaced uppercase labels. Font stack: "Geist Mono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", Menlo, monospace.
- **Spline Sans** (only for customer-story pages and product-ui) — Customer editorial treatment and application interface lettering. Rounded, friendly sans with open forms and less aggressive fitting than the marketing grotesk. Font stack: "Spline Sans", system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif.

- Display fitting is consistently -0.06em at 48px, 56px and 64px; do not turn this into bold type. The opening is smaller than the chapter headings.

### Surfaces

#### Canvas · level 0

**Use:** Continuous page background.

**CSS:** `background: #000000; color: #eeeeee`

#### Charcoal stage · level 1

**Use:** Illustration stages, output demonstrations and quote cards.

**CSS:** `background: #101010; border-radius: 16px`

**Observed:** Low tonal separation, no lifted drop shadow; media stages can add an inset #2e2e2e rim.

**Transfer:** Separate work from the black page using tone and fine edges rather than brighter panels.

#### Application frame · level 1

**Use:** Credible product demonstrations.

**CSS:** `background: #1d1d1d; border: 1px solid #2e2e2e; border-radius: 14px; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** Dark chrome around a near-black viewport, fine toolbar and side panel hierarchy.

**Transfer:** Show enough actual controls to explain the workflow while allowing the output to dominate.

#### Composer glass · level 2

**Use:** Opening interactive invitation.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(58, 58, 58, 0.72); border-radius: 16px; box-shadow: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.06) 0px 0px 0px 1px inset; backdrop-filter: blur(40px)`

**Observed:** Neutral smoky glass, not colorful frosted glass.

#### Ruled matrix · level 0

**Only for:** enterprise capability and pricing reference sections

**Use:** Dense factual capabilities.

**CSS:** `border-top: 1px solid #1e1e1e; border-left: 1px solid #1e1e1e`

**Observed:** Cells finish right and bottom edges; corners stay square, fields mostly remain black.

**Transfer:** Use shared rules rather than individually rounded cards for factual lists.

#### Premium service panel · level 1

**Only for:** enterprise service panels

**Use:** Human assistance or premium service alongside regular support.

**CSS:** `background: #101010; border: 1px solid rgba(255, 196, 120, 0.2); border-radius: 16px; padding: 40px`

**Observed:** Gold mono label, small gold icons and an internal divider; the panel itself stays charcoal.

**Transfer:** Use warm detail sparingly to signal a human-service layer.

## Imagery and graphics

### Dimensional result gallery

**Use:** A concentrated burst of finished-work color in the otherwise neutral page.

**Observed:** A broad 739×416px central result with 16px corners, flanked by much smaller 266×150px dimmed previews. The active example shows a tactile low-poly rover on peach dunes with gentle directional shadows; other work includes playful architecture and designed product objects. The scene occupies more space than its short caption.

**Transfer:** Use a polished, task-relevant output in the same central stage. Minimum acceptable version is a well-lit static scene or real product result, backed by a coherent environment rather than a floating stock icon.

### Application and output pairing

**Use:** Demonstrate how work is made and refined.

**Observed:** Large charcoal editor frames with near-black viewing areas, small chrome, avatars and selection handles. Alternating split sections let interfaces bleed past a page edge; the closing customer demonstration contains a colorful toy-like castle against a softly blurred real-world background, fading to black along the bottom.

**Transfer:** Place real output in a believable version of the new product's interface. Reduce control density if necessary, but preserve the contrast between muted working chrome and the dimensional result.

### Miniature process diagrams

**Use:** Illustrated explanations above step copy.

**Observed:** 298×200px charcoal stages hold centered, small constructions: overlapping application circles, nested rounded squares, an outlined mini-window with a selected cube and collaborator pointers, and a simplified phone holding pastel geometry. Thin gray contours dominate; color is concentrated in a few tiny objects.

**Transfer:** Reduce each workflow stage to a single concrete metaphor, using crisp geometry, neutral framing and a few soft accent markers.

**Build:** Use a #101010 16px-rounded stage. Center an SVG window or nested rounded rectangles with 1px #2e2e2e strokes, occupying roughly half the stage width. Add one small dimensional object built from three polygons with light, mid and dark gray faces; attach a thin #8eb2f2 selection rectangle with square corner handles. Short muted mono status text can sit below it.

### Live progress field

**Use:** Signature operational artwork near a conversion invitation.

**Observed:** A faint dot field spreads behind four black outlined progress capsules; the active row is readable while surrounding rows recede almost into the page. The entire motif remains monochrome and smaller than the text-side column.

**Transfer:** Retain the progress stack and dot field on a new marketing page even when no 3D media exists; use stages from the new workflow.

**Build:** Place four Progress capsules in a 12px-gap vertical stack. Behind them, repeat 1px rgba(255,255,255,0.08) circles on a 22px square SVG pattern and fade the field outward with a radial mask. Keep one capsule near full opacity and others around 0.4; place three 6px pale circles with 4px gaps at the active row's left. The dot spacing is a construction guide, not an essential stop value.

### Monochrome proof marks

**Use:** Social proof between the opening demonstration and capability chapters.

**Observed:** A slim horizontally flowing strip of gray company logos, roughly 30px high, widely spaced on black; customer stories expand proof into a five-column, three-row white logo wall.

**Transfer:** Normalize partner marks to equal optical presence and grayscale; never give each logo a separate tile.

### Device stage

**Only for:** multi-platform product demonstrations

**Use:** Show a result traveling from a web interface to a native device.

**Observed:** A 1320×640px charcoal stage holds a centrally placed near-black phone shell; the shell uses very rounded corners and a dark pill camera cutout.

**Transfer:** Use a minimal device silhouette only when platform delivery matters, and put a real result inside it.

**Build:** Use Charcoal stage, then a centered vertical #0c0c0c shell about 500px wide with 79px corners, faint double contour and a small black camera capsule at its top. Fit the new product's rendered output inside an inset rounded viewport.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small neutral outline SVGs: commonly 16×16px, 1.5px or 2px round strokes. Enterprise technical icons use 20px blue outlines; service icons use warm gold. Social and external service marks use compact filled silhouettes.

**Transfer:** Use simple rounded line icons beside labels and chevrons on linked cards. Keep full-color icons inside demonstrations rather than spreading them through the navigation.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Make something opening

**Observed:** Black canvas under Sticky navigation. A small announcement capsule sits above a centered, tightly fitted two-line promise, around 527px wide, set at 48px/56px and weight 450. The second line is muted gray with a small blue selection/caret detail at its end. A single 16px support line follows, then Opening composer and four 36px suggestion pills. The composer is the visual action rather than a large CTA pair; the persistent header contains the conversion pair. All this is visible in the first 900px, with the top of a 1320px-wide application demonstration entering around the lower sixth of the screen; most of that demonstration and the following proof strip lie below the fold. Layers are black ground, centered promise and composer, small interaction details, then the broad framed product stage.

**Transfer:** Keep the compact promise, usable task entry and broad real-output stage for a new product. Without interactive media, show a polished static output within an authentic application frame. Utility page variants instead open left-aligned at 64px/72px with a pale first line and gray continuation, support and action controls beneath; customer-story pages use a centered 40px Spline Sans title above a wide case-study carousel.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a huge full-bleed abstract orb, oversized 100px display headline or an unrelated dashboard floating behind the words. Keep the working invitation as the center of attention.

**Text style:** `opening` — Geist 450 · 48px/56px · tracking -0.06em

### Chapter heading pair

**Observed:** A 256px lead-in precedes a 12px mono label, 16px gap and 56px title. Equal-width title/support tracks have a 32px gutter and align at the bottom; supporting prose is muted 18px/28px. The result gallery uses a centered title instead.

**Transfer:** Introduce each meaningful capability with one concise promise, letting supporting copy explain the mechanism.

**Text style:** `heading` — Geist 450 · 56px/64px · tracking -0.06em

### Illustrated process row

**Observed:** Chapter heading pair above four equal steps with 43.2px gutters. Miniature process diagrams sit above numbered mono labels, 24px step titles and 14px descriptions; descriptions are outside the charcoal illustration stages.

**Transfer:** Use as many steps as the real workflow needs without turning the illustration boxes into tall text cards.

### Alternating workbench split

**Observed:** A five-to-seven split with a 72px gutter places a heading, brief lead and compact labeled benefits opposite an application demonstration. Subsequent chapters swap media sides. Content remains vertically centered against a roughly 605px-tall interface.

**Transfer:** Alternate viewpoint between user benefit and visible mechanism; each scene should demonstrate the adjacent claim.

### Result carousel

**Observed:** Centered 56px heading, 48px to Category chip row, then 32px to the central Dimensional result gallery. Caption and Outline button share a lower row, 36px beneath the result.

**Transfer:** Let a useful example provide color and playfulness; select examples through task-oriented categories rather than decorative controls.

### Proof cards and quote pause

**Observed:** Default proof is a heading followed by three Testimonial cards on a 24px-gap grid. The utility-page variant is a centered 720px-wide standalone 24px quotation with gray connective text, pale emphasized claims and a small portrait attribution beneath.

**Transfer:** Use the card row for several complementary experiences; use the standalone quote as a breathing space after dense factual material.

### Operational closing call

**Observed:** A 412px-tall open black section with 80px vertical padding uses a seven-to-five split and 40px gutter. Heading, muted lead, then a 32px gap to Primary button and Outline button sit left; Live progress field sits right. No colored band or enclosing card.

**Transfer:** End a new marketing page with the invitation and the live-work motif. The enterprise variant instead culminates in the contact form; the customer-story variant uses a centered 36px claim, blue action and broad output-in-interface image.

### Working directory footer

**Observed:** After 158.4px separation, a footer begins with 80px top padding. A wider brand/description column and four narrow navigation columns use a two-to-one-to-one-to-one-to-one grid, 24px gaps and stacked labeled groups. A small row of external assistant icons sits under the description. Bottom copyright and social links sit across a 1px dark rule with 16px vertical padding. Customer stories use a narrower 1080px rail, a small standalone brand symbol, four simpler link columns and a brighter 10% white bottom rule.

**Transfer:** Group real destinations by function and keep the footer compact in type, not in overall breathing space.

### Ruled capability matrix

**Only for:** enterprise pages

**Observed:** Chapter heading pair above square connected cells, typically three columns by two rows, with small blue icons, 18px titles and 14px copy. Certification-style proof uses three separate 16px-rounded charcoal cards above a ruled row. Support can pair three stacked cells with Premium service panel.

**Transfer:** Separate verifiable assurances from general capabilities and make each cell one concise fact.

### Plan comparison

**Only for:** pricing pages

**Observed:** A two-tone 64px introduction leads to four 330px plan columns within one rounded border; the recommended column alone gets #101010 fill and a pale button. Prices use 48px/52px type. A separate free option sits below. The long comparison table uses 49px rows, fine dark horizontal rules, centered values and checkmarks, with a sticky compact plan/action header.

**Transfer:** Keep visual emphasis on one recommendation, then provide precise side-by-side detail without colored plan towers.

### Directory chapters

**Only for:** directory pages

**Observed:** A left-aligned page introduction includes anchor capsules with counts. Each category starts with a mono count and 56px heading; 432px cards repeat in three columns with 12px gutters. Later categories have 128px top lead-ins.

**Transfer:** Use a consistent category label and benefit sentence; let repetition provide scan speed.

### Objections and contact

**Only for:** pricing and enterprise pages

**Observed:** FAQ title occupies a smaller left track; 751px-wide FAQ rows fill the right. Enterprise conversion then uses a 48px-gap two-column split: heading, lead and short checklist opposite Sales form, with 256px top and 160px bottom space.

**Transfer:** Move from short self-service answers to a concrete human next step rather than duplicating the same CTA band.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Invitation → visible work → credibility → capability chapters → proof or decision detail → useful next step → working directory.

### Product marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Centered two-tone headline, task composer and suggestions.
2. **Product demonstration** — Broad application stage followed by a muted logo strip.
3. **Mechanism** — Illustrated process steps explain the path from request to result.
4. **Possibility** — Large selected output with smaller neighboring previews and category chips.
5. **Capability chapters** — Alternating interface/text splits and a device delivery stage.
6. **Social proof** — Three compact customer quote cards.
7. **Closing call** — Action pair beside a live progress stack, then grouped directory footer.

**Transfer:** Use the product's real workflow and real output as the story; rearrange chapters to suit the user's decision.

### Self-service plan selection

1. **Decision framing** — Large left two-tone promise and compact billing switch.
2. **Pricing** — Shared four-column enclosure, one emphasized plan, separate free option.
3. **Credibility** — Slim logo strip.
4. **Explanation** — Numbered cells explain usage units.
5. **Comparison** — Long ruled feature tables with persistent plan header.
6. **Objection handling** — Standalone quote pause and split FAQ.
7. **Closing call** — Operational closing call and grouped footer.

**Transfer:** Clarify the commercial unit first, then let readers inspect details without repeated sales copy.

### Organization-level procurement

1. **Promise** — Two-tone left display, concise support and two actions.
2. **Product demonstration** — Organization administration interface and logo strip.
3. **Risk reduction** — Verification cards followed by ruled safeguards.
4. **Capability chapters** — Workspace matrix, quote pause and support/service pairing.
5. **Objection handling** — Split FAQ.
6. **Closing call** — Checklist and full sales form, then shared footer; no generic progress CTA.

**Transfer:** Replace broad possibility with verifiable assurances, ownership details and a clear human handoff.

### Browse applications and audiences

1. **Orientation** — Two-tone display and counted anchor capsules.
2. **Discovery** — Counted category headings and dense three-column benefit link cards.
3. **Closing call** — Operational closing call and shared footer.

**Transfer:** Use an honest taxonomy and distinct benefit statements; imagery is not necessary in every directory tile.

### Customer-story and credibility hub

1. **Orientation** — Centered friendly heading and concise support above a broad project carousel.
2. **Demonstrated outcome** — Case-study media, title/byline, story action and feature tags.
3. **Social proof** — Transparent quote cards then a broad monochrome logo wall.
4. **Conversion** — Sales invitation beside a charcoal form.
5. **Closing call** — Centered product claim, blue action, colorful output within a broad interface image.
6. **Wayfinding** — Simple four-column footer with social and copyright row.

**Transfer:** Use the narrower editorial type and blue actions consistently on story-focused pages, not mixed into the default landing-page theme.

## Writing

Matter-of-fact enthusiasm for making useful things. Confidence comes from explaining what people can do and how the product behaves, with warmth in invitations rather than superlatives.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Spline name, logo, product names, partner marks, customer names, quotations, proprietary artwork, certifications, performance promises, plan prices or usage allowances. Source examples illustrate sentence structure only.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use you and your team for capability and invitations; we appears when offering human assistance. Describe the product by name only when needed for orientation.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often two balanced clauses. Follow with one or two plain explanatory sentences. Parallel lists, full stops and occasional fragments keep technical detail scannable.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Name the user or team and a concrete outcome; use a muted continuation to finish the thought. | 6–12 words | “Your team and agents, building and shipping in 3D” |
| Capability heading | Pair two short active statements or summarize a complete workflow. | 4–9 words | “Agents build. Designers refine.”<br>“From prompt to production in one file” |
| Support | Explain the mechanism in concrete terms, then the handoff or practical consequence. | 15–30 words | “The agent gets the scene to a first version fast. Your team picks it up in the same file and takes it the rest of the way.” |
| Action | Verb plus useful object, not a slogan. | 2–4 words | “Start building”<br>“Make your own”<br>“Talk to sales” |
| Human assistance | A modest promise followed by a specific next conversation. | 5–20 words | “We help you get started”<br>“Tell us about your team. We’ll discuss your requirements and shape a plan around them.” |
| Objection handling | Ask the user's direct practical question, then answer with limits and conditions. | 5–12 words per question | “Can I use [Brand] for free?”<br>“How does seat pricing work?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; ship; refine; same file; your team; real-time; production-ready; get started; connect; publish

**Avoid:** revolutionary; unleash limitless potential; game-changing; 10x everything; effortless magic; excessive exclamation marks

**Rules:**

- Put the user action before the technology label.
- Support claims with visible mechanisms and specific limitations.
- Use short chapter labels as orientation, not as extra slogans.
- Write examples and progress labels as believable tasks.
- Use parallel syntax in feature lists and keep one idea per cell.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep default chapter titles at 56px/64px, weight 450 and -0.06em tracking.
- Keep the centered opening at 48px rather than inflating it to the utility-page 64px recipe.
- Use 16px-rounded charcoal stages and a true black page around them.
- Give major capability chapters 256px of lead-in space on the desktop composition.
- Pair 44px pale and outline conversion capsules with a 10px gap.
- Set section labels in 12px uppercase mono with 0.06em tracking.
- Concentrate saturated imagery inside one contained output stage at a time.
- Keep the progress artwork neutral, using 270×54px capsules and a 12px stack gap.

### Don't

- Do not make the whole page blue because story pages have blue actions; the default primary fill is #eeeeee.
- Do not replace 450-weight headline type with bold 700-weight startup typography.
- Do not turn every explanatory paragraph into 18px text; compact details use 14px/20px.
- Do not apply 16px corners to the connected capability matrix; its shared cell corners stay square.
- Do not surround logos with white badges or full-color card fills; proof marks sit directly on black.
- Do not give charcoal cards broad luminous drop shadows; separation comes from tone and hairlines.
- Do not use warm gold as a broad background; it belongs to small premium-service details.
- Do not flatten all gray text to #ffffff; muted continuations and the #bdbdbd footer link role matter.‌

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Action color/background/border transitions commonly use 0.2s; progress rows declare opacity, transform and border-color transitions of 0.4s.
- Progress dots declare a 1.2s linear infinite wave; the logo ticker declares a 48s linear infinite slide.
- The opening has a 0.9s step-end blinking detail. Application elements declare 0.35s ease-out entrance rises.

**Transfer:**

- Let the live progress stack change emphasis between real workflow stages; do not animate the entire closing section.
- Use the slow horizontal proof strip as continuous background credibility, with product scenes carrying the more expressive interaction.

**Unknown:**

- Exact runtime choreography and complete scene behavior are not established; do not infer timing from individual still positions.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop composition uses a 1320px content rail with 48px outer padding.
- Quote and premium panel padding follows clamp(24px, 3vw, 40px). Styles declare many width breakpoints, including 640px, 820px, 1080px and 1280px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrower screens, retain the compact promise and task entry before the demonstration; fit the 680px composer to the available width.
- Stack alternating text/interface chapters while keeping real output visible; reduce a gallery to its selected result rather than shrinking side previews into unreadable stamps.
- Allow taxonomy controls to scroll horizontally, and change dense directory or plan grids to readable stacked arrangements.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile layouts and exact breakpoint-to-layout changes are not established. The narrow-screen rules above are recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
