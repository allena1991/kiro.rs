# Sprout Social — rounded stages, vivid impact

## Overview

Bold, friendly business software marketing pairs near-black stages with bright white islands and fresh green accents. An extra-bold humanist sans gives practical promises a buoyant confidence, while real people, interface collages and colorful gradient frames make the technology tangible. Spacious storytelling gives way to denser pricing and proof modules without losing the strong black-and-white contrast.

### Essentials

- A friendly heavy headline, not a severe grotesk: Proxima Nova 800 at 57px/64px opens the page, 43px/50.7px leads sections and 76px/80px delivers the closing statement; tracking stays normal.
- A conversion-led opening sits above a human product scene: a centered white headline on #040404, a 52px email-and-green-button row, then a wide rounded photograph with floating interface cards.
- The page is built from rounded islands rather than flat stripes: 64px section corners, 24px media panels and small concave corner docks that let actions nest into the surrounding white surface.
- Actions are compact rectangles, not capsules: 6px radius, 2px borders and bold 16px/24px labels, with #98e58e for the main action and black, white or outline variants for context.
- Color arrives in concentrated stages: most of the page remains black and white; mint-to-green proof bands, pink-lilac demonstration backdrops and 8px multicolor frames supply vivid contrast rather than tinting every section.
- Proof is a shaped collage, not just a quote: combine a 76px statistic, rounded documentary imagery, a black 24px-radius quote panel and a small independent credential tile. Use a real outcome and a product-result image when customer photography is unavailable.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy narrative stages with compact controls and dense, carefully aligned comparison and footer areas.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **rhythm** 8px, vertical — Dominant vertical spacing rhythm; smaller control details also use 4px and 12px.

| Value | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 8px | Tight copy pairs and gradient-frame thickness. |
| 12px | Email/action gap and corner-dock inset. |
| 16px | Compact card-grid gaps and heading-to-plan-description space. |
| 24px | Support text spacing and standard tile padding. |
| 32px | Main grid gutters and headline-to-action separation. |
| 40px | Plan-card vertical padding and quote top inset. |
| 48px | Larger card interiors and section-title-to-grid gaps. |
| 64px | Opening top inset, section breathing room and generous panel padding. |
| 80px | Default large rounded-section vertical padding. |
| 96px | Major dark-section top padding. |
| 160px | Proof-stage vertical padding, allowing adjoining rounded islands to overlap. |

### Containers and grids

- **main-container**: max width 1360px · side padding 24px. Centered desktop shell; at 1440px it has 40px outer margins and a 1312px inner content area.
- **structural-grid**: 12 columns · gap 32px. Align split headings, media, text and conversion panels.
- **card-trio**: 3 columns · gap 16px. Compact plan cards, each about 427px wide in the desktop content area.
- **feature-trio**: 3 columns · gap 32px. Benefit cards of about 416px width.
- **partner-grid**: 7 columns · gap 16px. Two rows of equal white logo tiles on a black stage.
- **faq-column**: max width 864px. Narrow centered questions for pricing; broader product FAQs use 1088px.
- **footer-directory**: 3 columns · gap 32px. Directory occupies the right half of a two-column footer shell; brand and contact content occupy the left.
- **form-column** (only for lead-generation endings): max width 632px. Single-column demo inquiry form under a centered promise.

## Imagery and graphics

### Human workflow collage

**Use:** Main opening scene and product-story split panels.

**Observed:** Natural, richly colored photography shows a person actively working with a laptop or phone, often with plants and tactile interiors. The broad rounded photo occupies most of the lower first screen; small white metric cards, short AI prompts and bright numeric lozenges float across its edges without obscuring the face.

**Transfer:** Show a believable user doing the new product's work, then layer two to four simplified outputs. If photography is unavailable, use a composed workspace still life plus one credible workflow panel, maintaining a human-scale focal object.

**Build:** Use a wide object-fit:cover image with 24px to 32px corners, on black. Position small white 8px to 16px-radius cards above it near opposing corners, with fine neutral edges and restrained shadows; use miniature SVG sparklines and one green/mint numeric badge. Keep the central face or work object clear.

### Interface stage

**Use:** Primary product demonstration and alternating capability chapters.

**Observed:** A central desktop interface or isolated dashboard cards is enlarged enough to recognize the workflow. Auxiliary white cards overlap the main panel. Pink-lilac backing softens the core demonstration; the deeper product variant uses a green square stage or white UI directly on black.

**Transfer:** Use one real workflow rather than a wall of indistinguishable dashboards. A minimum version is one readable application window with one overlaid result card.

**Build:** Place the main interface at roughly three quarters of its colored stage width. Add one smaller callout at the lower-left and one at the upper-right. Use Pink product stage for the wide demonstration; for the focused variant use a 640px square #59cb59 stage with 32px corners.

### Notched panel geometry

**Use:** Signature joining device for product actions, customer proof and editorial panels.

**Observed:** Rounded rectangles interlock through concave white or background-colored cutouts. A bottom-right button dock can appear to bite into a dark media panel; a proof photo wraps between a statistic block and a logo block.

**Transfer:** Include a purposeful notched junction in each new landing-page system. Let the cutout hold a CTA or a proof tile, not an arbitrary decorative hole.

**Build:** Build a relative rounded panel and overlay a background-colored corner wrapper, inset 12px around its button, with a 16px top-left radius. Add a 24px to 32px SVG quarter-circle inverse corner at the joint so the panel appears concave. For proof, use a two-column grid and an SVG clipPath with rounded inward corners around separate stat and logo tiles.

### Proof collage

**Use:** Quantified proof on Fresh proof band.

**Observed:** A 416px-wide visual cluster sits beside an expansive black quote panel. A white 76px figure in a black rounded tile overlays a customer-context photograph; a separate white credential card occupies the far-right notch. Controls sit centered below as arrows and 16px dots.

**Transfer:** Pair an authenticated result with an image of the customer's setting, then put the first-person account in the large black panel. Substitute a result artifact for a portrait when appropriate.

### Editorial people and places

**Only for:** company storytelling

**Use:** Organization, culture, leadership and career stories.

**Observed:** Large rounded documentary video with an overlapping black italic-headline panel; evenly lit waist-up portraits on neutral gray with 1px black outlines and 16px corners; office and collaboration photos form staggered mosaics with gradient-filled blocks.

**Transfer:** Show real people and lived-in work settings, preserving consistent portrait scale and documentary lighting.

### Luminous abstract emblems

**Only for:** company culture stories

**Use:** Values or principle cards below an editorial video.

**Observed:** Dark rounded cards contain polished loop, crescent/sphere and knot-like sculptural symbols in purple, green, blue and gold, each with a localized glow rather than a page-wide haze.

**Transfer:** Use an abstract emblem per principle. Minimum acceptable execution is a clean SVG loop or interlocking pair of curves against a localized colored halo.

**Build:** On a black 304px-wide card, draw thick rounded SVG loops or intertwined rings in two related colors. Back the emblem with a radial gradient fading from colored rgba at the center to transparent at the edge over about 240px. Keep the glow confined to the symbol region and set the short label below.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small filled utility SVGs, typically 18px to 24px; 11px filled menu chevrons; simple checks for benefits and plus signs for expansion. Partner marks remain full-color, while social links reverse with their surface.

**Transfer:** Use simple filled or clean single-line utility forms with consistent optical size. Keep benefit checks subordinate to the text and avoid large decorative outline icons in the main headline area.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Human product stage opening

**Observed:** A sticky 76px Floating navigation rests above a black stage. The centered display headline spans almost the full 1312px content width with 64px top breathing room. Below it, a 52px Email field and Primary button form a compact centered row with 12px gap and 32px separation from the headline. There is no separate support paragraph. Beneath the form, the Human workflow collage fills most of the remaining screen: a broad rounded photograph framed in black with white output cards floating across its edges. In the first 900px the headline, form and virtually the whole photo are visible; the next white island and its heading appear below the fold. Layers run black ground, photo, floating outputs, then sticky navigation.

**Transfer:** Lead with a concrete product-category promise and a human-scale working scene carrying legible output cards. Without people photography, use the workspace-and-result fallback in Human workflow collage. Detailed product pages can open as a white two-column promise/media split; sales-led feature pages use a bordered white copy panel and supporting credential strip; company stories use a video with an overlapping italic statement panel. Pricing opens directly into offers.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the centered headline and wide human scene with a generic left-copy/right-dashboard SaaS hero for the default page. Do not add a long paragraph or huge decorative wordmark above the signup row.

**Text style:** `display` — Proxima Nova 800 · 57px/1.123

## Writing

Confident, practical and warmly ambitious. Concrete workflow language anchors broader claims about business impact, and small conversational turns prevent the enterprise message becoming stiff.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Sprout Social names, trademarks, logos, product names, customer names, quotes, awards, dates, customer counts, prices or performance claims. Replace them with the new product's verified assets and facts. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your team. Use we for organizational conviction and the product name when clarifying a concrete capability.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines usually run 5–12 words with strong verbs and plain nouns. Support is one short paragraph; benefit lists use parallel active phrases. Longer first-person quotes retain human detail rather than compressing into slogans.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Name a practical category and attach an immediate business advantage. | 6–12 words | “Powerful tools for social media management”<br>“Social media management built for day-one impact” |
| Section lead | Use a strong verb, a clear scope and an outcome; ampersands support compact three-part rhythms. | 5–10 words | “Launch, run & scale creator campaigns”<br>“Hear what you’d otherwise miss” |
| Supporting line | Connect the mechanism to a real working problem in direct language. | 1–2 sentences | “Juggling five tools to do one job is a workaround, not a system.” |
| Primary action | Verb plus an explicit low-friction offer or next step. | 3–6 words | “Try free for 30 days”<br>“Get a personalized demo” |
| Offer fit | Explain who the offer is best for before listing features. | 10–16 words | “Perfect for small teams managing a few profiles across social networks” |
| Objection handling | Ask the reader's actual decision question without euphemism. | 6–15 words | “What is the difference between annual and monthly billing?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** impact; built for; your team; workflow; scale; insights; from insight to action; get up and running; prove; simplify

**Avoid:** revolutionary disruption; unparalleled synergy; magical everything; vague superlatives without a mechanism; playful slang in plan details

**Rules:**

- Put the usable capability before the abstract aspiration in supporting copy.
- Use short parallel checked benefits to make feature chapters scannable.
- Put risk reduction close to the offer: trial duration, payment requirements or consultation expectations.
- Distinguish a bold marketing promise from a measured result; make the latter attributable.
- Let organizational copy be more expansive and aspirational than pricing copy.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 800-weight headlines with normal tracking, keeping the 57px opening and 76px closing roles distinct.
- Keep the main desktop shell at 1360px with 24px inner side padding.
- Use 64px corners for section islands and 16px corners for ordinary tiles so hierarchy remains visible.
- Keep primary actions fresh green with 2px borders and 6px corners.
- Build gradient rims as 8px padded wrappers around separate black cards.
- Use one bright proof stage amid predominantly black-and-white storytelling.
- Let a white dock interrupt a panel corner to give an action a deliberate home.
- Keep 16px compact card gaps separate from the 32px feature-grid rhythm.

### Don't

- Do not substitute ultra-tight negative tracking for the friendly breadth of the heavy type.
- Do not make every button green; black, white and outline actions are essential contextual variants.
- Do not round all controls into pills; 6px corners are the action default.
- Do not add drop shadows to every panel; the major shadow belongs to the floating navigation.
- Do not distribute pink, cream and yellow evenly across ordinary marketing pages; warm chapters are a specialist variant.
- Do not make gradient frames into 1px rainbow outlines; their thick 8px rim is part of the visual identity.
- Do not put the entire footer in tiny centered text; it is a broad split brand-and-directory layout.
- Do not shrink human photography into incidental avatars when it should anchor the product scene.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Action transitions declare 0.2s cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.7, 0.2).
- Customer-logo strips declare continuous horizontal scrolling at 90s linear, infinite.
- The notched demonstration carousel declares a 0.3s ease fade-in; carousel controls and pagination are visible.

**Transfer:**

- Apply the stated action transition to contextual fill and border changes.
- Use the slow logo loop as background proof motion, keeping headings and conversion actions stable.
- Keep the notched stage geometry fixed while its active workflow changes.

**Unknown:**

- Scroll-trigger timing, exact choreography and most video playback behavior are not established.
- Focus-visible appearance and reduced-motion variants are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Declared width breakpoints include 480px, 640px, 768px, 960px, 1024px and 1360px, with additional component-specific widths.
- The desktop structural grid uses 12 columns and the sticky navigation occupies a centered inset shell.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, preserve the headline → conversion → human scene hierarchy; stack the email and action without burying the main object.
- Stack split chapters while retaining the notched CTA joint and the distinction between section-scale and card-scale corners.
- Reflow proof into statistic/image then quote and credential, retaining the oversized numeral.
- Allow comparison content its own horizontal handling rather than shrinking the billing text to fit.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile typography, navigation transformation, panel corner sizes and comparison behavior are not established; these reflow rules are design recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
