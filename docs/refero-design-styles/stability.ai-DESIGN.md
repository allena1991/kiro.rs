# Stability AI — cinematic tools, human possibility

## Overview

A cinematic, technically fluent identity that puts creative outcomes ahead of software chrome. Charcoal and warm ivory carry most of the page, with lavender actions and muted purple emphasis; bold grotesk headlines sit alongside naturalistic photography, generated worlds and translucent technical annotations. The density moves between spacious editorial statements and compact practical cards, suiting creative tools, platforms and enterprise services.

### Essentials

- Headlines sound and look like a creative invitation: Archivo 700 at 61px/0.95 with -0.01em tracking in the opening, and 42px/0.98 for centered chapter statements with one phrase shifted to gray or muted purple.
- The opening is a cinematic scene with tools floating over it: a 779px full-width stage, a roughly 436px headline block on the left, imagery dominating the right half, and small glass panels connected by dotted paths. Carry this annotated-scene device into every new landing page; use a real workflow photograph and a simple data or detail inset when bespoke media is unavailable.
- Conversion controls are small capsules rather than heavy panels: 300px radii, lavender #a381ff fills, 14px Figtree 600 labels and 11px 22px padding; the paired secondary action has a 2px lavender outline.
- Color belongs to imagery and selective emphasis, not to every surface: charcoal #1e1e1e dominates the default page, a contiguous warm #ede5e1 chapter provides contrast, and lavender is concentrated in actions, links and small annotations.
- Proof is staged as editorial art: square 364px news pictures with 0.5px edges, a seamless three-panel moving-image strip, and outcome collages with translucent purple stat badges rather than generic dashboard cards.
- The closing invitation returns to the cinematic world: a full-width scene around 692px high, a centered 45px bold promise and one lavender capsule, followed by a deep charcoal directory with 12px uppercase headings and 13px links.

## Imagery and graphics

### Annotated cinematic scene

**Use:** Signature opening scene for marketing landing pages.

**Observed:** A woman in lavender outerwear fills the right side of a rain-dark green transit scene. The left fades into grainy charcoal for type. Floating detail images, a lavender waveform, dotted rounded connectors and small tool chips make the image feel actively editable.

**Transfer:** Use a human-scale workflow or product-in-context photograph with a readable dark left edge. If custom cinematic media is unavailable, one strong photograph, one zoomed detail and one relevant output trace are the minimum equivalent.

**Build:** Place a cover image across a 779px stage. Layer a left-to-right charcoal opacity gradient above it, then a low-opacity monochrome SVG noise layer. Position a roughly 244px by 185px detail pane near the image/text boundary and a roughly 202px by 77px waveform pane high on the right. Connect them with an SVG rounded path using a 1px white dashed stroke at about 0.6 opacity. Add two dark translucent rounded tool chips; apply Detail glass to panes. Draw the trace as two interleaved lavender sine paths rather than copying a product interface.

### Pale textured cards

**Use:** Small practical cards within dark or warm chapters.

**Observed:** Dusty lilac, pink and ivory softly blend with fine grain. Color is low-contrast and material-like, not a vivid neon glow; black text remains dominant.

**Transfer:** Recreate the soft paper-like gradient rather than using flat gray cards.

**Build:** Use warm ivory as the base, a broad lilac-to-transparent radial wash from the upper left and a dusty pink wash near the lower right. Overlay a monochrome SVG feTurbulence texture at low opacity. Keep the tonal shifts subtle enough that all black text reads equally clearly; exact stops are not essential.

### Outcome media strip

**Use:** Visual proof of multiple use cases.

**Observed:** Adjacent portrait scenes contrast everyday headphone use, a hot orange fantasy world and a live-action performer on a green stage. Media occupies far more area than its captions.

**Transfer:** Select outcome scenes with genuinely different palettes and contexts; maintain one equal-height strip rather than separate floating cards.

**Use:** News and customer proof.

**Observed:** Square covers combine bold type with grain, glossy black light forms or close human portraits. The case study pairs a rounded grayscale fashion collage with one translucent purple badge overlapping the left edge; a giant percentage sits above a tiny explanatory line.

## Writing

Confident and commercially useful, with the warmth of a creative collaborator. Headlines are conversational and rhythmic; technical specificity belongs in support copy and practical options.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse brand or product names, customer logos, proprietary imagery, funding amounts, performance metrics, licensing promises or testimonial claims. Replace them with truthful material for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your team; use we for a capable collaborator and our for tools or models, not a distant corporate narrator.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines favor short verbs, paired clauses and memorable repetition. Follow with one or two compact sentences; use direct action labels and occasional closing questions rather than exclamations.

| Action | Short verb phrase; product entry, learning or human contact. | 2–6 words | “Start creating”<br>“Let’s build together” |
| Closing invitation | A rhythmic contrast or question that returns attention to the reader's next result. | 5–10 words | “Less busywork. More great work.”<br>“What will your next production sound like?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** make; build; your process; production; control; customize; work alongside; open; ready

**Avoid:** revolutionary magic; effortless everything; disrupt the universe; unmatched synergy; excessive jargon chains

**Rules:**

- Lead with what someone can make, then explain the machinery.
- Use concrete deployment or workflow language in practical sections.
- Make each card title a short category or parallel phrase; let its paragraph explain the difference.
- Keep claims bounded and specific; support legal or performance assertions with real product facts.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening headline at 61px, weight 700 and -0.01em tracking, distinct from the 42px chapter scale.
- Use #a381ff capsules against #1e1e1e and muted #725d91 emphasis on warm light backgrounds.
- Keep the opening support paragraph near 350px wide so the image remains dominant.
- Connect small glass detail panes with delicate dotted paths rather than heavy interface frames.
- Use 17px gutters for editorial cards and no gutter within the tall three-scene strip.
- Make the warm practical chapter a full-width tonal change with compact 11px-radius cards.
- Reserve rim-light inset shadows for glass annotations and keep ordinary content surfaces flat.
- Carry the cinematic scene through to the final invitation before the 13px directory links.

### Don't

- Do not turn the lavender accent into the default full-page background; the deep-violet band belongs only to the light visual-product variant.
- Do not replace bold Archivo with a thin fashion display face or widespread monospace labels.
- Do not use one 24px corner radius everywhere: controls are capsules while news media uses 8px corners.
- Do not add drop shadows under all cards; depth comes from imagery and small translucent panes.
- Do not put news dates and titles inside a raised filled card; the 364px image wrapper alone has the hairline.
- Do not apply the 80px visual-product headline to every dark chapter; preserve the smaller default hierarchy.
- Do not enlarge directory labels to body size; the 12px uppercase labels and 13px links create the compact closing texture.
- Do not use tiled purple gradients as a substitute for photographic outcomes; textured gradients back practical cards, not the whole story.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The specialized audio action declares color, text-fill and background-image transitions of 0.4s; hover and focus-visible remove its background image and keep charcoal text.
- Opening and closing scenes use video or canvas presentations; no general marketing animation timing is established.

**Transfer:**

- Keep moving-image scenes behind stationary readable copy. Use the fine glass annotations to suggest an active tool without making the page feel like a running dashboard.

**Unknown:**

- Scene playback choreography, scroll timing and pane movement easing are not specified.
- No other reliable button hover or focus treatment is established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Width rules include 320px, 400px, 425px, 426px, 530px, 550px, 600px, 640px, 767px, 768px and 896px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the promise before the main subject and simplify annotations to one useful detail pane; keep the dotted-path motif without hiding text.
- Stack practical card grids and alternating media chapters while preserving their scoped dark or light chapter background.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile navigation, exact column collapse points and mobile type scaling are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
