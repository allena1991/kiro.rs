# Step — electric pink confidence

## Overview

A youthful, emphatic dark style that makes practical products feel like an upgrade in everyday life. Heavy grotesk headlines, a brush-lettered flourish, charcoal bento panels and electric pink accents combine with oversized device renders and tactile objects. Human photography and occasional white editorial bands soften the product spectacle; the overall density is sparse at section level but punchy inside each panel.

### Essentials

- Billboard confidence with a handwritten release: Inter 900 at 82px/0.98 with -0.01em tracking opens the page, while a single concluding word switches to an energetic brush face. Repeat that typographic contrast in a monumental closing call at 65px.
- An object and a promise share the opening equally: a 650×598px layered product scene sits left of a narrow, stacked headline, supporting paragraph and 250×48px action on the right, under a 72px fixed navigation bar.
- Pink is concentrated energy rather than the whole canvas: #111111 and #222222 dominate the page, with #f66299 actions, vivid pink clipped headings and selected pink-to-black stages. Keep broad color in isolated media panels or feature bands rather than tinting every section.
- Bento panels feel soft and substantial: 45px corners, 670px half-width cards, 48px text insets and roughly 20px gutters frame 65px benefit statements beside tightly cropped product imagery.
- Actions are small horizontal capsules against huge type: 48px height, 25px radius and 25px side padding; primary actions are pink with dark text, explanatory actions white, and inverted white sections can use black.
- Product benefits become physical scenes: black-bezel phones, fanned cards, glossy objects and floating badges overlap and extend to panel edges. Every new landing page needs a hero-scale product scene and a bright typographic proof panel; if no device or physical product exists, stage a clean interface panel with overlapping benefit tokens over the same pink glow.
- The ending is a second brand statement, not just a directory: a centered logo, 65px uppercase promise with brush emphasis, a pink action and a 1px muted divider precede three link columns, social icons, distribution badges and a subdued disclosure tail.

## Typography

- **Inter** — All marketing headings, paragraphs, navigation and attribution text. Broad, sturdy grotesk; regular text contrasts with very heavy display lettering. Font stack: "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **beast** — One emphatic word in the opening promise and closing brand statement. Forward-slanting, uppercase brush lettering with rough energetic terminals rather than a delicate handwritten script. If unavailable, use **Kalam**. Use 700, uppercase and a compact line box; preserve the broad, forceful word silhouette rather than imitating a signature. Font stack: "beast", "Kalam", cursive.

**Notes:**

- The hierarchy is a jump in scale and weight, not a progression through many medium sizes: 18px regular copy sits directly below 65px or 82px black-weight lettering.
- Large type uses line-height 0.98 and negative tracking. Preserve the distinction between -1px on benefit titles and -0.01em on the other large heading roles.
- Use all caps for the opening and chapter headings, but retain sentence case on benefit cards and editorial stories. There is no recurring small uppercase eyebrow system.
- The pink headline treatment is a fill inside the letters, not a glow or a colored box behind them. Keep the brush face limited to the promise, never paragraphs or controls.

### Containers and grids

- **Page container**: max width 1440px · side padding 55px. Centered desktop content shell for the hero, chapter headings and footer.
- **Bento stage**: max width 1360px · 2 columns · gap 20px · side padding 40px. Wide paired panels, typically 670px each; full-width panels span both tracks. The padding describes its desktop relationship to the page edges.
- **Hero split**: 2 columns · gap 0px. Equal visual and copy halves; the product scene has a 650px maximum width while the right copy half has 72px internal padding.
- **Chapter title**: max width 1000px. Centered heading and short support line above a capability group.
- **Benefit matrix**: max width 800px · 3 columns · gap 16px · side padding 55px. Compact square-tile matrix with a distinct central identity tile; desktop tile size is 219px.
- **Review row**: 3 columns · gap 24px · side padding 40px. 472px-high charcoal review panels, approximately 437px wide; the broader overview variant scrolls horizontally beyond the visible edge.
- **Editorial grid**: 3 columns · gap 36px · side padding 55px. Image-led articles or news with approximately 419px-wide items.
- **Question column** (only for question-and-answer sections): max width 846px. Narrow centered accordion beneath a much larger centered title.

### Surfaces

#### Dark canvas · level 0

**Use:** Default landing-page field.

**CSS:** `background: #111111; color: #ffffff`

#### Charcoal bento · level 1

**Use:** Feature and proof panels.

**CSS:** `background: #222222; border-radius: 45px; box-shadow: none`

**Observed:** The panels are distinguished by a modest value shift, not borders or floating shadows; imagery is clipped into the same soft silhouette.

**Transfer:** Give every panel one benefit or one object, not a nested dashboard of small cards.

#### Hero horizon · level 0

**Use:** Default opening background.

**CSS:** `background: linear-gradient(#111111 61.74%, #80254b 84.85%, #e35b8d 110.71%)`

**Observed:** Most of the hero remains near-black; pink gathers low behind the objects and action.

**Transfer:** Keep the headline's upper field dark and let the color pool near the lower boundary.

#### Pink feature stage · level 1

**Use:** A high-energy announcement or product chapter.

**CSS:** `background: linear-gradient(4deg, #111111 3.79%, #e64286 90.84%); border-radius: 45px`

**Observed:** Saturated pink at the top fades into near-black below. White lettering and one isolated object or graphic occupy opposite halves.

**Transfer:** Use for one concentrated story, retaining the dark lower fade rather than filling an entire page pink.

#### Navigation glass · level 2

**Use:** Fixed navigation above scrolling content.

**CSS:** `background: rgba(17, 17, 17, 0.6); backdrop-filter: blur(72px)`

**Observed:** The underlying page can softly color the middle of the header while the controls remain crisp.

**Transfer:** Keep blur on the header layer, not on card content.

#### White editorial band · level 0

**Use:** A human-centered explanation or wider audience story.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #000000`

**Observed:** A rare full-width inversion without an outer rounded container; generous padding and a rounded photographic image.

**Transfer:** Use a deliberate tonal pause among the dark product chapters.

#### Bright statement panel · level 1

**Only for:** company and mission storytelling

**Use:** A single high-impact purpose statement.

**CSS:** `background: #f66299; border-radius: 45px; color: #ffffff`

**Observed:** The big statement fills one half of a pair; the adjacent charcoal panel carries the quieter explanation.

**Transfer:** Use one strong organizational belief rather than a row of equally bright cards.

## Components

### Fixed navigation

**Use:** Persistent orientation and conversion.

**CSS:** `position: fixed; height: 72px; background: rgba(17, 17, 17, 0.6); backdrop-filter: blur(72px); z-index: 6`

**Transfer:** Keep navigation subordinate to the hero; preserve the clean one-line desktop silhouette.

### Primary button

**Use:** Main conversion action in hero, features and closing call.

**CSS:** `display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; background: #f66299; color: #000000; height: 48px; max-width: 250px; padding: 0px 25px; border-radius: 25px; box-shadow: rgba(34, 34, 52, 0.1) 0px 1px 0px 0px`

**Text style:** `body` — Inter 400 · 18px/1.24

**Observed:** Hero actions occupy a 250px-wide capsule; header and closing actions shrink to their short label. Labels are centered, with no arrow or decorative icon.

**Transfer:** Use a direct two-word verb phrase and one dominant action per story.

### Secondary button

**Use:** Explanatory navigation on dark feature panels.

**CSS:** `display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; background: #ffffff; color: #000000; height: 48px; max-width: 250px; padding: 0px 25px; border-radius: 25px; box-shadow: rgba(34, 34, 52, 0.1) 0px 1px 0px 0px`

**Text style:** `body` — Inter 400 · 18px/1.24

## Imagery and graphics

### Layered product sculpture

**Use:** Opening focal point and major feature stages.

**Observed:** Black-bezel phones show legible dark interfaces, with a front-facing screen and a second angled device or fanned card behind it. Cool violet cards and restrained edge reflections contrast with pink interface accents. The scene fills roughly half the hero and remains fully visible above its lower boundary.

**Transfer:** Use a real product view inside a dark dimensional frame, with one or two overlapping supporting objects. For a service without a device, stage an interface panel and physical benefit tokens with the same depth and lighting.

**Build:** Arrange one tall rounded front panel at roughly 500px high and a second narrower panel behind it, rotated by about -8deg. Add a soft charcoal edge, a restrained white highlight and a wine-to-pink lower backdrop. Use a realistic product view inside; the minimum version is one high-resolution interface panel plus two offset dark rounded cards, not a generic stock laptop.

### Pink clipped lettering

**Use:** Benefit headings, statement cards and process numerals.

**Observed:** The heavy letters carry a vivid fuchsia-to-pink texture with subtle tonal changes across the phrase; the underlying panel stays charcoal. This is particularly strong on long numerical claims.

**Transfer:** Carry the same colored-letter emphasis to one verifiable result or clear benefit, preserving crisp counters and strong contrast.

**Build:** Set the relevant heavy heading recipe, then apply background-clip:text and transparent text fill. Recreate the rich pink field with layered gradients using stage pink, action pink and a little wine at the edges; tune toward vivid fuchsia. Keep the transition broad and smooth, with no bevel, outline or text shadow.

### Tactile benefit collage

**Use:** Large media panels paired with short claims.

**Observed:** Everyday objects such as an ice-cream cone, metallic numerals and cards are enlarged into playful cutout scenes. Objects overlap circular badges, sit against a pink or dark atmospheric field and are clipped hard at the panel boundary. Materials remain photographic or polished 3D rather than flat cartoon line art.

**Transfer:** Choose one tangible metaphor from the new product's everyday use. A clean cutout, two simple circular supporting tokens and a soft pink backlight are the minimum acceptable composition.

**Build:** Place an oversized transparent-background object over a radial wine/pink light on charcoal; let it extend below the panel. Position two to four circular tokens around its perimeter, with white edges where useful, and place a dark angled supporting card behind. Clip the entire scene with the 45px panel radius.

### Human editorial photography

**Use:** Rare white or deep-black editorial bands and company narratives.

**Observed:** Natural, warmly approachable portraits and people using the product appear in approximately 500px-wide rounded images. Family photography can contain a bright cyan-and-pink wall, while work scenes retain natural muted environments. The copy is a separate column rather than overprinted on the photograph.

**Transfer:** Use authentic people and a plausible everyday context, preserving a large simple subject and uncluttered composition. Pair the image with a concise explanation rather than a gallery of tiny headshots.

### Glossy assurance object

**Only for:** trust and security pages

**Use:** An oversized symbolic hero object.

**Observed:** A metallic padlock dominates the right half on near-black, with cyan rim light and magenta reflections. The bottom of the object runs beyond the hero while the white uppercase promise sits well clear on the left.

**Transfer:** Use a single protective or dependable object relevant to the new service, not a pile of unrelated security badges.

**Build:** Create a simple extruded lock, shield or enclosure in a 3D tool with a dark metallic surface, broad cyan light from above and pink light from the side. Render on transparent background over near-black. If 3D is unavailable, use one high-quality isolated object photograph with colored edge-light overlays.

### Portrait-film opening

**Only for:** company and mission storytelling

**Use:** An emotional alternative to a product-scene opening.

**Observed:** A smiling face fills the right side of a 700px-high film stage. A teal environment and darkened left field support a white 82px uppercase promise and short white paragraph; black side fades blend the image into the canvas.

**Transfer:** Use a person whose experience embodies the product promise. Maintain text separation from the face and let the image supply warmth instead of adding illustrations.

**Build:** Use a wide photograph or film frame with subject on the right, object-fit:cover, and a left-to-right dark overlay that becomes transparent before the face. Add dark side vignettes to merge with the page.

### Supporter portrait ribbon

**Only for:** company and supporter galleries

**Use:** Human credibility in a horizontal, edge-clipped gallery.

**Observed:** Square portraits approximately 367px wide sit in a moving ribbon, with 28px heavy names and 14px muted roles underneath. Partial portraits at either edge imply continuation.

**Transfer:** Use actual contributors or supporters when relevant; retain the large faces and modest caption hierarchy rather than converting them to logos.

### Monumental comparison graphic

**Only for:** quantitative comparison sections

**Use:** Visualize a strong, substantiated difference.

**Observed:** A tall pink rounded bar towers over shorter comparison bars on the dark canvas, with a dark identity tile above it and a white metric label inside. It is centered below a 54px chapter heading with substantial surrounding space.

**Transfer:** Turn one legitimate comparison into a deliberately simple dimensional graphic, not a dense analytical chart.

**Build:** Draw a few rounded vertical bars aligned to a common baseline, with one dominant pink-gradient bar and smaller contrasting bars. Put the main metric inside the leading bar and a small identity tile above it. Scale heights to actual data and keep explanatory labeling sparse.

### Icons

**Observed:** Simple solid white pictograms sit above benefit labels; compact social icons are white. Pink filled stars are 40px in rating cards. Small utility symbols include 20px and 24px solid icons and 18px pink disclosure plus signs with 2px strokes.

**Transfer:** Use a cohesive solid icon set with simple silhouettes for capability tiles. Reserve thin strokes for small disclosure controls; do not mix elaborate multicolor illustrations into the utility icon system.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Product stage opening

**Observed:** A 72px Fixed navigation overlays a dark opening field. Beneath it, a 754px-high Hero horizon holds two equal halves: an approximately 650×598px layered phone-and-card scene left and a right-aligned column of left-aligned copy. The headline occupies about a third of the viewport width, breaking into three compact uppercase lines and a final brush-lettered word; it uses display and display-brush. The white 18px paragraph follows after about 33px, with a 250×48px Primary button below. Back to front: dark-to-pink horizon, fanned rear objects, front product view, crisp copy and action, fixed navigation. Within the first 900px the full scene, headline, paragraph and action are visible, plus most of the 93px credibility band beneath. The next feature panel begins below that first screen.

**Transfer:** Default to this object-left, promise-right composition for a new landing page. Without phone imagery, use the Layered product sculpture recipe with a real interface or a tangible representation of the service. Product-detail pages can reverse the split onto angular pink backplanes; trust pages open with the Glossy assurance object and a shorter promise; company stories can use the Portrait-film opening.

**Avoid:** Do not center a small headline over a token-sized device, spread the text across the full width, or substitute a flat grid of interface thumbnails for the main scene.

**Text style:** `display` — Inter 900 · 82px/0.98 · tracking -0.01em

### Credibility strip

**Observed:** A 93px full-width charcoal band places a bold audience-count graphic, white publication marks and a compact rating group on one horizontal line. Media generally has a 40px height, with 25px breathing room above and below.

**Transfer:** Use truthful scale, recognition or rating signals in a narrow monochrome band; preserve the contrast between huge opening content and compact credibility.

## Writing

Confident, youthful and benefit-first, with warmth rather than institutional distance. Practical explanations follow the big promises, and quantitative claims are paired with qualifications.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Step or Beast names, logos, slogans, proprietary product names, celebrity identities, testimonials, user totals, financial rates, eligibility claims or regulatory assertions. All new claims, quotations and source links must belong to the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to 'you' and 'your'. The product is an active helper; 'we' is used for purpose and explanation rather than corporate self-congratulation.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are short, emphatic and visually broken into compact phrases. Support paragraphs are conversational, generally two to four sentences, with occasional opening questions and three-part reassurance clauses. Actions use short title-case verb phrases.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A bold audience-centered aspiration or direct assurance, usually uppercase. | 3–8 words | “BANKING FOR THE NEXT GENERATION”<br>“YOUR SECURITY IS OUR TOP PRIORITY”<br>“YOU DESERVE BETTER” |
| Benefit heading | A concrete verb-led gain, often with a number that makes the benefit tangible. | 3–8 words | “Earn 1% cashback on everything”<br>“You decide how much you borrow” |
| Supporting paragraph | Start from the reader's situation, then explain the practical improvement in plain language. | 20–65 words | “Your everyday spending deserves a reward. Get unlimited cashback on every purchase with [Brand] Black.”<br>“Your [Brand] account is protected with biometric security. Only you can access your account.” |
| Chapter heading | An uppercase invitation, question or compact category phrase. | 1–7 words | “HOW DOES YOUR BANK STACK UP?”<br>“BORROWING WITH BENEFITS” |
| Primary action | Direct verb plus immediate destination or outcome. | 2–4 words | “Get Started”<br>“Apply Now”<br>“Read The Announcement” |
| Objection heading | Friendly, anticipatory language rather than a bureaucratic label. | 5–7 words | “WE KNOW WHAT YOU'RE WONDERING” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your; build; earn; get; grow; simple; in control; deserve better; everyday; no hassle

**Avoid:** synergistic; disruptive ecosystem; leverage solutions; exclusive luxury; guaranteed outcomes; formal banking jargon without explanation

**Rules:**

- Make each benefit title understandable without reading the paragraph.
- Pair numerical promises with a clear basis and any material qualification.
- Use practical everyday language before technical terminology.
- Keep product copy distinct from customer voice; reviews can retain their natural enthusiasm.
- Let one strong promise lead a panel instead of adding an eyebrow, subheading and multiple competing slogans.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use Inter 900 at 82px/0.98 for the opening and keep its final brush word equally forceful.
- Keep ordinary paragraphs at 18px/1.24 with white text on charcoal.
- Preserve 45px corners on large bento panels while review cards remain 12px.
- Keep the main action 48px high with 25px corners and a 250px hero width.
- Use 48px text insets and approximately 20px gutters to make major cards feel substantial.
- Stage a hero-scale product sculpture and a vivid pink typographic proof panel on every new landing page.
- Reserve 128px vertical padding for the calm photographic interludes.
- Repeat the grotesk-and-brush promise at 65px in the closing conversion block.

### Don't

- Do not turn the default dark system into a predominantly white website; white is a deliberate editorial interruption.
- Do not apply brush lettering to navigation, paragraphs or every heading; its contrast depends on restraint.
- Do not reduce benefit headings to a conventional 24px card title; the 65px treatment is a core device.
- Do not give major panels drop shadows or fine outlines; their #222222 fill supplies separation from #111111.
- Do not use the 45px bento radius for testimonial cards, tags or store badges; those have separate silhouettes.
- Do not fill supporting paragraphs with muted gray; reserve #a9a9a9 for low-priority utility and attribution text.
- Do not replace tactile scenes with tiny flat line illustrations scattered through every card.
- Do not enlarge footer links to compete with the 65px closing promise; utility navigation remains 14px.

## How to use this guide
