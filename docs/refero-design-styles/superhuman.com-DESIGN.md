# Superhuman — work in technicolor

## Overview

Warm, human-centered software marketing pairs soft off-white space with deep plum, wine and forest stages. A lightly weighted contemporary sans gives the page an approachable, editorial cadence, while luminous collages and floating interface panels make everyday work feel expansive. The overall density is spacious, with compact navigation and concentrated product demonstrations; corporate and pricing variants add photography and restrained serif testimonials.

### Essentials

- Headlines sound conversational and look almost weightless: default 49px Super Sans VF, weight 460, line height 1.2 and tracking -0.0275em; benefit and context often share a heading in contrasting text tones.
- The opening is a product theater, not a giant slogan: a 650px dark split stage places a two-line 49px promise and compact actions at left, an enlarged interface at right, and 57px product-selector rows at the lower left.
- Actions are softened rectangles rather than pills: 52px tall, 12px corners, 16px/1 semibold text, 20px horizontal padding; compact header and opening actions use 36px height and 8px corners.
- Color lives in bounded scenes: off-white #f7f5f2 carries the long reading stretches, while plum #281647, wine #421d24, forest #0c4243 and lavender #e8e0ff frame demonstrations or full-width punctuation. Bright violet #714cb6 chiefly marks actions and select feature panels.
- Product proof floats over a luminous world: white UI panels overlap translucent panels, cloudlike color or blurred light, clipped into 12px rounded stages. Include a glowing scene even without product imagery by pairing a simplified outcome panel with layered gradient planes.
- The ending has two scales of brand presence: a short luminous closing band with a 49px line and outlined action, then a wine-colored directory with a low-contrast wordmark spanning 1280px. Use the new product's own name as the monumental typographic artwork.

## Typography

- **Super Sans VF** — Marketing headings, body copy, navigation and actions. Open, rounded contemporary sans with a friendly single-storey feel and restrained variable weights. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use variable weights 200–700 and retain the tight display tracking. Font stack: "Super Sans VF", "DM Sans", system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", sans-serif.
- **Super Serif VF** (only for pricing testimonials) — Customer quotations and role descriptions within the blue proof band. Readable editorial serif that makes customer voices distinct from the sans interface. If unavailable, use **Source Serif 4**. Use regular text weight with compact 18px sizing. Font stack: "Super Serif VF", "Source Serif 4", Georgia, Times, serif.

**Notes:**

- Headlines use -0.0275em tracking consistently from 25px to 76px. Body copy instead uses -0.0075em, and navigation and actions have normal tracking.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy: broad 96px section padding and 80px media lead-ins surround relatively compact cards and actions.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **layout step** 8px — Dominant layout rhythm; 12px caption gaps and 20px button insets are deliberate smaller exceptions.

| Value | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 8px | Tight heading/description separation inside compact cards. |
| 12px | Eyebrow to media-panel description. |
| 16px | Action-pair gap, directory link gap and card content gap. |
| 24px | Headline to paragraph; pricing card padding. |
| 32px | Card padding and default grid gutter. |
| 40px | Corporate hero support-to-media/action gap. |
| 48px | Support paragraph to primary action group in section introductions. |
| 64px | Large split-column gutter and footer top padding. |
| 80px | Heading/action group to product media or editorial card rows. |

| 128px | Extra separation between narrow enterprise copy and large media. |

### Containers and grids

- **marketing-container**: max width 1280px · side padding 80px. Centered desktop content within a 1440px viewport. Full-bleed bands extend outside this content line.
- **centered-copy**: max width 900px. Centered headings, explanatory text and large quotation.
- **three-column-grid**: 3 columns · gap 32px. Editorial and feature cards, each about 405px wide at desktop.
- **split-panels**: 2 columns · gap 32px. Two 624px media cards or asymmetrical 728px/520px product mosaic.
- **split-editorial**: 2 columns · gap 64px. Balanced 608px copy/media opening or 576px square demonstration with narrower supporting copy.
- **catalog-grid** (only for category pages): 4 columns · gap 32px. 296px white capability cards on stone.
- **comparison-container** (only for pricing pages): max width 1080px. Narrower feature matrix with a broad description column and four 170px plan columns.
- **faq-container** (only for question-and-answer sections): max width 818px. Single centered stack of full-width accordion triggers.

## Imagery and graphics

### Luminous interface theater

**Use:** Opening and major product demonstrations; a defining visual device.

**Observed:** Large white interface panels float in front of blurred red, violet and blue light. Nested layers, translucent receding windows and selective fading direct attention to a single readable action. Some scenes combine purple geometry, peach cloud photography and fine dotted orbital arcs. The art often occupies half the viewport or most of a wide tile.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's real outcome UI. Without finished media, create one convincing white output panel over a cropped, simplified interface and a luminous layered backdrop; the glow and clear focal action remain essential.

**Build:** Within an overflow-clipped 12px stage, lay a plum base, oversized violet and lilac radial gradients and a red-violet translucent ellipse toward the upper right. Blur gradient shapes by 40–80px, then overlay two offset translucent interface planes and one sharp white foreground card. Add an SVG dotted elliptical arc using white at 35% opacity behind the card. Use existing palette hues and alpha; exact glow stops are not essential.

### Luminous closing field

**Use:** Short full-width closing band.

**Observed:** A 251px-high field of out-of-focus blue-violet streaks sweeps diagonally across deep green. White headline is left-aligned and an outlined lilac button is right-aligned; the background is soft enough to support both.

**Transfer:** Include this atmospheric punctuation even when no product video exists.

**Build:** Start with forest; overlay three broad violet/lilac translucent ellipses, rotate them about -30deg and blur 45–70px. Position one mostly beyond the left edge and two crossing the lower middle and right edge. Keep the center and headline area dark enough for white type.

### Monumental wordmark

**Use:** Bottom of the directory on every page type.

**Observed:** A very wide uppercase wordmark fills the 1280px content width in a darker/lighter wine-on-wine contrast, with a 123px-high artwork box. It reads as background typography rather than a second navigation logo.

**Transfer:** Set the new product name as a single broad typographic silhouette; adjust letter widths or SVG viewBox to preserve its wide footprint.

**Build:** Use an uppercase heavy sans SVG text or outlined lettering fitted into a wide 1280:123 viewBox. Fill with low-opacity white over wine, tuning opacity until it is visible but clearly subordinate to directory links.

### People-and-potential collage

**Only for:** corporate and recruiting pages

**Use:** Broad centered hero art below mission or recruiting copy.

**Observed:** Natural-color people at work appear in rounded rectangular cutouts. Lavender, blue, peach and teal gradient planes overlap behind and between them; fine grids, orbital lines, translucent task labels and occasional iridescent stickers create a playful editorial collage.

**Transfer:** Use authentic work or customer photography with two or three overlapping geometric panels; keep faces legible and avoid a stock-photo mosaic with equal tiles.

**Build:** Arrange two or three rounded portrait masks at different heights over a 1280px-wide composition. Add skewed lavender/peach gradient rectangles, one polygon plane and a faint SVG line grid behind. Place a few short translucent task-label pills across panel seams. Recruiting variants can add a small iridescent star sticker.

### Editorial history and location art

**Only for:** company history and location directories

**Use:** Three-column image-led editorial cards.

**Observed:** Founders are grayscale cutouts against saturated gradient and type-texture collages. Location cards isolate monochrome skylines along the bottom edge of a tilted pastel gradient rectangle. Both treatments make ordinary photography into authored graphic objects.

**Transfer:** Use grayscale subject cutouts from the new organization's people or places, with a restrained colored geometric backing.

**Build:** For locations, mask the skyline into a transparent foreground about one third of the image height. Place a large rounded gradient rectangle behind it rotated 5–10deg. For portrait histories, place monochrome waist-up cutouts over overlapping vivid gradient rectangles and a faint repeated-text layer.

### Icons

**Observed:** Navigation uses small 14–16px carets; action links use 20px open chevrons. Marketing features use compact dark pictograms within rounded square badges around 70px; product-specific icons can have softly colored backing. Social icons precede directory labels.

**Transfer:** Choose clear single-purpose glyphs with comparable visual weight. Keep directional chevrons finer than the filled feature pictograms and retain the small badge/large text relationship.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Product theater opening

**Observed:** A 67px white Navigation sits above a 56px lavender announcement strip. Below, a 650px Deep stage splits into equal copy and product halves inside the 1280px container. The 49px heading occupies the upper-left region in two lines, with the benefit in 65% white and product identity in solid white; compact lilac action and chevron link follow. The lower-left region holds the toolkit caption and Opening selector. On the right, luminous artwork fills the half-stage with an enlarged white interface panel and overlapping foreground output card; the media is intentionally cut by stage edges. Within 900px the entire stage and the next paper band's proof caption are visible; its logo grid and subsequent capability chapters fall below.

**Transfer:** Keep the same text/action/selector versus luminous demonstration balance for another product. When polished UI is unavailable, use the Luminous interface theater minimum recipe with one concrete output. Category pages instead open on lavender mist with a 61px left headline, 20px support, two actions and a 608px-wide image. Corporate pages use a centered 49px headline and broad collage below; pricing opens with a centered 61px title, suite summary and tier grid.

**Avoid:** Do not substitute a centered oversized slogan over a generic gradient, or let a full-screen video obscure the readable interface and lower-left selector.

**Text style:** `heading` — Super Sans VF 460 · 49px/1.2 · tracking -0.0275em

### Centered capability introduction

**Observed:** Paper canvas carries a small product lockup above a centered heading within 900px, then 18px support 24px below. Primary button and Chevron link follow with a 48px support-to-action gap. Product media starts after about 80px; headline pairs can place the outcome in charcoal and context in soft charcoal.

**Transfer:** Introduce a capability with a reader outcome first, then let the following media provide specific proof.

**Text style:** `heading` — Super Sans VF 460 · 49px/1.2 · tracking -0.0275em

### Product mosaic

**Observed:** Product media panel spans the container or forms a 728px large tile beside two stacked 520px tiles, with 32px gutters. Another pattern uses equal 624px tiles. Lower-left captions remain readable while interface content runs to the upper and right edges.

**Transfer:** Allocate area by importance rather than forcing all demonstrations into identical cards.

### Tabbed quick benefits

**Observed:** A horizontal row of sentence-case category tabs sits over a fine bottom rule; the active label is charcoal and others are muted. A 64px gap introduces three Capability cards with 32px gutters.

**Transfer:** Group a small set of tasks or use cases and keep one clear active category rather than exposing everything at once.

### Lavender customer pause

**Observed:** Lavender proof uses 96px vertical padding and a centered 900px quotation at 39px. One outcome phrase is weight 700; a small name/role attribution follows, then a Chevron link.

**Transfer:** Use one credible human outcome as a visual pause between substantial product chapters.

**Text style:** `quote` — Super Sans VF 460 · 39px/1.2 · tracking -0.0275em

### Split demonstration band

**Observed:** Colored band or white surface uses 96px vertical padding. Large square or landscape media takes one side; a 49px headline, short 18px paragraph and link occupy the other. The enterprise variant uses a 480px copy column and 672px media with 128px separation.

**Transfer:** Alternate text/image orientation where it helps the argument, while keeping the artwork visibly larger than a decorative illustration.

### Trust and benefit pillars

**Observed:** A centered heading and short lead sit above three unboxed columns with 70px icon badges, 31px item headings and 18px paragraphs. A recruiting variant uses forest and two 624px columns across several rows; company product summaries use the same unboxed rhythm on plum.

**Transfer:** Use this open construction for principles or assurances; reserve painted cards for discrete explorable capabilities.

**Text style:** `item-heading` — Super Sans VF 460 · 31px/1.2 · tracking -0.0275em

### Editorial proof and directories

**Observed:** Three-column editorial grids use 405px images, 24px image-to-heading and heading-to-body gaps, 31px titles and links below. History can repeat in multiple rows. Numerical company proof instead uses four 296px columns, 76px weight-200 figures, 31px labels and 18px explanations.

**Transfer:** Choose photographic cards for stories and the lighter numerical treatment for verifiable scale, not as interchangeable decoration.

**Text style:** `item-heading` — Super Sans VF 460 · 31px/1.2 · tracking -0.0275em

### Closing field and directory

**Observed:** The default close is a 251px Luminous closing field with 49px white line at left and Outline button at right, then Footer directory. Other page types use a 402–450px plain wine call with left-aligned headline, support and lilac action before the same directory. Pricing moves straight from comparison to the directory. The low-contrast Monumental wordmark completes every ending.

**Transfer:** Choose the short atmospheric close for a product landing page and the prose-led wine close for a more explanatory page; keep the wordmark base in either case.

**Text style:** `heading` — Super Sans VF 460 · 49px/1.2 · tracking -0.0275em

### Pricing comparison

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → tangible demonstration → credibility → capability chapters → human or organizational proof → focused next action → expansive directory.

## Writing

Confident, warm and practical. Aspirational language is grounded in the small frustrations and outcomes of a workday rather than in abstract technical supremacy.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Superhuman names, product identities, logos, proprietary artwork, customer quotations, company history or numerical claims. Replace all examples and claims with substantiated content for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to you and your team; the product helps, understands and adapts. Use we for company commitments and a more communal invitation in recruiting.
- **Rhythm:** Short benefit headlines, often 4–10 words, followed by one or two explanatory sentences. Occasional fragments give cadence; longer company stories remain plainspoken.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A tangible benefit paired with a familiar context or product. | 6–12 words | “Save 4+ hours each week with [Brand] Mail” |
| Capability headline | Direct verb and outcome, with a second phrase supplying context. | 5–10 words | “Get help from agents right where you work”<br>“Align your team with collaborative AI documents” |
| Supporting line | Explain what changes in the reader's workflow using concrete actions. | 18–40 words | “Go is the AI assistant that brings your agents into the tools you already use. Just type, and suggestions appear.” |
| Tile explanation | Start with the result, then a brief mechanism; fragments can emphasize immediacy. | 10–28 words | “Chat about what’s on screen with Go always by your side.”<br>“As you type, your agents underline what needs your attention. Mid-sentence. Mid-argument. Mid-mistake.” |
| Primary action | Plain verb plus product or next step. | 2–4 words | “Request a demo”<br>“View open roles” |
| Closing line | Positive invitation about the reader's next day or potential. | 5–9 words | “Make it your best workday yet”<br>“Ordinary day. Extraordinary you.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your best work; right where you work; clarity; confidence; in control; think, write, collaborate; helps; together

**Avoid:** revolutionary disruption; autonomous domination; technical jargon without a workflow; breathless exclamation; vague future-of-everything claims

**Rules:**

- Lead with the reader's outcome before naming the mechanism.
- Connect ambitious promises to a specific action or visible demonstration.
- Keep actions short and literal; use exploration links for deeper explanation.
- Describe assistance as amplifying human capability, not erasing the person.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use the 49px/1.2 weight-460 recipe for the default marketing headline.
- Keep the 1280px content line and 32px grid gutters coherent across changing surface colors.
- Pair 18px/1.5 support copy with 24px headline spacing.
- Use 52px/12px main actions and 36px/8px compact actions as two distinct sizes.
- Frame product proof in clipped 12px tiles with 32px caption insets.
- Give paper reading stretches room with 96px band padding before the next strong color block.
- Use luminous artwork and a low-contrast full-width wordmark as recurring visual signatures.
- Preserve the 76px weight-200 stat treatment when presenting company-scale proof.

### Don't

- Do not replace the rounded rectangles with 9999px pill buttons.
- Do not set all display headings in weight 700; it loses the open, conversational texture.
- Do not turn every neutral reading surface into pure white; paper and stone have distinct roles.
- Do not apply the pricing serif to default marketing headings or body paragraphs.
- Do not use strong drop shadows on every tile; most media tiles rely on flat colored backing.
- Do not distribute small saturated accent chips throughout every paragraph; concentrate color in scenes and chapter bands.
- Do not reduce all product UI to tiny full-window thumbnails; the foreground action must remain legible.
- Do not give the bottom wordmark the contrast of the white directory text.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- The opening uses a selectable product presentation; the settled state emphasizes one selector row and corresponding demonstration.
- No specific action transitions, animation durations or easing curves are established.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the active selector, headline and product proof synchronized; any animation should preserve a readable settled composition.

**Unknown:**

- Autoplay behavior, timing, transition curves and scroll-animation implementation.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- The desktop composition uses a 1440px viewport, 1280px content width and 67px persistent navigation.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve promise and action before the large demonstration; stack media tiles rather than shrinking their captions and UI to unreadable sizes.
- Reflow the directory while retaining the wide low-contrast name artwork beneath it. Treat the pricing matrix as a separate comparison experience rather than forcing four cramped cards across a phone.

**Unknown:**

- Exact breakpoints, mobile type sizes, mobile navigation behavior and narrow-screen matrix interaction.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
