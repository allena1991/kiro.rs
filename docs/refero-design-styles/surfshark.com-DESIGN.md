# Surfshark — playful protection collages

## Overview

An approachable technology style that makes invisible benefits feel tangible through expressive people, oversized props and friendly interface labels. Mostly white and pale gray, it punctuates the page with charcoal stages and bright turquoise, while berry-red actions add a commercial edge. A single contemporary grotesk carries clear, moderately spacious layouts, from playful product introductions to dense comparison tables.

### Essentials

- Confident sentences, not giant word sculptures: Inter 600 headlines at 60px/1.07 in the opening, 48px/1.21 for major section leads, and 32px/1.5 within paired feature tiles; tracking remains almost neutral.
- A hard-split theater makes the first screen: a 760px-high stage with 64px corners, charcoal through 61.91% and turquoise beyond, inset 32px from the page; left-aligned copy shares the stage with an overlapping human-and-product collage.
- Actions are compact rounded rectangles rather than universal pills: 48px height, 12px corners, 16px semibold labels and 12px 16px padding; white on the dark opening, charcoal on bright calls to action, berry red in navigation.
- Color is concentrated into stages and objects: most explanatory content sits on white or #f5f5f6, while #1ebfbf fills image panels and the bright closing invitation; do not tint every section.
- Human cutouts meet flat doodles and real interfaces: natural skin tones, turquoise or charcoal backing, white status strips and thin looping strokes; retain this collage device using a hand holding a simple product-shaped prop when elaborate photography is unavailable.
- A bright ripple invitation is a signature punctuation mark: a 1168px-wide turquoise panel with 48px corners, centered dark headline and button, and 2px concentric white circles fading outward.
- The bottom becomes a continuous charcoal information zone: a centered 48px FAQ lead, 768px-wide question stack, then a six-column footer with a subtly lighter platform-and-social strip.

## Layout

**Density:** Comfortable: spacious chapter breaks surround compact, information-rich cards and actions.

### Spacing

**Spacing system:**

- **horizontal step** 8px, horizontal — Primary horizontal gaps and padding; 16px and 32px are frequent multiples.

| Value | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 8px | Caption-to-value and compact stacked-label gaps. |
| 16px | Feature title-to-copy gap, action separation and container side padding. |
| 24px | FAQ side padding and compact card side padding. |
| 32px | Primary grid gutters, feature-row separation, card padding and lead-to-action gaps. |
| 48px | Feature text-panel side padding and opening-to-next-content breathing room. |
| 64px | Common chapter vertical padding and major block gaps. |
| 96px | Extra separation below broad section introductions and capability groups. |

### Containers and grids

- **container**: max width 1200px · side padding 16px. Centered shell with 1168px usable content; at desktop the visible content edges sit 136px from either side.
- **paired features**: max width 1168px · 2 columns · gap 32px. Equal 568px panels; alternate the text and media positions down a capability chapter.
- **benefit grid**: max width 1168px · 3 columns · gap 32px. Three 368px benefit or plan cards.
- **intro measure**: max width 968px. Centered major headings and explanatory lead paragraphs.
- **answer measure**: max width 768px. Centered FAQ stack; questions and answers remain left-aligned.
- **stage shell**: max width 1536px · side padding 32px. Broad rounded first-screen stage, wider than the regular content shell.
- **footer columns**: max width 1168px · 6 columns · gap 32px. One identity column plus compact grouped navigation columns.

## Imagery and graphics

### Human product collage

**Use:** Dominant opening object and recurring capability demonstrations.

**Observed:** Naturalistic cutout people and hands hold or interact with exaggerated flat symbols. The opening combines a leaping person, a realistic laptop, a black cartoon threat and white or black gesture lines on a turquoise/charcoal split. Media takes about half the content width, with elements crossing the split and a restrained contact shadow beneath the device.

**Transfer:** Stage a person demonstrating the new product's emotional benefit beside one recognizable product object. Minimum version: a well-cut hand or portrait, a simple device mockup, a flat benefit symbol and one status strip.

**Build:** Layer a solid backing, transparent photographic cutout, then a flat black-and-white SVG prop with turquoise detail; place the device lower and in front. Add one open looping SVG path and two or three straight accent rays around the interaction, with thin high-contrast strokes. Keep shadows limited to physical contact.

### Oversized prop tiles

**Use:** Media half of alternating feature rows.

**Observed:** Real hands hold a tape roll, wrench or small cartoon symbol against flat turquoise, gray or secondary charcoal. Foreground art is 416px high above a 352px panel, projecting 64px beyond its top; a white rounded feature-toggle label sits near the bottom. The image is larger and more playful than the compact text block beside it.

**Transfer:** Choose a familiar everyday object as a metaphor for each benefit. Preserve the scale mismatch between photographed hand and flat symbolic prop, not literal security imagery.

**Build:** Use a 24px-radius backing and a separate overflow-visible foreground layer aligned to its bottom. Back it with a thin looping path and three gesture rays. Place a small white status strip over the lower foreground; use one turquoise on-state indicator.

### Ripple field

**Use:** Signature bright invitation, repeated wherever a decisive benefit needs punctuation.

**Observed:** Five centered concentric white outline circles extend beyond the turquoise rounded panel; inner circle is strongest, outer circles fade successively. Text remains dark and unobstructed.

**Transfer:** Keep the field abstract and product-independent. This device supplies a strong branded scene even without photography.

**Build:** Inside Ripple invitation, absolutely center five 50%-radius circles of 549px, 682px, 815px, 948px and 1082px diameter. Give each a 2px white border and opacity 1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4 and 0.2 respectively; translate(-50%, -50%) and clip at the panel edge. Place text and button in a higher stacking layer.

### Everyday photographic panels

**Only for:** portfolio overview pages

**Use:** Portfolio overview feature grids and reassurance sections.

**Observed:** Warm, believable domestic scenes show people using phones or laptops, often with a teal wall or object in the scene. Framing is close, with foreground faces and hands. White outline loops, rounded translucent status labels and small UI fragments explain the benefit without taking over the photograph.

**Transfer:** Photograph the new product in real use, preserve natural room light, and overlay just one concise benefit signal.

**Build:** Fill a 24px-radius panel with edge-to-edge photography; add a single rounded status label using a faint white fill, a thin white border and high-contrast text. Use thin white SVG loops to connect the label to the relevant gesture.

### People and milestones

**Only for:** institutional and culture pages

**Use:** Team carousels and institutional storytelling.

**Observed:** Waist-up photographic portraits are isolated against secondary charcoal, then captioned on white. Timeline cards alternate gray, charcoal and turquoise and combine a large year, short paragraph and oversized photographic/illustrated prop. A dark recruiting invitation pairs text with expressive cutout people over a turquoise filmstrip motif.

**Transfer:** Use actual people and meaningful milestones. Retain the portrait-on-charcoal treatment and colorful object metaphors rather than corporate stock-photo grids.

### Icons

**Observed:** Predominantly filled SVG silhouettes, commonly 16px in navigation, 20px beside category text, 24px in actions and 32px for platform marks. Turquoise checkmarks appear in comparisons; monochrome platform marks and restrained chevrons keep utility areas neutral.

**Transfer:** Choose simple filled symbols and small chevrons. Keep expressive illustration in media panels, not in the navigation icon set.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Split theater opening

**Observed:** Below the fixed 118px header, the Split stage begins after a 16px white gap and fills the remaining first screen: 1376px wide and 760px high at desktop, with 32px outer insets. A 1168px inner composition divides into two 568px columns with a 32px gutter. On the left, Stage capsule, a three-line Display headline, a subdued two-line Lead, Light button plus secondary bundle link, and a small compatibility line are vertically centered. Copy occupies roughly the left half; the turquoise backing begins farther right, at 61.91% of the whole stage. On the right, Human product collage layers a leaping figure behind a foreground laptop and black/white doodles, crossing the color boundary. Headline, support, actions, compatibility and the entire main object are visible within 900px; the following centered introduction starts below the fold.

**Transfer:** Use this as the default landing-page opening. Without elaborate art, create the hand-plus-prop minimum collage and a clean product mockup. A lighter capability opening uses a pale-gray 64px-radius stage, 48px headline and circular portrait collage; a pricing opening uses a compact centered 40px title and term tabs; an institutional opening uses a centered identity mark, 48px mission sentence and wide collage below.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the hard split with a blurred gradient, center the default copy over the image, or let the product object displace the first-screen actions.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible capability demonstrations → supporting proof → wider product context → decisive invitation → reassurance and utility.

### Explain a flagship product through memorable demonstrations

1. **Promise** — Split theater with human/product collage and visible first-screen action.
2. **Capability chapters** — Centered introduction followed by alternating paired feature panels.
3. **Quantified proof** — Small benefit cards and circular test-score strip.
4. **Product context** — Joined capability chapters show the broader offering.
5. **Closing call** — Bright ripple invitation followed by a centered avatar quotation.
6. **Objection handling and utility** — Dark FAQ continues directly into the full footer.

**Transfer:** Default narrative for a new product landing page; place demonstrations and proof according to the buyer's questions.

### Present a broad offering without overwhelming the reader

1. **Promise** — Centered headline over a turquoise city-and-person photographic opening, small offer action and review marks.
2. **Product map** — Unequal-span bento cards summarize major benefit families.
3. **Reassurance** — Expandable benefit list beside a large lifestyle image and bright statistics.
4. **Capability chapters** — Alternating white and pale chapter bands with photo cards and a horizontal carousel.

## Writing

Friendly, confident and practical, with occasional playful everyday metaphors. Technical capability is translated into personal convenience before it is elaborated.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, logo, product names, proprietary artwork, promotional prices, download counts, certifications, guarantees, customer quotes or unsupported security claims. Replace them with the new product's truthful content. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader directly as you and your; use we or our when explaining the maker's commitment. Name the product when defining it, then return to the reader's benefit.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are short sentence-case benefit statements. Support usually takes one or two sentences; occasional brief conversational questions or fragments add levity before a factual explanation.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Direct verb plus the user's desired outcome and product category. | 5–9 words | “Secure your devices with Antivirus” |
| Supporting line | Explain the protection or convenience without interrupting ordinary life. | 12–24 words | “Experience protection against sneaky viruses and malware that doesn't interrupt your flow.” |
| Feature explanation | Start with a light everyday remark, then state the concrete mechanism. | 2–3 short sentences | “Your life is not a movie. Keep it that way. Lock your camera and get alerts if any app or website attempts to access your webcam without your permission.” |
| Primary action | Short verb plus product, plan or useful next step. | 2–4 words | “Get [Brand]”<br>“Watch video”<br>“See open positions” |
| Closing invitation | A positive personal outcome, not a technical feature list. | 5–11 words | “Take your identity security seriously”<br>“Ready for a safer online experience?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** your; easy; keep; get; set and forget; works backstage; no worries; one app; without interrupting

**Avoid:** impenetrable; military-grade hype; synergy; revolutionary ecosystem; fear-heavy catastrophe language; abstract enterprise jargon

**Rules:**

- Put the user's result in the heading and the mechanism in the paragraph.
- Use light humor to make a technical feature familiar; do not make the reader feel foolish.
- Pair quantitative proof with a clear label, attribution and any relevant qualification.
- Use sentence case in headings; reserve capitals for small category labels and short reassurance lines.
- Keep FAQ questions literal and useful rather than turning them into promotional slogans.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use Inter 600 for the 60px opening and 48px major headings.
- Keep 32px gutters consistent across paired panels and three-column grids.
- Give main feature tiles 24px corners and the opening 64px corners.
- Anchor opening copy to a 568px column with a 468px support measure.
- Let media foregrounds project 64px above their 352px feature backings.
- Use 48px-high rounded-rectangle actions and preserve the light-on-dark inversion.
- Include the turquoise ripple panel as a visual punctuation device, independent of the section order.
- Carry a continuous #16191c surface through FAQ and footer.

### Don't

- Do not replace near-neutral headline tracking with a strongly compressed -0.02em setting.
- Do not make every button a pill; 12px is the default action radius.
- Do not add soft drop shadows to every feature tile; most panels are flat.
- Do not spread berry red into large section backgrounds; it is an action and brief-emphasis color.
- Do not shrink expressive collages to tiny icons beside long text walls.
- Do not make the entire page dark; explanatory chapters rely on white and pale gray.
- Do not use 48px headlines inside every card; 24px and 32px carry card hierarchy.
- Do not give each footer group its own card or bright background; utility belongs in a restrained continuous ending.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
