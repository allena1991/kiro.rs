# Temporal — neon systems optimism

## Overview

A dark, spacious technical style with the optimism of neon science-fiction graphics rather than austere enterprise software. Fine, light grotesk headlines and lavender prose sit on charcoal, punctuated by mint markings, violet actions and luminous rainbow bands. Geometric illustrations, readable system diagrams and real interface demonstrations make it particularly suited to developer platforms and complex infrastructure; human photography brings warmth to company stories.

### Essentials

- Air-light headlines carry unusually large amounts of the story: Aeonik 68px/1.1, weight 300, -2px tracking; the monumental statement expands to 128px/1.1 without becoming bold.
- The opening is a luminous coordinate field, not a floating dashboard mockup: a 496px atmospheric stage beneath an 80px navigation, centered headline and support with 32px gaps, perspective grid, stars and diffuse violet/mint light.
- Actions look like terminal commands in sharp rectangular housings: Noto Sans Mono 16px/24px weight 500, 56px height, 14px 24px padding, violet gradient fill or a 2px gradient edge; no pill-shaped primary buttons.
- Charcoal remains the dominant canvas through the long explanatory body. Mint #59fda0 marks eyebrows and small details, violet #444ce7 and #b664ff power actions and edges; full-spectrum light is concentrated in the opening, bright proof bands and monumental statement.
- Technical optimism is drawn as geometry: fine grids, outlined boxes, directional connectors and grainy neon blocks occupy roughly half of split chapters. Include this graphic language even without product artwork by drawing a small, meaningful SVG system diagram.
- Proof changes the material, not just the copy: white quotation sheets have 32px padding and hard black 8px 8px shadows against a textured rainbow field. Include a bright proof treatment and a monumental light-weight statement somewhere in a new landing page; placement follows its story.

### Surfaces

#### Canvas · level 0

**Use:** The uninterrupted dark ground for explanatory content.

**CSS:** `background: #141414; color: #f8fafc`

**Observed:** Most chapters share the same ground without enclosing section panels.

**Transfer:** Let typography, diagrams and spacing separate chapters.

#### Hairline card · level 1

**Use:** Feature and utility content groups.

**CSS:** `background: #141414; border: 1px solid #243349; border-radius: 0px`

**Observed:** Nearly the same darkness as the canvas, defined by a faint blue edge rather than a shadow.

**Transfer:** Use for parallel ideas with consistent visual weight.

#### Ultraviolet edge · level 1

**Use:** Story cards and highlighted commercial panels.

**CSS:** `background-image: linear-gradient(#141414, #141414), linear-gradient(45deg, #444ce7, #b664ff); background-origin: border-box; background-clip: padding-box, border-box; border: 1px solid transparent; border-radius: 0px`

**Observed:** A restrained colored perimeter surrounds a dark, unraised interior.

**Transfer:** Reserve the chromatic edge for proof and conversion groups.

#### Proof sheet · level 2

**Use:** Customer quotations.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; color: #141414; padding: 32px; box-shadow: 8px 8px 0px 0px #000000`

**Observed:** Tall editorial sheets with dark readable prose and an attribution row anchored below.

**Transfer:** Allow natural height from authentic quotations; keep the hard offset.

#### Luminous atmosphere · level 0

**Use:** Opening coordinate field.

**CSS:** `background: linear-gradient(254deg, rgba(68, 76, 231, 0.35), #141414); position: relative; overflow: hidden`

**Observed:** Perspective grid and sparse stars remain visible through huge, soft magenta, violet, mint and warm light pools.

**Transfer:** Use the coordinate-field build recipe over this dark base; match the soft glow rather than treating it as a bright flat gradient.

**Use:** Bright proof and monumental statement bands.

**CSS:** `background: radial-gradient(ellipse at 10% 0%, rgba(89, 253, 160, 0.2), transparent), linear-gradient(25deg, #444ce7, #b664ff, #fecb2f)`

**Observed:** Broad blue-violet base, pink upper middle and warm orange edge with fine grain; no grid competes with the type or quotation sheets.

**Transfer:** Use the rainbow-field build recipe to reproduce a luminous, lightly textured field rather than evenly spaced stripes.

#### Indigo catalog band · level 0

**Only for:** solution catalogs and pricing FAQs

**Use:** Long resource collections and objection handling.

**CSS:** `background: #1f203f; padding: 64px 0px`

**Observed:** A cool alternate dark ground separates dense content from the charcoal explanatory story.

**Transfer:** Keep the contained cards dark so the band remains background, not an additional card layer.

#### Technical glass frame · level 1

**Only for:** interactive product demonstrations

**Use:** Outer frame around a multi-panel demo.

**Transfer:** Contain a real stateful example with readable controls; omit irrelevant interface detail.

## Imagery and graphics

### Coordinate field

**Use:** Signature opening atmosphere.

**Observed:** Fine blue-violet perspective lines spread toward the lower edges under a sparse star texture. Soft magenta light blooms at upper right, mint-blue at lower left and a smaller warm glow at the right edge; type remains much brighter than the field.

**Transfer:** Recreate the scene with vector lines and layered light. It should read as a technical space behind the promise, not an illustration of outer-space travel.

**Build:** On a dark full-bleed clipped stage, draw an SVG grid of 1px lines in rgba(127,134,241,0.15), with rays converging above the center and increasingly separated horizontal lines toward the bottom. Scatter small white circles at 0.1–0.25 opacity. Place a roughly 1212px by 597px ellipse partly off the upper right: linear-gradient(263deg, rgba(219,53,208,0.8) 18%, rgba(182,100,255,0.64) 49%, rgba(182,100,255,0.16) 70%), blur 175px. Place a 1278px by 521px blue/mint ellipse off lower left, gradient 92deg with rgba(68,76,231,0.5) and rgba(31,241,165,0.5), also blur 175px. Add a smaller 245px by 435px yellow ellipse at the far right. Keep all glow layers behind the lines and text.

### Rainbow field

**Use:** Signature bright proof and monumental statement backdrops.

**Observed:** Full-width luminous blue at the left and base, pink/purple across the upper middle, rust-orange at the right. Very fine grain gives the surface body; gradients are broad and soft rather than separate visible shapes.

**Transfer:** Make an equivalent field for a proof interruption and a monumental statement, keeping white quotation sheets or off-white type legible.

**Build:** Layer large blurred radial pools over a violet base: cool blue at left, orchid and magenta across the upper middle, warm yellow/red toward the right. Use 150–200px blur and overlap the pools widely; add an SVG fractal-noise tile at very low opacity with a 160px repeat. Match the broad light distribution; exact stops are not essential.

### Neon geometric vignettes

**Use:** Signature conceptual art beside explanatory chapters.

**Observed:** Rectangular neon ribbons, a dark arch with lavender stairs, outlined grids, sparkle forms and simplified devices. Saturated mint, magenta and violet appear as local blocks on dark ground, with fine grain and modest glow rather than photorealistic 3D. Art typically occupies about 546–577px width beside a narrower text column.

**Transfer:** Draw one metaphor for the chapter's benefit. Minimum version: one dark outlined geometric object, a coordinate-grid patch and two vivid colored ribbons, composed with open space around them.

**Build:** Use SVG rectangles, arches, stepped polygons and thin strokes; keep a dark center shape, a violet outline and a 1px grid behind. Cross the composition with two horizontal mint-to-violet or pink-to-violet bars. Overlay a faint SVG noise texture. A four-point star can use a lime center with soft low-opacity halo; avoid turning every edge into a glow.

### System diagrams

**Use:** Explanations of how product parts relate.

**Observed:** Flat schematic boxes on the charcoal canvas with small mono labels, thin white connectors, mint application boundaries and indigo service regions. One diagram develops progressively over several explanatory chapters; later art includes two equal hosting blocks joined by angular routes.

**Transfer:** Show the actual new product's entities and direction of work. Keep a legible small system instead of substituting a generic network cloud.

**Build:** Use SVG with 1px connectors, arrowheads and dashed outer boundaries; combine dark rectangles with mint outlines and indigo fills. Add only short labels and a few schematic icons. Reuse the same entities in later chapters while adding one meaningful relationship.

### Product demonstration

**Only for:** interactive product sections

**Use:** Direct behavioral proof below a promise or alongside an explanation.

### Culture emblems

### Timeline history

**Only for:** company-history sections

**Use:** Compact graphical chronology between editorial chapters.

### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly 24px action icons and 32px feature icons; white or indigo on actions, with occasional mint signals. Technical pictograms combine fine outlines and small solid forms; outlined icons use round caps around 1.25–2px. Arrows point horizontally and play circles are mint.

**Transfer:** Choose simple schematic icons with consistent stroke, align them with the mono actions and use two-color mint/violet treatment sparingly in feature cards.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Luminous promise opening

**Observed:** A 44px violet announcement strip sits above Navigation. Beneath it, a 496px Luminous atmosphere stage uses 112px vertical padding. Back to front: dark violet ground, clipped colored light pools, perspective grid and sparse stars, then centered text and actions. The 68px light headline spans most of the content field in one line, support sits 32px below in two lavender lines, and a Primary button plus Ghost action follow after another 32px. There is no foreground product object: the atmosphere itself fills the stage. Within the first 900px the complete headline, support, actions, a 104px monochrome logo strip and the next 68px demonstration heading are visible; the large demonstration body falls below.

**Transfer:** Use a concise high-confidence promise with a matching coordinate-field scene, even if the product has no hero media. Keep the glow and thin type as the first impression. Solution pages instead use a left-aligned promise beside a technical command panel on a star field; company pages use subdued grid/stars plus a photo collage; recruiting pages use a clipped photo wall; commercial pages use a plain charcoal split with a moving capability pill cloud.

**Avoid:** Do not insert a giant device mockup into this centered composition, shrink the promise into a small eyebrow, or replace the delicate field with a solid bright-purple rectangle.

**Text style:** `display` — Aeonik 300 · 68px/1.1 · tracking -0.0294em

### Explanatory split chapter

**Observed:** Canvas chapters use 64px vertical padding, a 459px text column with display headline and 24px gap to body, related paragraphs separated by 12px, and vertically centered media approximately 577px wide. Eyebrow appears 24px before a headline when useful. Actions sit below prose rather than across the illustration.

**Transfer:** Explain one mechanism or benefit per chapter; let a progression of diagrams add understanding without changing the base layout.

**Text style:** `display` — Aeonik 300 · 68px/1.1 · tracking -0.0294em

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → direct or social proof → mechanism and capability chapters → deeper evidence → relevant invitation → operational footer.

### Flagship platform landing page

1. **Promise** — Centered luminous coordinate field and paired actions, then moving monochrome logos.
2. **Product demonstration** — Large interactive example demonstrates a failure and recovery before detailed explanation.
3. **Mechanism** — Repeated split chapters develop diagrams alongside light display headlines and lavender prose.
4. **Breadth and proof** — Feature grid, video story cards and bright white quotation sheets.
5. **Confidence** — Open development, production maturity and operating choices expressed in split chapters.
6. **Closing call** — Monumental statement on rainbow light, outlined actions, then operational footer.

**Transfer:** Lead with a result, prove it in action, and only then unpack the system; adapt the number of chapters to the product.

### Commercial evaluation page

2. **Pricing** — Split main offer and volume pricing panel, then support add-on columns.

## Writing

Confident and technically specific, with playful relief from engineering frustration. The promise is bold, but the supporting explanation names mechanisms, failure conditions and practical next steps.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Temporal names, logos, proprietary artwork, customer names, quotations, uptime claims, pricing, credits, counts or production-history claims. Examples illustrate tone only; all new claims require the new product's own support.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the builder as you and your; use we for the people behind the product and the product name for technical behavior.
- **Rhythm:** Short assertive headlines, often 5–10 words, followed by longer explanatory sentences. Use compact negative lists for relief, concrete verbs for actions and occasional conversational challenges. Company prose is warmer and more narrative than commercial prose.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | State a strong outcome in direct language, with the reader's task implied. | 6–10 words | “The world’s best AI runs on [Brand]”<br>“Build and run the most capable AI” |
| Mechanism heading | Start with a verb and end with the pain removed. | 6–11 words | “Write code as if failure doesn’t exist”<br>“Replace your brittle state machines” |
| Relief line | A crisp list of problems that no longer need attention. | One sentence | “No lost progress, no orphaned processes, and no manual recovery required.”<br>“No commitment. No minimums.” |
| Section marking | A small, plain-language frame for what follows. | 3–6 words | “As Reliable as Gravity”<br>“Deploy it your way” |
| Primary action | A clear verb and immediate destination or result. | 2–6 words | “Get Started for Free”<br>“Watch a Demo”<br>“View Job Listings” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; run; code; reliable; recover; your; automatically; in production; plain-language contrasts; specific mechanisms

**Avoid:** unexplained transformation jargon; vague AI superlatives without proof; ornate metaphors in technical explanations; forced corporate enthusiasm

**Rules:**

- Pair an ambitious headline with a concrete account of how the result happens.
- Explain the failure case before naming the mechanism that handles it.
- Keep monospace labels terse; let prose carry nuance.
- Use real operating details in pricing, benefits and proof rather than a generic list of advantages.
- Reserve conversational playfulness for headlines and short relief lines, not billing definitions.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 68px weight-300 display type for major promises and section leads, preserving -2px tracking.
- Keep the 24px/1.6 lavender prose comfortably readable beside the illustrations.
- Retain 56px square-cornered actions and their monospace labels.
- Use 20px card gutters and 24px inner padding for ordinary feature content.
- Make the bright proof sheets white with 32px padding and a hard 8px black offset.
- Keep the explanatory body predominantly charcoal; concentrate luminous color in designed scenes and bands.
- Give the monumental statement its own 128px light recipe and 48px action gap.
- Distinguish pricing, recruiting and company-story variants through their scoped components rather than changing the whole brand. 

### Don't

- Do not turn every headline bold or replace the light grotesk with a heavy futuristic display face.
- Do not round primary buttons into 9999px capsules; that radius belongs to commercial capability pills.
- Do not replace faint #243349 outlines with bright white borders around every card.
- Do not apply soft elevation shadows to ordinary dark cards; only quotation sheets have the characteristic hard offset.
- Do not spread mint across large ordinary section backgrounds; it functions as a signal against charcoal.
- Do not convert 24px marketing prose into dense 16px body text merely to shorten the page.
- Do not make company statistics giant isolated numerals; the proof cards use the 32px heading treatment with descriptive nouns.
- Do not use photorealistic glossy 3D renders in place of the grainy geometric vignettes and fine schematic diagrams.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared action transitions commonly use 0.15s cubic-bezier(.4, 0, .2, 1) for color/border or box-shadow.
- Opening light ellipses declare 6s ease infinite alternate motion with staggered delays.
- Horizontal marquees declare 30s linear infinite motion; the capability pill tracks use a variable speed with linear infinite motion.
- Recruiting photo columns declare 60s linear infinite vertical carousel motion.

**Transfer:**

- Keep atmosphere slow and peripheral; the promise and actions stay stable while glows drift.
- Move repeated logos or capability tracks horizontally inside clipped regions with fading edges; do not animate the explanatory paragraphs as a ticker.
- Use the vertical photo movement only for the recruiting collage treatment.

**Unknown:**

- Exact scroll-trigger choreography and runtime animation behavior are not established.
- No general focus-visible visual treatment is established for marketing actions.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Card class variants specify three or four columns at the widest tier, two at an intermediate tier and one at the narrowest.
- Small-screen page-grid classes use one column and 20px side padding; job rows switch between stacked and horizontal arrangements.
- Navigation has separate desktop and small-screen structures; announcement strip is hidden at smaller widths.

**Transfer:**

- Preserve the light headline, grid atmosphere and clear actions when stacking; let long headlines wrap rather than squeezing them beside art.
- Stack split-chapter media after its explanation, retaining the diagram's relationships and readable labels.
- Convert recruiting photo columns to a compact clipped strip so the promise is not displaced by the collage.
- Let commercial panels and job rows stack their secondary content beneath their primary information.

**Unknown:**

- Exact breakpoint widths and mobile type sizes are not established; narrower compositions are implementation guidance.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
