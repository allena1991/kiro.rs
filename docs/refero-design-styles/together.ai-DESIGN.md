# Together AI — translucent systems playground

## Overview

An airy technical identity that makes complex infrastructure feel tangible and optimistic. Pale blue light, black geometric sans typography and tiny monospaced markings frame glossy abstract sculptures, product interfaces and candid people photography. Mostly light and spacious, it uses concentrated midnight-blue chapters for intellectual weight and compact, utilitarian controls for conversion. It suits ambitious technical platforms with both self-service and enterprise audiences.

### Essentials

- Headlines act like two-tone statements rather than posters: 64px/1.1, weight 500, -0.03em tracking; the continuation is black at 0.4 opacity while the lead stays solid.
- The opening is a balanced laboratory display: a 656px text column faces a 656×438px sculptural scene on a pale radial blue field, with 48px side margins and a subdued logo rail underneath.
- Actions are small equipment controls: 40px high, 4px corners, black or 8% black fills, and 11px uppercase mono labels with 0.05em tracking; never pill-shaped.
- Color belongs to staged objects and bounded chapters: keep the long reading canvas white, use ice-blue atmospheric bands and cyan selected surfaces, and interrupt the light page with a midnight #010120 proof chapter rather than tinting every section.
- Connected slabs are a signature device: 4px-radius cards separated by 16px are joined at mid-height by 48×10px lavender bars. Use them for related evidence, principles or technical ideas, not as ornamental dividers everywhere.
- Make a tangible abstract object and a monumental ending part of the identity: glossy translucent discs and colored extrusions can become a simple original SVG assembly; enlarge its shapes behind the closing call and the 16px-radius translucent footer, with an oversized pale wordmark below the link columns.

## Typography

- **The Future** — All marketing headings, paragraphs and navigation. Rounded geometric sans with circular bowls, open spacing in small text and tightly assembled medium-weight headings. If unavailable, use **DM Sans**. Use 400 and 500; retain negative tracking and generous round forms. Font stack: "The Future", "DM Sans", Arial, sans-serif.
- **PP Neue Montreal Mono** — Action labels, tags, metadata and technical table headings. Compact utilitarian monospace, usually medium weight and uppercase. If unavailable, use **IBM Plex Mono**. Use 500; keep labels small rather than enlarging them to body size. Font stack: "PP Neue Montreal Mono", "IBM Plex Mono", ui-monospace, monospace.

**Notes:**

- Medium weight carries the hierarchy; avoid using bold as a substitute for the scale changes.
- Tracking tightens from -0.01em in body copy to -0.03em in the display. Mono captions use almost no tracking, while action labels deliberately open to 0.05em.
- Apply opacity to the continuation span, not the whole headline. Section support is often at 0.4 opacity, while longer descriptions are at 0.7.
- The monumental footer identity is artwork fitted into an 890×205px region, not another normal text heading.

## Imagery and graphics

### Optical systems sculpture

**Use:** Signature hero object and enlarged closing backdrop.

**Observed:** A floating assembly of intersecting translucent pale-blue discs, glossy blue and purple elliptical slabs, a purple directional extrusion and a warm orange-red end plate. Sparse fine leader lines connect tiny black technical labels to the object. It fills the right half of the opening without a visible frame, with soft light and crisp bevels rather than deep dramatic shadows.

**Transfer:** Create an original abstract assembly that explains movement or connection in the new product. Minimum acceptable version is an SVG scene of layered translucent ellipses, a beveled linking bar and one warm end plate, with two or three terse callouts.

**Build:** Use an SVG viewBox around 656×438. Layer a large pale-blue circle at center, split into two offset arcs by a narrow diagonal gap. Add two tilted elliptical discs on the lower-left using blue and violet gradients; simulate thickness with a duplicate path offset 8px downward. Run a violet stepped bar diagonally toward the upper-right into a red-orange beveled polygon. Use translucent cyan fills at 0.35–0.65 opacity, narrow white rim highlights and small blurred blue shadows under intersections. Add 1px gray leader lines and uppercase mono labels outside the silhouette. For the closing background, enlarge the same original geometry until its curved edges extend beyond the viewport; soften its contrast behind the panel.

### Framed product demonstration

**Use:** Capability panel and centered product-detail opening.

**Observed:** A white interface is layered over a cyan-blue glassy geometric background, sometimes with a dark code panel overlapping the foreground. In split panels the visual is about 640×563px; in centered openings the media spans about 1416×684px with narrow outer margins and rounded edges.

**Transfer:** Use the new product's genuine interface, showing one useful task. If an interface is unavailable, use a legible architectural flow drawing on the same pale cyan glass backing, not a decorative fake dashboard.

**Build:** Create a pale cyan stage with a broad translucent diagonal blue plane and a small pink reflection in one corner. Place a white rounded application rectangle inset from the stage edges with a pale outer rim; add a smaller dark code or activity panel overlapping only if it represents the product. Keep visual backing low contrast so the interface remains dominant.

### Connected slab geometry

**Use:** Signature links between evidence cards; adapted to principle cards on company pages.

**Observed:** A short lavender horizontal bar bridges adjacent slab edges exactly at their middle. Each card's opposite edge has a small dark or canvas-colored interruption, suggesting a slot-and-tab mechanical joint.

**Transfer:** Reuse this device to make separate ideas read as one system; retain it even when no specialist imagery exists.

**Build:** Place each card relative. At 50% height add a 48×10px #bdbbff rectangle spanning the 16px gutter, overlapping each card by 16px. Cut a matching 16×10px notch from the left edge using the band background. Keep title text away from the notch region.

**Use:** Stories and organizational introductions.

**Observed:** Customer photos include sunny outdoor groups and softly lit portrait video stills; story cards darken them for white overlaid text. Company openings use very wide candid office scenes and group photographs rather than isolated stock models.

**Transfer:** Show real people in believable environments, keep natural color, and choose compositions with clear space for type.

### Compact leader portraits

**Only for:** company and team pages

**Use:** Repeatable people directory.

**Observed:** 152×172px head-and-shoulder portraits against pale neutral backgrounds sit beside names and tiny uppercase role labels. Eight entries form four columns and two rows with light top rules, not tall boxed profile cards.

### Icons

**Observed:** Utility arrows and chevrons are usually 16×16px outline SVGs with 1.5px strokes and butt caps, black on light and white on dark. Capability icons often sit in 40×40px rounded square tiles; other identity icons are 24–32px.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Split optical opening

**Observed:** A black announcement strip tops a pale Radial blue stage; Floating navigation sits in front of the atmosphere. Within the 724px hero region, the left text and right Optical systems sculpture share a 1344px content span with 48px side padding. The headline spans roughly half the page: solid first phrase and a gray continuation at identical display size. Body-lead support sits below with 24px block separation, then a 32px interval to Primary button and Secondary button. The 656×438px object is vertically centered beside the text and carries fine technical callouts. A muted trusted-logo rail runs beneath both columns. Within the first 900px the entire headline, support, actions, sculpture and logo rail are visible, plus the next section's heading; the detailed product panel starts below.

**Transfer:** Replace the statement and object with the new product's promise and an original assembly explaining its mechanism; use the SVG minimum version when bespoke 3D is unavailable. Product-detail and employment pages instead open centered on white with a 13px mono eyebrow, the same 64px display, support, actions and a wide media stage 64px below; organizational introductions can omit actions. Pricing opens directly into a left-indexed commercial heading, filters and a table rather than a sculpture hero.

**Avoid:** Do not convert this into a centered headline over a huge dashboard by default, or make the illustration an opaque rectangular card. Do not push the actions and object below the first screen.

**Text style:** `display` — The Future 500 · 64px/1.1 · tracking -0.03em

**Observed:** A 40px heading and 18px medium support line, separated by 20px, commonly centered above a 64px content gap. Major sections usually have 100px vertical padding. Editorial and dark chapters can use left-aligned headings with actions on the right.

**Only for:** company and employment pages

**Observed:** Centered white opening with near-edge-to-edge people photography. Narrative then moves through either an 800px centered statement or a two-column statement-and-paragraph block. Principle slabs use the four pastel colors and connector hinges; employment benefits use pale blue cards with 22px titles, mono sublabels and compact checkmarked lists.

**Transfer:** Substitute the organization's own operating beliefs and tangible commitments; use photography to establish scale and human context.

### Filterable opportunity list

**Only for:** employment or directory pages

**Observed:** A 40px heading leads category filters and a wide ruled list. Each row has a small left tag, a central 28px item title with tiny location metadata beneath, and a right-aligned Primary button. Rows are separated by top hairlines rather than enclosed cards; a centered load-more control follows.

**Transfer:** Keep long names in the main column and secondary details subordinate; adapt to programs, opportunities or directories.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible mechanism → evidence of depth → human or commercial proof → useful next step → atmospheric closing identity.

### Broad platform marketing

1. **Promise** — Two-tone split headline, sculptural mechanism and subdued logos.
2. **Product demonstration** — Category tabs, expanding capability rows and one shared visual stage.
3. **Authority** — Dark connected-card chapter and recognition rail.
4. **Social proof** — Wide photo story with compact numerical portrait outcomes.
5. **Freshness** — Featured editorial item beside compact updates.
6. **Closing call** — Centered action above enlarged sculpture and translucent monumental footer.

**Transfer:** Lead with the overall product promise, let visitors identify their use case, then substantiate it with expertise and outcomes.

### Focused product-detail marketing

1. **Promise** — Centered eyebrow, display claim, support and two actions above a broad interface stage.
2. **Benefits** — Three cyan feature summaries.
3. **Breadth** — Two scrolling rows of identity tiles and an inset custom-option callout.
4. **Mechanism** — Split capability accordion and diagram.
5. **Quantified proof** — Dark selector-and-chart chapter.
6. **Choice** — Four cyan option cards with best-fit lists and paired actions.
7. **Objection handling** — Security text paired with certification or partner tiles.
8. **Social proof** — Large photographic story carousel.
9. **Closing call** — Product-specific call and shared art-backed footer.

**Transfer:** Combine capability explanation, evidence and implementation choices, adapting the number of beats to the purchase decision.

### Company credibility and identity

1. **Introduction** — Centered promise above panoramic group photography.
2. **Purpose** — Narrow mission statement with a tiny uppercase label.
3. **Principles** — Four linked pastel slabs.
4. **Ecosystem proof** — Two rows of subdued partner tiles on a pale gradient.
5. **People** — Compact portrait directory with role labels and load-more control.
6. **Closing call** — Recruitment photo banner followed by the shared translucent footer.

**Transfer:** Explain why the organization exists, show how it operates, and give its people a concrete presence.

### Recruiting and opportunities

1. **Invitation** — Centered aspirational headline, two actions and candid panoramic media.
2. **Purpose** — Two-column statement and supporting paragraphs.
3. **Actionable inventory** — Category filters and roomy ruled opportunity rows.
4. **Commitments** — Four pale-blue benefit cards with check lists.
5. **Closing call** — Learn-more invitation above the shared sculptural footer.

**Transfer:** Move from belonging to practical opportunities and tangible support; retain the restrained technical typography.

### Commercial comparison and cost planning

1. **Orientation** — Left index and compact heading introduce the first table immediately.
2. **Comparison** — Filterable rates with unit labels and a batch toggle.
3. **Cost planning** — Calculator connects usage inputs to capacity and cost metrics.
4. **Buying options** — Successive table chapters with grouped columns and short fit explanations.
5. **Reassurance** — Muted trusted-logo strip after the dense catalog.
6. **Closing call** — Centered action and shared art-backed footer.

**Transfer:** Favor findability and comparable units over promotional plan cards when the offering is a large rate catalog.

## Writing

Confident, technically literate and optimistic without a hard-sell tone. Practical claims about speed, control and production readiness sit alongside inviting language about building and discovery.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, product names, customer identities, logos, proprietary artwork, research titles, performance claims, prices, statistics or quotations. The examples illustrate phrasing only; all claims and proof must belong to the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to the builder with imperatives and occasional you/your. Use we for organizational commitments and impersonal descriptive sentences for specifications.
- **Rhythm:** Short verb-led headlines and one-sentence support lines; longer explanations break into two or three direct sentences. Mono labels are terse noun phrases, while actions are usually two to four words.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | An active invitation followed by a concrete category or environment. | 3–10 words | “Build what's next”<br>“Shape the forefront of AI” |
| Support line | Define the product and state the mechanism or reassurance in plain technical language. | 8–25 words | “Full-stack AI platform, powered by cutting-edge research.”<br>“High-performance inference, powered by our in-house research. No infrastructure to manage, no long-term commitments.” |
| Section headline | Name a capability, decision or source of authority rather than a vague emotional benefit. | 2–8 words | “Grounded in cutting-edge research”<br>“Deployment options”<br>“What we offer” |
| Primary action | Verb plus tangible next step. | 2–4 words | “Start building”<br>“Browse models”<br>“See open roles” |
| Proof qualifier | A prominent result gets a precise, compact comparator underneath. | 4–12 words | “Cost reduction per turn vs. gpt-5 mini” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; production; powered by; control; performance; open; scale; purpose built; no infrastructure to manage

**Avoid:** magical one-click everything; revolutionary game-changer; effortless perfection; unqualified fastest or best; excessive exclamation marks

**Rules:**

- Put the useful capability before the implementation detail.
- For every comparative metric provide the comparison basis and relevant conditions.
- Use concrete lists to explain best-fit scenarios rather than repeating the headline.
- Keep labels noun-like and actions verb-like.
- Let technical specificity carry confidence; avoid adding superlatives to every heading.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep display text at 64px/1.1 and weight 500 with -0.03em tracking.
- Use 48px desktop side padding and 16px card gutters to align disparate chapters.
- Pair a solid opening phrase with an identically sized 0.4-opacity continuation.
- Reserve 100px vertical padding for standard chapters and 120px for the midnight proof band.
- Make connected slabs with 48×10px lavender hinges, not thin full-width connector lines.
- Use 40px square-cornered controls with 4px rounding and 11px uppercase mono labels.
- Stage detailed interfaces inside broad white panels on pale blue atmosphere.
- Finish with enlarged original sculpture, a 16px-radius translucent panel and monumental pale identity.

### Don't

- Do not use weight 700 for ordinary marketing headings; medium weight and scale provide emphasis.
- Do not turn the 40px action controls into 9999px pills or oversized consumer-app buttons.
- Do not spread the company-principle pink, peach and lilac fills across ordinary product sections.
- Do not apply the navigation's broad 75px shadow to every card; other surfaces are predominantly flat.
- Do not set descriptions or long passages in uppercase mono; reserve it for small markings.
- Do not replace photographic outcome numbers with huge freestanding dashboard counters; keep the 40px number anchored within its story.
- Do not make technical proof cards glossy gradients; the default dark slab is only white at 0.08 over midnight.
- Do not put every content item inside an outlined box; editorial rows, opportunities and team entries use rules and open space.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Navigation and product tabs declare 0.3s ease transitions on background-color, color, opacity, border-color, transform, padding, flex and width.
- Category filters declare box-shadow 0.45s, border-color 0.35s, opacity 0.35s cubic-bezier(0.215, 0.61, 0.355, 1), transform 0.35s, color 0.35s and background-color 0.35s.
- Horizontal collections declare 30s linear infinite translateX and translateXRev animations; their declared play state is paused.

**Transfer:**

- Use the declared short transitions for tab and accordion changes rather than theatrical page-wide movement.
- Preserve the horizontally continuous feel of catalog and logo rails; user-controlled carousels suit larger proof cards.

**Unknown:**

- Actual rail playback, scroll reveal choreography and sculpture animation are not established.
- Focus-visible visuals and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare width breakpoints at 479px, 584px, 767px, 768px, 991px and 992px; the product-detail styles also declare 430px.

**Transfer:**

- For narrow layouts, stack promise before sculpture while preserving the two-tone headline and nearby actions; keep the whole object legible rather than clipping its callouts.
- Turn connected horizontal slabs into an accessible horizontal sequence or a stack with adjusted hinge direction, preserving the slot-and-tab motif.
- Keep pricing units and row identities attached to values using a scrollable table; do not shrink mono labels into illegibility.
- Collapse footer columns without losing the oversized pale identity and atmospheric backdrop.

**Unknown:**

- Exact mobile font sizes, side paddings, menu behavior and stacking order are not established; these transfers are implementation guidance rather than fixed responsive measurements.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
