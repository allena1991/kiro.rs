# Udemy — human momentum in purple

## Overview

An approachable, optimistic marketing style that makes practical progress feel personal. Mostly light pages pair a rounded humanist sans with deep purple, soft gray, pale aqua and vivid violet actions. Real people, playful drawn accents and small interface overlays bring energy to spacious feature stories, while compact cards and comparison tables keep the offer concrete.

### Essentials

- A readable promise sits inside the spectacle rather than becoming the spectacle: a 40px/1 regular headline in a white 584px-wide, 24px-radius panel over a deep-purple photographic stage.
- People are outlined with playful momentum: combine a large natural-light cutout with broad organic masks and mint, yellow and white hand-drawn accents; use a product object with the same outline-and-mask treatment when people are unavailable.
- Purple is punctuation and theater, not the entire page: concentrate it in the 579px opening scene and 48px-high conversion buttons; return to white feature chapters and pale-gray proof bands through most of the body.
- Actions are compact rounded rectangles, not pills: 8px corners, 12px horizontal padding and 16px/1.2 bold labels for main actions; reserve pill silhouettes for search and category selectors.
- Feature stories are bigger than their surrounding furniture: regular 42px/1.2 headings face 576×326px human-and-interface scenes, with 96px between chapters; utility section headings step down to 32px/1.1 medium.
- Proof has two deliberately different scales: near the opening, four compact 16px/1.2 metric labels with 14px/1.6 explanations sit in a dark translucent strip; testimonials use white rounded cards and an oversized purple quotation mark.
- The finish feels like a useful destination: pale-gray objection handling gives way to an ink-colored directory, a darker corporate-link tier and a ruled bottom row, using 48px side padding and 14px/1.6 links.

## Typography

- **Udemy Sans** — All default marketing headings, body, labels and controls. Open, friendly humanist sans with a generous x-height; regular display text, light paragraphs and medium compact headings. If unavailable, use **Open Sans**. Use 300, 400, 500 and 700; preserve the normal tracking and compact display leading. Font stack: "Udemy Sans", "Open Sans", "Noto Sans JP", Vazirmatn, "SF Pro Text", -apple-system, system-ui, Roboto, "Segoe UI", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif.
- **Udemy** (only for enterprise marketing) — Enterprise editorial headings and actions; regular and bold cuts form one family. Plainspoken sans, with regular large headings and emphatic bold calls to action. If unavailable, use **Open Sans**. Use 400 and 700 with the wider enterprise heading measures. Font stack: "Udemy", "Open Sans", system-ui, Roboto, "Segoe UI", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif.

## Layout

**Density:** Airy feature storytelling surrounding compact transactional groups; 96px chapter gaps contrast with 16px card grids.

### Spacing

- **layout step** 8px — Dominant larger layout intervals; 4px and 12px remain for compact labels and controls.

### Containers and grids

- **marketing container**: max width 1296px · side padding 24px. Default centered landing-page shell; produces 1248px usable width at desktop.
- **feature split**: 2 columns · gap 96px. Two 576px content regions within the default shell: human-product imagery and benefit text alternate sides.
- **testimonial grid**: 3 columns · gap 16px. Three equally weighted quote panels within the default shell.
- **comparison grid**: max width 1100px · 2 columns · gap 32px. Two 534px-wide plan cards; grid uses repeat(auto-fit, minmax(240px, 1fr)).
- **question column**: max width 600px. Narrow centered question list; broad catalog variant expands to 1034px.
- **discovery container** (only for catalog and discovery pages): max width 1340px · side padding 24px. Wider catalog shell with 1292px usable width.
- **enterprise container** (only for enterprise marketing): max width 1160px. Centered editorial shell with broad photographic bands; the opening headline and support occupy unequal columns.

## Imagery and graphics

### Outlined human stage

**Use:** Signature opening art for a marketing landing page.

**Observed:** A smiling person absorbed in a phone fills the right half, extending behind the offer panel. The natural photographic cutout sits in broad looping organic photo masks on deep purple; a mint contour follows the silhouette, yellow and white strokes suggest motion, and a small expressive sticker adds wit. The image is larger than the text panel and reaches the top and bottom of the scene.

**Transfer:** Use a real person engaging with the new product, or a single product object if no appropriate portrait exists. Preserve the large cutout, photographic organic mask, colored contour and small expressive mark rather than substituting a generic rectangular stock photo.

**Build:** Use an SVG clipPath with three broad overlapping curved lobes across the right 60% of the stage, revealing softly defocused environmental photography. Layer a sharp transparent subject above it. Draw a partial 10–14px rounded mint contour around the outer silhouette and two short 6–8px yellow and white offset strokes near the head or active object. Add one small flat SVG sticker with a dark outline near the torso or object; keep the left offer panel unmarked.

### Human and interface feature scenes

**Use:** Alternating feature chapters.

**Observed:** Natural indoor photography in a wide softly rounded frame, roughly 576×326px, with one or two small opaque white interface overlays crossing the lower or side edge. People are focused rather than posed; light is soft and neutral, with restrained purple buttons and progress accents in the overlaid UI.

**Transfer:** Show the product in a believable working context and overlay one comprehensible result, progress or interaction card. The minimum is a warm user photograph plus a single clean white status card; keep tiny interface labels subordinate.

### Sculptural category imagery

**Only for:** catalog and enterprise discovery

**Use:** Visual entrances to broad categories.

**Observed:** Colorful polished 3D objects and symbolic arrangements: layered cards emerging from a tablet, a trophy against architecture, or colorful analytic blocks on a pale set. Enterprise category tiles emphasize luminous lavender screens, abstract badges and hands on a keyboard. Large images carry more visual weight than their compact white captions.

**Transfer:** Commission or assemble a simple symbolic object scene for each category, with soft studio shadows and one dominant purple, teal or yellow accent; use a clean rendered object on a pale backdrop if a complex scene is unavailable.

### Illustrated support theater

**Only for:** recruitment marketing

**Use:** Process guidance and reassurance.

### Enterprise photo constellation

**Only for:** enterprise closing calls

**Use:** A memorable final conversion scene.

**Observed:** A centered question, short supporting paragraph, black action and widely separated small-team link are surrounded by staggered vertical and square photographs of people at work. ### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly filled, simple SVG symbols at 20px; 16px icons accompany compact actions and badges, and 48px violet quotation marks lead testimonials. Fine violet checkmarks align to the first line of benefit lists. Circular white 48px arrow controls can overlap the edge of carousels.

**Transfer:** Use a consistent filled icon family for utilities and simple slender checks for benefits. Keep colors to ink, violet, muted slate or reversed white; do not replace these with multicolor emoji.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Purple human-stage opening

**Observed:** A 72px white Navigation precedes a full-width 579px purple scene and attached 150px Dark metric strip. From back to front: Purple stage glow, broad organic photographic masks, right-weighted human cutout with drawn contour, then White offer panel anchored to the left of the 1248px content area. The panel is 584px wide with 32px inset and 24px corners; its 40px/1 regular headline wraps over two lines above an 18px/1.5 support paragraph, a violet Primary button and 14px reassurance. The image extends well beyond the right half and visually meets the panel. Within the first 900px, the full headline, support, action, human scene, all four metric explanations and the next proof-section heading are visible; testimonial cards begin below the fold.

**Transfer:** Default new marketing pages should pair one clear offer panel with an equally large human or product-object stage. If no photography is available, put a clean product-object cutout in the organic mask and retain the drawn contour and small sticker. Catalog introductions instead use a shallow cool-gray band with a 48px heading; recruitment openings use a 600px light portrait billboard and a 56px headline; enterprise openings use an editorial two-column text row over a wide photo-and-purple-art band.

**Avoid:** Do not center a giant white headline on an empty purple gradient, shrink the subject into a thumbnail, or let art compete with the panel's text.

**Text style:** `display` — Udemy Sans 400 · 40px/1

### Compact proof strip

**Observed:** Four left-aligned columns on Dark metric strip with 48px gaps and 32px vertical inset. Each combines a compact number and category label at 16px/1.2 medium, then a 14px/1.6 explanation separated by 16px.

**Transfer:** Use verified figures that explain what the buyer gets. Keep the labels modest rather than turning every figure into an oversized statistic.

**Text style:** `metric-label` — Udemy Sans 500 · 16px/1.2

### Proof cards and trust band

**Observed:** Cool band holds a centered 32px title and Quote cards in three columns with 16px gaps. A separate centered trust line uses muted 18px text above a row of grayscale logos with balanced optical sizes.

**Transfer:** Separate individual testimony from institutional trust; use either where the story needs reassurance, without mixing them into one noisy grid.

**Text style:** `heading` — Udemy Sans 500 · 32px/1.1

## Writing

Encouraging, practical and direct. The language connects personal ambition to concrete activities, using proof to support optimism rather than grandiose futurism.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Udemy names, logos, partner artwork, proprietary illustrations, quotations, customer identities, adoption numbers, prices or performance claims. Examples illustrate cadence only; all new claims must belong to the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak to the reader as you and your; use we or our when explaining support. Product descriptions can be plain and factual, while invitations address the reader directly.
- **Rhythm:** Short promises and imperative actions contrast with one or two useful explanatory sentences. Parallel benefit phrases and paired sentences give forward motion; headlines are sentence case rather than slogan-like capitals.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | A compact completeness or invitation statement, focused on the reader's next step. | 4–11 words | “The best of [Brand], all in one plan”<br>“Come teach with us” |
| Feature heading | Action plus meaningful outcome, sometimes split into two conversational sentences. | 7–12 words | “See where you stand. Practice until you're ready.”<br>“Build the AI skills employers are hiring for” |
| Support | Start with a recognizable change or need; explain the practical value without an abstract manifesto. | 15–35 words | “Equip every team to adapt, innovate, and deliver results.”<br>“Become an instructor and change lives — including your own” |
| Action | Verb plus clear destination or commitment. | 2–4 words | “Subscribe now”<br>“Request a demo”<br>“Compare plans” |
| Reassurance | Short factual fragments that answer the immediate commitment concern. | 3–8 words | “Auto-renews. Cancel anytime.” |
| Closing invitation | Ask an outcome-oriented question or invite participation directly. | 5–10 words | “What will learning do for your organization?”<br>“Become an instructor today” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; practice; ready; explore; real-world; hands-on; get started; your next step; advance; support

**Avoid:** disrupt everything; magic; revolutionary ecosystem; dominate; guaranteed success; exclusive elite access

**Rules:**

- Lead with the useful outcome, then name the mechanism and only then supporting breadth.
- Use numerals as verifiable proof of scope, not as decorative spectacle.
- Keep action wording literal: a purchase, exploration and demo request should sound different.
- Place reassurance close to the action that creates the concern.
- For enterprise copy, connect individual activity to organizational results without switching to impersonal jargon.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep default opening type at 40px/1 regular inside its 24px-radius white panel.
- Use 42px/1.2 regular headings for the spacious alternating feature stories.
- Maintain 16px card gaps and 96px between major feature chapters.
- Pair violet 48px actions with 8px corners; use 40px controls in dense navigation.
- Use compact 16px metric labels and 14px explanations in the attached opening strip.
- Retain opaque white interface overlays against natural photography.
- Separate the ink directory from the deeper #202230 corporate footer tier.
- Give the recommended plan lavender compartments, a bright ribbon and a full-width violet action.

### Don't

- Do not replace normal headline tracking with tightly compressed negative tracking.
- Do not make every heading bold: the 40px and 42px primary marketing recipes are weight 400.
- Do not turn conversion buttons into fully rounded pills; that silhouette belongs to search and selectors.
- Do not carry the dark purple stage through every body section; white feature stories should dominate the reading journey.
- Do not raise every card with a heavy black shadow; ordinary content cards rely on faint edges.
- Do not enlarge opening metric numbers to the recruitment page's 48px statistic scale.
- Do not use colorful logos in the grayscale institutional-trust row.
- Do not mix the enterprise 4px black button treatment into the default violet-action system.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
