# Unicorn Studio — violet light playground

## Overview

A near-black digital playground with smoky violet light, soft lavender lettering, and compact, tool-like controls. Tight grotesk headlines gain an expressive serif word, while abstract rendered objects and real interface demonstrations carry most of the color. It suits creative software and technically sophisticated products that want to feel approachable, playful, and ready for professional use; dense capability grids alternate with spacious cinematic moments.

### Essentials

- Headlines speak in a soft metallic voice, not bright white: Overused Grotesk at 64px/1, weight 500, -0.04em tracking, with a Sprat word at 61.4px/1 in the same lavender-gray.
- The opening is a dark light-stage rather than a split sales layout: a centered 640px headline and 500px support line float above violet energy rising from the bottom of a 670px-tall scene.
- Controls feel like small desktop-tool buttons: 3px corners, 10px 20px padding for the main action, inset edges for glass, and radial violet fills for conversion actions.
- Color behaves like emitted light: almost all structural area remains #08080a or #0d0d12; saturated purple belongs to artwork, selected actions, and a single luminous emphasis panel rather than ordinary card fills.
- Capabilities form an almost seamless instrument wall: 5px gutters, 10px card corners, 30px inner padding, centered 32px/1 headings, and a large graphic below each short support line.
- A light-born graphic and a luminous edge must anchor the visual identity: use flowing violet rays and a smoky inset frame; when no product rendering exists, construct abstract light ribbons and a glowing proof panel instead.
- The ending exhales instead of shouting: a 48px/1 invitation over a wide dark scene with a tiny horizon glow, followed by a small left brand block and restrained right-hand link columns.

## Typography

- **Overused Grotesk** — Headlines and capability titles. Compact contemporary grotesk, rounded and open, with tightly joined headline rhythm. If unavailable, use **Inter Tight**. Use weight 500 and preserve negative tracking. Font stack: "Overused Grotesk", "Inter Tight", -apple-system, system-ui, sans-serif.
- **Sprat** — One expressive word or parenthetical inside a grotesk headline. High-contrast display serif with sharp, sculptural curves; upright rather than a cursive accent. If unavailable, use **Bodoni Moda**. Use weight 500; tune width to keep the phrase in the grotesk line. Font stack: "Sprat", "Bodoni Moda", Georgia, serif.
- **-apple-system** — Body copy, navigation, prices, buttons, and footer. Familiar system sans that keeps controls utilitarian beside the display faces. Font stack: "-apple-system", system-ui, "Segoe UI", Roboto, Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif.

## Imagery and graphics

### Violet energy stage

**Use:** Signature opening backdrop and related atmospheric accents.

**Observed:** A black upper field opens into translucent violet and blue-violet ribbons rising from the lower edge. Bright folded filaments and occasional tiny star points sit within broad smoky rays. The scene spans almost the full page width; its brightest areas are below the copy.

**Transfer:** Use abstract energy shaped around the product's promise rather than a literal mascot. A layered light-ribbon composition is the minimum equivalent when a rendered scene is unavailable.

**Build:** Place an absolute SVG behind the hero in a 1430px by 670px stage. Draw 8–12 curved paths fanning upward from a low central vanishing region toward the lower sides. Use violet strokes at 0.15–0.45 alpha, widths 20px–80px and Gaussian blurs of 12px–35px. Overlay a few 2px–6px pale-lilac paths with 3px–8px blur; mask the entire field from transparent at the top to opaque near the bottom. Add 2px white points with thin crossing rays and violet halos. Keep the central headline zone dark; match the soft light rather than exact gradient stops.

### Luminous inset frame

**Use:** Signature material for an emphasized panel.

**Observed:** A rectangular violet halo appears to bend around the inner rim, with irregular smoky pooling and a dark center. It looks like light trapped behind glass rather than a conventional outer box shadow.

**Transfer:** Apply to one proof, offer, or result card; the material can carry emphasis even without product artwork.

**Build:** Inside the 10px-rounded panel, place an absolute rounded rectangle inset 2px. Give it a 10px border in rgba(142,108,228,0.45), blur 12px, and an inset lilac glow. Add several blurred elliptical highlights along the upper edge and corners in rgba(171,143,241,0.4). Cover the middle with a near-black rounded inset plane 25px from the sides. Keep content on a separate foreground layer.

### Capability objects

**Observed:** Tilted dark sample cards fan into a compact pile; other panels stage a violet marbled sphere above a dim timeline. Colored effects are concentrated inside the objects and fade into the black backing. Graphics occupy more area than the short centered copy.

**Transfer:** Show a tangible output or interaction from the new product. Use a few angled artifact cards or a single dimensional object over a faint authentic UI fragment; retain the dark backing and localized color.

**Build:** For an artifact fan, arrange 7–10 small dark rectangles with thin graphite edges around a low center, rotating each between -25deg and 25deg. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Atmospheric promise → credible product demonstration → visual capabilities → creative and human proof → offer clarity → quiet invitation and utility footer.

### A flagship product landing experience that makes sophisticated creative tooling feel usable and exciting

1. **Promise** — Centered mixed-face headline, small announcement, and glass action on a violet light stage.
2. **Social proof** — Subdued customer logos form a narrow bridge into the real interface.
3. **Product demonstration** — Large browser-framed product view with an inset active workflow.
4. **Capability chapters** — A centered transition statement gives way to joined media-led cards, a wider demonstration, and compact icon benefits.
5. **Outcome proof** — Moving creative work, short highlighted quotations, and complete customer examples vary the pace.
6. **Offer clarity** — Two adjacent plan columns; one is enclosed by smoky violet light.
7. **Closing call** — Short invitation above a tiny horizon light, followed by a widely spaced functional footer.

**Transfer:** Borrow the contrast between atmospheric invitation and concrete proof. Adjust the length and order of proof chapters to the new buying decision while retaining light-stage identity, joined panels, and a quiet ending.

## Writing

Confident creative-tool language with a practical shipping instinct. It mixes plain capability names with occasional irreverent delight, but most copy is useful rather than theatrical.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the brand name, logo, proprietary artwork, plan names, customer logos, testimonial identities or quotations, prices, effect counts, performance figures, awards, or product-specific claims. Replace them with truthful material for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use direct verbs and occasional 'your' to put the reader in control. Refer to the product by name where useful rather than speaking constantly as 'we'.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are compact, often three to eight words; support is usually one short sentence. Lists use brief noun phrases. A rare witty parenthetical has more impact than humor in every block.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | An imperative creative verb plus the thing produced, ending with a concise real-world outcome. | 5–9 words | “Craft Interactive Graphics that Ship” |
| Supporting line | Explain what can be made and the practical working medium in one sentence. | 12–20 words | “Design production-ready web components with shaders, media, 3D, and interactive motion in a visual canvas.” |
| Capability title | A concrete capability or direct performance benefit, with no elaborate setup. | 2–6 words | “Built for speed”<br>“Ship to your favorite platform” |
| Capability support | A rhythmic series of verbs or one clear benefit with a short qualifier. | 6–14 words | “Mix, match, and stack endless combos for a unique result.”<br>“Approachable for beginners, powerful for pros.” |
| Primary action | A short verb-led invitation into the activity. | 2–4 words | “Start creating”<br>“Start exploring” |
| Chapter statement | A plain journey from experimentation to a tangible result. | 8–14 words | “Go from playground to production in a few clicks.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** craft; create; ship; explore; mix, match, and stack; playground to production; powerful; in a few clicks

**Avoid:** revolutionize your business; unparalleled synergy; enterprise-grade innovation platform; magical claims without a concrete demonstration; constant exclamation marks

**Rules:**

- Name the capability before explaining its implementation.
- Pair an expressive promise with a practical detail such as an integration, workflow, or export.
- Keep technical terms when they describe real functionality; do not bury them in inflated benefit language.
- Use one restrained moment of irreverence in a broader proof statement, not in every control or list row.
