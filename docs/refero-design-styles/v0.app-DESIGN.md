# v0 — a gallery of possibilities

## Overview

An almost-white software showroom pairs tightly packed grotesk headlines with expansive breathing room. Black actions and fine gray edges keep the frame restrained while vivid interfaces supply the spectacle. It suits tools that turn intention into tangible work, balancing approachable promises with dense, credible product demonstrations.

### Essentials

- A compact promise floats above a wall of finished work: centered 64px/1 GeistSans at weight 600 and -0.05em tracking, a 576px text column, then six narrow columns of colorful output.
- Color belongs to the work, not the wrapper: #fafafa canvas, white panels, #171717 actions and #4d4d4d support text; saturated color stays inside demonstrations apart from tiny diagram connectors.
- Actions change shape with their job: 48px-high pill conversions with 20px side padding, but 32px-high navigation actions use 8px corners.
- Proof scrolls like an exhibition: a low-contrast monochrome logo ribbon with 128px edge fades and tightly spaced, rounded output tiles. Include a ribbon and a convincing output gallery; when no customer assets exist, use original workflow symbols and authored examples.
- Capabilities feel like objects on a white workbench: 16px panel corners, 1px edges, 32px padding and small monochrome icons; diagrams use thin connectors rather than decorative illustrations.
- The final ask is a small island in open space: a simple outline symbol above a 40px/48px tightly tracked line and one black pill, followed by a detached four-column footer.

## Imagery and graphics

### Output exhibition

**Use:** Signature visual proof beneath the opening and elsewhere as needed.

**Observed:** Six staggered columns mix dark dashboards, pale editorial sites, charts and commerce layouts. Each narrow image is independently rounded; heights vary substantially and the lower wall fades into the canvas. Color is rich inside the work while the surrounding page stays nearly white.

**Transfer:** Create a varied collection of plausible outputs for the new product. The minimum is several polished original examples with different densities and light/dark treatments; repeat them through staggered columns rather than substituting abstract stock art.

**Build:** At desktop, use six equal grid columns with 12px gaps and 24px outer gutters. Stack differently tall image panels in each column, offset some columns upward, clip the wall to 640px, and overlay a 192px bottom gradient from transparent to #fafafa.

### Monochrome proof ribbon

**Use:** A low-volume horizontal counterpoint to colorful output.

**Observed:** Gray customer marks repeat across a 56px-high band; 128px side masks fade to the canvas. Logos vary in width but share a modest optical height.

**Transfer:** Use relevant permissioned marks; otherwise make a ribbon of original category or workflow symbols at the same restrained contrast.

**Build:** Lay out a duplicated horizontal flex row with nonshrinking SVG marks and generous gaps; tint the SVGs gray with reduced opacity. Overlay 128px-wide left and right gradients between #fafafa and transparent.

### Workflow schematic

**Use:** Explains an underlying capability in the broad text-and-diagram panel.

**Observed:** A tiny white terminal joins a dark circular hub, then three fine colored paths branch through lock chips to device outlines. The diagram occupies roughly the right half; it stays airy, flat and mechanical.

**Transfer:** Diagram a real input, central process and several useful destinations. Use generic symbols, not a large pictorial illustration.

**Build:** Draw thin SVG paths on a transparent background: one dotted input segment, a 58px dark hub, and three gently curved outgoing paths with restrained red, green and blue strokes. Add 38px white rounded icon chips with 1px alpha-black borders; keep colored strokes confined to the connections.

### Workbench miniatures

**Only for:** interactive product entry pages

**Use:** Illustrates individual capabilities inside white cards.

**Observed:** Floating circular integration badges, isolated interface fragments and tiny grayscale output mosaics sit below brief text. Some mosaics bleed to card edges; integration fields dissolve through white edge fades. Shadows are soft and limited to these miniatures.

**Transfer:** Show one recognizable operation or relationship per illustration, using a functional UI fragment rather than arbitrary geometric decoration.

**Build:** Use clipped white 16px-radius cards. Place a few white circular symbol discs or a small UI fragment centrally below the copy. For a symbol field, repeat 40px circular badges on a grid and apply horizontal and vertical white-to-transparent edge masks.

### Portrait-format output rail

**Only for:** interactive product entry pages

**Use:** Shows that outputs work at narrow screen widths.

**Observed:** Four tall, almost phone-proportioned previews form a horizontal rail, about 340px wide and 723px tall each. The last card extends beyond the visible content edge; author and popularity captions remain outside the media.

**Transfer:** Present real narrow-screen outputs without adding ornamental device bezels.

### Icons

**Observed:** Mostly monochrome SVGs: 12px chevrons, 16px action symbols and 20px outline checklist icons with 1.5px rounded strokes. Small filled marks supplement the outline set; the closing call has a solitary outline rocket.

**Transfer:** Use a consistent simple outline family for utility icons. Preserve small optical scale and choose a closing symbol tied to the new product's outcome.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** promise → visible results → relevant capabilities → trust and human proof → decision support → isolated invitation → utility navigation

### Flagship marketing story for an organizational buyer

1. **Promise** — Centered two-line headline, support and single action.
2. **Visible results** — Monochrome proof ribbon and staggered output exhibition.
3. **Capability chapters** — Audience selector with large demo, checklist cards and workflow schematic.
4. **Social proof** — Asymmetric quotation grid.
5. **Decision support** — Three compact plans inside an inset gray band.
6. **Closing call** — Outline symbol, clipped rhythmic sentence and one pill on open canvas.
7. **Utility navigation** — Detached wordmark and four link columns.

**Transfer:** Lead with finished outcomes, explain how different users benefit, then remove trust and purchase friction before a spare final ask.

### Interactive entry and discovery

1. **Immediate participation** — Small announcement pill, direct question, input composer and starting-point chips.
2. **Product demonstration** — Browsable output examples with small author and popularity captions.
3. **Capability chapters** — White illustrative card matrix.
4. **Social proof** — Low-contrast moving marks.
5. **Product demonstration** — Tall narrow-screen output rail.
6. **Closing call** — Centered heading, support and pill, then detached four-column footer.

**Transfer:** Let users try the core action before reading the story; use examples to help them imagine a first result.

### Purchase comparison and billing clarity

1. **Orientation** — Large lightweight left-aligned title.
2. **Pricing** — Four contiguous plan columns with one neutrally highlighted choice.
3. **Decision support** — Full-width unit-cost table.
4. **Objection handling** — Large side heading beside numbered expandable questions.
5. **Utility navigation** — Sparse shared footer; no additional sales close.

**Transfer:** Keep costs and exceptions explicit, with the more technical comparison below the primary plan choice.

## Writing

Direct, optimistic and operational. Promises sound effortless, while supporting copy quickly supplies practical mechanisms rather than grand brand philosophy.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse v0 or Vercel marks, customer logos, testimonial wording, named integrations as implied partnerships, prices, numerical performance claims or compliance claims. Use substantiated facts and original examples for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Headlines often address the reader through an imperative or a direct question. Supporting text speaks about what you can do; product naming is brief and unobtrusive.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentence-case headlines, often four to seven words. Support is usually one or two compact sentences; occasional three-part, full-stop-separated phrases supply emphasis.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Compress the desired outcome into a plain verb-led idea. | 5–9 words | “Create at the speed of thought”<br>“What do you want to create?” |
| Capability heading | Name the operation directly with a verb and object. | 3–6 words | “Sync with a repo”<br>“Edit with design mode” |
| Support | Explain the action and concrete result without preamble. | 1–2 sentences | “Build better product prototypes connected to real data. Test with users early, get feedback in days—not weeks.” |
| Closing line | Use a clipped sequence of outcome verbs. | 3 short clauses | “Dream it. Build it. Ship it.” |
| Primary action | Simple familiar verb phrase rather than a sales pitch. | 2–4 words | “Get Started”<br>“All Pricing and Plans” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** create; build; ship; publish; connected; real data; one-click; secure; go from … to …

**Avoid:** revolutionary; unparalleled; synergy; magic-powered; unlock limitless potential

**Rules:**

- Lead with the thing users can accomplish; explain implementation in the support line.
- Make lists specific enough to evaluate: controls, compatibility and workflow outcomes rather than repeated adjectives.
- Keep button labels shorter than the surrounding claims.
- Use real customer phrasing for proof and exact units for commercial details.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Set the default hero at 64px/1, weight 600 and -0.05em tracking.
- Keep the main marketing content near 1200px wide while letting the six-column output wall spread outward.
- Use 48px pill actions for primary conversion and 32px compact actions in navigation.
- Group capability details inside 16px-radius white cards with 32px padding.
- Separate major modules by 72px and give substantive sections 80px vertical padding.
- Use thin monochrome icons around 16–20px with restrained text.
- Retain the sparse symbol-led final call and detached four-column utility footer.

### Don't

- Do not spread a saturated brand hue across the canvas; the colorful area is primarily the displayed work.
- Do not apply the 450-weight pricing treatment to ordinary marketing headlines.
