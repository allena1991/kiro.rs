# Weaviate — luminous wireframe gardens

## Overview

A dark technical marketing style with an unexpectedly editorial serif opening and friendly geometric sans-serif chapters. Electric green, turquoise, blue and violet live inside rounded graphic stages, while the surrounding page stays nearly black. Organic wireframe forms, clear architecture diagrams and colorful proof numbers give complex products a tangible, optimistic feeling; spacious storytelling gives way to denser comparison and resource areas.

### Essentials

- Editorial promise inside a technical garden: the default opening uses Fields Display 68px/1.25, weight 600, -0.03em tracking over a 1280px-wide, 496px-high illustrated stage with 34px corners.
- Color is contained, not spread across the canvas: keep #111111 as the dominant ground and concentrate saturated green-to-cyan and cyan-to-violet in media, 30% gradient cards, short numbers and conversion edges.
- Actions are broad rounded rectangles, not pills: the opening pairs two 300px-wide, 58px-high buttons with 10px corners and a 16px gap; the first is pale and the second translucent dark.
- Soft organisms meet engineered grids: blue translucent mesh forms and lime rounded volumes frame an open center; repeat this graphic language in a later conversion or demonstration stage. An SVG mesh of deformed ellipses is the minimum equivalent when bespoke 3D is unavailable.
- Chapters speak in mint labels and pale geometric headings: 16px uppercase eyebrows with 0.12em tracking precede 40px or 48px Plus Jakarta Sans headings, then 18px/32px Inter support.
- Proof gets a dedicated numeric lane: 820px-wide rounded testimonial cards combine prose with a 220px side column of 56px/0.92 gradient numbers; retain this device with genuine product outcomes.
- Conversion feels like choosing a route: paired 648px-wide cards put a 250px artwork strip beside a dark checklist and a gradient action; the final shared footer shifts to #090b10 with green-gradient column labels.

## Typography

- **Fields Display** — Default marketing opening headline. Full-bodied editorial serif with rounded, expressive forms; a deliberate contrast to the technical graphics. If unavailable, use **Fraunces**. Use weight 600 and tune width to keep the two-line block compact. Font stack: "Fields Display", "Fraunces", "Times New Roman", serif.
- **Plus Jakarta Sans** — Headings, eyebrows, gradient metrics and footer column headings. Friendly geometric sans with round counters; dense bold headings without an industrial condensed look. Font stack: "Plus Jakarta Sans", sans-serif.
- **Inter** — Prose, actions, navigation and captions. Neutral, highly readable sans that lets the expressive display and graphics lead. Font stack: "Inter", -apple-system, system-ui, "Segoe UI", "Noto Sans", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif.
- **SF Mono** (only for code demonstrations) — Readable code in the product demonstration. Small regular monospace with restrained syntax color. Font stack: "SF Mono", Menlo, Monaco, Consolas, "Liberation Mono", "Courier New", monospace.

## Imagery and graphics

### Wireframe garden

**Use:** Signature opening artwork, with an echo in a later demonstration or conversion stage.

**Observed:** Translucent organic blue lobes and gear-like ripples crowd the right edge; lime and yellow-green egg-shaped forms cluster at the left. Fine cyan, green and violet mesh lines reveal volume, with small glassy bubbles suspended between them. A navy-blue center with a soft green glow stays open for pale text. Art fills the wide rounded stage and is vertically clipped in conversion-card strips.

**Transfer:** Create original volumetric forms with the same translucent grid treatment. The minimum version is a carefully composed SVG of deformed wireframe ellipsoids and scalloped lobes, not a generic gradient behind text.

**Build:** Use an SVG viewBox of 0 0 1280 496, clipped to 34px corners. Place overlapping rotated ellipsoids partly beyond the left edge, with dark blue-to-green translucent fills; put three scalloped radial silhouettes partly beyond the right edge. Map curved longitudinal and latitudinal lines over each volume using cyan or lime strokes at 0.3–0.55 opacity. Back with a navy field, a green radial glow near the center-right and a soft black vignette. Add 8–12 small radial-gradient circles with a bright rim for bubbles. Keep roughly the central two thirds clear enough for the headline.

### Product demonstration stage

**Use:** Technical product proof.

**Observed:** A dark code window floats on a bright mint-to-cyan wireframe backdrop. A narrow left rail of feature tiles anchors the demonstration. Another bright stage places a before/after code comparison beside frosted benefit cards with a white divider and circular green drag handle.

**Transfer:** Show a genuine small workflow, output comparison or interface scene. If code is irrelevant, use a real task panel with the same dark inset and generous colored backing.

**Build:** Create a 984px-wide rounded backdrop, overlay the Wireframe garden mesh at low contrast and add the green-to-cyan wash. Center a dark 720px-wide inset with 24px corners and a compact tab strip. For comparisons, clip two scenes in the same frame and use a 2px white divider with a 50px green handle.

**Only for:** product and enterprise explanations

**Transfer:** Draw the new product's real inputs, processing stages and outcomes with a small set of legible nodes; preserve the dark nodes and colored grouping planes.

**Build:** Use SVG groups for 2–3 translucent rounded planes, 6–10 dark node rectangles and thin orthogonal connectors. Fill planes with low-opacity green/cyan or cyan/violet gradients; use mint line icons and pale small labels. Give the outer backing 30px corners and 40px internal clearance.

**Only for:** individual product resource grids

**Use:** Top media band of resource cards.

**Observed:** A 424px by 190px band uses subdued green-teal, violet or blue-purple gradients with faint orbit diagrams, product symbols and small geometric marks.

**Transfer:** Translate a topic into one centered symbolic motif with faint rings and lines; keep the image lower-contrast than the opening art.

**Build:** Place a line icon in the middle of a horizontal gradient field, draw 2–4 thin concentric circles and scatter a few small squares or nodes around them. Apply a slight dark radial vignette and clip to the card's upper corners.

### Icons

**Observed:** Capability icons are dimensional mint or cyan symbols inside hexagonal outlines, usually 64px. Supporting feature icons are smaller green line or solid symbols on dark rounded-square plates. Social glyphs are 20px inside 48px tiles; checks are mint and often enclosed in small rounded squares.

**Transfer:** Choose one consistent geometric icon family. Use dimensional hexagonal framing for primary capabilities and plain dark plates for supporting functions; do not assign an unrelated multicolor icon style to each section.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Illustrated editorial opening

**Observed:** A 50px indigo announcement band and 80px sticky Navigation establish a slim top edge. Beneath 40px of charcoal clearance, a 1280px-wide, 496px-high Wireframe garden stage with 34px corners sits inset from the page. Back-to-front layers are organic mesh artwork, subtle dark vignette, a centered translucent update chip, the two-line Display headline, a 720px-wide Lead and the Primary button plus Secondary button. The headline spans about two thirds of the stage, with the organisms framing its edges rather than replacing the text. Actions sit 36px below the support and 16px apart. Within the first 900px, the complete stage, actions and a row of six dark customer-logo tiles with a small tracked trust label are visible; the next capability chapter begins below that first screen.

**Transfer:** Make the first screen a bounded illustrated world with an editorial promise and two clear routes. Without bespoke media, build the SVG Wireframe garden equivalent and preserve the center's legibility. Enterprise and individual-product pages instead use a left sans-serif promise and right architecture diagram with green-gradient primary and outlined secondary actions. Pricing opens with a compact centered sans promise over full-width artwork and a small product switch; company pages use a subdued art field, centered mission and a narrow four-stat strip.

**Avoid:** Do not replace the default with an unframed full-bleed dashboard, generic purple glow or giant sans-serif headline; do not put detailed mesh behind every text line.

**Text style:** `display` — Fields Display 600 · 68px/1.25 · tracking -0.03em

**Transfer:** Give each chapter a brief category label, a benefit-led heading and one concrete support paragraph.

**Transfer:** End a marketing narrative by clarifying real next steps, such as self-service versus assisted onboarding; reuse the organic graphic language rather than adding an unrelated closing illustration.

### Trust and deployment choices

**Only for:** enterprise and pricing pages

Trust sections use two wide rounded cards with large mint certification abbreviations on tinted square plates beside a heading and short explanation.

### Artwork-backed closing call

**Only for:** product and enterprise landing pages

**Transfer:** Use this compact call when there is one principal next step, while retaining artwork at the edges and a clear dark center.

### Pricing decision sequence

**Only for:** pricing pages

**Observed:** Three Plan cards lead to a two-part calculator with a larger control panel and smaller estimated-cost panel. ### Company editorial chapters

**Only for:** company and culture pages

**Transfer:** Organize an organization's story around its own people, working principles and public work; the distinctive devices are the introduction-as-grid-cell, circular portraits and illustrated editorial ending.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** promise → tangible mechanism or capabilities → evidence of outcomes → evaluation and reduced risk → a clear next step → newsletter and directory

### Broad platform or product-family introduction

1. **promise** — Illustrated editorial opening followed by compact logo proof.
2. **capability chapters** — Colored capability cards and the metric proof track establish breadth and outcomes.
3. **product demonstration** — Large technical scene with a narrow task rail, then a bright comparison stage.
4. **closing call** — Paired route cards clarify how to begin; newsletter and directory follow.

**Transfer:** Use a strong visual promise, explain the product's breadth through bounded cards, then show a real task before asking the visitor to choose a path.

### Risk-sensitive organization evaluation

1. **promise** — Split sans-serif opening with architecture diagram, then logos.
2. **social proof** — Large testimonial track with dedicated outcome lanes.
3. **evaluation** — Deployment cards, annual-plan panels and a four-pillar support grid.
4. **objection handling** — Two trust cards answer review concerns.
5. **closing call** — Artwork-backed single contact call, then shared footer.

**Transfer:** Lead with operational dependability and make deployment, support and trust visible as distinct decisions.

### Explain and activate an individual technical product

1. **promise** — Product-name gradient line and pale sans promise beside a diagram.
2. **capabilities** — Four icon-led capability cards.
3. **mechanism** — Large diagram and explanation panel followed by three capability tiles.
4. **activation** — Four-step process beside a workflow diagram.
5. **closing call** — Artwork-backed action, then three resource cards and shared footer.

**Transfer:** Make the mechanism understandable before the activation steps, and offer learning resources after the principal action.

### Establish organizational character and credibility

1. **mission** — Centered compact mission over subdued art, paired actions and four-stat strip.
2. **human proof** — Team video and a values grid with an introductory cell.
3. **people** — Filter pills, circular-portrait cards and a recruiting action.
4. **external credibility** — Backing-logo composition and asymmetric news grid.
5. **editorial ending** — Three illustrated articles and a centered editorial action before the shared footer.

**Transfer:** Use authentic people and original editorial imagery; let the closing invite further reading rather than force a sales message.

### Help visitors compare costs and select an entry point

1. **promise** — Compact art-backed headline with product switch.
2. **pricing** — Three aligned plans, calculator and two usage-service panels.
3. **detailed evaluation** — Long category-grouped comparison table.
4. **objection handling** — Trust cards and narrow FAQ stack.
5. **closing call** — Horizontal gradient action band, split contact form and shared footer.

**Transfer:** Preserve a clear quick-choice layer above the dense reference layer and finish with both self-service and assisted paths.

## Writing

Practical, optimistic and builder-friendly. The tone makes complex infrastructure feel approachable through clear actions, concrete capabilities and credible operational details rather than grandiose futurism.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse the Weaviate name, logos, product names, artwork, customer marks, quotations, customer statistics, certifications, pricing or performance claims. Source examples demonstrate voice only; supply original content and independently support every new claim. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Address the reader as you and your team. Use we for the provider's responsibility and assistance; product names appear when clarifying a concrete mechanism.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines commonly use 5–10 words or a short parallel phrase. Support is usually one or two sentences. Action labels are short verb phrases; technical lists use compact benefit statements.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Active verbs build toward a complete outcome rather than naming a category alone. | 6–10 words | “Design, build and ship complete AI experiences” |
| Chapter headline | Parallel short statements or a benefit joined to a practical condition. | 4–9 words | “Real stories. Real results.”<br>“Easy start, boundless scale, deploy anywhere” |
| Support | Explain what the reader does, then what the product handles. | 1–2 sentences | “Spin up a cluster, point it at your data, and go. [Brand] can take care of embeddings, ranking, and auto-scaling so you can ship features, not infrastructure.” |
| Technical explanation | Contrast a common weak approach with the product's concrete model. | 2 sentences | “Memory shouldn’t be an ever-growing pile of context—it should be actively maintained. Engram treats memory as structured, evolving infrastructure.” |
| Action | Simple verb plus an obvious destination or task. | 1–4 words | “Start building”<br>“View Pricing”<br>“Browse Open Positions” |
| Closing invitation | Warm collaborative invitation followed by a specific next step. | 3–8 words | “Let's build together”<br>“Ready to get started?” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** build; ship; production-ready; managed; open source; scale; your team; get started; deploy anywhere; reliable; learn

**Avoid:** revolutionary; unleash limitless potential; magic platform; game-changing; disrupt everything; effortless perfection

**Rules:**

- Pair every broad benefit with a mechanism, operational detail or genuine example.
- Use technical terms when they clarify a task; explain unfamiliar terms in plain prose.
- Separate fast-start language from enterprise assurance language instead of mixing both in every paragraph.
- Make claims measurable where possible, and keep attribution adjacent to testimonial proof.
- Use warm invitations for closing copy and direct task language on buttons.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the default opening serif at 68px/1.25 and weight 600 while chapter headings remain geometric sans.
- Use the 1320px container and 24px card gaps to align unrelated chapters.
- Give major chapters 96px vertical padding and their visual groups about 48px clearance.
- Use 28px corners and 32px padding for colored capability cards and proof cards.
- Make a dedicated 56px numeric lane in proof cards rather than enlarging the whole quote.
- Echo the blue-and-green mesh artwork in a later stage or conversion strip.
- Keep footer columns dense with 14px/28px links and 20px gradient titles.
- Use scoped split-sans openings for enterprise and single-product explanations rather than forcing the editorial composition everywhere.

### Don't

- Do not turn the 58px opening buttons into fully rounded pills; pills belong to chips and filters.
- Do not use pure white as the page canvas because the palette depends on charcoal surroundings.
- Do not make every panel a neon gradient; neutral panels carry information-heavy groups.
- Do not apply -0.06em company tracking to normal 40px section headings.
- Do not tint ordinary paragraph text electric green; reserve mint for labels, links and short accents.
- Do not shrink the 64px capability illustrations to tiny generic bullet icons.
- Do not use broad hard black drop shadows as borders; edges are subtle alpha highlights.
- Do not replace the dense four-column directory ending with a sparse logo-only footer.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared: opening actions transition all properties over 0.2s with cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1); navigation links use 0.15s and the same curve.
- Declared: capability-card transitions use 0.3s with cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1).
- Declared: pricing cards use transform, border-color, box-shadow and background transitions of 0.25s; several company and pricing actions lift by 2px on hover.
- Proof cards form a horizontally translated continuous track. The comparison scene uses a draggable-looking central divider.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the opening buttons visually stable on hover; brighten their material instead of adding a lift.
- Use lateral movement for a proof track and material changes for cards; keep numeric proof readable during movement.

**Unknown:**

- Exact artwork animation, track timing, comparison interaction behavior and reduced-motion handling are unspecified.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- The desktop design uses four capability columns, three resource columns, two route columns and a persistent navigation strip.
- Opening CSS includes smaller headline steps of 40px and 62.4px before the 68px desktop display; support shifts from 17px to 19px.
- Grid declarations include intermediate two-column resource layouts and stacked opening actions.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the framed organic stage but move mesh density toward the edges and stack the two actions.
- Reduce four-card and three-card groups to two columns and then one; keep card padding and icon prominence rather than compressing text.
- Stack split diagrams below their promise; retain process-step order and keep connector labels legible.
- Stack route-card text and art without losing the bounded gradient edge; allow comparison tables their own horizontal scrolling region.

**Unknown:**

- Exact breakpoint-to-layout mappings and mobile menu behavior are not established; narrow-screen transformations here are design recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
