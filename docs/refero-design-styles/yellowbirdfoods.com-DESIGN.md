# Yellowbird — sunshine with a bite

## Overview

A loud, sunny, slightly mischievous style for tangible products with personality. Saturated yellow and black dominate, softened by cream product stages and full-color, close-up photography. Rubbery retro serif lettering, tightly set grotesk paragraphs and typewriter-like labels give the page the feeling of a cartoon poster crossed with a neighborhood shop. Big visual gestures alternate with dense shopping and editorial details.

### Essentials

- The opening is a cartoon poster, not a sales deck: a 540×457px character rises behind a nearly edge-to-edge 1414×238px word sculpture, with a tilted 27px label in a burst beside it.
- Headlines have two deliberately different voices: soft Gooper at 91px/1.05 with -2.6px tracking for short titles, and ABC Monument Grotesk at 60.8px/1.1 with -0.04em tracking for oversized conversational statements.
- Yellow is the architecture, not an accent: #ffe845 covers most of the page, #000000 supplies text and strong 3px edges, and #fbfaf2 stages the products; white is reserved for a contrasting editorial band. Other vivid colors live in photography and packaging.
- Actions look like little torn cartoon tickets: black, yellow uppercase 27px/1 text, 16px 24px padding and scalloped top and bottom edges rather than a conventional pill.
- Products are larger than their explanations: three 435×580px cream display windows with 40px gutters, rounded 36px corners interrupted by a 45px chamfered variant, and isolated front-facing objects.
- Handmade graphic punctuation runs through the experience: black outline characters, scalloped bursts, a repeating 67px media ticker and a staggered photographic community ribbon. Recreate these with an original object-character, burst labels and product-in-use photographs when custom campaign media is unavailable.

## Typography

- **ABC Monument Grotesk** — Statements, navigation, product names, body and actions. Plain, compact grotesk with regular weight and assertive negative tracking. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use 400 and 500; retain tight tracking and match line breaks. Font stack: "ABC Monument Grotesk", "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Gooper** — Short section titles and soft retro display lettering; italic for small collection badges and sticker-like word emphasis. Bulbous, friendly serif with heavy-looking rounded terminals even at its regular cut. If unavailable, use **Fraunces**. Use soft, heavy-looking shapes; tune optical size and width to preserve the broad silhouettes. Font stack: "Gooper", "Fraunces", Georgia, serif.
- **Pitch Sans** — Typewriter-like captions, tickers, footer links and graphic labels. Monospaced sans with conspicuous word spacing and a utilitarian printed feel. If unavailable, use **Space Mono**. Use regular for captions and bold for the 600-weight link role. Font stack: "Pitch Sans", "Space Mono", monospace.

**Notes:**

- Regular weight does most of the work. The serif's soft swollen outline supplies emphasis without making all sans headings bold.
- The 60.8px statement is a paragraph at headline scale, not a short slogan. Let it span most of the content width and wrap into several closely spaced lines.
- Graphic word sculptures are custom lettering, separate from the text scale. Fit original lettering to a shallow, almost full-width silhouette instead of stretching a normal font.
- Keep the label face visually distinct from the grotesk. Uppercase captions generally stay at 16px; footer links alone add positive 0.05em tracking.
- For the sticker word, preserve yellow fill and an approximately 3px black outline, allowing letters to lift and rotate independently.

## Surfaces and depth

### Surfaces

#### Sunshine canvas · level 0

**Use:** The default continuous page surface.

**CSS:** `background: #ffe845; color: #000000`

**Observed:** Broad uninterrupted yellow grounds the drawings, lettering and photography. Black rules divide bands without changing their fill.

**Transfer:** Use yellow as the dominant area, not merely as accents on a neutral site.

#### Cream window · level 1

**Use:** Large isolated product display.

**CSS:** `background: #fbfaf2; border-radius: 36px; overflow: hidden; outline: 3px solid #000000`

**Observed:** Portrait windows have flat cream interiors and a strong black edge. One category substitutes cut-off corners for rounded ones.

**Transfer:** Center a clear product silhouette on cream with room around it; make shape variation part of the category vocabulary.

#### Chamfered window · level 1

**Use:** A second shape in a category triptych.

**CSS:** `background: #fbfaf2; clip-path: polygon(0px 45px, 45px 0px, calc(100% - 45px) 0px, 100% 45px, 100% calc(100% - 45px), calc(100% - 45px) 100%, 45px 100%, 0px calc(100% - 45px))`

**Observed:** All four corners are cut diagonally; the black perimeter follows the cut shape.

**Transfer:** Layer the same polygon in black behind a slightly inset cream polygon to retain a clean outline.

#### Editorial paper band · level 0

**Use:** Contrasting editorial content among the yellow scenes.

**CSS:** `background: #ffffff; padding: 70px 24px 60px; border-top: 3px solid #000000; border-bottom: 3px solid #000000`

**Observed:** A full-width white interval holds a playful serif heading and three photographic article cards.

**Transfer:** Reserve white for a meaningful editorial change of pace, not alternating every section.

#### Catalog paper · level 1

**Only for:** catalog cards and range-shopping rows

**Use:** Tightly tiled shopping surface.

**CSS:** `background: #fbfaf2; outline: 3px solid #000000`

**Observed:** Square-cornered cream tiles join into a continuous black grid; there are no soft shadows.

**Transfer:** Keep the grid as a shop-like shelf rather than turning it into separated floating cards.

## Imagery and graphics

### Cartoon word poster

**Use:** The opening's dominant identity gesture.

**Observed:** A bold black rubbery word sculpture nearly fills the page width. A large smiling line-art character rises above and partly behind it. A tilted yellow burst overlaps the lettering at the right; nothing sits on a photographic background.

**Transfer:** Create original soft, swelling lettering for the new name or a short promise. Pair it with an original character related to the product. The minimum version is a clean word silhouette plus a simple expressive object face and a burst label.

**Build:** Use a responsive SVG with a wide viewBox: word outlines occupy the lower third, approximately 98% of the canvas width. Place a black-outlined character in the upper center at about 38% of page width. Use rounded joins, black filled pupils and mouth, and yellow interiors. Place a roughly 300×200px concave-scalloped burst over the word's upper-right edge, rotate its label 15deg and retain black outlines.

### Outline cartoon chapters

**Use:** Storytelling and graphic punctuation throughout the page.

**Observed:** Large black line drawings sit directly on yellow: expressive characters cook, garden, travel and interact with physical objects. Outlines are rounded, emphatic and deliberately simple, with solid black facial details and minimal white highlights.

**Transfer:** Show one new product-related action per drawing. Keep the same two-color flatness and generous scale rather than using small stock icons as illustration.

**Build:** Draw an original SVG scene using black strokes with round caps and joins, approximately 6–9px at a 673px-wide scene. Use yellow fill to erase overlapping lines and black shapes for pupils and mouths. Combine a few broad outlines, short motion marks and simple props; leave much of the background empty.

### Product portraits

**Use:** Category windows and shopping tiles.

**Observed:** Front-facing bottles, packs and pouches are isolated on warm cream. Bright packaging remains fully colored; lighting reveals glossy surfaces with restrained contact shadows. Objects stand tall and occupy most of the image height.

**Transfer:** Photograph or render the new product against cream, isolate it cleanly, and preserve a consistent viewpoint. For a nonphysical product use one simple branded object or diagram with an equally clear silhouette.

### Documentary product media

**Use:** A broad video chapter with ticker bands.

**Observed:** Close-up growing and production footage is colorful and tactile: hands, ingredients and process details fill the frame, with black side backing where the film's aspect ratio needs it. A yellow control card overlays the upper-left corner.

**Transfer:** Show real making, handling or use instead of an abstract brand montage. If video is unavailable, use a richly detailed documentary still in the same large frame.

### Editorial food and use photography

**Use:** Editorial cards and a community ribbon near the ending.

**Observed:** Photographs are close, colorful and appetizing, mixing overhead dishes, hands pouring product, casual people and exaggerated sauce drips. Article images are nearly square; community images use mixed portrait and landscape proportions, staggered vertically in a clipped horizontal ribbon with black rounded frames.

**Transfer:** Show the new product creating a sensory or practical result. Retain a mix of polished close-ups and candid use, not a uniformly pristine studio grid.

### Burst graphics

**Use:** Opening label and small editorial callouts; larger cream versions carry brand principles.

**Observed:** Scalloped bursts use concave sides rather than sharp triangular star points. Black outlines contrast yellow or cream interiors; small labels are slightly rotated, while large principle panels keep text level.

**Transfer:** Use bursts for a short promise or meaningful proof point. The larger cluster belongs to brand-principle storytelling, not every feature list.

**Build:** Construct an SVG closed path with 12–16 outward cusp points connected by inward quadratic curves. Use yellow fill for callouts or cream fill for principle panels with a 3px black outline. For a large panel target roughly 500×314px and inset text into a central 350px column. Stagger panels rather than aligning them like identical rectangular cards.

### Founder toast portrait

**Only for:** company-origin storytelling

**Use:** Personal sign-off within a narrative page.

**Observed:** A broad candid outdoor photograph shows two people clinking products together, in a slightly tilted black-outlined rounded frame. Oversized yellow, black-outlined celebratory lettering overlaps its bottom edge; a brief typewriter-like signature follows.

**Transfer:** Use an authentic human moment that demonstrates affection for the work. Pair it with one short expressive word rather than a formal executive portrait.

**Build:** Place a real photograph inside an approximately 900×620px rounded SVG or CSS frame, rotate the frame slightly, and overlap a curved yellow word with black outline at the bottom. Keep the people and gesture unobscured.

### Partner sign landscape

**Only for:** retail or partner-acquisition closing sections

**Use:** A distinctive contact invitation immediately before the shared footer.

**Observed:** White directional signboards with bold black outlines and offset black underside shapes rise from the bottom on yellow poles, each carrying a partner identity. The signs tilt at different angles beneath a centered contact heading and wavy action.

**Transfer:** Use genuine partner identities or short partner types in an original illustrated sign landscape; keep the call to action above the signs.

**Build:** Draw arrow-ended sign polygons with a notched tail, white fill and 5px black outline, then add a black copy offset down and right to suggest a chunky underside. Mount each on a narrow yellow black-outlined pole, rotate signs independently and let poles run beyond the bottom boundary.

### Icons

**Observed:** Black drawn arrows and transport symbols share the cartoons' blunt, rounded personality. Interface search and cart icons are approximately 39–48px with 2–3px rounded strokes; repeated attribute pictograms are about 13×16px with 2.1px strokes, mixing filled and outline states.

**Transfer:** Choose a consistent round-stroke family for controls, then use a single simple silhouette for repeated attribute ratings. Keep large illustrated scenes distinct from utility icons.

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Identity spectacle → plainspoken product promise → tangible choice and proof → human process or useful inspiration → community or contact → newsletter and navigation.

### Brand-led marketing landing page

1. **Identity hook** — Cartoon poster opening with monumental lettering and tilted promise burst.
2. **Promise and choice** — Oversized grotesk explanation followed by three large category windows and a shop action.
3. **Social proof** — Single monumental customer quotation in a ruled yellow band.
4. **Product process** — Ticker-framed documentary scene with compact playback overlay.
5. **Human explanation** — Illustrated split chapter with a story action.
6. **Practical inspiration** — White editorial interval with three colorful use-case articles.
7. **Community and retention** — Staggered social photo ribbon, newsletter, footer columns and legal strip.

**Transfer:** Adapt the beats to the product's strongest proof. Keep the dramatic graphic gestures but use documentary and editorial blocks only where they carry real content.

### Task-first location or availability page

1. **Task framing** — Compact serif title and conversational directions above a large locator.
2. **Community proof** — Colorful staggered use photography and follow invitation.
3. **Partner conversion** — Centered contact action over a landscape of illustrated directional signs.
4. **Retention and navigation** — Shared yellow newsletter footer and separate legal strip.

**Transfer:** Make the tool the immediate destination; use the partner invitation to serve a second audience near the end.

### Catalog browsing and comparison

2. **Story chapters** — Alternating outline scenes and large grotesk narrative paragraphs.

## Writing

Warm, cheeky and proudly enthusiastic, with the confidence of someone handing you something delicious. Specific craft and sensory language make the playfulness feel earned rather than merely quirky.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Yellowbird's name, wordmark, mascot, social handles, proprietary art, product names, customer quotations, founding history, partner identities or claims of being the world's tastiest. Replace these with truthful original assets and claims for the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak as we and our to you. Use direct invitations, contractions and occasional affectionate nicknames when they belong naturally to the new brand.
- **Rhythm:** Short punchy invitations alternate with conversational paragraphs. Headlines can be a few playful words; the core promise can be a full multi-sentence paragraph at display scale. Em dashes create lively pivots; exclamation marks belong to genuine celebration rather than every action.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | An exuberant category claim packaged like a hand-lettered slogan. | 4–7 words | “THIS WORLD’S TASTIEST HOT SAUCE” |
| Product explanation | Contrast an expected benefit with a more appealing specific one, then explain the craft behind it. | 2–3 conversational sentences | “Our hot sauce isn’t just hot—it’s delicious. We source killer ingredients for the most flavor in every squeeze.” |
| Task heading | A clear destination with a playful alliterative twist. | 3–5 words | “Find Your Fiery Flavor” |
| Primary action | A short direct verb phrase, or a friendly conversational invitation. | 1–2 words | “SHOP ALL”<br>“SAY HEY” |
| Story heading | A warm human claim made vivid by a concrete abundance. | 8–12 words | “Homegrown with love and thousands of perfectly ripe peppers.” |
| Retention invitation | An ordinary newsletter invitation with one small brand-world pun. | 4–7 words | “Join our high-flying newsletter.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** homegrown; crafted; flavor; ripe; curious; hands dirty; say hey; killer; smooches; concrete sensory verbs and affectionate humor

**Avoid:** enterprise-grade; frictionless ecosystem; unlock synergies; disruptive innovation; sterile performance jargon; manufactured urgency

**Rules:**

- Tie humor to the product's world rather than adding unrelated jokes.
- State what makes the product good in ordinary language before explaining more details.
- Practical instructions can be friendly but must stay useful; acknowledge real availability limits plainly.
- Use compact uppercase for actions and labels, sentence case for longer explanations.
- Let one pun carry a heading; keep surrounding copy straightforward.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the main canvas yellow and use black 3px edges to organize it.
- Preserve the 91px/1.05 soft-serif versus 60.8px/1.1 grotesk contrast.
- Keep the desktop word artwork nearly full width and the character at poster scale.
- Stage category objects on cream windows with 36px corners and 40px gutters.
- Use 16px typewriter-like labels as small visual anchors around oversized scenes.
- Make primary actions black scalloped tickets with 27px yellow capitals.
- Use colorful, close product-in-use photography inside rounded black-edged frames.
- Carry yellow through the shared footer, with a 3px underlined email field and compact link columns.

### Don't

- Do not reduce yellow to a button color on a mostly white layout.
- Do not add soft elevation shadows to cream cards; flat black contours provide separation.
- Do not make every headline a heavy geometric sans; the soft serif and regular grotesk have different jobs.
- Do not convert all actions into capsule pills; the scalloped ticket is the conversion signature.
- Do not round catalog tiles like category windows; the shopping matrix uses square corners and 3px seams.
- Do not introduce arbitrary pastel feature panels to create variety; use shape, photography and the white editorial interval.
- Do not set long explanatory text in the monospaced label face; it is reserved for compact markings.
- Do not replace the ending with an oversized dark footer or a generic centered email card.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Wavy buttons declare a 20s linear infinite wave animation in a paused state and a 0.4s ease-in-out transition.
- Media tickers declare 10s linear infinite marquee animation.
- One decorative element declares a 2s ease bounce followed by 5s ease-in-out infinite float with a 2s delay.
- Many media elements declare opacity transitions of 0.4s ease-in-out; catalog controls also declare 0.4s ease-in-out transitions.

**Transfer:**

- Use the repeating media ticker for the continuous-motion gesture and keep surrounding paragraphs still.
- Limit independent letter or character movement to expressive graphic moments, so the page retains its printed-poster structure.

**Unknown:**

- Exact playback triggers, wave activation, scroll choreography and reduced-motion behavior are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare width breakpoints at 600px, 700px, 749px, 750px, 768px, 769px, 1000px, 1024px and 1114px.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow screens, preserve the near-full-width word silhouette by scaling the SVG as a single scene rather than wrapping its letters into ordinary text.
- Stack illustrated chapters while keeping scene and explanation adjacent; let product rows scroll or reduce columns without shrinking product names into tiny labels.
- Wrap footer groups below the newsletter and retain its simple underlined field.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile type sizes, navigation substitution, exact column counts and breakpoint-specific behavior are not established.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
