# Zendesk — human warmth, electric lime

## Overview

A people-first technology style with warm photography, approachable grotesk typography and flashes of electric lime. Mostly white and pale stone, it shifts into forest-green demonstration stages and near-black endings. It suits complex business products that need to feel useful and easy rather than technical or intimidating; spacious storytelling surrounds compact interface fragments and practical proof.

### Essentials

- An outcome reads like a spoken promise, not a poster slogan: 68px/1.05 medium grotesk headlines, normal tracking, with 14px bold uppercase labels above and 16px/1.45 support below.
- The opening puts human experience at cinematic scale: an inset 1408px-wide, 772px-tall photographic stage with 32px corners, a person dominating the right half and a roughly 656px text column on the left. Use a warmly lit person-in-context image with a small product interaction if bespoke media is unavailable.
- Lime marks the next useful action rather than coloring the whole site: #d1f470 buttons at 48px high with 16px corners and 18px semibold labels; pair them with transparent outlined actions, not matching filled twins.
- White is the connective tissue; forest green is a destination: use 88px section padding and a 1150px container for the light majority, concentrating #203524 and green-to-#11110d atmospheres into product stages, proof bands and the closing area.
- Software is shown as a helpful fragment, not a wall of chrome: float a few white interface cards over a soft lime halo or a forest backdrop. Include a glowing product scene on every new landing page; a simple two-card workflow with a luminous edge is the minimum equivalent.
- Proof has its own scale and the ending offers a human hand: pair 40px numerals with smaller 26px percent signs in customer proof, and finish with a near-black directory plus a 42px help question and lime underlined contact phrase. Preserve these devices even with a shorter directory.

**Notes:**

- Keep display and section headings at weight 500 with normal tracking; their friendliness comes from open letterforms and wrapping, not compressed spacing.
- Use uppercase only for compact labels. A single leaf-green word can complete a black heading without changing its size or weight.
- Customer proof separates the 40px figure from its 26px suffix. Standalone animated proof can be more monumental: target the visual scale of the 68px display rather than inheriting the surrounding paragraph size.

### Containers and grids

- **Content container**: max width 1150px · side padding 24px. Centered outer container; 1102px usable content at desktop. Keep headings, proof and media tied to these edges.
- **Navigation container**: max width 1600px · side padding 24px. Wider navigation with brand at left, menus centered and actions at right.
- **Split chapter**: 2 columns · gap 24px. Balanced text/media chapters, with approximately 515px text and 445px media or 539px media and 445px accordions; leave the remaining width as breathing space.
- **Resource grid**: 4 columns · gap 24px. Four approximately 258px cards; some collections continue horizontally beyond the viewport.
- **Benefit row**: 3 columns · gap 24px. Unboxed icon-led benefits under a centered heading; four-column variant for audience-specific benefit summaries.
- **Footer directory**: 5 columns · gap 24px. Five aligned link groups above a full-width help-and-social row.

## Components

### Navigation

**Use:** Persistent orientation and conversion access.

**CSS:** `position: sticky; z-index: 10; background: rgba(255,255,255,0.9); backdrop-filter: blur(30px); border-bottom: 1px solid rgba(17,17,13,0.1)`

**Observed:** Initial header is 110px tall: a small right-aligned utility row above the main menu. The brand occupies the left, dropdown chevrons sit after menu text, and two compact actions sit at the right. Scrolled presentation is a simpler single row with a wordmark.

**Transfer:** Keep the two-tier hierarchy without reproducing the original menu taxonomy.

### Primary button

**Use:** The default conversion or forward action.

**CSS:** `background: #d1f470; color: #11110d; border-radius: 16px; padding: 10px 20px; height: 48px; box-sizing: border-box`

## Imagery and graphics

### Warm human opening

**Use:** Default landing-page hero.

**Observed:** A close portrait of a person wearing green headphones fills the right side, warmly lit in amber and natural skin tones. The left holds dark golden negative space behind pale type. Two small white conversation cards overlap the lower-right without obscuring the face; the image fills the rounded stage rather than sitting inside a device mockup.

**Transfer:** Use a real person experiencing the product's benefit, with warm directional light, a simple contextual setting and enough dark negative space for the promise. Overlay one short input-and-result exchange or equivalent product moment.

**Build:** Place a cover photograph in a 32px-radius overflow-hidden stage. Add an absolute full-size overlay with linear-gradient(to right, rgba(0,0,0,0.4) 0%, rgba(0,0,0,0.25) 35%, rgba(0,0,0,0) 60%). Put text above the overlay and stagger two white rounded interaction cards near the person's lower torso.

### Luminous product fragments

**Use:** Signature product demonstrations and capability chapters.

**Observed:** White panels, small avatars, workflow nodes and selected UI excerpts float in open space. Pale lime halos mark useful output, while dark green stages give white interfaces a luminous rim. Illustrations remain much less dense than real software screens.

**Transfer:** Compose two or three accurate interface fragments for the new product. The minimum is one request card and one resolved state with a soft green glow; retain meaningful text and an obvious causal relationship.

**Build:** On a 539px-wide artboard, layer two white rounded rectangles, offset diagonally, with a thin pale edge. Behind the important panel add a broad radial-gradient ellipse using rgba(209,244,112,0.6) at the center and transparent at the edge, blurred about 24px. On a dark stage use the Green product atmosphere surface and add a narrow translucent lime rim around the white panel. Keep shadows soft and local, not a uniform heavy card shadow.

### Connected process illustration

**Use:** Mechanism explanations.

**Observed:** Sparse floating message cards sit inside wide gray Process panels linked by a fine vertical spine. A small circular question or status symbol marks progress. Text and visual are balanced rather than packed.

**Transfer:** Translate the mechanism into a before-and-after interaction, with a clear change across the panels.

**Build:** Draw a centered 1px vertical line in rgba(17,17,13,0.18) behind the panels, fading at its ends. Add a small lime-washed circle with a dark symbol on the spine; place compact white state cards on the right side of each panel.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Photographic promise opening

**Observed:** A dark 58px promotional strip, when relevant, precedes a 110px white Navigation. The hero starts 16px below it with 16px outer side insets. Its 1408px by 772px rounded photo stage occupies nearly all the remaining first screen. Layers run from warm cover photo to left darkening overlay, pale headline and support, then small white interaction cards at right. Text aligns to the Content container, uses about 656px of width and sits vertically near the center; the person fills roughly the right half. The eyebrow precedes a three-line display promise by 8px; support follows by 24px. A short trial reassurance sits above an Email field and Primary button row. Within 900px the navigation, headline, support, form and main portrait are visible; the stage's bottom and subsequent trust row fall below the fold.

**Transfer:** For a new landing page, write an outcome-led promise and photograph a person in its real context; use a short interaction overlay to make the benefit tangible. Without custom photography, art-direct a suitable warm portrait with the same right-side mass and quiet left-side background. Product and corporate detail pages instead use a white split opening with the Light horizon surface, black display type and a 445px square image or diagram; pricing opens with a compact segmented selector, a 42px heading and comparison cards.

**Avoid:** Do not turn the default opening into a centered text-only hero or put the person in a tiny thumbnail below the headline. Avoid placing pale type across a busy face or using a full dashboard as the background.

**Text style:** `display` — Vanilla Sans 500 · 68px/1.05

## Page flow

**Storyline:** Outcome promise → tangible mechanism → breadth and proof → practical next step → reassurance → human contact.

### Broad marketing landing page

1. **Promise** — Warm photographic stage, measurable outcome and email-led trial action.
2. **Social proof** — Trust logos and a compact timely promotion.
3. **Mechanism** — Centered promise, connected process panels and aggregate figures.
4. **Capability breadth** — Alternating chapters, dark demonstration theater and paired product cards.
5. **Credibility** — Editorial validation band, customer story and integration field.
6. **Decision support** — Resource shelf and pale pricing invitation.
7. **Closing call** — Forest trial/demo invitation, compact FAQ invitation and near-black contact directory.

**Transfer:** Select enough episodes to explain one new product; retain the contrast between human imagery, mechanism and proof without inheriting the original section count.

### Purchase and plan comparison

1. **Choice framing** — Segmented audience selector and concise centered heading.
2. **Pricing** — Four aligned plan cards plus separate add-on cards.
3. **Alternative fit** — Dark split offer and a lighter entry-level program chapter.
4. **Value proof** — Logo field and savings illustration beside an explanatory action.
5. **Objection handling** — Split heading and long ruled FAQ stack.
6. **Closing call** — Green-lit invitation offers independent exploration or team assistance, followed by the shared contact directory.

**Transfer:** Make the offer comparison the hero; use proof and FAQs to resolve buying uncertainty rather than inserting a photographic marketing opening.

### Platform or technical product explanation

1. **Promise** — White split display hero with a lime horizon and square orchestration diagram.
2. **Benefits** — Three icon-led outcomes and a chapter jump list.

## Writing

Confident, practical and warmly conversational. Technical capability is translated into a useful outcome, then supported by concrete mechanisms, credible numbers or named experience.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Zendesk names, logos, proprietary product terms, campaign names, customer marks, quotations, performance figures, pricing, awards or factual claims. Source examples illustrate tone and structure only; use verified facts and original content for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Use you and your for benefits and next steps; use we for commitments and human reassurance. Product descriptions can name the product directly rather than speaking in an abstract corporate voice.
- **Rhythm:** Headlines are short spoken clauses, often 4–10 words; a larger promise can extend across three lines. Support usually takes one compact paragraph. Use crisp parallel fragments for benefits and short verb-led actions.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening promise | Lead with a concrete improvement, not a list of technologies. | 6–14 words | “Automate up to 80% of interactions with [Brand] AI agents.”<br>“AI that delivers real employee resolutions” |
| Chapter headline | Link a capability to a practical result; paired clauses can add a human beat. | 4–10 words | “Connect your ecosystem. Your way.”<br>“Set up fast, resolve even faster” |
| Support | Explain the mechanism and outcome in plain language, with proof when available. | 15–45 words | “Built to improve with every resolution. Proven across 4.8B+ resolutions.” |
| Action | Use a direct verb and clear destination or commitment. | 2–5 words | “Try for free”<br>“Read customer story”<br>“Calculate savings” |
| Reassurance | Acknowledge hesitation without pressure. | 4–14 words | “Still not sure? Explore on your own or with our team.”<br>“How can we help?” |
| Corporate aside | A factual story can include a restrained, self-aware turn. | One short aside | “Well, actually it was a door.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** built to; resolve; simplify; connect; your way; faster; without the complexity; get started; learn more

**Avoid:** revolutionary synergy; limitless disruption; magic with no explanation; fear-based urgency; jargon stacked into noun chains

**Rules:**

- Pair a claim with how it works or a verified proof point.
- Use parallel outcome phrases for selectable demonstrations.
- Make CTA labels describe a real next step, not a vague emotional promise.
- Keep warmth in plain phrasing and occasional wry detail, not exaggerated enthusiasm.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use 68px medium display type for the main promise and 42px for most section leads.
- Keep the 1150px outer container and 24px side padding consistent across text, proof and cards.
- Let 88px vertical padding separate substantive episodes rather than adding separators everywhere.
- Pair 48px lime actions with equally sized transparent secondary actions and a 12px gap.
- Give large editorial panels 40px corners while keeping resource cards and controls at 16px.
- Show one legible product moment over a lime halo instead of shrinking a whole workflow into unreadable detail.
- Place captions and metric labels directly beneath their figures, with 26px suffixes beside 40px proof numerals.
- Finish with the ruled help/contact row even when the preceding footer directory is short.

### Don't

- Do not make all buttons pills; 16px corners are the default, while fully rounded selectors belong to pricing.
- Do not replace medium headlines with heavy 700-weight display type or negative tracking.
- Do not paint ordinary body sections solid lime; reserve it for actions, accents and controlled illumination.
- Do not give every card a drop shadow; strong elevation belongs to the recommended pricing plan, while editorial grouping is mostly flat.
- Do not use bright lime for small paragraphs on white; use ink text and leaf green only for selective emphasis.
- Do not treat pricing figures as giant promotional stats; keep them at 20px with explicit billing text.
- Do not turn capability lists into a field of outlined boxes; the compact index is icon-and-text on white.
- Do not substitute cold blue technology renders for the warm people photography and forest-green product atmosphere.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Declared links animate background-position over 600ms cubic-bezier(0.7,0,0.3,1), producing a moving thin underline.
- Declared resource cards use 300ms cubic-bezier(0.7,0,0.3,1); the navigation position uses top 0.15s linear.
- The dark product theater declares a view-timeline expansion with range entry 65% cover 50%. Several continuous strips declare 40s linear infinite animation.

**Transfer:**

- Keep the underline motion attached to text rather than moving whole buttons.
- Use the expanding dark demonstration as a focused scene change; preserve a stable readable state for each product moment.

**Unknown:**

- Exact playback choreography of canvas interactions, counters and video is not established.
- Mobile animation behavior and reduced-motion variants are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Desktop composition is defined at 1440px. Declared CSS includes boundaries near 768px, 970px, 1150px and 1600px.

**Transfer:**

- As design guidance, stack the opening text above its portrait when two columns no longer fit; preserve the warm negative space and separate interaction cards from the face.
- Allow resource shelves to continue horizontally with the circular controls instead of compressing four narrow cards.
- Stack metrics and quotation as coherent groups, keeping each figure attached to its label and each quote attached to attribution.

**Unknown:**

- Exact narrow-screen type sizes, spacing, menu behavior and breakpoint-specific layouts are unknown.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
